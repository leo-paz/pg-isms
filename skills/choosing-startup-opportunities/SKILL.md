# Choosing Startup Opportunities

Choose a problem and wedge; treat the idea as a hypothesis.

## Route by unresolved job

Use while the direction is unsettled.

- Route readiness, career, or location to `preparing-to-found`.
- Route interviews or observation for a selected direction to `learning-from-users`.
- Route acquisition-channel execution, activation, or expansion for a segment with real use or payment to `acquiring-and-growing-users`.
- Route pricing, sales, or channel architecture after credible need to `designing-business-models`.
- Route what to build or release for a selected direction to `shipping-and-iterating-products`.
- Route cross-domain uncertainty with no specialist owner to `making-calibrated-decisions`.

For a new direction, work in this order: opportunity choice frames candidates; `learning-from-users` owns each hypothesis, sample, evidence standard, and interpretation. Before validation, growth may only recruit that sample or seed a narrow beachhead under the learning plan. Hand primary ownership to growth only after one segment shows real use, payment, or an equally discriminating commitment defined in advance.

## Build the opportunity record

For each candidate, record:

1. named user, observed problem, current workaround, and cost;
2. founder experience, access, capability, and genuine interest;
3. intensity evidence and the adoption effort even a crude version must overcome;
4. smallest useful wedge and a plausible expansion path, not a distant blueprint;
5. the enabling change behind “why now,” with dated current evidence;
6. concrete user benefit and a mission worth sustained effort; and
7. disqualifiers plus the next cheap, discriminating test.

Market size, trends, pitches, clicks, and empty competitor lists do not prove demand.

## Choose the search branch

If founders directly understand a real missing need, test that organic direction first.

Otherwise search deliberately inside genuine expertise: shadow work, become the user, consult for one demanding user, attempt a sale, and log anomalies, awkward workarounds, neglected edges, and newly feasible changes. Unfashionable or strange directions deserve inquiry when domain evidence explains why consensus is wrong; novelty alone is not evidence.

Prefer a narrow group that urgently tries a crude version over broad indifference. Keep ambitious upside only when the wedge has a credible path outward.

Use type-appropriate evidence: deployed enterprise workflows; staged hardware prototypes or capital-heavy milestones; reversible monitored exposure for regulated/high-harm products.

## Narrow or pivot without erasing learning

When demand is weak, separate problem, segment, wedge, access, timing, and delivery assumptions. Preserve actual users, workflows, constraints, and reusable capabilities. Deepen around a small intense group; otherwise test an adjacent problem supported by what the team learned. Do not alternate between rigid persistence and unrelated restarts.

## Return the decision artifact

Give every candidate a dated `deepen`, `stop`, or `discard` disposition and falsifier. Name the chosen problem and wedge, the decisive evidence, remaining timing and expansion risks, and the next gate. Then hand `learning-from-users` an explicit hypothesis, sample, evidence threshold, and review date.

Read [references/provenance.md](references/provenance.md) for claims and exact ranges; revalidate historical timing.
