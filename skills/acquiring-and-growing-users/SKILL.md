# Acquiring and Growing Users

Own distribution changes—not need, artifact, commerce, machinery, participant policy, or survival.

## Route ownership first

- If default-dead or time-to-zero binds, use `managing-runway-and-survival` first.
- `choosing-startup-opportunities` owns direction. `learning-from-users` owns whether/why need exists, sample, standard, interpretation.
- Before validation, execute only learning's recruitment or narrow-seeding instruction. Ownership transfers after one target segment shows real use, payment, or an equally discriminating predeclared commitment; deployment qualifies only when it meets that standard.
- `shipping-and-iterating-products` owns artifacts/exposure; growth specifies cohort outcome. `engineering-for-leverage` owns machinery.
- `designing-business-models` owns payer, price, packaging, sales model, partner-channel economics, and company boundaries; growth executes/measures acquisition channels.
- `competing-and-positioning` owns differentiation and platform dependence; `communicating-clearly` adapts settled facts. Growth owns channel evidence/execution.
- Growth owns marketplace cold-start liquidity. `governing-platforms-and-communities` defines interaction-quality, trust, ranking, reputation, moderation, abuse, transparency, and recourse policy; engineering implements machinery; shipping stages exposure.

## Fill the Growth Record

Label facts/assumptions. Record:

1. company/product/survival state, product type, evidence latency, reversibility, error cost, binding constraint, and current facts to verify;
2. selected segment and inclusion/exclusion rule; urgent or repeated behavior; seed users or sides; predeclared handoff evidence or the learning instruction still governing work;
3. acquisition source → encounter → start → activation or liquidity → retained use/delight → referral or expansion, with rivals such as weak exposure, unclear value, friction, switching/integration, social penalty, missing supply, or weak product;
4. beachhead density and plausible adjacent path; manual action, owner, cohort, channel dependency, and smallest bounded intervention;
5. baseline numerator/denominator, segment/source cohort, trustworthy interval, activation or liquidity event, retention outcome, cost/subsidy/support, confounders, accountable user effects, and privacy/consent/truth guards;
6. calendar review and precommitted keep-manual, repair, expand, pause, or stop thresholds and branches; and
7. decision, preserved alternative, switch condition, and next owner per result.

## Apply the predicates

Pre-gate, recruit cold and warm participants directly under learning's rules. Log source, contact, nonresponse, and scheduling. Clicks, opens, press, praise, waitlists, signups, and subsidies diagnose recruitment or exposure; they do not validate demand. Return behavioral contradictions to learning. Do not optimize a scalable channel yet.

Post-gate, locate the first broken transition before prescribing a channel. Start manually: recruit, onboard, supply, serve, or seed content directly. Scale only after the loop works without hidden founder rescue.

For marketplaces measure qualified request, useful offer, match time, fulfillment, repeat behavior on both sides, cohort retention, service quality, and subsidy dependence. Raw participants, listings, jobs, or volume are not liquidity. If participant harm or policy is binding, governance becomes primary even when aggregate growth is strong.

If faster acquisition degrades activation, retention, delight, economics, interaction quality, or accountable user effects, cap growth and repair the product or channel. Otherwise invest in the proven loop. Never manufacture apparent independent momentum.
