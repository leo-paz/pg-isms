# Preparing to Found

Turn founder ambition into tests. Preserve options without delaying teams with urgent users.

## Route first

Use this skill for an individual's readiness or one active team's operating-location choice.

- Idea selection: `choosing-startup-opportunities`.
- Cofounder terms: `forming-founder-partnerships`.
- Regional founder, capital, talent, or institutional supply: `building-startup-ecosystems`.
- Company roles: `building-and-evolving-organizations`.
- Financing execution: `raising-and-governing-capital`.

For a regional relocation policy, design the ecosystem intervention first, then apply this skill separately to each team. Never turn a regional objective into a blanket mandate.

## Choose a path

Create a decision record:

1. Name the decision, actor, deadline, and commitments that would become hard to reverse.
2. Inventory owned work, direct user contact, domain knowledge, tested collaborators, urgent demand, runway, and health, caregiving, immigration, contractual, and career constraints.
3. Identify the binding unknown. Separate readiness from missing opportunity, partner, capital, or structure. Prefer performed work over age, credentials, stereotypes, networking, or enthusiasm.
4. Select the branch:
   - **Prepare** when cheap, reversible owned work can discriminate readiness.
   - **Begin** with a bounded operating trial when a trusted team has urgent users, payment evidence, and a testable product.
   - **Join** when seeing a real startup operate closes the highest-value gap.
   - **Not now** when motivation, obligations, or evidence do not support founding; preserve the option explicitly.
5. Design one experiment: ship and support something useful for real users, work inside the domain, or complete demanding work with a prospective collaborator. Give it an owner, duration, evidence target, and dated continue/change/stop rule.
6. Before a trial becomes a company, clarify commitment, roles, ownership expectations, and personal runway. Route detailed terms to `forming-founder-partnerships`.

Reject startup theater—pitch events, speculative fundraising, indiscriminate networking, or credentials without users, ownership, or difficult execution.

## Make a location decision by mechanism

Treat a hub as possible access, not a command. For each team, record:

- its industry and needed mechanism: customers, talent, investors, peers, regulation, suppliers, or cultural permission;
- evidence the destination improves that mechanism;
- family, immigration, cost, existing local customers or committed capital, and distraction burdens;
- substitutes and a reversible test; and
- a dated revisit condition.

Move only when the access advantage exceeds full cost and cannot be replaced deliberately. Otherwise stay and build the missing access on purpose. Recheck immigration law, funding geography, remote-work norms, and industry clusters instead of treating old hub evidence as timeless.

## Return the decision

Return the decision and actor, evidence and constraints, binding unknown, next experiment, commitment boundary, observable rule, review date, and neighboring route. End with the next owned action and dated gate, not encouragement.
