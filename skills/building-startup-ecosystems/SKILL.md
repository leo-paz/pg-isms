# Building Startup Ecosystems

Diagnose constraints before hub artifacts; treat deliberate creation as a hypothesis.

## Route the actor and mechanism

Use for regional founder supply, selection, attraction, density, investor access, or institutional friction.

- One active team's location choice: `preparing-to-found`.
- Support delivery for an already selected cohort: `advising-and-supporting-founders`.
- Interaction quality inside a product community: `governing-platforms-and-communities`.

For a regional policy that also affects team locations, design the ecosystem mechanism first; then apply `preparing-to-found` separately to each team.

## Establish the flow baseline

Define geography, actor, horizon, objective, and controllable outcomes. Date current evidence for law, immigration, capital, remote access, and industry geography.

Measure:

- founder/company flows and peer/operator density;
- early-investor expertise, speed, trust, and outside links;
- specialized talent, customers, and university or city friction; and
- jobs, capital, and durable network creation or leakage.

Separate founder mobility, company relocation, jobs, capital, and relationships; departure is not automatically failure.

## Find the limiting reagent and feasibility gate

List every observation as a rival causal hypothesis; require a discriminating measure before naming the weakest.

Before targeted hubs, attraction, cohorts, or eligibility-limited funding, record four gates: controllable constraint, credible selection or reach, community interaction, and local preconditions. If one fails, do not allocate. Evidence-backed diagnosis, general-friction removal, or outside-link work may proceed as a bounded experiment without cohort gates; state why they are inapplicable.

Do not substitute offices, events, compulsory classes, pitch theater, branding, or subsidies for a missing mechanism. When selection matters, use specialist judgment and demonstrated work.

## Design a bounded experiment

Record:

1. mechanism, target population, and explicit uncertainty;
2. named owner, budget, duration, comparison, and cohort or selection rule when targeted;
3. mechanism evidence and multi-year formation, survival, growth, and flow outcomes;
4. mobility, selection, gaming, incentives, reputation feedback, harms, and cost bearers; and
5. separate dated stop, change, expand, and remeasure rules.

Match cadence to evidence latency. Attendance, leases, press, and one cohort do not prove a hub. Separate institutional actions from regional outcomes.

## Preserve mobility without banning attraction

Reject post-award residency locks and local-only rules that isolate founders and may select applicants with fewer alternatives.

A voluntary attraction offer can be tested with credible selection, outside access, and community density. The program owns the regional hypothesis; `preparing-to-found` analyzes each team's industry, family, immigration, cost, and access.

Treat alumni, returners, outside investors, and cross-region ties as assets. Test measured leakage bottlenecks; do not trap people.

## Return the decision record

For a targeted allocation, begin with `Gate check`: controllability, credible selection or reach, community density, and local preconditions; do not allocate until all four appear. For diagnostic, friction, or link work, mark cohort gates inapplicable and why. Then give the baseline, constraint, owner, experiment, mobility, separate stop/change/expand/remeasure rules, and handoffs.
