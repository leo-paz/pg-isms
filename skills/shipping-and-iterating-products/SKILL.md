# Shipping and Iterating Products

Own artifact/exposure; others may own hypothesis, policy, machinery, or commerce.

## Route by ownership

- Learning owns unresolved need, sample, threshold, and interpretation; shipping owns its prototype and exposure.
- `engineering-for-leverage` owns internal technology, tests, architecture, and migration machinery; shipping owns user-affecting exposure.
- Before its gate, growth recruits only under learning. After one segment demonstrates predefined use, payment, or an equivalent commitment, growth owns channel, activation, retention, liquidity, and expansion; shipping owns visible rollout. Do not scale beyond released-workflow evidence.
- Test willingness to pay in learning; route payer, price, packaging, and sales economics to `designing-business-models`.
- `governing-platforms-and-communities` owns ranking, norms, moderation, reputation, and abuse; `engineering-for-leverage` owns machinery, shipping rollout.
- Hand settled facts to `communicating-clearly`; announcement theater is not shipping.

## Fill the Release Record

Use scenario facts; label assumptions. Record:

1. product type and release/redesign state; target user/job; current artifact and validated evidence;
2. smallest complete outcome, uncertainty and rivals, scope, and deferrals;
3. error cost, users, harms, reversibility, and safety, authority, legal, security, or privacy gates;
4. owner; calendar release/review; cohort/inclusion; rollout; support; monitoring; migration; fallback;
5. comparison, outcomes, operational pass/change/stop thresholds including error/harm limits, evidence window, and confounders;
6. pause/rollback trigger; consent; and, when funds move or credible harm exists, incident/restitution owners; and
7. decision branches and next owner for each result.

## Choose the gate before building

For a first release, ship the smallest complete utility for one job. Defer work that neither enables it nor reduces risk. Crudeness is acceptable; useless, deceptive, broken in the core path, or unsafe is not.

For a redesign, installed workflows, shortcuts, compatibility, interruption recovery, trust, and migration cost are requirements. Compare representative new and experienced users. Preserve fallback, stage access, and support migration.

When errors are cheap and reversible, use bounded exposure, core-path testing, named support, monitoring, fallback, and a precommitted pause rule. For safety, money, identity, security, privacy, legal, trust, or community harm, separate performance, workflow or authority, harm, and response-readiness hypotheses. Sequence replay or sandbox, qualified review, shadow operation, explicit consent, human confirmation, staged access, monitoring, rollback, incident response, restitution, and current legal or security checks. Never defer minimum security, authorization, privacy, or data-integrity safeguards.

Match cadence to product type and the shortest trustworthy evidence interval; do not impose weekly software loops on hardware, enterprise integration, or capital-heavy science.

## Observe, decide, repeat

Use informed taste for an initial design and behaviorally equivalent choices; do not design by feature vote. When trustworthy behavior contradicts taste, test novelty, training, migration, segment, measurement, and confounding before revising.

Observe real paths, outcomes, errors, abandonment, repeated use, support, and harm—not approval or launch completion. At the calendar review, expand, make the smallest discriminating change, redesign, pause, rollback, or stop against the recorded thresholds. Return only a named need uncertainty to learning; never pivot silently.
