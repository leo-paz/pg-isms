# Raw evaluation output

Case ID: mayor-office-park-trigger
Reviewer ID: ecosystems-baseline-001

The first job is to learn what actually limits startup formation and survival in this region. Filling the office park is a possible instrument, not a useful definition of success.

For the first 90 days, appoint a small accountable ecosystem team and build a baseline from founder interviews and administrative data. Measure new firms by stage and sector, repeat founders, university commercialization, angel and seed checks, follow-on capital, technical and commercial talent movement, customer access, and where firms move at each stage. Interview founders who stayed, left, failed to start, and recently arrived; map the specific decisions that pushed them. Publish the baseline, gaps, and data limitations.

During months four through six, choose at most three bottlenecks that public action can plausibly affect. For example, the city might test a founder-in-residence program tied to university labs, a visiting-customer program for a concentrated local industry, or a small matching mechanism that brings experienced outside investors into locally led rounds. Give each intervention an owner, target population, budget, leading indicator, and stopping rule. Let founders use the office park only when cheap, flexible space solves an observed problem.

In the second half of the year, run the tests as limited cohorts and compare them with credible historical or matched groups where possible. Track qualified founder starts, relevant introductions that advance, hires filled, first customer contracts, capital raised from independent investors, survival, and moves into or out of the region. Report quarterly, including failures and unintended effects. At year end, continue only programs with evidence of changing founder or company behavior; redesign or close the rest. The first-year deliverable is therefore a measured ecosystem baseline plus a portfolio of small evaluated interventions, not a ribbon-cutting plan.
