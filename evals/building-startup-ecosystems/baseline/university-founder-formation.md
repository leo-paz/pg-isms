# Raw evaluation output

Case ID: university-founder-formation
Reviewer ID: ecosystems-baseline-001

The university should optimize for repeated, serious attempts to solve real problems, not attendance, pitch decks, or occupancy. Start with a semester of discovery: identify labs with potentially transferable work, students and researchers already building, alumni founders and operators willing to help, and the contractual or career barriers that stop people from trying. Establish a baseline for serious projects started, user conversations, prototypes, team formation, licensing time, and projects that continue after a course or competition ends.

In year one, fund small voluntary project teams rather than require entrepreneurship classes. Teams earn modest milestone grants by interviewing users, making a prototype, or testing a scientific risk. Pair them with domain operators and alumni founders through weekly office hours. Create a fast, published path for IP decisions with standard founder-friendly terms and a deadline for the university to respond. Offer students, postdocs, and faculty protected time, leave options, and access to labs. Run small founder dinners and project reviews where unfinished work and disconfirming evidence are welcome; do not award prizes for presentation polish.

Advance only teams that demonstrate learning or demand into a summer build period with stipends, customer introductions, and appropriate lab access. Let teams dissolve or change direction without stigma. Recruit collaborators across departments around specific projects, not generic networking.

In year two, expand the parts that produced persistent teams, independent users, technical progress, or incorporated companies. Add alumni apprenticeships and a visiting-founder program only if year-one evidence shows missing experience is a bottleneck. Measure continuation six and twelve months later, external usage or revenue, founder quality and diversity, team durability, time to license, and follow-on capital from independent sources. Publish cohort-level outcomes and close programs whose main output is events, certificates, or pitches.
