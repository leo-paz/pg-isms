# Raw evaluation output

Case ID: residency-lock-condition
Reviewer ID: ecosystems-baseline-001

Do not adopt either rule. A ten-year residency lock and local-only hiring requirement impose large, uneven costs on founders, restrict access to specialized talent, invite gaming, and may repel the strongest applicants. They also confuse keeping grant recipients with producing more durable local startup activity.

First establish what is happening. For prior grantees, measure when companies moved, which functions moved, their stage, founder and employee residence, capital and customer changes, and the stated reasons for departure. Compare this with eligible non-recipients and interview companies that stayed, left, or declined the grant. The state may discover that a missing lab, investor, customer, or senior hire matters more than the grant terms.

Then test an incentive with a narrow duration and explicit counterfactual. Randomize or phase in a two-year package among otherwise eligible applicants: flexible local workspace or lab access, matching funds for in-state university or supplier relationships, introductions to local customers and investors, and relocation or hiring support that teams may choose. Require ordinary reporting and repayment only for fraud or clearly defined early grant misuse, not for later business necessity.

Pre-register outcomes such as qualified firms applying, jobs and payroll located in the state, local supplier or university relationships, follow-on capital, survival, and retention at 12, 24, and 36 months. Also track applicant quality, program take-up, administrative burden, and whether constrained firms underperform. Use a randomized offer when demand exceeds slots; otherwise use a staged rollout or matched comparison. Continue components that create durable local ties at acceptable cost, and stop those that merely delay an inevitable move.
