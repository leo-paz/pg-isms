Create one 24-month **spinout transaction-and-capital pilot**, owned by the vice president for research, instead of funding the compulsory course, pitch contest, or branded incubator.

**Gate check:** this is institution-wide friction removal plus outside-link work, not a targeted hub, attraction offer, cohort, or eligibility-limited fund. The cohort-selection, community-density, and local-precondition gates are therefore inapplicable; the university is testing mechanisms it controls. It should not make a cohort or incubator allocation unless those gates are later evidenced.

During the first 60 days, publish a dated baseline for every commercialization case from the prior two years: time at each IP-review stage, abandonment reason, founder departure, company formation, investor meetings, financings, and relevant industry. Interview faculty, students, counsel, and investors, and test rival explanations: approval delay, weak inventions or teams, poor deal terms, missing investor expertise, or lack of local peers. The explicit uncertainty is whether shortening transactions and adding credible outside capital access will increase durable formation.

Use the $4 million ceiling in staged tranches:

- $1.6 million for a dedicated deal team, standard term sheets, fixed service deadlines, and a transparent escalation path open to all spinout cases;
- $1.2 million for specialist venture partners who assess demonstrated technical work and make structured introductions to local, alumni, and outside investors;
- $400,000 for independent measurement and matched comparisons with the prior two years and, where feasible, departments adopting the process later; and
- hold $800,000 for the month-12 expand decision rather than committing it to facilities or events.

Track median approval time, stage-level abandonment, companies formed, financing conversion, follow-on capital, 12- and 24-month survival and growth, and durable founder-investor relationships. Report founder, company, jobs, capital, and relationship flows separately. Do not require founders to remain local; alumni, departures, returners, and outside investors can strengthen the network.

Set distinct rules: remeasure process latency and case flow at month 3; stop new spending at month 6 if the university cannot document the workflow or materially reduce controllable delay; change the investor mechanism at month 6 if approvals improve but qualified introductions and financing do not; expand at month 12 only if approval time, formation, and investor conversion beat the dated comparison without evidence of gaming or harmful terms; and remeasure formation, survival, growth, and network ties at month 24. A later incubator proposal should be a separate decision, not an assumed sequel.
