# Raw microtest output

Gate check: this is university-wide IP-friction removal and outside-link work, not a targeted hub, cohort, or eligibility-limited award. Cohort selection and community-density gates are therefore inapplicable; the controllable mechanism is the university’s eighteen-month approval process, while the causal role of investor access remains uncertain.

Use the two years and $4 million for a **spinout-throughput experiment**, owned by the vice president for research—not for compulsory classes, a pitch contest, or a branded incubator.

- Spend $2.0 million on a small empowered licensing team, standard founder-friendly terms, and a published 60-day IP decision service level; $1.2 million on recurring diligence and office hours with qualified outside early-stage investors; and $0.8 million on measurement, founder support during approvals, and contingency.
- Start university-wide, comparing applications submitted after launch with matched pre-launch departments and historical cohorts. Track approval time, abandonment, spinout formation, outside-investor diligence and investment, founder departures and return ties, and—after formation—survival and growth. Treat attendance and pitch counts as diagnostics, not success.
- Remeasure quarterly. At month 6, stop the investor-link component if it produces no qualified diligence; change its sourcing or format if diligence occurs but investment bottlenecks lie elsewhere. Stop or redesign the IP track at month 12 if median decision time has not fallen below 90 days or founders report no reduction in uncertainty. Expand only after the 60-day service level is sustained for two quarters and formation improves without worse deal quality or gaming. At month 24, remeasure formation, survival, capital flows, departures, and durable outside relationships before deciding on permanent funding.

Do not require graduates or founders to remain locally. Count alumni, returners, outside investors, and cross-region ties as ecosystem assets rather than treating every departure as failure.
