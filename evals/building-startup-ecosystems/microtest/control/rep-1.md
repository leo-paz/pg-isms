Do not spend the $4 million on a compulsory course, pitch contest, or branded incubator. Use it to create a two-year university spinout fast lane whose single goal is to get credible teams from research or student projects to independent companies quickly.

In the first six months, publish standard founder-friendly IP terms, appoint a small empowered approval team, and impose a 30-day decision deadline. At the same time, recruit an experienced seed investor to run a modest pre-seed fund and hold open weekly office hours for faculty and students. During months 6–24, invest in small tranches only after teams show evidence of real customer or user demand; pair each investment with rapid company formation, outside mentors, and introductions to follow-on investors. Make participation voluntary and open to people already building, including alumni and local founders, rather than filtering through coursework or pitch performance.

Measure the program by median IP-decision time, companies incorporated, founders who remain active after twelve months, outside capital attracted, and ambitious builders retained locally. If the approval time has not fallen below 30 days by month six, fix that bottleneck before expanding the fund. If teams form but leave because they cannot raise a next round, shift year-two money from programming into investor recruitment and syndication. This addresses the binding constraints—slow formation and missing early capital—while creating visible successes that can gradually make the city more attractive to future founders and investors.
