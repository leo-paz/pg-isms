Use the two years and $4 million to create a **spinout fast lane**, not another entrepreneurship program. The university's binding constraints are the eighteen-month IP process, loss of ambitious founders, and lack of first-check capital; a compulsory course, pitch contest, and branded incubator would add activity without removing those constraints.

Put one accountable leader in charge of a two-year pilot with three linked commitments:

1. Replace bespoke IP negotiation with published standard terms, a small dedicated review team, and a 60-day approval target. Escalate exceptions rather than making every team wait for them.
2. Retain potential founders with twelve- to eighteen-month fellowships, prototype grants, and shared technical support for faculty-student teams. Select teams for demonstrated commitment and evidence of a real problem, then release support against concrete milestones such as prototypes, customer interviews, incorporation, and hiring.
3. Assemble an early-capital network around those teams: recruit local alumni and regional angels, fund regular investor office hours, and use a limited university sidecar fund to match qualified outside checks instead of choosing winners alone.

Budget roughly $1 million for IP-process staff and legal redesign, $2 million for fellowships and milestone-based prototype grants, and $1 million for investor recruitment and the matching sidecar. Review quarterly, but judge the pilot after two years by median IP approval time, credible spinouts formed, founder teams still operating locally, outside capital committed, and follow-on survival—not course enrollment, event attendance, or pitch-contest applications.
