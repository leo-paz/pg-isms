Make one coordinated bet: a two-year **Spinout Fast Track** that removes the university's transaction bottleneck and finances the first proof, instead of funding the compulsory course, contest, or incubator.

Use roughly $1 million to replace case-by-case IP negotiation with published standard terms, a small empowered review team, and a 60-day approval deadline. Put $2 million into competitive proof-of-concept and pre-seed checks for faculty-student teams that reach that deadline, with outside technical and commercial reviewers. Use the remaining $1 million for founder fellowships, visiting operators, and quarterly investor days that bring regional and national early-stage investors to the teams; participation should be optional and tied to building a company, not taking a class.

Set public two-year targets before launch: reduce median IP approval from eighteen months to under sixty days; produce at least twenty investable spinout attempts; retain a meaningful share of founding teams in the city for twelve months after formation; and secure at least one dollar of outside capital for every university dollar invested. Release the second year's capital only if the first-year leading indicators—approval time, qualified teams, and outside-investor engagement—are improving.

This is one program with a single theory of change: faster permission plus initial capital and investor access will convert existing research and ambitious people into companies. A course or pitch contest may increase activity that is easy to count, but neither addresses the stated reasons companies are not forming or staying.
