Use the $4 million for a two-year spinout-bottleneck program rather than making the course, contest, and incubator the centerpiece. The university already appears to have ambition; it is losing companies because formation is slow, founders leave, and first checks are scarce.

In the first six months, set a 60-day faculty-IP decision standard, publish standard license terms, and fund a small deal team empowered to close routine licenses. At the same time, recruit an independent investment committee and outside operators. Over the remaining 18 months, deploy most of the capital through milestone-based founder fellowships and a university-linked pre-seed fund that requires outside matching capital; reserve a smaller share for experienced founders and investors to hold office hours and form teams around promising research. Fellows should receive living support and workspace, not be forced through a generic class.

Release funding in stages against four public measures: median IP-decision time, companies legally formed, outside capital committed, and founders still operating locally after 12 months. If the university cannot cut approval time or attract credible co-investors by month six, stop funding branding and facilities and redirect the remaining money to those two bottlenecks.
