Use the $4 million to create a two-year university spinout pipeline, and make faster commercialization—not classroom participation or pitch-show attendance—the program's success measure.

In the first six months, establish a small commercialization team with authority and service-level targets: screen disclosures within two weeks and approve a standard, founder-friendly IP license within sixty days. Give faculty and student teams clear ownership rules, template agreements, conflict-of-interest help, and a named case manager. If the university cannot shorten the current eighteen-month process, new programming will merely send more frustrated founders into the same bottleneck.

Allocate roughly $1.5 million to competitive proof-of-concept grants for technically credible teams, released in stages against customer discovery, prototype, and regulatory or technical milestones. Reserve about $1 million for a professionally managed pre-seed fund that co-invests only when an independent outside investor participates; this attracts local and regional investors without asking university committees to pick winners alone. Spend another $1 million on the commercialization team, legal process redesign, experienced entrepreneurs-in-residence, and targeted founder recruitment. Keep $500,000 for follow-on support, measurement, and contingencies.

Replace the compulsory course with optional, selective support for teams actively commercializing research or validating a product. A pitch event can remain as a sourcing and investor-convening tool, but it should not be the centerpiece. Likewise, use existing space rather than funding a branded incubator unless teams demonstrate a genuine need for labs or specialized equipment.

Track time from disclosure to decision, licenses executed, teams reaching customer or technical milestones, independent capital committed, founders retained in the city, and companies still operating after eighteen and twenty-four months. Review the pipeline every quarter. At the end of two years, continue only the mechanisms that demonstrably reduced approval time and produced externally validated spinouts.
