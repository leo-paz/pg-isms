# Raw held-out output

Case ID: industrial-software-anchor-access

Generator ID: ecosystems-heldout-9c90-generator

Do not launch the Founders Stay Here Fund as proposed. Its generalist selection, five-year residency lock, and demo-day activity do not establish that grants address the limiting constraint; the lock also risks selecting companies with fewer alternatives and turning healthy outside ties into apparent failure.

Gate check for that targeted allocation:

- Controllable constraint: not established. Slow manufacturer procurement, weak specialist selection, thin peer density, and weak outside-investor follow-through are rival causes.
- Credible selection or reach: absent. Economic-development generalists have not shown industrial-software judgment.
- Community interaction: unproven. Twenty local offices and monthly demos do not guarantee useful repeated contact among relevant founders, operators, customers, and investors.
- Local preconditions: incomplete. A nine-month vendor process could prevent the customer access that would make this location valuable.

Because these gates fail, withhold the eligibility-limited grants. Start a population-wide, two-year industrial-software access experiment instead. Cohort gates are inapplicable because any qualifying startup can use the changed procurement path and tracked introductions; this removes general friction rather than selecting winners.

Name the city economic-development director and one manufacturer procurement executive as joint owners. Reserve a bounded portion of the $12 million to create a standard security and procurement path with a published service-level target, recruit several manufacturers to offer problem briefings and paid pilot routes, and connect founders to specialist industrial-software investors. Instrument every introduction from first meeting through diligence, investment, pilot, customer conversion, later collaboration, and durable relationship. Compare approval time, pilot conversion, formation, survival, fundraising, headquarters, local jobs, and outside relationships with the prior two years and with similar nonparticipating manufacturers where possible.

At the same time, commission an independent baseline that distinguishes the rival hypotheses. Track the formation funnel from student or engineer project to incorporation; survey founders at formation, fundraising, and relocation about ranked causes; measure manufacturer approval time and rejection reasons; have credible outside specialists review a blinded sample of teams; map repeated peer and mentor interactions rather than event attendance; and record whether outside-investor introductions reach diligence and whether relocation preserves local jobs, customers, alumni help, or future investment. A move after seed funding is not by itself ecosystem loss.

Set separate dated rules. Stop the procurement experiment after two review cycles if manufacturers will not honor the process or founders cannot reach real buying decisions. Change it if approval time falls but pilots do not convert, using recorded rejection reasons. Expand it population-wide across more manufacturers only if approval time and qualified pilot conversion improve without shifted compliance harms. Remeasure formation and survival annually, and durable investor, customer, alumni, and headquarters flows at years two and four.

Consider a later targeted fund only if the evidence identifies a controllable capital or formation gap after general friction is reduced, specialist selectors can reliably reach and assess promising teams, a sufficiently dense community can generate repeated useful interaction, and customer, talent, legal, and follow-on-capital preconditions are present. Any trial should then have a named owner, fixed budget and duration, transparent specialist selection, a comparison, multi-year outcomes, and no residency mandate. Use voluntary attraction and strong outside links; evaluate regional value through durable companies, jobs, capital, customers, and relationships rather than forced headquarters retention.
