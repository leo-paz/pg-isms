# Held-out design audit: attempt 002

## Verdict

**Invalid as confirmatory evidence.** Do not use the reported 2/5 score or rejection to judge the skill. C4 cannot be truthfully satisfied from the prompt, and C1/C2 contain hidden exact requirements that reject reasonable prompt-grounded behavior. The response can remain exploratory material, but confirmatory evidence requires a new preregistered design and fresh attempt.

Scope was limited to `attempt-002-design.json`, `prompt.txt`, `response.md`, `score.json`, and `review.md`. The skill and other evaluations were not inspected.

## Fairness and consistency

The scorer is internally consistent: `score.json` and `review.md` agree, and the scorer generally enforced the written strict-conjunctive rule. The problem is primarily criterion design. C1 also shows an application problem because the scorer did not accept a stricter, prompt-grounded relief test as semantic evidence of the intended behavior.

Strict 5/5 acceptance makes these defects decisive. One unavailable backup name makes the entire attempt fail even if every behavior the prompt can fairly test is present. The resulting 2/5 therefore measures rubric brittleness as much as response quality.

## Required remediation

- Supply any fact that must be named literally, especially the temporary-authority backup identities, or accept an unambiguous reference to the April matrix.
- Rewrite C1 to accept any observable, dated, prompt-grounded relief test that routes persistent or structural authority failure to organization work. Do not require one hidden threshold or exact route label.
- Rewrite C2 around decision-relevant uncertainty. Allow a bounded cancellation to close an initiative without a separate research row.
- Preserve C3 and C5's behavior-level checks, while splitting oversized conjunctive criteria when independent failures should remain diagnosable.
- Run a fresh held-out attempt after preregistering the corrected design. A post-hoc rescore of this exposed response may be exploratory, not confirmatory.
