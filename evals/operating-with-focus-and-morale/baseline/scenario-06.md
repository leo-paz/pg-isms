# Two-week operating plan

The financing process should lead for the next two weeks, specifically the two live paths that can produce a decision: resolving the expiring term sheet and completing Friday's diligence request. This is a temporary deadline-driven choice, not permission for fundraising to consume the whole company. Customer continuity is the protected constraint; broad investor networking, optional product work, and speculative partnerships yield to those two priorities.

## Outcomes by the end of two weeks

1. Decide whether the term sheet can become acceptable after governance and liquidation terms are resolved; accept or decline it deliberately before expiry.
2. Deliver a complete, accurate data room and a small set of well-prepared customer references by Friday, then determine whether that fund remains a credible alternative.
3. Keep customer response times and critical product reliability within explicit minimum standards without relying on late nights.
4. End the sprint with a clear financing decision or a tightly limited next step, then return the company to a product-and-customer-led cadence.

## Stop and defer immediately

- Cancel or postpone most of the 25 introductory calls. Keep only meetings with investors who fit the round, can move on the current timeline, and have a plausible path to partnership. Offer everyone else a polite update after the current term-sheet window. Momentum is measured by progress with qualified investors, not meeting count.
- Evaluate the two partnership meetings against one test: can this partnership materially affect revenue or financing within the next 60 days? Keep at most the qualifying meeting, delegate it away from the CEO if possible, and defer the other. Do not create follow-up work during this sprint unless the opportunity is exceptional.
- Pause nonessential features and the promise of a normal weekly release. Ship only customer-critical fixes or work already safe to release. Announce the temporary release policy internally and to affected customers where necessary.
- Reject new diligence requests that are duplicative, low-value, or lack a clear deadline and decision process.

## Assign clear ownership

The CEO owns the financing outcome, investor qualification, negotiation, and final decisions. The CEO should not own document collection or scheduling.

Name one person as diligence coordinator for the two weeks. That person maintains a single request tracker, assigns owners, checks the data room for consistency, and batches questions to the team. Each request must have an investor, purpose, owner, deadline, and status. Investors receive existing materials first; bespoke analysis requires CEO approval.

The product lead should not coordinate fundraising. For two weeks, remove weekly-release ownership from their load and protect their time for customer support triage and critical product decisions. Assign a rotating daily support responder from the rest of the team, with the product lead handling only escalations. A second teammate should own release execution for emergency fixes.

For customer references, choose a few customers with strong, recent outcomes. The CEO asks for consent and explains the context; an assigned teammate prepares each customer with likely topics and keeps calls short. Do not surprise customers or repeatedly use the same references.

Use outside counsel to turn the unresolved term-sheet issues into a short decision memo: proposed language, economic or control impact, acceptable position, and walk-away point. Schedule one focused negotiation rather than letting comments sprawl across messages.

## Daily operating cadence

Hold a 15-minute morning check-in with only three categories:

- Financing blockers due within 72 hours
- Customer or reliability risks
- Work that should be stopped or reassigned

The diligence coordinator publishes one written status update by late afternoon. Batch all requests into one daytime window; no direct investor requests should go to individual employees. Establish a hard cutoff for routine work in the evening. Only a production incident or a genuinely time-sensitive negotiation can break it, and the CEO must explicitly make that call.

Track a small scoreboard: term-sheet issues remaining, Friday diligence completeness, qualified investor next steps, oldest unresolved customer issue, critical incidents, and after-hours exceptions. Do not add general productivity metrics.

## Decision gates

By the day before the term-sheet deadline, the CEO and counsel decide among accept, continue only with a written extension, or decline. No silent expiration and no acceptance of unresolved control or liquidation consequences simply because the team is tired.

After the deadline, reassess the round. If there is an acceptable signed term sheet, stop new investor introductions and move into a controlled closing process. If not, retain only the strongest active prospects and set a fixed weekly cap on fundraising time and meetings. The product-and-customer workflow again becomes primary.

At the end of two weeks, run a 30-minute retrospective: what created avoidable work, what customer commitments slipped, and what recovery time is needed. Cancel the temporary cadence, restore sustainable product ownership, give compensatory time after exceptional late work, and communicate the next operating priority in writing. The morale reset comes chiefly from fewer simultaneous priorities, real permission to defer work, protected evenings, and visible follow-through from the CEO.
