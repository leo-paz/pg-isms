# Decide survivability before optimizing focus

The first decision is whether the company can remain solvent through the next eight weeks, not how to improve its calendar. At current figures, dependable net burn is about $185,000 per month ($210,000 of fixed obligations less $25,000 of gross profit), so $320,000 represents roughly 1.7 months of runway before considering payment timing or one-time costs. The possible renewal arrives after that window and is not dependable; an unsigned bridge should be valued at zero until funds are committed and closing conditions are understood.

## In the next 24 hours

The CEO and finance lead should produce a day-by-day, 13-week cash forecast showing opening cash, payroll, taxes, debt, rent, vendor payments, receivables, and minimum operating expenses. Confirm that payroll and associated taxes can be paid in twelve days, identify any restricted cash, and check the legal consequences of delaying each obligation with counsel and the accountant. Do not improvise by missing payroll.

At the same time, freeze discretionary spending, new hiring, purchases, and nonessential contracts. Accelerate collectible receivables and ask vendors or the landlord for extensions where doing so is credible. Require written answers from bridge investors: amount, terms, decision maker, remaining diligence, signing date, funding date, and failure conditions. Seek alternatives in parallel rather than letting bridge discussions substitute for a plan.

The board plan tomorrow should present three explicit cases:

1. **Funded case:** the minimum bridge amount and date required to provide a defensible runway, with named closing milestones.
2. **Self-preservation case:** immediate reductions needed to bring cash outflow below a chosen survival threshold, including the timing, cash cost, and operational effect of each reduction.
3. **Failure case:** the trigger date for an orderly wind-down or other formal action if funding is not secured, with legal guidance and employee/customer communication responsibilities.

Choose the triggers now—for example, signed financing by a specific date and cash received by another—not “when confidence drops.” Any headcount decision should be made promptly and humanely from the cash plan; waiting for the renewal could consume the remaining options. Protect payroll, statutory obligations, customer commitments, core assets, and an orderly process ahead of preserving the current plan or avoiding a difficult message.

## Focus work that belongs now

Only work tied to cash, financing, retention of dependable gross profit, or preserving enterprise value belongs in the immediate plan. Assign one accountable owner to each of these streams:

- CEO: financing, board alignment, and decision triggers.
- Finance/operations: daily cash control, collections, obligations, and contingency execution.
- Product and engineering: only delivery or reliability work required to retain current paying customers, satisfy a near-term contractual milestone, or materially strengthen financing.
- Commercial lead: collect receivables, protect current accounts, and advance the renewal without including it in the survival forecast.

Pause speculative features, broad strategy work, internal process projects, and ordinary calendar redesign. A short daily cash-and-blockers check and a twice-weekly written company update are enough; cancel meetings that do not serve the four streams above.

Morale should be addressed through clarity, fairness, and speed rather than a reset event. Tell the team what is known, what is uncertain, which decisions will be made by what dates, and when they will hear next. Do not promise that the bridge or renewal will close. Once the company has a funded or executable survival plan, it can redesign the calendar and restore longer-horizon focus. Until then, focus means concentrating the organization on extending decision time and preserving its ability to act.
