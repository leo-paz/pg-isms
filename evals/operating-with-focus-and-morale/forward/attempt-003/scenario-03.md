# Four-week focus and morale operating record

## Header

**Date:** July 15, 2026. **Decision:** handle the conference without extending the damaging push, restore release judgment and user contact, and test one customer outcome by August 12. **Primary route:** `building-and-evolving-organizations`, because late-night founder approvals and reversed decisions are now an authority bottleneck. **Supporting route:** `operating-with-focus-and-morale`. There is no survival or financing route: sixteen months of runway, no near-payroll threat, and no active fundraise are reported. The CEO is accountable; the two engineering leads own delegated product and release branches. Checks are July 17, July 22, July 29, and August 5; final review is August 12.

## Route and immediate action

For the first 48 hours, install a written authority map before creating any “focus blocks.” Engineering Lead A becomes final release authority and incident commander; Engineering Lead B becomes final authority for the selected customer problem and routine scope choices. Decisions inside agreed scope no longer wait for the CEO. The CEO retains only pricing, material legal or fiduciary exposure, and irreversible commitments. A board director is the backup for an unavailable or impaired CEO. Existing support, production, payroll, privacy, legal, and customer-continuity duties remain protected.

## Evidence ledger

**Observed:** the company has 12 people, 70 paying customers, and 16 months of runway. The CEO has worked about 78 hours weekly for ten weeks, including most nights. In the last month she reversed three implemented release decisions, the team rolled back three production changes, nine user calls were missed, and two leads waited for late-night approvals.

**Claimed:** another four-week push is necessary to demonstrate seriousness. That is not evidence of customer value, conference value, or sound judgment.

**Consequential unknowns:**

- Conference owner, appointed by the CEO, will confirm the exact event date, contractual commitments, attendee list, and scheduled customer meetings by July 17. If there are fewer than five qualified customer meetings, the company sends one representative and does no conference-specific build.
- Lead B will contact the nine missed users by July 17 and complete eight calls by July 24. If fewer than five independently describe the selected pain as frequent and consequential, no feature is built; the team selects the next pain from those calls.
- Lead A will reconstruct each reversal and rollback by July 18. If two or more involve fatigue, ambiguous authority, or late approval, the authority change remains in force beyond August 12 and the board reviews the CEO role design.
- The board director checks privately with the CEO by July 16 about sleep, health symptoms, and decision capacity. Acute symptoms go promptly to qualified medical help; the CEO steps out of release decisions until fit. This is a safeguard, not a diagnosis.

## One state-changing priority

By August 12, validate and safely deliver one narrow improvement to a problem confirmed by at least five of eight interviewed paying customers, with at least five target users completing the improved workflow within seven days, zero rollback, and no decision waiting more than one business day.

The July 15 baseline is nine missed calls, three recent rollbacks, and two blocked leads; workflow-use baseline is unknown and Lead B records it before development. Lead B owns customer evidence and scope; Lead A owns implementation and release. The proposed mechanism is that direct user evidence plus delegated authority produces a smaller, useful, reversible release. The rival explanation is that the real constraint is product reliability, not approval latency. Earliest honest customer checkpoint is July 24; usage can be judged seven days after release. A rollback, fewer than five pain confirmations, or fewer than five successful uses falsifies the current tactic.

## Conference and stop/defer list

- **Bound:** the conference owner gets at most 24 staff-hours through the event for logistics, a stable-product demo, and prebooked customer meetings. The demo uses released or staging-tested capability; no production promise depends on conference day.
- **Cancel:** custom booth features, launch theater, and unscheduled collateral, effective July 16; Lead A enforces the release boundary.
- **Delegate:** all logistics and meeting scheduling to the conference owner on July 16. The CEO may attend prebooked customer meetings but is not the logistics owner.
- **Defer:** unrelated roadmap work, optional media, new productivity tooling, and internal redesign beyond the temporary authority map until August 12. Existing customer incidents and critical maintenance are exempt.

## Protected execution and workload

The CEO takes two full recovery days by July 19, has no routine night or weekend work, and is capped at 45 hours weekly through August 12. This is a ceiling, not a target. Any exceptional incident work is offset the next day and ends when the incident closes. Lead A covers releases and P0 incidents; Lead B covers customer and scope decisions. Escalations use a one-page decision note with evidence, options, reversibility, and deadline. Only P0 customer harm, material security/privacy risk, or legal/fiduciary exposure can interrupt recovery; response ownership sits with Lead A or the qualified functional owner before the CEO.

Lead A and the delivery pair receive four three-hour maker blocks weekly. Lead B protects two user-call blocks weekly and names a backup for every booked call. Meetings are limited to a 20-minute Monday plan, 15-minute daily release-risk check, and 30-minute Friday evidence review. Notifications are off during maker blocks; approval requests receive a business-hours response within four hours from the delegated owner.

## Morale, persistence, and decision rules

The morale signal is not “insufficient seriousness”; it is reversals, rollbacks, skipped users, blocked ownership, and prolonged overload. Restore agency through real decision rights, visible user evidence, a four-week finish line, private board support, and recovery. The goal premise is that solving a recurring paying-customer problem can improve retention and product pull. The failing tactic is conference-led urgency mediated by continuous CEO approval. Today’s decision is **adapt** that tactic, not abandon the premise.

At each check, Lead B records calls, pain confirmations, workflow use, and confounders; Lead A records approval latency, defects, reversals, rollbacks, and safeguard status. **Continue**, owned by both leads, if pain confirmation, usage, release safety, and workload limits hold. **Adapt**, owned by Lead B, if the pain is confirmed but usage misses: change one variable—scope, workflow, or segment—and run one bounded follow-up. **Reopen-goal**, owned by the CEO with the board director on August 12, if two discriminating tests across plausible workflows or segments each fail to produce five pain confirmations or five uses. **Stop**, owned by Lead A for the release and the board director for the push, on a rollback, acute health/safety boundary, or material legal/security risk. The preserved option is a modest conference presence using the stable product and booked customer conversations. Handoff back to ordinary focus operation occurs August 12 only if delegated authority works, CEO workload is within limit, and user and release cadence are restored.
