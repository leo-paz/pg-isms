# Two-week financing decision sprint

## Header

- **Record date:** July 15, 2026. **Horizon:** July 15–29. **Exact decision:** resolve the live Series A choice without sacrificing customer continuity or team judgment.
- **Primary route:** `raising-and-governing-capital`, because diligence, references, unresolved terms, and an expiring offer create dated financing decisions. **Supporting route:** `operating-with-focus-and-morale`, used to bound attention and workload. This is not primarily a runway emergency: reported runway is twenty months. It is not presently an organization redesign.
- **Accountable owner:** CEO. Product lead owns release/support continuity. Qualified financing counsel and the board own legal, governance, liquidation, and fiduciary review. Checks: July 17, 20, 22, and 24; final review July 29.

## Evidence ledger

**Observed as of July 15:** 55 paying customers; reported twenty-month runway; weekly releases and support owned by the product lead; a three-week-old $3 million raise; 25 accepted introductions; two partnership meetings due within ten days; a Friday data-room/reference request; one term sheet expiring July 22 with unresolved governance and liquidation terms; slipping user calls; and team fatigue from late-night requests. The CEO verifies cash/runway with the finance record by July 16; a result below twelve months immediately reopens routing.

**Claimed:** the CEO says every meeting is required to preserve momentum. Call volume is not evidence of financing quality. The team’s tiredness is credible context; the operating evidence is late-night requests and slipped user calls.

**Consequential unknowns:**

- By July 16, counsel and CEO produce a one-page term comparison covering control, dilution, liquidation, and closing risk. The board marks acceptable bounds. Terms outside a hard bound make signing unavailable; movement inside them keeps negotiation live.
- By July 17, the CEO scores all 25 investors by check fit, stage, decision speed, references, and partner access. At most eight scoring at least four of five proceed; fewer than three means seek an extension or prepare to pause, not restore all calls.
- By July 17, the product lead reports support response, release risk, missed user contacts, after-hours requests, and capacity. Any uncovered critical incident or two safeguard breaches cuts financing meeting volume immediately.
- Customer willingness to act as references is unknown. The CEO requests explicit consent by July 16; at least three willing, representative customers supports the reference process. No customer is pressured or surprised.

## One state-changing priority

By July 22, obtain a board-authorized financing path: either an amended term sheet inside preapproved economic and governance bounds, or a documented decline/extension decision supported by credible alternatives; then close, pause, or end the sprint by July 29. Baseline is one expiring unresolved offer and no verified comparison set. Target is one acceptable path plus a dated closing plan, while weekly release/support coverage and two direct customer conversations per week remain intact.

The proposed mechanism is concentrated negotiation plus a small high-fit comparison set. The rival is that broad meeting volume creates a better offer. Evidence latency is days for investor engagement and term movement; July 20 is the earliest honest signal. The tactic is falsified if screened investors produce neither diligence nor partner-level follow-up and the current fund will not move or extend by July 22. The cycle ends July 29.

## Stop, defer, and bound

- **Defer** the unscreened remainder of the 25 calls today; CEO owns courteous rescheduling after July 29. **Bound** qualified calls to eight total, 45 minutes each, inside two daily financing windows.
- **Defer** both partnership meetings until after July 29 unless the CEO documents a contractual or near-term revenue deadline by July 16; if one qualifies, cap it at 30 minutes and delegate preparation.
- **Bound** the data room to the requesting fund’s written checklist, controlled access, and counsel-reviewed materials. CEO batches team requests once daily at 2 p.m.; no bespoke analysis without a financing decision use.
- **Delegate** weekly releases, support decisions, and incident command to the product lead. **Cancel** nonessential internal status meetings and new product scope for two weeks.

## Protected execution and morale

The product lead gets 9 a.m.–noon maker blocks Monday–Thursday, interrupted only for a defined critical incident. The CEO has financing blocks 10 a.m.–noon and 2–4 p.m.; other availability is one 30-minute daily escalation window. One CEO and one product-lead customer conversation are protected weekly, excluding investor references. Support, safe releases, payroll, privacy/security, and legal/fiduciary duties remain essential.

No routine diligence request goes to the team after 5 p.m.; it enters the next 2 p.m. batch. No compulsory nights or weekend work. Team members cap at 45 hours weekly and the CEO at 55 for this dated sprint, with one fully off evening daily and one full weekend day. The product lead is backup for urgent customer continuity, not financing judgments; counsel/board are the escalation destination for term decisions. The exceptional cadence ends July 29.

Morale is treated as a signal of fragmented work, lost user contact, unclear duration, and recovery debt—not proof that the market or financing goal is invalid. The goal premise is that suitable capital now accelerates a validated business on acceptable terms. The current tactic is a 25-call breadth strategy. Visible daily request queues, restored user contact, private check-ins on July 17 and 24, and a fixed finish line test whether operating conditions improve. Continued exhaustion despite these bounds triggers workload reduction and, for health concerns, qualified help.

## Cadence and precommitted decision

The CEO updates a daily financing ledger: investor stage, next decision, term movement, owner, and date. The product lead updates continuity and workload measures on July 17 and 24. Counsel reports term risks July 16 and negotiated movement July 21. The board decides July 22 and reviews the final state July 29.

- **Continue:** acceptable term movement or credible competing partner diligence appears by July 21, and continuity/workload safeguards hold.
- **Adapt:** investor interest exists but the offer misses bounds; change one variable—structure, investor set, or timeline—and obtain a written extension capped at seven days.
- **Reopen-goal:** by July 29, two credible investor/structure tests fail to produce an acceptable path, or verified growth needs no longer justify the raise. CEO and board then reconsider timing and amount.
- **Stop:** counsel/board identifies a hard legal or fiduciary boundary, the fund rejects a nonnegotiable bound by July 22, or customer/health continuity cannot be protected.

The decision now is to lead with the financing workflow and reject a generic reset. The preserved option is to pause fundraising and return to product/customer evidence with verified runway. Handoff back to `operating-with-focus-and-morale` occurs July 29 only when the offer is signed, declined, or paused; external obligations are closed; and ordinary availability is restored.
