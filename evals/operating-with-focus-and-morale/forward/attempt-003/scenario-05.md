# Six-week operating record

**Header.** Date: July 15, 2026. Decision: remove the CEO as the routine decision queue while preserving control of exceptional risk. Horizon: July 15-August 26. Primary route: `building-and-evolving-organizations`; supporting route: `operating-with-focus-and-morale`. Accountable owner: CEO. Operating owner: a lead the CEO names today, preferably the existing operations leader. Checks: July 22, July 29, August 5, August 12, August 19; final review August 26.

## Route and evidence

Seventeen months of runway and no fundraising mean no survival or financing process currently outranks this problem, subject to the finance lead verifying cash and payroll by July 17. The binding evidence is repeated authority failure: 18 decisions waited over four business days, five signed commitments reached product after signature, and two incidents lacked one decision owner. Therefore calendar optimization cannot be primary. Security, legal, fiduciary, safety, employment, and customer-continuity matters retain qualified owners and immediate escalation.

**Observed:** the CEO approves five decision classes; the six-week delays and commitment failures above occurred; both incidents had multiple responders; department leads exist; decision rights are unwritten. **Claimed:** the CEO's real problem is calendar crowding, and meeting cancellation, new software, and two deep-work mornings would solve it. **Unknown:** which decisions genuinely require CEO judgment; whether lead capacity, incentives, or missing cross-functional gates cause the queue; and whether delegation can improve speed without customer or financial harm.

The operating lead will export six weeks of approval messages, contracts, incident records, roadmap changes, and hiring decisions by July 18, classify every item by type, wait time, reversibility, and impact, and interview the five functional leads by July 21. If at least 70% fit pre-agreed, reversible bounds, they leave the CEO queue. Heads of Sales and Product will audit the five late commitments by July 22; any promise signed without documented feasibility becomes subject to a pre-signature delivery gate. Head of Engineering will run an incident-command tabletop by July 23; every simulated Sev-1/2 must name one commander within ten minutes or the model is revised before live use.

## One priority

By August 26, at least 90% of in-bound pricing, delivery, incident, roadmap, and senior-hiring decisions will be decided by a named role within two business days; no customer commitment will first reach product after signature; and every Sev-1/2 will have one decision owner within ten minutes. Baselines are 18 decisions over four days, five late commitments, and two ownerless incidents in six weeks. The mechanism is explicit bounded authority plus visible escalation rules. Rivals are insufficient lead capacity and broken sales/product handoffs. A flat queue after two weeks of live delegation, repeated unsafe decisions, or customer harm falsifies the current tactic.

## Six-week action

- **Week 1, July 15-21:** name the operating owner; freeze new discretionary CEO approvals; inventory decisions; immediately install an incident commander rota and require Product feasibility on nonstandard promises. Existing customer, incident, payroll, legal, and hiring deadlines continue.
- **Week 2, July 22-28:** the CEO and leads publish a one-page decision-rights register containing owner, bounds, required consultation, response time, evidence artifact, escalation trigger, backup, and temporary rollback rule for each class. Resolve every aged item and tell affected teams who now decides.
- **Week 3, July 29-August 4:** run delegation live. Leads decide inside bounds and log outcomes; the CEO observes without pre-approving. A 30-minute twice-weekly exception clinic handles only decisions that cross written thresholds.
- **Week 4, August 5-11:** remove the CEO from workflows that met the safety and quality thresholds. Audit contracts, incidents, reversals, and decision time. If one function remains slow, fix its capacity or interface rather than restoring every approval.
- **Week 5, August 12-18:** test backups during one planned absence per owner, revise ambiguous thresholds, and sample affected employees and five customers or account owners for delay or surprise.
- **Week 6, August 19-26:** stabilize the register, assign monthly audit ownership, publish results, and take the precommitted final branch.

The initial rights are concrete: Head of Sales owns pricing within an approved discount and margin band; Finance owns the band, and the CEO sees exceptions below its floor. Head of Product owns roadmap changes inside the current strategic outcome; only a strategy change returns to the CEO. Head of Product or Delivery must accept nonstandard delivery promises before signature. The on-call incident commander owns live priorities, escalating legal, safety, material-security, or continuity risk. The hiring executive owns senior searches and offers within approved headcount and compensation; the CEO retains direct-report hires and exceptions outside those bounds.

## Stop/defer and protected execution

Cancel the productivity-system purchase for six weeks. Defer conferences, media, and optional investor meetings unless a dated external decision emerges. Do not blanket-cancel recurring meetings: each lead removes the CEO only after authority and coverage transfer, and bounds remaining status meetings to written updates plus exceptions. Freeze new roadmap initiatives unrelated to this outcome.

A personal focus plan belongs only after delegation releases real capacity. Beginning Week 3, the CEO may protect Tuesday 9-11 for strategy and Thursday 9-11 for direct customer learning; these blocks replace approval work and do not override incidents or essential coordination. Leads receive two-hour maker blocks twice weekly. The operating lead is backup for the register; functional backups cover each decision class, with urgent escalation acknowledged within 30 minutes and recorded in the shared decision log. Cap normal work at 45 hours per week, preserve one meeting-light half-day and two recovery days weekly, and authorize no compulsory nights. There is no exceptional push; health or judgment degradation triggers immediate recovery and backup coverage.

## Morale, cadence, and decision rules

Treat morale as an operating signal, not a verdict. The relevant observed signals are waiting, surprise commitments, duplicated incident response, and constrained lead agency. Restore visible ownership and customer evidence; review mistakes privately and without blame. The goal premise is that capable leads can own bounded decisions safely. The tactic is this rights register, not CEO endurance or calendar polish.

Every Friday the operating lead reports median and 90th-percentile decision time, aged queue, CEO-escalation share, late commitments, incident-owner time, reversals, customer harm, lead workload, and skipped recovery. On August 26: **continue** if the 90%/two-day target and both zero-failure targets hold with safeguards; **adapt** one bound, interface, or capacity constraint if speed improves but a target misses without material harm; **reopen-goal** if two materially different delegation tests still leave over 20% aged or produce repeated serious errors; **stop** the affected delegation immediately for legal, safety, security, fiduciary, payroll, or material customer harm. The preserved option is a documented 48-hour rollback for the affected class, not restoration of all CEO approvals. The CEO owns every final branch.

**Lineage.** Route precedence, evidence classes, record structure, and branch thresholds are cross-source taxonomy synthesis, not an attributed founder recipe. Qualified owners must revalidate current legal, financial, employment, security, health, and customer facts.
