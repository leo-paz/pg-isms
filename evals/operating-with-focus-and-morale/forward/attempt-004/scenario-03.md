# Focus and morale operating record

## Header

**Date:** July 15, 2026. **Decision:** shrink the conference commitment, remove the CEO as the release-approval bottleneck, and restore one trustworthy customer-learning/release loop. **Horizon:** four weeks, ending August 12. **Primary route:** `building-and-evolving-organizations`, because two leads are blocked on repeated late-night CEO approvals and decision authority is unclear. **Supporting route:** `operating-with-focus-and-morale`, for attention, recovery, and persistence rules. There is no active financing process or near-payroll threat. The CEO is accountable for the transition; Engineering Lead 1 owns releases, Engineering Lead 2 is backup. Checks: July 17, July 22, July 29, August 5; final review August 12.

## Evidence ledger

- **Observed:** 70 paying customers and 16 months of runway; about 78 CEO hours weekly for ten weeks, including most nights; three post-implementation decision reversals, three production rollbacks, nine skipped user calls, and two leads waiting for late-night approval in the last month. These records justify immediate workload and authority safeguards, not a conclusion about demand.
- **Claimed:** the CEO says four more weeks of pushing proves seriousness. Hours and conference visibility do not establish seriousness, customer value, or market demand.
- **Unknown—conference value:** by July 22, the customer owner will contact 12 target attendees/customers and log whether they will take a meeting or evaluate the current product. Threshold: at least six confirmed relevant meetings, including three paying customers or qualified prospects. Below that, reduce attendance to one relationship owner with no custom build; at or above it, keep only the bounded plan below.
- **Unknown—approval cause:** by July 17, Engineering Lead 1 will audit the last ten release decisions and document which required CEO judgment. Threshold: at least 80% decidable under written release/rollback rules. If met, authority transfers immediately; if not, the CEO and leads define the few reserved decisions and a daytime escalation rule.
- **Unknown—customer problem and adoption:** by July 22, the customer owner will complete eight rebooked calls and rank one repeated problem. Six of eight must place it among their top two pains. Otherwise, do not build it; select a better-supported problem or reopen the segment assumption.

## One priority

By August 12, five paying customers will use or explicitly validate one small improvement selected from the eight calls, with no rollback and no release approval waiting longer than one business day. The dated baseline is nine missed calls, three rollbacks, three reversals, and two approval queues in the prior month; current usage of the candidate improvement is unknown. Engineering Lead 1 owns delivery, and the customer owner owns validation.

The proposed mechanism is that direct user evidence, narrow scope, and delegated release authority restore a reliable learning loop. The rival is that customers do not care enough about the chosen problem, regardless of execution quality. Earliest honest product checkpoint is one week after five customers receive the improvement. Falsifier: fewer than three of five use it twice or confirm it solves the ranked pain, or a rollback caused by bypassing required review. Essential support, incident response, payroll, security, legal/fiduciary work, and existing customer commitments remain protected.

## Stop and defer now

- **Cancel:** conference-only features, a bespoke demo, and production changes whose only purpose is stage polish; Engineering Lead 1 enforces this from July 16.
- **Bound:** at most two staff on conference preparation, no more than 40 combined hours through August 12; CEO time is capped at four daytime hours weekly. Use the stable product. The CEO owns the cap.
- **Defer:** optional media, investor outreach, broad redesign, new tooling, and unrelated roadmap work until August 12; their existing owners record them in a parking list.
- **Delegate:** conference logistics to one named relationship owner by July 16. Replace daily status meetings with one 25-minute twice-weekly decision review; Engineering Lead 2 maintains the written handoff.

## Protected execution and workload

After a two-day handoff and recovery window, the CEO works no nights or weekends, no more than 48 hours in the first transition week and 45 thereafter. She has two recurring 90-minute daytime strategy blocks, but does not re-enter delegated releases. Engineering Lead 1 has two three-hour maker blocks on four weekdays; the customer owner has two call blocks weekly. Notifications are off during blocks.

Engineering Lead 2 is release backup and may roll back immediately under the written runbook. Reserved CEO decisions receive a daytime response within four business hours; after that, the backup rule governs. Incidents, security, safety, legal duties, and urgent customer-continuity issues override focus blocks and go to the qualified current owner. On July 17, a trusted board member or adviser privately checks sleep, health symptoms, conflict, and judgment. Concerning symptoms go to qualified medical help; they are not treated as a motivation issue.

## Morale, cadence, and decision rules

The morale signal is behavioral: reversals, rollbacks, missed users, and blocked leads. Test workload-driven judgment degradation and blocked agency before treating the hard month as proof the company or market is invalid. The goal premise is that a recurring customer problem can support valuable product improvement. The current tactic is an intense conference-centered push; the tactic is not the goal. Give each owner visible weekly progress, direct customer evidence, private support, and a credible August 12 finish line.

At every Wednesday check, the CEO records completed calls, customer signal, adoption, rollbacks, decision-wait time, conference hours, CEO hours, nights worked, and safeguard breaches. **Continue** if the customer threshold and release safeguards hold. **Adapt** one material variable—problem, scope, segment, or delivery—if the problem is supported but fewer than three of five validate the improvement. **Reopen-goal** after two bounded tests across plausible problems or segments both miss their precommitted validation threshold. **Stop** conference-specific work immediately if it causes a safeguard breach, customer-continuity failure, or unplanned production risk; stop the product premise if repeated tests invalidate it or a safety, legal, or survival boundary is crossed. The CEO owns continue/adapt; the CEO plus board owns reopen/stop.

**Decision now:** delegate approvals, recover, and run the bounded customer loop. Preserve a small stable-product conference presence, reversible after the July 22 evidence check. The operating structure, route precedence, evidence classes, and combined thresholds are cross-source synthesis, not a Paul Graham prescription. Revalidate the workload, health, customer, security, legal, fiduciary, and runway facts with their qualified owners.
