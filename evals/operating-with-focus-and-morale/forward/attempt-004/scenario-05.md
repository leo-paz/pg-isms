# Six-week operating record

**Header.** Date: July 15, 2026. Decision: replace the CEO approval queue with explicit decision rights and escalation paths. Horizon: July 15–August 26. Primary route: `building-and-evolving-organizations`; `operating-with-focus-and-morale` supplies the supporting cadence only after authority is released. The company has 48 people, 17 months of runway, no financing process, and no stated acute survival risk. The CEO is accountable; by July 16 she appoints one department lead as Decision Systems Owner (DSO). Checks are July 22, July 29, August 5, August 12, and August 19; final review is August 26.

**Evidence ledger.** Observed: over the six weeks ending July 15, 18 decisions waited more than four business days for the CEO; five signed commitments reached product after signature; two incidents had several responders and no single decision owner; department leads exist and rights are unwritten. The DSO will verify these from approval logs, contracts, incident records, and interviews by July 18. Claimed: the CEO says the problem is her calendar; this is context, not diagnosis. Unknown: the volume and delay by decision category, whether leads have enough context and capacity, and whether poor intake or understaffing rivals unclear authority as the cause. The DSO samples all five categories and 30 recent decisions by July 18. If more than 25% lack a clear intake artifact, repair intake alongside rights; if a lead lacks capacity, remove work or name a different owner before delegation.

**One priority.** By August 26, at least 90% of pricing, delivery, incident, roadmap, and senior-hiring decisions will have a named owner and be decided within their published service level; no enterprise commitment will be signed without recorded product/delivery acceptance; every qualifying incident will have one incident commander within 15 minutes. Baselines are 18 waits over four days, five late commitments, and two ownerless incidents. The mechanism is authority plus context, service levels, and escalation; the rival is inadequate capacity or intake. Weekly records provide honest evidence. Any customer, safety, legal, security, or fiduciary harm caused by a delegated category is its falsifier and triggers immediate containment and review.

**Six-week action.** 

- **July 15–18:** The CEO and DSO map the five decision types, recent examples, current wait, stakes, and legitimate CEO-only boundaries. They publish a one-page register naming the decision owner, required inputs, consultees, service level, backup, escalation threshold, and log location.
- **July 21–August 1:** Pilot three high-frequency paths. The sales lead decides pricing inside published margin bands; only below-floor exceptions escalate. Product/delivery accepts or rejects enterprise promises before signature. The on-call incident commander owns incident priorities, with a named backup and severity-based CEO escalation. The DSO audits every decision weekly.
- **August 4–15:** Extend the register to roadmap changes and senior hiring. The product lead owns roadmap tradeoffs inside the agreed company outcomes. Hiring leads and panels own ordinary senior hiring; only predesignated executive-critical roles receive a CEO decision in a fixed weekly slot. Leads may decide without post-hoc CEO approval when the register applies.
- **August 18–26:** Interview affected leads and five account/product counterparts, correct one ambiguous boundary at a time, document examples, and make the working register the operating default.

**Stop/defer choices.** Cancel the productivity-system purchase now (CEO). Defer blanket recurring-meeting cancellation until the July 18 evidence map; then each organizer cancels meetings with no decision, coordination, or continuity purpose. Bound CEO deep work to two 120-minute mornings starting July 22 only if the register has removed the corresponding approval coverage; it is not stacked atop the queue. Defer optional media, conferences, and nonessential internal status work through August 26. Preserve payroll, support, incidents, contracts, legal/fiduciary duties, and current customer delivery.

**Protected execution and morale.** Each lead gets two recurring 90-minute decision/maker blocks and one weekly direct-customer or frontline review. Notifications are off during blocks; urgent issues use the register’s escalation channel. Every category has a backup with the same artifact and a four-business-hour acknowledgement window; incidents follow their faster severity window. Work is capped at 45 hours per week with one work-free day, no routine nights, and no exceptional push planned. The DSO checks overload, reversals, conflict, errors, and health concerns weekly; acute concerns go immediately to qualified support. Morale is tracked through decision wait, reversal rate, commitments caught before signature, incident ownership, and lead-reported agency—not hours or public pressure.

**Cadence and branches.** The DSO posts the five measures each Tuesday; the CEO and leads hold a 30-minute Wednesday review. Continue if at least 80% meet service levels by August 5 and 90% by August 26, with zero late commitments and safeguards intact. Adapt one boundary, intake field, or owner if a weekly category misses its service level but the customer outcome remains valid. Reopen the goal on August 19 if two consecutive compliant weekly tests still miss in two or more categories, indicating capacity or structure—not personal focus—is the deeper constraint. Stop delegation in an affected category immediately for material harm or a legal/safety breach, name a qualified owner, and preserve the option of narrow CEO approval while redesigning it. On August 26 the CEO decides to institutionalize, adapt, or reopen the organization design. Personal focus remains supporting infrastructure and becomes primary only after decision queues and continuity failures are no longer the binding constraint.

**Lineage.** The route, evidence classes, record, and combined thresholds are cross-source taxonomy synthesis, not a claimed Paul Graham recipe. Current company records, contracting authority, employment implications, and safety/legal escalation requirements require revalidation by their responsible owners.
