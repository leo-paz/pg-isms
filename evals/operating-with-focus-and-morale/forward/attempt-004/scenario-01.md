# Four-week focus and morale operating record

**Header.** Dated July 15, 2026. Decision: redirect the founder’s next four weeks from optional external activity to restoring retailer trust and reliable data sync. Horizon: July 15–August 12. Primary route: `operating-with-focus-and-morale`; no survival, financing, acquisition, or organization route currently supersedes it. The company has 14 months of runway, no active fundraise, and clear decision rights. Customer continuity around the defect is a protected obligation, owned jointly by the engineering lead and customer lead. The CEO is accountable for the plan. Checks: July 17, then July 22, July 29, August 5, and final review August 12.

**Evidence ledger.**

- **Observed:** 38 paying retailers; nine affected by the data-sync defect; defect open three weeks; reporting redesign partially finished; six retailer calls rescheduled twice; 31 founder hours last week spent on external events, investor coffees, podcasts, conference planning, and status meetings. The CEO owns calendar evidence; engineering owns defect records; the customer lead owns contact records.
- **Claimed:** external activity builds credibility; every commitment is important. Neither claim establishes customer or company impact.
- **Unknown:** defect root cause, scope, data integrity, and security/privacy implications. Engineering will produce a root-cause and affected-account audit by July 17. Any integrity, security, or privacy risk triggers immediate qualified escalation and customer-continuity handling. Also unknown: whether the nine customers can sync successfully after remediation and whether deferred external commitments have material value. The customer lead will obtain verification from all nine by July 22; success means nine verified syncs with no defect-related recurrence for seven days. The CEO will log any concrete customer, hire, or financing outcome attributable to each external category by August 5; absent such evidence, those activities remain optional.

**One priority.** By July 29, restore reliable data sync for all nine affected retailers and regain direct confirmation from at least eight, with the ninth reached through a documented escalation path. Baseline is nine affected and zero recorded post-fix confirmations on July 15. The mechanism is a focused engineering fix plus direct retailer diagnosis and recovery; the rival is customer-specific configuration or source-data quality rather than a common product defect. Evidence latency is days, not hours. Falsifier: fewer than eight verified successful syncs, recurrence in two or more accounts, or evidence that the proposed fix does not address the dominant cause. Essential support, incidents, payroll, legal, fiduciary, and team commitments continue.

**Stop/defer list, effective July 15.** Cancel internal status meetings whose information fits the shared written update; operations owns the update. Defer accelerator events, investor coffees, podcasts, and new conference planning until August 12; the CEO’s assistant or operations lead sends declines or rescheduling notes. Bound already-contracted or materially consequential external obligations to one two-hour block weekly, with the CEO documenting why each qualifies. Pause the reporting redesign; engineering preserves the branch and notes so work can restart. Delegate routine approvals under existing decision rights.

**Protected execution.** The CEO reserves 9:00–12:00 Monday through Thursday for defect work, retailer preparation, and decisions, with notifications off. She holds four retailer calls weekly, prioritizing the nine affected and the six twice-rescheduled; the customer lead schedules, attends, and maintains the recovery ledger. Meetings occupy Tuesday and Thursday afternoons only, except incidents. The engineering lead is backup decision-maker during focus blocks; urgent customer-impact issues go through the customer lead with a 30-minute acknowledgment and two-hour CEO escalation window. Work is capped at 45 hours weekly, with no compulsory nights or weekend availability and one meeting-free recovery half-day each Friday. Any exceptional incident push ends within 48 hours and requires compensatory recovery and backup coverage.

**Morale and persistence.** The operating signal is stalled customer work while visible but weakly evidenced activity consumes attention. Test blocked progress and delayed user evidence before treating morale as a verdict. Goal premise: retailers will keep relying on the product if inventory sync is dependable and failures are handled credibly. Current tactic: a bounded fix-and-recovery cycle, not merely more hours. Weekly private reviews emphasize customer evidence, clear ownership, progress, and recovery; competitor visibility, one hard month, or confidence alone does not invalidate the goal.

**Cadence and decision.** On July 17, engineering reports scope, root cause, risk, owner, and fix plan. At each weekly check, the CEO records accounts fixed and verified, recurrences, retailer calls completed, external hours, total workload, approval waits, and any health or judgment warning; owners attach source records and confounders. **Continue** through July 29 if verified accounts rise weekly and safeguards hold. **Adapt** on July 22 if fewer than five accounts verify or the common-fix hypothesis fails: change one material variable—configuration workflow, segment, or remediation—under the engineering lead. **Reopen-goal** on August 12 if two discriminating remediation tests across affected configurations still yield fewer than eight stable accounts; the CEO and board decide whether the product promise or segment should change. **Stop** the current tactic immediately for material customer harm, legal/security risk, or a superior validated remediation. Decision now: begin the recovery cycle. Preserved option: restart the reporting redesign and selectively resume evidence-producing external work after review. The CEO owns every branch and the August 12 handoff back to normal prioritization.
