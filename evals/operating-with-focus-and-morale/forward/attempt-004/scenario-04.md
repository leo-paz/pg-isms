# Focus and morale operating record — July 15, 2026

## Header and decision

**Decision:** Treat this as a survival problem, not a calendar or morale problem. `managing-runway-and-survival` is primary; `operating-with-focus-and-morale` only supports humane execution and customer continuity inside the survival envelope. The CEO is accountable. A finance lead, or the CEO if none exists, owns the cash model. The first checkpoint is the board plan on July 16; cash and payroll are checked daily through July 27; the final initial survival-envelope review is July 29.

Cash is $320,000 against fixed monthly obligations of $210,000 and dependable monthly gross profit of $25,000: roughly $185,000 monthly net burn and 1.7 months of simple runway before timing, transaction costs, or unexpected outflows. Payroll is twelve days away, while the possible $180,000 renewal cannot arrive for at least eight weeks. A discussed, unsigned bridge is not cash. The company must first decide which bounded combination of collections, financing, deferrals, and cost reductions makes the next payroll and a credible survival horizon achievable.

## Evidence ledger

**Observed:** July 15 cash is $320,000; fixed obligations are $210,000 per month; dependable gross profit is $25,000 per month; payroll is due July 27; the renewal cannot close before September 9; nothing for the bridge is signed; product ownership is clear; the board requires a plan July 16.

**Claimed:** the team has “lost focus”; a bridge may happen; the renewal may produce $180,000; fewer meetings and a morale reset will improve the company state. None should enter available-cash calculations.

**Unknown:** exact payroll amount and bank date; daily cash schedule; which obligations can legally and operationally be deferred; receivables and collection dates; bridge amount, probability, conditions, and earliest funding date; renewal probability and implementation costs; minimum staffing needed for safe product and customer continuity. By 6 p.m. July 15, the finance owner will produce a 13-week weekly cash forecast plus a daily forecast through July 31, reconciled to bank balances, payroll, payables, receivables, taxes, debt, and restricted cash. The threshold is cash sufficient for payroll plus a board-approved minimum buffer under a zero-bridge, zero-renewal case. A miss triggers immediate deeper reductions or a controlled wind-down contingency, not calendar optimization.

The CEO will obtain written bridge milestones and earliest-funds dates by July 16 and have the renewal owner document buyer steps and the earliest honest close by July 17. Only signed, collectible commitments change the base case.

## One priority and survival envelope

**Priority through July 29:** secure and verify enough cash actions to fund July 27 payroll and establish a board-approved survival path that does not depend on the eight-week renewal.

Baseline is $320,000 cash and approximately $185,000 monthly net burn on July 15. By July 16, the board receives base, downside, and upside cash cases; named actions with amounts, dates, probabilities, and owners; payroll assurance; and explicit restructuring or wind-down triggers. By July 22, executed actions—not verbal optimism—must cover payroll and its minimum buffer. The mechanism is rapid cash visibility plus reversible expense containment and parallel, time-bounded financing/collection work. The rival explanation is that obligations are mistimed or incomplete, making headline runway misleading. The cycle is falsified if executable actions cannot meet the July 22 threshold.

Essential obligations remain: payroll and employment-law handling, taxes and fiduciary duties, safety and security, incident response, critical support, evidence preservation, and continuity for current customers. Qualified legal, employment, and board advisers must review any payment deferral, layoff, insolvency, or fiduciary decision.

## Stop, defer, and protected focus

- **Cancel now:** morale workshops, calendar redesign, optional events, media, speculative partnerships, and nonessential purchasing; CEO owns this July 15.
- **Defer to July 30:** roadmap expansion, broad redesign, and internal process projects; product owner maintains only contracted or continuity-critical work.
- **Bound:** bridge work to a CEO-led process with a written ask, data room, decision dates, and a July 22 close/pause test. Renewal work gets one owner and does not count as near-term rescue cash.
- **Delegate:** finance reconciliation to the finance owner; critical support to the product owner with a named backup.

Focus work belongs now only where it changes survival evidence or preserves minimum customer/product motion. The CEO gets two daily 90-minute survival blocks. The product team gets one uninterrupted three-hour block each weekday for critical reliability and renewal prerequisites, plus two scheduled customer-contact windows weekly. Meetings are limited to a 15-minute daily survival check, required customer/incident coordination, and the board session. Emergencies route to the product owner, then CEO, with a written handoff and a two-hour response window.

Cap routine work at 45 hours per person per week; no compulsory nights or continuous availability. Any exceptional push ends July 29, includes a rotating backup, and preserves one full recovery day weekly. Sleep loss, errors, conflict, or health symptoms trigger immediate workload reduction and qualified support.

## Morale, cadence, and branches

Morale is presently a **claim**, not a diagnosis. On July 17 the people owner will privately check for observed exhaustion, blocked decisions, errors, missed customer contact, or unclear finish lines. Restore agency with honest cash facts, named ownership, visible daily progress, private support, and a dated endpoint—not motivational pressure or hours-as-commitment.

The goal premise is “a viable core business can survive long enough to reach dependable customer cash.” The current tactic is the two-week survival envelope; it is not “work harder.” Daily, the finance owner reports bank cash, next-14-day obligations, executed savings/collections, payroll coverage, bridge evidence, customer risks, and safeguard breaches.

- **Continue** on July 22 if executed cash actions cover payroll plus the approved buffer and safeguards hold.
- **Adapt** if the premise remains plausible but a collection, bridge, or deferral tactic misses; change one lever and reforecast immediately.
- **Reopen-goal** by July 22 if credible downside cash cannot protect payroll or minimum continuity, or by July 29 if repeated executable options fail.
- **Stop** the current plan and enter a board- and counsel-governed wind-down or restructuring path if payroll, legal, safety, or fiduciary boundaries cannot be met.

The preserved option is an orderly reduction or wind-down that protects employees, customers, records, and remaining cash. Ordinary focus redesign resumes only after the board confirms a funded survival envelope and hands the company back to normal operations. This record’s routing, evidence classes, safeguards, and branch thresholds are cross-source synthesis; all current cash, legal, employment, and fiduciary facts require revalidation by qualified owners.
