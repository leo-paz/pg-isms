# Six-week decision-rights operating record

## 1. Header and route

- **Record date:** July 15, 2026
- **Exact decision:** Replace the CEO approval bottleneck with bounded decision rights across pricing exceptions, enterprise promises, incident priority, roadmap changes, and senior hiring; decide where personal focus changes fit after capacity is actually released.
- **Horizon:** July 15-August 26, 2026
- **Primary route:** building-and-evolving-organizations. Repeated approval queues, commitments reaching delivery teams after signature, ownerless incident decisions, and unwritten authority make organization design the state-changing constraint.
- **Supporting route:** operating-with-focus-and-morale. It supplies the calendar, user-contact, workload, recovery, and evidence cadence inside the organization redesign.
- **Current state:** A 48-person operating company reports 17 months of runway and no fundraising. There is no stated near-payroll, live financing, sale, acute health, legal, security, or safety event. Customer-continuity and incident risk do require named current owners.
- **Accountability:** The CEO is accountable for the six-week outcome. By July 16 she names one existing department lead as **operating lead** to run the change; that is a role assignment, not a new approval layer. Each decision domain has a lead and a backup named in the published policy.
- **Checks:** July 17 for survival and immediate-risk verification; July 22 for baseline and policy publication; July 29, August 5, August 12, and August 19 for evidence checks; August 26 for the branch decision.

The personal focus plan is not the primary intervention. Canceling meetings, buying a tool, or putting two mornings on an unchanged approval load would leave the queue intact. CEO focus blocks become supporting infrastructure only after decision rights, backups, and escalation limits have removed work from her.

## 3. One state-changing priority

**Outcome:** By August 26, designated department leads make routine decisions in all five domains within published guardrails, without waiting for CEO approval, while customer, incident, legal, financial, and workload safeguards hold.

This changes the company state because it removes a repeated authority bottleneck instead of helping one person process the same queue faster.

- **Baseline:** 18 waits over four business days, five after-the-fact signed commitments, and two incidents without one decision owner in the six weeks ending July 15. CEO-free decision rate and policy-conformance rate are unknown until July 22.
- **Target:** From policy activation on July 22 through August 26, no more than two decisions wait over four business days, with zero such waits in the final two weeks; zero signed commitments reach delivery teams after signature; zero incident begins work without a named incident commander; at least 90% of sampled in-guardrail decisions are made without CEO approval; 100% of material exceptions are escalated to the specified qualified owner; and there is no material customer, safety, security, legal, or fiduciary harm attributable to the pilot.
- **Accountable owner:** CEO. **Execution owner:** operating lead. **Domain owners:** named below by July 16 and published with individual names and backups by July 22.
- **Affected users and work:** Enterprise customers receiving delivery promises, customers affected by incidents, and the sales, product, engineering, customer-success, finance, people, and leadership teams.
- **Proposed mechanism:** Explicit single-owner rights, guardrails, backups, and a visible intake record let competent leads decide at the point of information and make promises visible before signature.
- **Causal rival:** Missing information, weak capability, cross-functional disagreement, or insufficient delivery capacity—not CEO availability—may be the real source of delay or bad commitments.
- **Evidence latency:** Incident ownership and commitment visibility are observable immediately; a trustworthy queue signal needs at least seven business days after policy activation. July 29 is the earliest honest operating checkpoint; August 12 supports a two-test comparison.
- **Falsifier:** After two consecutive two-week tests with at least 80% policy use and complete inputs, the rate of decisions waiting over four days has not fallen by at least 50%, or late commitments recur, without a confounding safety or continuity event. That undermines the written-rights tactic and may undermine the premise that authority distribution is the main constraint.
- **Cycle end:** August 26, or earlier if a hard survival, legal, safety, security, fiduciary, or material customer-harm boundary is crossed.

### Decision-rights policy to publish by July 22

Every row must name one individual, one backup, the decision boundary, required inputs, an escalation trigger, and a response window. “Consulted” roles cannot silently become approvers.

| Domain | Default decision owner and right | Mandatory boundary and escalation |
|---|---|---|
| Pricing exceptions | Head of Sales decides inside floors, discount bands, terms, and packaging guardrails co-authored by Finance; Finance owns economic exceptions | Finance decides outside ordinary economics; counsel reviews nonstandard contract terms; CEO sees only company-model or fiduciary exceptions explicitly listed in policy |
| Enterprise delivery promises | Product/engineering delivery lead accepts feasibility before signature; Customer Success owns the commitment register and customer handoff | Sales cannot sign an unaccepted delivery promise; capacity or contractual conflicts go to the named product/engineering executive and counsel as applicable |
| Incident priorities | A named incident commander has authority from the start of every incident | Security, privacy, safety, legal, and customer-notification triggers go to qualified owners; CEO is informed at the defined severity but is not the default commander |
| Roadmap changes | Head of Product decides sequencing within approved company outcomes and capacity, with Engineering input | Changes to company objectives, material budget, regulated obligations, or signed commitments use the named executive/qualified escalation path |
| Senior hiring | Functional lead owns the selection; People owns process, level, and approved compensation | CEO approves only her direct reports or explicitly defined executive roles; board, counsel, or compensation approval remains where legally or fiduciarily required |

Essential obligations remain protected: payroll and cash control, active customer support and delivery, incident response, security and privacy, legal and fiduciary duties, existing signed commitments, teammate coordination, and necessary hiring fairness and compliance.

## 6. Protected execution, availability, and recovery

- **Released-capacity condition:** The CEO's two focus mornings start only after the July 22 policy is named, normal approval steps are removed, every domain has a backup, and the operating lead confirms that in-guardrail requests are going to the new owners. They are not stacked on the old queue.
- **CEO focus blocks:** Beginning no earlier than July 27, reserve two recurring 2.5-hour mornings each week for CEO-only work on company objectives, executive-role design, and review of direct user and operating evidence. The blocks are not for catching up on delegated approvals. The operating lead covers ordinary escalations; an incident commander or qualified legal/security/finance owner can interrupt only for a defined hard trigger.
- **Domain-owner work:** Each domain owner reserves two contiguous two-hour blocks weekly for decisions, policy refinement, and delivery work, with a named backup covering urgent continuity. The operating lead removes or reassigns equivalent work first.
- **Direct user contact:** The CEO and Head of Customer Success conduct one 45-minute direct enterprise-user conversation each week focused on promises, delivery friction, and incident experience. Head of Customer Success owns scheduling, consent, notes, and follow-up and remains the user-continuity owner; user contact does not restore CEO approval.
- **Bounded availability:** Normal escalations go to two 30-minute CEO office-hour windows each week with a complete handoff artifact: decision, owner, evidence, boundary crossed, options, and deadline. The policy specifies same-day response only for time-sensitive customer or operational exceptions; hard safety, security, legal, fiduciary, payroll, or incident triggers use their current emergency path.
- **Meetings and status:** One 30-minute weekly evidence review replaces ad hoc approval chasing. The decision log holds the details. No public ranking of people, raw-hours scoreboard, or performative daily check-in is introduced.
- **Workload limit:** The CEO, operating lead, and domain leads plan no more than 45 hours per week during this cycle, with no planned nights or weekends. The operating lead must remove or reassign work before adding pilot tasks. No exceptional push is authorized; a real incident follows its own bounded protocol, ends at containment/handoff, and is followed by recovery and backup coverage.
- **Recovery and health:** Protect at least one full work-free day in every seven and normal overnight off-hours outside the incident rotation. Repeated reversals, errors, sleep loss, conflict, or health symptoms trigger load reduction and backup within one business day. Acute medical or mental-health risk goes to qualified help, not a productivity intervention.

## 7. Morale and persistence

There is no direct morale observation in the scenario, so the plan must not invent one. What is observed is delayed agency, surprise work arriving after signature, and ambiguous incident authority. The hypothesis is that these conditions may create rework, conflict, or helplessness; the rival is ordinary workload, capability gaps, or unrelated health and team factors.

The People lead runs a confidential three-question baseline on July 17 and weekly thereafter: “I know which decisions I own,” “I can make them without waiting for hidden approval,” and “my workload permits sound judgment.” By August 12, at least 80% of affected leads should answer positively to the first two, with no sustained workload red flag. Each domain owner has a named backup/peer for private problem-solving. The CEO names one trusted peer or advisor by July 20 for a private weekly judgment check. These are support channels, not public pressure.

- **Goal premise:** Bounded authority close to the relevant information can reduce waits and commitment failures without increasing material harm.
- **Current tactic:** A written decision-rights policy, explicit guardrails, one owner and backup per domain, a commitment register, a lightweight decision log, bounded escalation office hours, and two live tests.
- **Hard phase:** Early questions or one correctable miss while waits fall, users remain protected, and workload limits hold.
- **Tactic failure:** Rights are used and inputs are complete, but waits do not fall; or definitions, training, intake, or capacity repeatedly block decisions. Change one of those variables.
- **Premise failure:** Two discriminating tests across a plausible original and adapted tactic fail, or distributed authority produces material harm despite qualified guardrails. Reopen the organization goal rather than demanding more determination.

The precommitted branches are:

- **Continue:** At the July 29 and later checks, commitment and incident targets hold, wait rate is falling, policy use reaches the dated threshold, and harm and workload limits hold. Domain leads continue the policy; the operating lead maintains the weekly review through August 26.
- **Adapt:** At July 29 or August 5, any non-hard outcome threshold misses while the premise remains plausible. The operating lead uses cause-coded cases to change exactly one material variable—boundary clarity, input quality, training, capacity, or escalation window—for the next two-week test. The relevant domain lead owns the change and its dated measure.
- **Reopen-goal:** By August 12 or August 26, two consecutive two-week tests with at least 80% policy use and complete inputs fail to reduce the over-four-day wait rate by at least 50%, late commitments recur, or evidence shows that capability/capacity rather than authority is dominant. The CEO and department leads reopen the organization design, including role, staffing, and cross-functional ownership, and name a new bounded test. Low confidence or a competitor move alone does not qualify.
- **Stop:** Immediately stop the affected delegation tactic if it crosses a material safety, security, legal, fiduciary, employment, or customer-harm boundary; the relevant qualified owner takes control and the CEO decides the bounded remediation. Stop this ordinary six-week plan and route to managing-runway-and-survival if the July 17 or later cash check shows a default-dead or near-payroll state. A new live dated financing or buyer process is separately routed and bounded rather than silently added.

**Decision now:** Route to organization redesign and start the stabilization work. Do not buy a productivity system. Do not cancel meetings wholesale. Add the CEO focus plan only after authority and coverage release real capacity.

**Preserved option:** The company can temporarily return one unsafe or unclear decision domain—not all domains—to its qualified executive owner while revising that domain's guardrails. The simple log is reversible, the software purchase remains unmade, and CEO escalation windows remain available without recreating continuous approval.

**Handoff back to personal focus as primary:** After two consecutive weeks with zero late commitments, zero ownerless incidents, zero decisions over four business days, at least 90% in-policy CEO-free decisions, and workload safeguards holding, the operating lead closes the authority-queue state. If attention allocation is still the main constraint, operating-with-focus-and-morale can then become primary.
