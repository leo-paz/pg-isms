# Four-week focus and morale operating record

## 1. Header and route

- **Record date:** July 15, 2026
- **Exact decision:** For the next four weeks, the founder-CEO will make recovery of reliable retailer data sync and retailer trust the company's one state-changing priority. She will clear external visibility work and the reporting redesign out of the way while preserving essential customer, incident, payroll, legal, fiduciary, and team obligations.
- **Horizon:** July 15-August 12, 2026
- **Primary route:** `operating-with-focus-and-morale`. The company has 14 months of runway, no active fundraise, and clear decision rights; the facts given do not support routing first to survival, financing, acquisition, or organization redesign.
- **Supporting route:** Customer continuity is a hard boundary inside this plan. The engineering lead owns technical incident response; the CEO owns communication and retailer recovery. Any evidence of security, privacy, contractual, legal, or material data-integrity exposure goes immediately to the appropriate qualified owner and is not handled as a morale issue.
- **Current state:** Six people, 38 paying retailers, nine known affected by a data-sync defect, six retailer calls twice rescheduled, and 31 CEO hours last week spent on events, investor coffees, podcasts, conference planning, and status meetings. The defect has waited three weeks behind a reporting redesign.
- **Evidence latency:** Scope and containment can be known within 48 hours; repair and account reconciliation within one week; customer confirmation and recurrence evidence require one to three weeks.
- **Reversibility and error cost:** Deferring external activity and freezing the redesign are readily reversible. A bad data repair can harm inventory decisions and customer trust, so changes require staged rollout, reconciliation, monitoring, and rollback.
- **Accountable owner:** Founder-CEO for the four-week outcome; engineering lead for the repair and technical evidence.
- **Intermediate checks:** July 16, July 18, July 22, July 29, and August 5.
- **Final review:** August 12, chaired by the CEO with the whole team.

## 3. One state-changing priority

**Outcome:** By August 12, all nine known affected retailer accounts have reconciled inventory data, every affected retailer has received direct recovery communication, at least eight have explicitly confirmed that their data is correct or supplied a remaining discrepancy, and the repaired sync completes a seven-consecutive-day monitored period with no severity-1 or severity-2 recurrence.

This changes the company's state because it restores the core promise to paying users, produces direct retention evidence, and removes an active trust risk. The CEO is accountable; the engineering lead is responsible for technical delivery. The affected users are the nine known retailers, with monitoring protecting the other 29.

- **Baseline on July 15:** Nine affected accounts; zero documented as reconciled or confirmed; defect open for three weeks; six retailer conversations delayed twice.
- **Milestones:** scope and customer contact by July 16; containment and root-cause decision by July 18; safe repair plus reconciliation of all nine accounts by July 22; retailer validation and recurrence review July 29; seven clean monitored days by August 5 or, after the last material patch, no later than August 12.
- **Proposed mechanism:** Freeing the CEO and engineering lead from low-latency external work will speed diagnosis, repair, communication, and retailer trust recovery.
- **Causal rival:** The main problem may be deeper architecture or workflow mismatch, not attention allocation; the redesign may contain a necessary repair component; or retailer trust may be driven by another unmet need. The reproducer, staged repair, account reconciliation, and interviews discriminate among these explanations.
- **Falsifier:** The current repair tactic is falsified if no root cause or safe containment is established by July 18, if reconciled data cannot be produced for all nine by July 22, or if a severity-1/2 recurrence appears after deployment. The broader goal premise is challenged only after two materially different, safely run repair approaches fail or reliable operation is restored and at least six affected retailers independently identify a different dominant reason they would not continue.
- **Essential obligations kept:** retailer support and incident response; security/privacy and data preservation; payroll, cash controls, legal and fiduciary duties; critical product operation; necessary team decisions; and current customer commitments. These are covered, not allowed to expand into unbounded availability.

The CEO's contribution is customer recovery, prioritization, and fast decisions—not substituting herself for the engineering lead. User calls and the minimum build work are two parts of the same evidence loop, not parallel initiatives.

## 5. Protected execution and workload envelope

Calendar changes are funded by the cancellations above, not added on top of the old load.

- The engineering lead and anyone assigned to the repair receive 9:00 a.m.-noon contiguous maker blocks Monday-Friday. Notifications are off; the incident backup handles incoming support.
- The CEO protects 9:00-11:00 a.m. Monday, Wednesday, and Friday for recovery decisions, account review, and retailer follow-through. She holds retailer calls Tuesday and Thursday 1:00-4:00 p.m., using the first slots for the six twice-rescheduled calls and the nine affected accounts.
- Incident decisions occur Tuesday and Friday for 25 minutes, with an asynchronous evidence packet: scope, reconciliations, test results, rollout percentage, recurrence, customer status, and explicit decision needed.
- A named teammate serves as customer-continuity backup during maker blocks, acknowledges urgent retailer issues within two business hours, and escalates operational outage or suspected data loss immediately to the engineering lead and CEO. The engineering lead's deputy can approve rollback or disablement if the lead is unavailable. The incident log is the handoff artifact.
- CEO decision requests go into one queue with office hours 4:00-4:30 p.m. daily and a four-business-hour response target. Security, safety, legal, payroll, or active customer-continuity emergencies bypass the queue.
- Scheduled work is capped at 45 hours per person per week, no routine nights, and no weekend work. The initial incident exception ends July 18 and still requires at least 12 hours between workdays. After-hours pages are limited to severity-1 events and rotate to a backup.
- Each person has one full recovery day weekly. If hours exceed the cap, sleep loss or health symptoms appear, two repair rollbacks occur, or judgment becomes inconsistent, the CEO removes scope immediately and the backup owner takes rollout authority. Health concerns go to qualified help.

## 6. Morale and persistence

The useful morale signals here are observable: customer contact has been postponed, a known defect has stalled, and visible external work has displaced a core user obligation. Low morale itself would not prove that the company or market is wrong. The first test is whether a clear finish line, direct retailer evidence, real ownership, protected time, and recovery restore controlled progress.

- **Goal premise:** Retailers will continue to pay for and rely on the product when inventory data sync is trustworthy and failures receive prompt, candid recovery.
- **Current tactic:** Isolate and safely repair the defect, reconcile all affected accounts, communicate directly, and monitor recurrence while freezing unrelated work.
- **Hard phase:** Diagnosis is difficult but dated evidence improves, customers remain engaged, and safeguards hold.
- **Tactic failure:** The July 18 or July 22 thresholds miss, or the deployed repair recurs. Change the repair approach, not the threshold after seeing the result.
- **Premise failure:** Two distinct, discriminating reliability attempts fail, unacceptable harm or survival cost emerges, or restored reliability plus consistent retailer evidence shows that another problem—not sync trust—determines continued use.

Progress is reviewed privately from the evidence packet. No public pressure, heroic-hours score, or performative event withdrawal is used as proof of seriousness. The CEO names one trusted peer or adviser for a 30-minute weekly private reality check, bounded away from the team's execution time.

At every check, the incident record captures observed results, confounders, customer impact, workload safeguards, and the applicable branch:

- **Continue:** July 22 repair and reconciliation milestones are met, retailer validation is progressing, no severe recurrence appears, and workload safeguards hold. Continue monitoring through the final review.
- **Adapt:** Root cause/containment is absent July 18, any account remains unreconciled July 22, confirmation is below eight because discrepancies persist, or a severe recurrence occurs. Pause rollout, use rollback/manual reconciliation, obtain an independent technical review, and test one materially different repair path with a new dated seven-day window. Keep the goal and customer communication steady.
- **Reopen-goal:** After two different safe repair approaches, reliable sync cannot be achieved within the company's capability and survival bounds; or reliability is achieved but at least six affected retailers identify the same different dominant blocker to continued use. The CEO and full team choose the next outcome from documented customer and operating evidence within two business days.
- **Stop:** Stop the current rollout immediately for suspected security/privacy exposure, unreconciled destructive writes, material legal/contractual risk, or customer harm that rollback/manual operation reduces. A qualified owner decides restart. Stop the broader goal only if the reopened review shows the premise invalid or its safe cost exceeds the company's precommitted survival bound.

**Decision now:** Start the recovery cycle and clear the calendar today. The preserved options are the frozen reporting redesign, politely deferred external relationships, rollback/manual reconciliation, and an independent technical review. The next decision date is July 18 for repair method and August 12 for the outcome. The CEO owns every branch; the engineering lead owns technical stop/adapt calls. Handoff back to ordinary prioritization occurs only when all nine accounts are reconciled, the customer loop is closed, seven clean monitored days are recorded, and workload coverage is stable.
