## Protected execution and workload

- **Survival analysis:** CEO and bookkeeper/payroll provider get two uninterrupted 90-minute blocks on July 15 for reconciliation and alternatives. Notifications are off; the product team is not pulled into finance work unless it owns a specific fact.
- **Product/customer continuity:** The product owner protects two three-hour maker blocks each weekday for the small team needed to keep shipped hardware/software safe and current customers supported. Work is limited to incidents, material defects, contractual needs, and the minimum renewal evidence; no roadmap expansion.
- **Direct user contact:** The named customer owner holds one bounded daily customer window and records customer, question, observed answer, commitment, and next date. The enterprise contact is evidence and relationship preservation, not assumed revenue.
- **Meetings:** One 20-minute daily survival huddle for CEO, cash-preparation owner, and product/continuity owner. One 25-minute team briefing after the July 16 board decision. Other meetings require an incident, legal obligation, or state-changing decision.
- **Availability and backup:** CEO names a board director as decision escalation and the product owner as customer/product continuity lead by July 15, 5:00 p.m. The product owner names an incident backup and keeps a one-page handoff with current customers, open incidents, renewal next step, and escalation contacts. Incidents and acute legal/safety issues escalate immediately; customer-continuity issues receive acknowledgment within one business hour during coverage.
- **Workload:** Seven team members remain on their normal schedules. Through July 18, the CEO and one finance-support person may use a bounded push of at most ten working hours per day, with at least eleven continuous hours off and no more than six consecutive days. From July 19 the limit is 50 hours per week. The exceptional push ends July 18 at noon; extending it requires board review, a named backup, and a new end date. No compulsory nights, permanent availability, public pressure, or hours-as-seriousness metric.
- **Recovery/health gate:** Any sustained sleep loss, acute distress, safety error, rollback cluster, or impaired judgment triggers immediate removal from solo high-consequence decisions, backup coverage, and qualified help where appropriate. The CEO checks this on July 16 and July 18, not at the end of the crisis.

Protected blocks are funded by the cancellations above; they are not stacked on top of unchanged work.

## Morale and persistence

- **Observed morale signal:** none yet beyond the CEO's claim. The July 16 pulse must convert the label into behaviors such as blocked decisions, errors, skipped users, conflict, excessive hours, or invisible progress.
- **Most plausible current mechanism:** a near-payroll threat and unclear finish line can degrade attention and agency. Calendar density may contribute, but it is not yet the demonstrated cause.
- **Immediate response:** give the team a truthful private briefing after the board decision, clear owners, a dated finish line, normal workload for nonessential responders, direct customer evidence, and a confidential escalation path. Do not promise bridge or renewal money and do not use a public morale ritual.
- **Goal premise:** a viable core company can meet near-term obligations while preserving essential customers and product capability.
- **Current tactic:** reconcile cash, ring-fence payroll, execute controllable cash actions, bound financing/renewal work, and preserve the minimum operating core.
- **Hard phase vs. tactic failure vs. premise failure:** anxiety or a difficult week alone is a hard phase. A missed collection or unsigned bridge is tactic evidence and causes adaptation. Inability to protect payroll/continuity after verified alternatives, repeated failed funding/cost tests, or a legal/safety boundary is premise-level evidence requiring the board to reopen the company objective.
