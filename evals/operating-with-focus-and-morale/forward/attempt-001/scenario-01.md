# Four-week focus and morale operating record

- **Record date:** July 15, 2026
- **Decision:** For the next four weeks, make restoration and customer validation of reliable inventory sync for the nine affected retailers the single company-changing priority.
- **Horizon:** July 15-August 12, 2026; checks July 22, July 29, and August 5; final decision August 12.
- **Primary route:** `operating-with-focus-and-morale`. Fourteen months of stated runway, no active fundraise, and clear decision rights do not currently trigger a survival, financing, or organization route. Revalidate cash math and any contractual external commitments by July 17.
- **Accountable owner:** Founder-CEO. The CEO designates one sync engineering owner and one customer-continuity owner by 5 p.m. July 15.
- **Current state:** Attention is displaced from users and a three-week-old defect toward work-like external activity. Essential customer, payroll, legal, fiduciary, security, privacy, and incident obligations remain protected.

## Evidence ledger

**Observed as of July 15:** 38 paying retailers; stated 14 months of runway; clear decision rights; no active fundraise; 31 founder hours last week in accelerator events, investor coffees, podcasts, conference planning, and status meetings; six retailer calls rescheduled twice; nine customers affected by a sync defect; the defect has waited behind a partially finished reporting redesign for three weeks.

**Claimed:** External activity builds credibility; every commitment is important. Neither claim establishes customer value or priority.

**Consequential unknowns:**

- **Defect cause, severity, and customer harm:** Sync engineering owner reviews logs, reproduces the fault, checks for corruption/security/privacy impact, and writes a defect brief by July 17. Threshold: a reproducible cause and affected-account list. If corruption, security, privacy, or material continuity risk appears, pause this cadence and open the appropriate incident with the CEO as escalation owner.
- **Retailer impact:** Customer-continuity owner completes the six delayed calls plus contacts the other three affected retailers by July 22, recording operational impact, workarounds, and acceptance criteria. Threshold: at least seven conversations or explicit responses. Their evidence sets fix order and validation criteria.
- **Value of external activity:** CEO lists each commitment, contractual cost, attributable qualified leads, retailer introductions, and dated decisions by July 17. Threshold for keeping any item: a contractual obligation or a specific retailer opportunity that must occur before August 12 and cannot be delegated. Everything else stays deferred.
- **Runway accuracy:** CEO verifies cash balance, monthly net burn, and next payroll with the finance owner by July 17. Any near-payroll threat or default-dead result reroutes the company to `managing-runway-and-survival`.

## One priority

**Outcome:** By August 12, restore trustworthy scheduled inventory sync for the nine affected retailers: all nine accounts receive the fix or an accepted account-specific resolution, each shows seven consecutive days of successful scheduled syncs, at least seven retailers confirm the result meets the agreed acceptance criterion, and no new severity-1 or severity-2 sync incident is attributable to the change.

**Baseline:** Nine affected accounts, defect open three weeks, zero documented customer acceptances on July 15. **Owner:** CEO, with the designated sync engineering owner responsible for delivery. **Affected users:** the nine retailers first; all 38 receive continuity protection.

**Mechanism:** Direct retailer evidence plus uninterrupted diagnosis, repair, release, and observation will resolve the defect and rebuild trust. **Rival:** the defect may be caused by retailer-specific data or an upstream integration, so a single code patch may not resolve every account. **Latency:** technical signals appear after the next scheduled sync; honest stability evidence requires seven days. **Falsifier:** two materially different, instrumented remediation attempts fail the agreed customer criteria, or evidence shows reliable sync is not material to the affected workflow. The cycle ends August 12.

## Stop, defer, and delegate

Effective July 15, the CEO:

- **Pauses** the reporting redesign; the engineering owner preserves its branch, notes, and restart point.
- **Defers** investor coffees until after August 12 because there is no active fundraise.
- **Cancels or defers** podcasts and optional accelerator events; honors only contractual sessions that pass the July 17 test, capped at two total hours over four weeks.
- **Delegates** conference planning to a teammate with a written budget and decision boundary, or defers it if no teammate can own it; CEO review is capped at 30 minutes weekly.
- **Replaces** recurring internal status meetings with a written defect/customer update and one 30-minute Wednesday decision review.
- **Delegates** routine support and operating approvals under existing decision rights while retaining escalation for payroll, legal/fiduciary matters, major incidents, and customer-threatening decisions.

## Protected execution and morale

The CEO protects 9 a.m.-noon Monday, Tuesday, Thursday, and Friday for this evidence loop: customer preparation/calls, unblock decisions, release review, and follow-through. Customer calls have two 90-minute windows each week until all nine respond. Notifications are off during blocks; the CEO has two daily 20-minute escalation windows.

The sync engineering owner can diagnose, instrument, roll back, and release within existing safety controls without waiting for a status meeting. The customer-continuity owner sends affected retailers an update every two business days until resolution. Their shared handoff artifact is the defect brief containing account status, evidence, decisions, owner, and next action. Active data loss, security/privacy risk, or widespread outage reaches the CEO immediately; a blocked release or customer commitment receives a decision within four business hours. A designated teammate is backup decision-maker when the CEO is unavailable.

Limit each person to 45 hours per week, with no planned nights or weekends. Preserve one full day off weekly and two meeting-free evenings. Any exceptional release push ends July 24; if it requires more time, rescope or rotate coverage. Sleep loss, repeated reversals, conflict, errors, or health symptoms trigger workload reduction and backup review within one day, not pressure for more hours.

The observed morale signal is blocked customer progress, not proof that the team or market lacks conviction. Make progress visible privately on the shared defect brief, give the two operating owners real authority, review customer evidence weekly, and hold a 20-minute Friday retro on obstacles and recovery. The goal premise is that reliable inventory sync is necessary to customer trust; the current tactic is focused diagnosis, customer-defined acceptance, repair, and monitored rollout. One hard week does not invalidate the premise; missed technical thresholds change the tactic; repeated discriminating misses can reopen the goal.

## Cadence and decision rules

- **July 22:** CEO verifies seven customer responses, a reproducible cause or bounded cause tree, and a staged fix/resolution plan. If present and safeguards hold, `continue`. If customer evidence supports the premise but cause or staging misses, `adapt` one variable—instrumentation, rollout scope, or account segment—and run a seven-day test.
- **July 29 and August 5:** Engineering owner reports accounts fixed, consecutive successful syncs, incidents, rollbacks, and confounders; customer owner reports acceptance and continuity. Continue only with improving technical and customer evidence and intact workload limits.
- **August 12:** `continue` into normal monitoring if the target is met. `Adapt` with one new bounded tactic if the premise remains supported but one attempt missed. `Reopen-goal` if two materially different attempts fail or fewer than three of seven-plus responding retailers find sync reliability material and usage/support evidence agrees; CEO then selects a new company constraint within two business days. `Stop` the rollout immediately for data corruption, security/privacy harm, or customer impact beyond rollback limits, and route to the qualified incident owner.

**Decision now:** `continue` with this four-week outcome. **Preserved options:** retain the reporting-redesign restart point and reschedule valuable relationships after August 12. **Handoff back to ordinary planning:** CEO owns it on August 12 after closing the defect state, documenting customer results, and verifying workload recovery.
