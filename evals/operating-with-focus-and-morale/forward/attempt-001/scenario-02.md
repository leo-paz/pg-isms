## Evidence ledger

**Observed:** Clinic 1 used the workflow daily and recorded an 18% no-show reduction. Clinic 2 signed but procurement has prevented launch. Clinic 3 opened 22% of alerts and staff reported double entry. Fourteen recent demos yielded two paid pilots (14%). A funded competitor announced a similar product. Two engineers said the market does not care; that statement is observed, but its conclusion is a claim.

**Claimed:** The market “clearly” does not care; the unchanged plan will succeed if people work nights; competitor funding determines customer choice; low morale means the goal is invalid. None is demand evidence.

**Consequential unknowns:**

- **Runway/state route:** CEO reviews cash, payroll, financing, legal, security, and customer-continuity obligations by July 16. Threshold: funded through August 14 with no near-payroll threat; otherwise route to survival and resize this test.
- **Friction cause:** Product lead observes five Clinic 3 staff completing the workflow and maps time/steps by July 18. If at least three identify duplicate entry as the principal avoidable failure, build the workaround; otherwise adapt to the leading observed cause.
- **Adoption effect:** Data engineer compares eligible alerts, opens, completed reminder actions, duplicate entries, errors, and staff time weekly. Target by August 14: at least 60% of eligible alerts opened, 70% of opened alerts acted on, and median duplicate-entry time reduced at least 75%, across two consecutive weeks. Missing any threshold changes the tactic, not automatically the goal.
- **Value repeatability:** Customer lead validates Clinic 1's cohort definition and 18% result by July 22 and obtains an August renewal/expansion decision date. If the result cannot be reproduced from records, treat efficacy as unknown and reopen the goal sooner.
- **Willingness to adopt:** CEO asks the two paid-pilot buyers by July 25 which problem, budget, workflow, and success threshold caused purchase. If neither bought primarily to reduce missed appointments, narrow the claimed segment/value proposition.

## One priority

By August 14, make Clinic 3's reminder workflow routinely usable without double entry: raise alert opens from 22% to at least 60%, achieve action on at least 70% of opened alerts, and cut duplicate-entry time at least 75% for two consecutive weeks, while Clinic 1 remains supported and daily.

This can change company state because it tests the strongest observed rival to demand failure: workflow friction. The proposed mechanism is that single-entry operation increases staff adoption; rivals are low perceived value, poor alert timing, training, or wrong buyer/user fit. Earliest honest product checkpoint is July 25; outcome review is August 14. The cycle is falsified if the workaround works technically but adoption remains below 40% after two weeks, or five staff observations show double entry is not the principal barrier. Patient safety, privacy/security, contractual support, payroll, and incident response remain protected.

## Protected execution and morale safeguards

The product lead and two assigned engineers receive Monday-Thursday 9:00-noon maker blocks. Customer lead holds two Clinic 3 observation/support sessions weekly and one Clinic 1 continuity check. Notifications stay off in maker blocks; the operations lead triages support, with the product lead as backup for severity-1 incidents within 30 minutes. Decisions, user notes, metric definitions, releases, and rollback steps live in one handoff record.

Cap work at 45 hours per person per week, no planned nights or weekends, one full recovery day weekly, and no exceptional push. Security, privacy, patient-impact, or data-integrity concerns stop rollout and go to the qualified owner; the prior version remains rollback-ready. CEO holds private July 17 and weekly check-ins for workload, sleep loss, errors, conflict, ownership, and health concerns, routing acute health concerns to qualified help.

Low morale is presently a label plus two discouraging statements, not a market verdict. The CEO will record anonymous pulse scores (clarity, agency, workload, confidence) and concrete blockers on July 17 and weekly. The response is visible user evidence, one owned test, a credible finish line, peer review, and recovery—not exhortation.

## Cadence and decision rules

The data engineer posts weekly adoption and friction measures; customer lead posts user observations and Clinic 1 continuity; CEO posts route/workload safeguards. At each Friday review, record results, sample size, confounders, incidents, and branch without changing thresholds.

- **Continue:** August 14 targets are met, Clinic 1 remains daily, and safeguards hold; package the workflow and run one matched-clinic replication.
- **Adapt:** opens are 40-59%, action is below 70%, or observation supports another fix while users still value fewer missed appointments; change one variable (timing, training, workflow step, or segment) for one bounded test owned by the product lead.
- **Reopen-goal:** adoption stays below 40% after the technically verified fix, Clinic 1's outcome cannot be validated, or two discriminating workflow/segment tests fail to show use tied to reduced missed appointments. CEO decides with product and customer leads.
- **Stop:** payroll/survival, safety, legal, privacy, or patient-harm boundary is crossed; or repeated tests invalidate the premise and a better option dominates.

**Decision now:** adapt the tactic and continue the goal. **Preserved option:** keep the existing reminder core, Clinic 1 continuity, Clinic 2 contract, rollback version, and buyer relationships while avoiding irreversible expansion. **Next handoff:** product lead brings the August 14 record to the CEO; a successful test returns to matched-clinic growth, while a miss enters `adapt` or `reopen-goal` under the rules above.
