# Six-week operating record: decision authority before personal focus

## Header

- **Record date:** July 15, 2026
- **Exact decision:** replace the CEO approval queue with a tested, written decision-rights and commitment-control system, then reserve only the CEO capacity the new system actually releases.
- **Horizon:** July 15-August 26, 2026. Checks: July 17, July 22, July 29, August 12; final review August 26.
- **Primary route:** `building-and-evolving-organizations`. **Supporting route:** `operating-with-focus-and-morale` for the CEO's calendar, workload, and attention after authority is reassigned.
- **State:** 48 people, 17 months' runway, no fundraising; neither survival nor financing is primary.
- **Accountable owner:** CEO. The operations lead, or a CEO-appointed chief of staff if no operations lead exists, owns the decision log and rollout.

## Evidence ledger

**Observed:** 18 decisions waited over four business days; five signed commitments reached product after signature; two incidents lacked one decision owner; department leads exist; rights are unwritten. Operations verifies these against approval, contract, and incident records by July 17 and establishes median/p90 decision time, reversals, and missed handoffs.

**Claimed:** calendar crowding is the root cause. The proposed meeting purge, productivity system, and deep-work mornings have no demonstrated causal link to the queues.

**Consequential unknowns:**

- Whether waits come from rights ambiguity rather than capability or missing information. Operations codes all 18 by July 17. If fewer than 60% involve CEO approval/unclear authority, fix the dominant workflow instead.
- Whether commitments bypass product because of approval rights or handoff failure. Commercial and product leads audit all five by July 18. Any missing pre-sign feasibility review triggers a mandatory gate.
- Whether leads can decide safely within guardrails. Each domain lead records decision, rationale, consulted parties, and reversal for the pilot. More than 10% material reversals or one severe customer/safety/legal breach pauses that domain and narrows the guardrail.
- Whether meetings are coordination controls rather than waste. Meeting owners state decision/output and attendance by July 22; only meetings without a necessary output are canceled.

## One state-changing priority

**Outcome:** by August 26, the company operates a delegated decision system in the five named domains such that at least 90% of in-scope decisions close within two business days, zero customer commitments are signed before required feasibility review and product handoff, and every incident names one incident commander within 15 minutes.

Baseline is 18 decisions over four days, five late signed commitments, and two ownerless incidents in six weeks. The CEO owns the outcome; functional leads own their domains. The mechanism is explicit authority, guardrails, pre-sign handoffs, and escalation paths. A causal rival is that leads lack context or capability; another is broken intake tooling. Evidence should appear weekly. The cycle is falsified if, after three full pilot weeks with at least ten logged in-scope decisions, fewer than 80% close in two days or handoff/incident-owner failures recur without ambiguity being the cause.

Essential support, payroll, legal/fiduciary review, security, active incidents, and signed-customer continuity remain protected.

## Six-week action and stop/defer list

**July 15-17 — contain and map.** The CEO names an incident commander and executive backup for every live incident; response window is 15 minutes. Operations logs the 25 failures. The CEO and functional leads draft a one-page matrix for the five domains: decider, inputs, limits, escalation, backup, and response time.

**July 20-22 — assign rights.** Publish version 1 in one lead session. In-band pricing goes to the commercial lead; exceptions escalate to CEO and finance/legal. Enterprise promises require product/engineering feasibility sign-off before signature. Incident priority belongs to the incident commander, with engineering lead escalation. Product owns roadmap changes within quarterly outcomes and capacity. Hiring managers decide approved senior non-executive hires; CEO retains direct-report and company-shaping executive hires.

**July 23-August 12 — pilot and coach.** Leads decide within their guardrails without pre-clearance. Operations reviews the log weekly; CEO office hours are Tuesday and Thursday for escalations, not shadow approvals. Reversals become guardrail or coaching inputs, not automatic recentralization.

**August 13-26 — harden.** Adjust one weak domain at a time, appoint permanent backups, incorporate rights into onboarding and the deal/incident templates, and remove obsolete CEO approval steps.

Competing work: **cancel** purchase of a productivity system (CEO, now); **defer** blanket cancellation of recurring meetings until the July 22 output audit (operations); **bound** the audit to 90 minutes per lead and cancel only no-output meetings; **defer** two protected CEO mornings until July 23, then schedule them only from released approval time. Delegate status collection to operations; no new internal process initiative starts before August 26 without CEO exception.

## Protected execution, morale, and safeguards

From July 23, protect Tuesday and Thursday 9:00-11:30 for CEO strategy/user work when backup is active; blocks never override incident, legal, safety, teammate, or customer-continuity escalation. Hold two 45-minute escalation windows and one customer conversation weekly. Commercial and product jointly own commitment continuity; engineering owns incident coverage.

Limit the CEO to 45 hours weekly, no routine nights, one meeting-free half-day, and two full days off. Any incident surge gets an end date within 24 hours. Operations privately checks recovery, reversals, conflict, and skipped users. Rising errors, health symptoms, or two serious weekly reversals trigger backup coverage and recovery; acute concerns go to qualified help.

Morale is unknown; crowdedness is not a morale measure. Private weekly lead checks record blocked decisions, ownership, conflict, and workload. The premise is that faster, safe distributed decisions improve delivery; the tactic is this guarded pilot. One miss is a hard phase; repeated domain misses indicate tactic/capability failure; failures across redesigned guardrails and qualified owners challenge the premise.

## Cadence and decision

Operations reports weekly: in-scope volume, median/p90 decision age, percent within two days, CEO escalations, reversals, pre-sign feasibility compliance, incident-commander time, customer harm, and workload limit. July 17 closes acute containment; July 22 approves rights; July 29 checks adoption; August 12 chooses adjustments.

- **Continue:** at least 90% close within two days, zero commitment/incident-owner failures, no severe reversal, and safeguards hold.
- **Adapt:** 80-89% meet time or one non-severe domain miss occurs; change that domain's guardrail, context, or owner for a two-week test.
- **Reopen goal:** after two distinct bounded adjustments, fewer than 80% meet time or serious reversals show distributed authority is unsafe; CEO and leads decide by the next weekly review whether capability, role design, or workflow is primary.
- **Stop:** customer, legal, security, fiduciary, or survival harm crosses a hard boundary; restore temporary qualified approval only in the affected domain, with an owner and dated redesign.

**Decision now:** route organization design first and start containment. Preserve the option to restore narrow CEO approval in a failed domain, not the entire queue. Final branch owner is the CEO on August 26; hand back to ordinary focus planning only when rights are published, backups active, and two consecutive weeks meet the operating threshold.
