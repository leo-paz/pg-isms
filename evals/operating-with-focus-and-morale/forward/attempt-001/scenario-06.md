# Two-week financing operating record

## Header

- **Date and horizon:** July 15-29, 2026.
- **Decision:** reach a board-ready decision on the expiring Series A term sheet while testing only the strongest alternatives, without losing weekly product delivery or customer contact.
- **Primary route:** `raising-and-governing-capital` because dated diligence, negotiation, and acceptance decisions are live. `operating-with-focus-and-morale` supplies the supporting cadence. Reported 20-month runway means no survival emergency.
- **Accountable owner:** CEO. The CEO names a diligence coordinator by 3 p.m. July 15; startup counsel owns legal analysis and the board owns approval. The product lead owns product and customer continuity, not fundraising administration.
- **Checks:** daily 4:30 p.m. financing check through July 22; workload/continuity checks July 17 and 21; term decision July 22; process close, pause, or continuation decision July 29.

## Evidence ledger

**Observed:** 55 paying customers; reported 20-month runway; a three-week-old $3 million process; a Friday data-room/reference request; a term expiring July 22 with unresolved governance and liquidation provisions; 25 accepted introductions; two partnership meetings by July 25; slipped user calls; weekly releases and support owned by the product lead. The coordinator verifies runway and deadlines by July 16.

**Claimed:** the CEO says every investor meeting preserves momentum; the team reports fatigue from late-night requests. Neither claim proves meeting value, process quality, or sustainable capacity.

**Unknowns and tests:**

- By noon July 16, CEO and counsel produce a one-page term rubric covering governance, liquidation, dilution, control, investor fit, reference feedback, and closing risk. Counsel marks each unresolved term acceptable, negotiable, or a red line. A red line still open July 22 means decline or obtain a written extension; no signature by deadline pressure alone.
- By July 16, the CEO ranks all 25 introductions from written fund fit, check size, partner involvement, decision timetable, and conflict history. Only the top four with a credible partner decision by July 22 proceed; the rest are deferred or declined.
- By July 17, the CEO seeks five consenting references and fund confirmation that the data room is complete. Fewer than three produces a narrower consent-based list and disclosure, not customer pressure.
- By July 16, the CEO asks each partnership host for the decision, expected 90-day customer/revenue effect, and required attendees. Without a dated decision or credible near-term effect, the meeting moves past July 29.
- On July 16 and 21, the coordinator privately checks late work, sleep disruption, errors, and blocked product/customer work. Safety or health concerns go to qualified help; results change workload, not ratings.

## One priority

**Outcome:** by July 22, the board will accept or renegotiate an executable term meeting its precommitted rubric, or explicitly decline it and choose a bounded alternative. By July 29, it will either continue with no more than two funds that have dated partner-level next steps or pause the raise. This resolves the present company-binding decision; it does not pretend the round can close in two weeks.

**Baseline:** one expiring offer with unresolved material terms and an unfiltered 25-call queue. **Target:** counsel-reviewed comparison, board minutes, a written accept/renegotiate/decline decision, and no more than two active alternatives. CEO owns the target; customers and the eight-person team are affected.

**Mechanism:** concentrating on decision-ready funds improves information and negotiating attention. **Rival:** broad volume creates competition; the ranked-four test can detect that. Evidence comes from written responses, counsel review, references, and partner decisions. **Falsifier:** no acceptable term and fewer than two dated partner-level next steps by July 29. Weekly release, support, incidents, payroll, legal/fiduciary duties, customer commitments, and consent remain protected.

## Stop, defer, and bound

- **Investor introductions:** CEO bounds the 25 to four 30-minute calls in two pre-set windows through July 21; defer or decline 21 on July 16. No new introductions before July 22.
- **Partnerships:** CEO defers any meeting failing the July 16 test; qualifying meetings are bounded to 45 minutes, require an agenda, and cannot use the product lead unless a product decision requires it.
- **Diligence:** coordinator batches requests at 10 a.m. and 4 p.m. Cancel unrequested analysis and bespoke decks. Counsel handles legal materials; CEO handles investor judgment; product lead supplies one product packet, then exits.
- **Internal work:** cancel routine all-hands/status meetings through July 22; publish one written update daily. Defer media, conferences, nonessential redesign, and new internal initiatives until July 30.

## Protected execution and morale

The product lead retains Monday, Tuesday, and Thursday 9 a.m.-noon maker blocks, weekly release, and three customer conversations weekly. A named teammate covers first-line support; the product lead owns severity-one escalation with 15-minute acknowledgement. The coordinator backs up financing logistics; counsel and a designated director back up CEO decisions. Every request has an owner, deadline, and handoff note.

Investor availability is 10 a.m.-6 p.m. weekdays. No team diligence after 7 p.m.; next-day exceptions require CEO approval, a volunteer, and recovery within 48 hours. Cap team weeks at 45 hours and CEO at 50, with two meeting-free evenings and one full weekend day. The exception ends July 22. A missed release, over one skipped customer conversation, two support or safeguard breaches, or any judgment/health warning shifts work to CEO/coordinator backup.

Morale is a signal, not a verdict. Indicators are slipped user calls and late requests; fatigue requires private checks. Restore visible progress, user contact, ownership, recovery, and a July 22 finish line. Accountability stays private. The goal premise is that suitable Series A capital can accelerate a 55-customer product; the tactic is a bounded, ranked process.

## Cadence and decision rules

At each daily check, the CEO records fund status, next decision, rubric movement, and confounders; the product lead records release/support/customer-contact status; the coordinator records hours and safeguard breaches.

- **Continue:** on July 22, sign or continue toward closing only if counsel and board approve the rubric and continuity safeguards hold.
- **Adapt:** if the offered term remains negotiable but unresolved by July 18, request a written extension and concentrate on the top two alternatives; do not add calls. If reference or data-room evidence misses, narrow the artifact or sequence rather than expanding hours.
- **Reopen goal:** on July 29, if both the term negotiation and ranked-alternative test yield neither an acceptable term nor two dated partner steps, the board reassesses whether raising now is superior to six more weeks of product/customer progress.
- **Stop:** decline the term if a legal/governance red line remains on July 22. Pause the process if the board selects the preserved option, or immediately if a legal, fiduciary, health, safety, or customer-continuity boundary cannot be restored.

**Decision now:** run the financing workflow through July 22 under this envelope. **Preserved option:** pause with runway, customer relationships, a clean data room, and product cadence intact, then resume from stronger evidence. The CEO owns every financing branch; the board owns accept/pause; product continuity hands back to ordinary focus cadence on July 23 after a documented term decision, or July 29 after the process decision.
