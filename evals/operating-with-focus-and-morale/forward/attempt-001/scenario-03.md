## Morale, cadence, and decision rules

The morale signal is skipped users, rework, rollbacks, blocked owners, and nights. Test workload/authority and technical-quality explanations. In a private July 16 reset, acknowledge reversals, publish decision rights and the finish line, and measure customer evidence and stable releases, not hours.

The goal premise is that a recurring customer problem can be solved by a small, reliable improvement. The current tactic is a ten-customer bounded release, separate from the conference.

At each check, Lead A records calls, approval waits, releases, reversals, rollbacks, adoption, CEO hours/night work, confounders, and safeguard breaches.

- **Continue:** Four-of-six need evidence appears, safeguards hold, calls are not skipped, approval waits stay within one business day, and the release reaches five-of-ten seven-day use with at most one rollback.
- **Adapt:** Need remains credible but adoption misses or a technical gate fails. Change one variable—scope, workflow, segment, or release gate—and run one more bounded test; do not increase hours.
- **Reopen-goal:** Two successive tests across plausible workflows or customer segments miss the precommitted use threshold, or interviews fail to reproduce the problem. CEO decides with Lead A and the Customer Lead by August 12.
- **Stop:** Halt the conference release immediately for a second rollback/reversal, a material security/legal/customer-harm risk, or a health/sleep safeguard breach. Use the preserved option: attend minimally with the stable existing product and customer meetings, or withdraw if the five-meeting threshold is absent.

**Decision now:** Adapt the operating tactic and authority model; do not abandon the customer-value goal. The next owner is Lead A, with the first handoff review July 17. Return focus leadership to the CEO only after two consecutive weekly reviews show no late approval queue, no skipped calls, safeguards holding, and stable releases.
