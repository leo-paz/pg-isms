# Decision memo — CanopyTrace — May 11, 2026

**Decision and horizon.** Through June 15, make the sole company-changing priority: produce a comparable, decision-grade battery-life record that selects exactly one lead technical path for a representative 90-day field trial, while determining whether battery visits matter enough to customers to keep the 90-day objective. The CEO is accountable; the CTO owns technical evidence and the product lead owns customer evidence. Checkpoints are May 15, May 25, June 1, June 8, and June 15.

**State and route.** This is primarily a focus-and-morale problem: runway is 22 months, no financing or sale process is active, decision rights are written, and no acute safety, continuity, or authority failure is reported. Current grower service remains a binding obligation. The sensors provide planning data, but any change still requires appropriate certification, contractual, and safety review; certified irrigation and alarm systems do not excuse unsafe experimentation.

**Evidence ledger.** Observed: 412 sensors reached a median first change on day 57 and caused 94 visits across two cycles; no crop loss or missed response window was recorded. The sleep profile improved simulated life 36% but slowed field alerts from 20 to 95 seconds. The dense cell reached 108 lab days but adds $18, has a quoted 12-week lead time, and has unscoped certification. Solar results differ sharply between sunny and shaded rows. Trials are not comparable. Reassignments, 49-hour averages, weekends, single-person field coverage, and a pulse decline from 7.3 to 5.0 are also observed.

Claimed, not established: that the cell is “the answer,” that no crop loss means visits do not matter, and that seven concurrent initiatives learn fastest.

Consequential unknowns must get precommitted tests. By May 15, the CTO will publish a common sensor revision, reporting interval, environments, measures, sample rationale, and selection thresholds before reviewing new results; inability to normalize existing evidence prevents a June “winner” claim. By May 22, operations and a qualified reviewer will scope dense-cell supply, unit economics, and certification; leadership must set an affordability and lead-time ceiling before results. By June 8, the product lead and field owner will interview a deliberately varied set of current growers and quantify visit labor, disruption, acceptable alert delay, and stated renewal or expansion effect. That can guide selection but cannot substitute for observed renewal behavior; actual renewal, expansion, and service-cost data must be tracked through the next relevant renewal and completed crop cycles.

**Priority test.** The baseline is no representative 90-day field result and incomparable trials. The June 15 target is one lead, one explicitly preserved fallback, and an approved common-protocol 90-day field test. The proposed mechanism is that stable ownership and comparable testing will discriminate faster than task switching. Rivals are that performance is environment-specific or that the customer problem is too small to justify the cost. A June 15 choice is falsified if evidence remains non-comparable, the leading signal violates the newly established alert requirement, or certification and economics are unresolved.

**Stop and defer now.** Defer the enclosure refresh, mobile analytics screen, small-farm kit, and reseller packaging until June 15; the respective leads document handoffs by May 13. Cancel ad hoc engineer reassignment. Bound all three battery approaches to the common protocol—no feature expansion, promotional claims, or unsanctioned field changes. Continue ordinary support and existing contracted responses.

**Protected execution and morale.** Cap everyone at their normal scheduled week and, for the four engineers, at 40 hours; no routine weekends. The CEO removes work rather than stacking focus blocks onto it. Engineers receive four recurring three-hour morning blocks weekly. Product and field staff reserve two weekly grower-contact blocks. By May 15, the field owner trains two rotating backups, with a written handoff, existing contractual response windows, and escalation to the CTO; weekend duty earns equal weekday time off. Essential incidents override focus time. Review workload, errors, coverage, and the private pulse on May 15, June 1, and June 15. Treat morale decline as a signal plausibly caused by fragmentation, unclear finish lines, and overload—not as proof the goal is wrong. Any health symptom or acute risk goes promptly to qualified help.

**June 15 decision.** `Continue` with one lead only if normalized evidence supports 90-day feasibility and the alert, certification, cost, and support gates pass; then run the full representative cycle without claiming success early. `Adapt` if the 90-day premise remains plausible but one tactic or segment misses: change one material variable and set another bounded test. `Reopen-goal` only after repeated discriminating technical tests plus later observed renewal, expansion, labor, and service-cost evidence undermine the premise. `Stop` a path immediately for a safety, legal, certification, contractual, or precommitted economic boundary; stop the objective only when that broader premise is invalidated or a preserved option clearly dominates. Preserve the current configuration, service capability, raw trial data, and the runner-up path. On June 15 the CEO owns the branch and the next dated test.

**Caveat and lineage.** June 15 can select the next test, not prove 90-day field performance. This operating-record structure is cross-source synthesis; current technical, employment, contractual, certification, and safety facts require qualified revalidation.
