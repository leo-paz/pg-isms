On May 11, 2026, you are advising CanopyTrace, a ten-person hardware startup whose wireless greenhouse sensors are used by 19 paying growers. It has 22 months of runway, no active fundraise or acquisition discussion, written decision rights, a named field-support owner, and no open safety or customer-continuity incident. The sensors provide planning data; separate certified systems control irrigation and safety alarms.

CanopyTrace's current company objective is for a sensor deployment to last an entire 90-day crop cycle without a battery-change visit. Across 412 deployed sensors during the last two completed cycles, the median first battery change occurred on day 57 and growers requested 94 visits. Service records show no lost crop or missed contracted response window. The company has not measured how the visits affect renewal, expansion, or grower labor.

Three technical approaches are in progress. A sleep-mode firmware profile improved projected battery life by 36% in a 30-device bench simulation; its 18 days of field data also show alert delivery slowing from a median 20 seconds to 95 seconds. A higher-density cell ran 108 days in the lab, but would add $18 per device, its supplier quotes a 12-week lead time, and certification work has not been scoped. Solar caps have run 72 days without a change in two sunny greenhouses, while four of ten units in shaded rows crossed the low-battery threshold before day 50. No approach has completed a representative 90-day field cycle, and the trials use different sensor revisions and reporting intervals.

The same team is also building a cosmetic enclosure refresh, a mobile analytics screen, a lower-priced small-farm kit, and reseller packaging. Work logs record 21 engineer reassignments among the trials and these efforts in eight weeks. Four engineers averaged 49 hours a week during that period, including unplanned weekend trial checks; one field specialist currently covers every grower visit. The weekly team pulse fell from 7.3 to 5.0 out of 10, while ordinary support response remained within its prior-quarter range.

The CTO says the 108-day lab result proves the new cell is the answer. The product lead says the absence of crop loss proves growers do not care about battery visits. The CEO says keeping all three approaches and all four roadmap efforts active is the fastest way to learn. The field specialist says the weekend pattern cannot continue but has not proposed coverage. The board has asked for an operating recommendation at its June 15 meeting, but has not required a technical or product choice.

Write a decision memo dated May 11, 2026, no longer than 1,200 words. Recommend what CanopyTrace should do now, what it should not do during the next five weeks, how the team should work through June 15 without extending its current hours, and what leadership should decide then, including any later evidence it must wait for. Preserve service to current growers, state material caveats, and do not assume facts not provided.
