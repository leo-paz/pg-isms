# Decision memo: CanopyTrace battery-cycle focus

**Date:** May 11, 2026  
**Decision:** what to run through June 15 and what leadership decides then  
**Owner:** CEO accountable; CTO owns technical evidence; product lead owns grower evidence  
**Primary route:** `operating-with-focus-and-morale`  
**Checks:** May 15, May 29, June 8; final review June 15

## State and decision now

CanopyTrace is not in a survival, financing, acquisition, authority, safety, or continuity crisis: runway is 22 months, decision rights are written, and no incident is open. The current constraint is fragmented attention producing incomparable evidence while workload and morale deteriorate.

For the next five weeks, make the sole state-changing priority: **reach a defensible June 15 choice of one lead path for a representative 90-day field trial, while keeping contracted service performance intact.** This is not a claim that the 90-day objective can be proven by June 15. The earliest honest proof is a completed representative crop-cycle trial.

Use a common protocol on the current sensor revision and reporting interval. The CTO should lead a bounded, reversible sleep-profile field test because it can produce near-term field evidence; do not broaden deployment until alert latency is checked against contracts and grower needs. Continue collecting already-running cell and solar observations, but start no additional field cohorts. By May 22, obtain a written cell certification scope, supply confirmation, and per-deployment cost impact; document solar performance by light condition. These are option-preserving investigations, not three parallel builds.

## Evidence ledger

**Observed:** 412 sensors had median first change at day 57 and generated 94 visits over two cycles; no crop loss or missed contracted response window is recorded. Sleep mode improved simulated life 36%, with 18 field days and median alert delivery worsening from 20 to 95 seconds. The cell reached 108 lab days but adds $18, has a quoted 12-week lead, and lacks certification scope. Solar results differ sharply between sunny and shaded placements. There were 21 engineer reassignments, four engineers averaged 49 hours, weekends became unplanned work, and pulse fell from 7.3 to 5.0 while support response stayed stable.

**Claimed, not established:** the cell is the answer; growers do not care about visits; keeping every effort active accelerates learning.

**Consequential unknowns:**

- CTO, by June 8: under one protocol, estimate each viable path's day-90 battery trajectory and alert behavior, recording revision, interval, environment, failures, and confounders. A path remains eligible only if it has a credible route to 90 days without violating a current contractual or safety-related requirement.
- Product lead, by May 29: interview a documented sample of the 19 paying growers and review service records to determine visit labor, disruption, renewal or expansion relevance, and acceptable alert latency. Report counts and contrary cases; do not infer indifference from absence of crop loss.
- Operations owner designated by the CEO, by May 22: verify cell lead time, certification work, and $18 unit-economics effect. Material certification, supply, or margin failure removes the cell from immediate selection.

Current facts about contracts, certification, supplier capacity, and grower workflows require revalidation.

## Stop/defer and protected execution

Effective May 12, defer the enclosure refresh, mobile analytics screen, small-farm kit, and reseller packaging through June 15. CEO owns the freeze and may authorize only incident, contractual, legal, payroll, or continuity work. Cancel routine trial reassignments; bound status meetings to one 30-minute evidence review weekly and use one shared decision log.

Protect four contiguous engineering blocks per week and two scheduled grower-contact blocks. Keep the named field-support owner on service, but by May 15 the CEO must name and train a backup with a written handoff, escalation destination, and response window. No planned weekends; cap each person's schedule at 40 hours weekly through June 15. If service, health, fatigue, or errors threaten judgment, reduce trial scope before adding hours. Review workload and pulse privately each Friday. The morale drop is an operating signal plausibly tied to switching, unclear finish lines, and weekends—not proof that the objective is wrong.

## June 15 decision and later gate

Leadership should select one lead 90-day field tactic, one backup option, its representative cohort, owner, budget, and end date. **Continue** if one path meets the precommitted eligibility gates and safeguards hold. **Adapt** one material variable—profile, segment, hardware, or placement—if the 90-day premise remains plausible but no tactic clears its gate. **Reopen the goal** only after two discriminating, representative tests across plausible paths fail, or grower, economics, certification, harm, or opportunity-cost evidence undermines the premise. **Stop** a path immediately for a hard contractual, safety, certification, continuity, or unit-economics boundary.

Do not declare success on June 15. Wait for the chosen path to complete a representative 90-day field cycle, then compare battery visits, alert performance, service response, grower burden, failures, and economics with the May 11 baseline. Preserve the unchosen path's records and supplier/certification findings; do not keep its team active. The operating structure and combined branch thresholds here are synthesis, not a claim of a single-source recipe.
