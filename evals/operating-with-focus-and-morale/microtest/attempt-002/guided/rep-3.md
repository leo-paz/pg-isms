# Decision memo: one battery-life evidence loop

**Date:** May 11, 2026  
**Decision owner:** CEO; technical evidence owner: CTO; grower-evidence owner: product lead; continuity owner: field-support owner  
**Horizon:** May 11–June 15, with checks May 18, May 25, June 1, and June 8; evidence freeze June 12

## Decision now

Use focus-and-morale as the primary route. CanopyTrace is not in a survival, financing, acquisition, organization-authority, safety, or continuity crisis on the facts given. Its constraint is fragmented attention around an important but still unproved product outcome.

Make the sole company-changing priority: **by June 12, produce comparable field evidence that identifies which, if any, battery tactic merits a representative 90-day crop-cycle trial without weakening an agreed alert-service bound or grower service.** The June 15 meeting is a progression gate, not a declaration that 90-day durability has been proved.

Baseline is median first change on day 57 and 94 requested visits across 412 sensors in two cycles. The June target is one standardized dataset—same sensor revision, reporting interval, telemetry definitions, and representative sunny/shaded placement—in which a candidate has a conservative depletion projection reaching day 90, stays inside a written alert-latency limit, and has bounded unit-cost, supply, and certification implications. The CTO owns the protocol by May 18. A rival explanation is that revision, reporting interval, or site exposure—not the tactic—drives the apparent gain. Any unexplained service miss, noncomparable arm, or projection below day 90 falsifies advancement under this gate.

## Evidence ledger

- **Observed:** the deployment baseline; no crop loss or response-window miss; firmware's 36% bench projection and 20-to-95-second field alert slowdown; the cell's 108 lab days, $18 premium, 12-week quote, and unscoped certification; solar's sunny/shaded split; 21 reassignments; four engineers at 49 hours; falling pulse; normal support response.
- **Claimed:** the cell is already the answer; no crop loss means visits do not matter; keeping seven efforts active learns fastest.
- **Unknown:** tactic performance in a comparable 90-day field cycle; acceptable planning-alert latency; visit effect on grower labor, renewal, and expansion; certification scope; supplier reliability; and causal drivers of morale. By May 18, the product lead will interview a representative subset of current growers and document an alert bound plus visit burden; results determine the service threshold and whether visit reduction remains the objective. By May 25, the CTO will obtain a written certification assessment and supplier terms; unacceptable lead time, compliance path, or economics removes the cell. Weekly standardized telemetry determines advancement. The CEO will review workload and pulse privately each Friday; continued excess hours or worsening errors triggers scope reduction, not extra effort.

## Stop and protected execution

Effective May 11, the CEO **defers** the enclosure refresh, analytics screen, small-farm kit, and reseller packaging until June 15. Cancel roadmap status meetings; bound company status to 30 minutes weekly. Existing grower support, incidents, payroll, legal/fiduciary duties, and current contracts remain protected.

The CEO assigns people released from roadmap work to a single CTO-owned experiment, not three independent projects. Engineers receive two recurring three-hour maker blocks on four weekdays; notifications and meetings are excluded. The product lead schedules two grower-contact blocks weekly. By May 13, the field-support owner trains a backup and publishes the visit/incident handoff and escalation channel. Routine availability ends after business hours; no planned weekend checks. Cap work at 40 hours weekly through June 15, with one recovery day after any genuine incident callout. The CEO is backup decision-maker with a four-hour urgent-response window.

## Morale, persistence, and June 15 branches

Treat the pulse decline, reassignments, long weeks, and weekend checks as operating signals, not proof the goal is wrong. The leading hypothesis is fragmentation plus weak finish lines; weekly pulse, reassignments, errors, and support performance test it. Progress is reviewed privately against evidence, never hours.

On June 15 leadership will:

- **Continue** one candidate into a full representative 90-day trial if it clears all June gates and workload/service safeguards hold.
- **Adapt** one material variable—tactic, segment, reporting interval, or scope—if the premise remains plausible but one gate misses; set a new bounded test then.
- **Reopen the goal** only after two comparable representative full-cycle tests across plausible tactics miss, or grower/economic evidence shows visits are not worth solving.
- **Stop** a tactic immediately for a service, safety, legal/certification, supply, or precommitted economic boundary breach.

Preserve the current sensor/service configuration and paused roadmap as options. Final product proof must wait for a representative 90-day field cycle and later renewal/expansion evidence; June projections cannot substitute for either. Current certification, supplier, workload, and grower facts require owner revalidation. The routing, evidence classes, record, and branch thresholds here are cross-source synthesis, not attribution to any single essay.
