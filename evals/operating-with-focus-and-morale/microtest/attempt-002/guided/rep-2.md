# Decision memo — CanopyTrace

**Date:** May 11, 2026  
**Decision horizon:** May 11–June 15, 2026  
**Primary route:** focus and morale; no survival, financing, acquisition, organization-authority, safety, or continuity condition currently takes precedence.  
**Accountable owner:** CTO, with the CEO responsible for capacity and field coverage. Checks: May 18, May 25, June 1, and June 8; board decision: June 15.

## Decision now

Run one five-week battery-reliability evidence sprint: determine whether sleep-mode firmware has a credible path to a representative 90-day deployment without unacceptable delivery delay. It is the fastest reversible tactic with field data, not a chosen final answer. Use one sensor revision, one reporting interval, a control group, and deployments spanning relevant greenhouse conditions. By May 13, the CTO must publish the sample, assignment, battery measurement, latency measure, confounders, and a conservative rule for translating depletion into a 90-day projection; an independent technical reviewer should challenge the rule before enrollment.

The dated baseline is a median first change on day 57 and 94 visits across 412 sensors in two cycles. The eventual target is a representative full cycle without a battery-change visit. For June 15, the interim target is a conservative projected 90-day survival bound plus delivery latency within a limit established from grower workflow evidence. The proposed mechanism is reduced radio activity; rivals include revision, reporting interval, greenhouse conditions, and battery variation. Thirty-five days can expose early depletion and latency but cannot validate the 90-day claim. A full representative 90-day field cycle remains mandatory.

## Evidence ledger

**Observed:** runway is 22 months; there is no live transaction; decision rights and support ownership exist. Battery changes occur at day 57 median, with 94 visits and no recorded crop loss or missed response window. Sleep mode improved simulated life 36%, while 18 field days raised median delivery time from 20 to 95 seconds. The cell lasted 108 lab days but costs $18 more, has a quoted 12-week lead time, and unscoped certification. Solar results differ sharply between sunny and shaded placements. The team logged 21 reassignments; four engineers averaged 49 hours; pulse fell to 5.0; ordinary support held.

**Claimed, not proved:** the cell is “the answer”; absence of crop loss means visits do not matter; parallel work accelerates learning.

**Unknown:** acceptable delivery latency and visit burden on labor, renewal, and expansion. The product lead will interview a representative set of current growers by May 22, record workflow-specific limits and consequences, and translate them into a predeclared latency threshold. Also unknown are comparative field performance, certification scope, cell supply reliability, and solar segmentability. The CTO owns standardized trial evidence. Operations gets a written certification and supply assessment, capped at eight person-hours, by June 1. No claim advances on confidence alone.

## Stop, defer, and protect

Effective May 12, defer the enclosure refresh, mobile analytics screen, small-farm kit, and reseller packaging through June 15; the CEO owns enforcement. Pause new cell and solar engineering. Continue existing solar observation only if it requires no weekend work or protocol change; preserve its data and the cell supplier relationship. Cancel ad hoc engineer reassignment. Bound meetings to the trial, grower continuity, and essential company duties.

Protect recurring contiguous engineering blocks and weekly grower interviews. The CEO must name and brief backup field coverage by May 12; the field specialist is not the sole weekend responder. Existing contracted response windows, ordinary support, payroll, legal, certification, incident, and customer-continuity obligations override focus blocks. Use a rotating escalation path and written handoff; no planned weekends or continuous availability. Cap each person at 40 hours weekly through June 15, with at least one full recovery day. If coverage cannot fit, reduce trial scope—not safeguards.

The morale signal is the pulse decline alongside churn, long hours, and weekend checks; its cause is unknown. Weekly private checks should track reassignments, hours, recovery, errors, support performance, and pulse. The CEO owns a zero-unplanned-reassignment target and stable coverage. Hours are a health limit, never evidence of commitment.

## June 15 decision

**Continue** sleep mode to the full 90-day test if its conservative projection clears 90 days, grower-derived latency limits hold, service is intact, and safeguards hold. **Adapt** one variable if battery or latency misses while the 90-day premise remains plausible; choose cell work if certification, supply, and unit economics are viable, or a bounded solar segment test only if light conditions reliably identify success. **Reopen the goal** only after at least two representative, discriminating tactics fail or measured grower value and opportunity cost undermine the premise. **Stop** a tactic immediately for customer-continuity, safety, legal, certification, or material reliability harm.

Leadership should choose the next bounded tactic on June 15, not declare 90-day success. It must wait for a representative completed 90-day cycle and later renewal, expansion, grower-labor, and visit-cost evidence before validating the company outcome or its economics. The preserved option is the higher-density cell; its lead time is a planning constraint, not proof. All current technical, certification, supply, employment, and customer-workflow facts require revalidation by their qualified owners.
