# Decision memo — May 11, 2026

**Decision and route.** Through the June 15 board meeting, CanopyTrace should make one outcome the company priority: establish a defensible lead path into a representative, common-protocol 90-day battery trial while maintaining grower service and returning the team to sustainable hours. This is an `operating-with-focus-and-morale` constraint, not a runway, financing, acquisition, authority, safety, or continuity crisis. The CEO is accountable; the CTO owns technical evidence, the product lead owns grower evidence, and the field-support owner owns service. Checkpoints are May 15, May 22, May 29, June 5, and June 12; leadership decides on June 15.

**What is true.** Observed: 22 months of runway; no live transaction; median first change at day 57 and 94 visits across 412 sensors; no crop loss or missed response window; incomparable trials; 21 engineering reassignments; four engineers averaging 49 hours; unplanned weekends; one-person field coverage; and a pulse decline from 7.3 to 5.0. Claimed, not proven: the 108-day lab result identifies the winner; no crop loss means visits have no customer cost; keeping seven initiatives active accelerates learning. Unknown are visit effects on labor, renewal, and expansion; representative battery performance; acceptable alert latency; cell economics and certification; solar's viable greenhouse segment; and the cause of the pulse decline.

Resolve those unknowns with owned gates. By May 15, the CTO must issue one protocol using the same sensor revision, reporting interval, environment strata, and success/failure definitions; noncomparable data may inform hypotheses but cannot choose a winner. The CEO and finance owner must also set a written allowable unit-cost and schedule envelope; a qualified certification reviewer must scope the cell by May 22. A cell outside either envelope is parked. By May 22, the product lead must contact all 19 growers and record visit labor plus renewal/expansion consequences. Repeated labor burden or credible commercial risk across at least three growers supports the objective's economic importance; fewer than 12 usable responses leaves it unknown, not unimportant. The CTO and product lead must set an evidence-based alert-latency limit by May 15; firmware exceeding it fails that tactic. Solar may remain segment-specific, but its present shaded-row result disqualifies it as a general solution.

**Priority and falsifier.** The baseline is day 57 and 94 visits. The June 15 target is one lead tactic that passes the precommitted interim technical, customer, certification, cost, service, and workload gates and is enrolled in the common 90-day trial. The mechanism is reduced energy demand or increased supply; rivals include reporting cadence, revision differences, environment, and selection bias. June evidence is necessarily interim. If no tactic clears the gates, the five-week cycle ends without naming a winner and leadership adapts the experiment.

**Stop/defer now.** Effective May 12, the CEO defers the cosmetic enclosure refresh, mobile analytics screen, small-farm kit, and reseller packaging until June 15. Do not start new battery approaches. Bound the three existing approaches to protocol alignment, necessary data collection, firmware-latency testing, cell certification/economics scoping, and solar segmentation. Cancel status work not required for these gates; delegate ordinary support to its existing owner with backup coverage.

**Protected execution and morale.** Cap everyone at 40 hours weekly, with no planned weekend work. If a contracted visit or incident requires off-hours work, remove equivalent weekday work; hours are a health limit, not a determination score. By May 15, the CEO must name and train a backup for field support, with a written visit log, escalation path, and response window preserving current contracts. Protect recurring contiguous engineering blocks Tuesday through Thursday, two weekly grower-contact blocks, and two short daily support/decision windows. Essential support, incidents, payroll, legal, and fiduciary duties override deep work. Review hours, weekend calls, response performance, reassignments, pulse, and backup readiness privately each Friday. A repeated workload breach or service deterioration stops optional trial work and triggers recovery and coverage repair.

**June 15 decision.** `Continue` with one lead through the full representative cycle if its interim gates pass and safeguards hold. `Adapt` one material variable—tactic, segment, protocol, or scope—if the 90-day premise remains plausible but the tactic misses. `Reopen-goal` only after two discriminating representative cycles across plausible tactics or segments undermine the premise, or customer economics, harm, survival, or opportunity cost changes it. `Stop` a tactic immediately for an unacceptable latency, certification barrier, service harm, or hard cost boundary. Do not declare the technical objective achieved before a representative 90-day field cycle completes; also wait for observed renewal/expansion behavior when its latency permits. Preserve the current service model and deferred roadmap as options. This routing, evidence ledger, and branch structure are cross-source synthesis, not a Paul Graham recipe; current certification, employment, customer, and technical facts require qualified revalidation.
