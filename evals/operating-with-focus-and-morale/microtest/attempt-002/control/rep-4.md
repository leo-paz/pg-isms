# Decision memo

**Date:** May 11, 2026  
**To:** CanopyTrace leadership  
**Subject:** Focus the next five weeks on comparable battery evidence and sustainable service

## Recommendation

Keep the 90-day crop-cycle objective, but do not declare a technical winner now. The available results are promising but not comparable: they use different revisions and reporting intervals, none covers a representative 90-day field cycle, and each approach has an unresolved limitation. For the five weeks through June 15, make battery life the single development priority, use sleep-mode firmware as the primary field-learning track, conduct only bounded feasibility work on the higher-density cell, and pause solar-cap development. Freeze the four unrelated roadmap efforts.

This is a learning decision, not a production-selection decision. Sleep mode has the quickest field feedback and avoids the cell's unscoped certification and 12-week supply lead time, but its slower alert delivery could be unacceptable. The cell's 108-day lab run does not establish field performance, economics, certification timing, or availability. Solar results are too small and environment-dependent to justify team attention now.

## Actions through June 15

1. **Create one test protocol and one decision record.** Within the first week, the CTO should name a technical owner to define a common sensor revision, reporting interval, battery metric, greenhouse-condition segments, alert-latency measure, sample accounting, and failure criteria. Preserve the existing results, but label them non-comparable rather than combining them. Predefine what result would advance, revise, or stop each approach; leadership must explicitly set an acceptable alert-latency threshold rather than treating either 20 or 95 seconds as self-evidently acceptable.

2. **Make sleep mode the only active field experiment.** Continue it on a deliberately varied, documented set of sites without representing the five-week result as proof of 90-day endurance. Track battery trajectory, alert latency, low-battery events, visits, and support effects. Roll back or stop the profile if it breaches the predefined latency or service guardrail.

3. **Time-box cell feasibility, not implementation.** Assign a small, fixed block of regular-hours work to scope certification, supplier timing and risk, unit economics, physical compatibility, and a comparable field-test plan. Do not order a production commitment or redesign around it. Pause solar work and retain its data for a later targeted study of light exposure and shaded rows.

4. **Measure the customer problem.** The product lead should interview the 19 paying growers and review the 94 visits to estimate grower labor, disruption, trust, renewal or expansion concerns, and which visits were avoidable. Record evidence separately from interpretation. No crop loss shows that continuity has been preserved; it does not show that visits have no cost.

5. **Protect current service.** Maintain contracted response performance and the existing named support ownership. Train and schedule one backup for the field specialist, publish coverage before each week begins, and move trial inspections into weekday windows. Customer incidents take precedence over experiments. Any support degradation pauses added trial activity.

## What not to do

Do not restart the enclosure refresh, mobile analytics screen, small-farm kit, or reseller packaging before June 15. Do not run three engineering programs in parallel, switch engineers among initiatives, promise growers a 90-day result, treat projected life as observed life, or interpret five more weeks as a complete crop-cycle validation. Do not compensate for an overfull plan with weekends or hours above the team's current schedule.

## Working model

Leadership should publish the five-week stop list and stable assignments, with one owner per workstream. Hold a short weekly review of battery evidence, grower service, workload, and risks; change assignments only for a documented service escalation or a failed test guardrail. Cap work at normal weekday capacity. Remove or defer trial checks that do not fit. The field specialist's backup coverage is a leadership responsibility, not a proposal the specialist must invent.

## June 15 decisions

Leadership should decide whether battery-visit reduction remains the company objective after considering technical trajectories and customer evidence; which single approach earns a representative 90-day field trial; the staffing and explicit roadmap items that remain stopped; and the alert, service, cost, and workload guardrails for that trial. If the evidence does not distinguish an approach, choose the next discriminating experiment rather than a winner.

Leadership must wait for a completed representative 90-day field cycle before claiming the objective is met or broadly rolling out a solution. A cell decision must also wait for certification scope, supply assurance, and total economics. A solar decision must wait for representative shaded and seasonal exposure data. Product-priority claims about retention or expansion must wait for direct grower evidence and, where needed, observed commercial behavior.
