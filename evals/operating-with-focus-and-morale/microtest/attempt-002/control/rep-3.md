# Decision memo

**Date:** May 11, 2026  
**To:** CanopyTrace leadership  
**Subject:** Focus the next five weeks on a credible battery decision and sustainable service

## Recommendation

Make the 90-day crop-cycle objective the company’s single development priority through June 15. Pause the four unrelated roadmap efforts, stabilize the battery trials under one comparable protocol, and protect current-grower service with explicit backup coverage. Do not declare a technical winner yet. The evidence identifies promising paths and important tradeoffs, but none of the three approaches has demonstrated the objective in a representative 90-day field cycle.

This is an operating decision, not a conclusion that battery visits are currently causing customer harm. Ninety-four visits are a substantial service burden, but CanopyTrace has neither observed crop loss or response failures nor measured effects on renewal, expansion, or grower labor. Both facts should shape the plan.

## What to do now

The CEO should name one accountable owner for the battery program and freeze engineer assignments for five weeks. That owner should establish a common trial protocol: production-relevant sensor revision, reporting interval, deployment conditions, battery threshold, alert-latency measure, visit count, and failure/stopping rules. Existing results should be retained but labeled non-comparable; they should not be pooled into a winner claim.

Use the next five weeks to resolve the uncertainties that can be resolved by June 15:

- Scope certification work, supply risk, unit economics, and the earliest field test for the higher-density cell. Its 108-day lab run is encouraging, not proof of field performance.
- Continue a bounded field assessment of sleep mode, explicitly measuring whether the 20-to-95-second alert slowdown is acceptable for the product’s planning use. The separate certified control and alarm systems reduce safety concern, but do not establish customer acceptability.
- Preserve and document the solar results, segmenting sunny and shaded placements. Do not expand solar deployment until the team can explain the shaded-row failures and define where the approach is applicable.
- Contact a manageable sample of current growers using a consistent script to learn who performs battery-visit work, how disruptive visits are, and whether they affect renewal or expansion intent. Treat this as directional evidence, not a substitute for measured behavior.

## What not to do through June 15

Do not restart the enclosure refresh, mobile analytics screen, small-farm kit, or reseller packaging. Do not launch a new battery design, place a production-scale cell order, promise 90-day life, change trial configurations midstream, or use the absence of crop loss to infer that visits do not matter. Maintain only necessary fixes and support for the 19 paying growers.

## Sustainable operating cadence

Set regular-hours capacity before allocating work; do not plan weekend trial checks or extend the four engineers’ current hours. Use a fixed weekly review instead of repeated reassignments. Only a safety or customer-continuity incident should interrupt assigned work, and none is currently open.

Keep the named field-support owner responsible, but cross-train at least one backup and publish an on-call and visit schedule that fits within normal hours. Batch non-urgent visits where contracted response windows allow; preserve existing response performance. Leadership should review workload, support demand, trial exceptions, and the team pulse weekly. If the work exceeds capacity, reduce trial scope rather than add hours.

## June 15 decision

Leadership should choose one lead approach and, only if capacity and a distinct risk justify it, one backup. The choice should use pre-agreed criteria: credible route to 90 days, alert performance, representative field behavior, device cost, supplier timing, certification scope, deployment constraints, support burden, and team capacity. It should also decide whether the four paused roadmap items remain paused, ranking any restart behind the battery program and current service.

June 15 cannot be a validation gate for 90-day field performance: only 35 days will have elapsed. Broad rollout and objective-completion claims must wait for at least one representative, production-relevant 90-day field cycle using consistent reporting. Any commercial claim that fewer visits improve retention or expansion must also wait for evidence measuring those outcomes. The 22-month runway and absence of a fundraise, acquisition process, safety issue, or continuity incident provide room to make that decision deliberately.
