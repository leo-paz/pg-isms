# Decision memo

**Date:** May 11, 2026  
**To:** CanopyTrace leadership  
**Subject:** Focus the next five weeks on a comparable battery program and sustainable grower service

## Recommendation

Make completing a 90-day deployment without a battery-change visit the company’s sole development priority through June 15. Pause the four unrelated roadmap efforts, stabilize field coverage, and put the three battery approaches through one common decision framework. Do not declare a technical winner or deploy any approach broadly yet. The available results are promising but not comparable, and none demonstrates performance through a representative crop cycle.

This is an operating correction, not an emergency response. Current growers have suffered neither crop loss nor a missed contracted response window, and certified systems—not CanopyTrace sensors—control irrigation and safety alarms. Nevertheless, a median first change at day 57 and 94 requested visits show a substantial gap against the company objective. The customer and commercial consequences remain unknown, while the reassignment count, weekend checks, falling team pulse, and dependence on one field specialist are present operational risks.

## What to do now

Use the existing written decision rights to name one accountable program owner and freeze staffing for the five-week period. In the first week, that owner should publish a shared protocol covering sensor revision, reporting interval, greenhouse conditions, battery endpoint, alert latency, visit counting, and data-quality rules. Separate cohorts should test sleep mode, the higher-density cell, and solar caps under comparable conditions; do not blend their results. Preserve the existing trials as directional evidence, but do not treat them as head-to-head tests.

Define advancement criteria before reviewing more results. At minimum, they should cover probability of reaching day 90 without a change, alert-delivery performance, grower-visible service burden, unit cost, supply timing, and certification feasibility. Scope certification work and supplier risk for the new cell now, but do not order production quantities. Continue solar evaluation across sunny and shaded placements. Continue sleep-mode evaluation while explicitly tracking the observed latency increase; the absence of a safety-control role reduces one risk, but does not establish that growers accept 95-second alerts.

In parallel, conduct a small, structured review with current growers using existing relationships. Ask how battery visits affect their labor, confidence, renewal, and expansion decisions, and identify any alert-timing expectations. Record answers consistently rather than inferring indifference from the absence of crop loss. This work should inform product tradeoffs, not substitute for field performance evidence.

## How to work through June 15

Set a normal-hours capacity plan. Planned trial checks occur on weekdays. Reserve explicit field capacity for contracted support before allocating people to experiments, and designate and train at least one backup for the field specialist so every visit no longer depends on one person. Use a rotating, compensated exception roster only where current service commitments truly require out-of-hours response; experimental convenience is not such a requirement. If planned work exceeds available hours, reduce cohort size or defer work rather than extending the week.

Hold one short weekly review of service status, hours, trial quality, and decision-relevant changes. Reassign people only through the program owner and only when customer continuity requires it or the experimental plan is formally changed. The pulse score and individual hours should be reviewed weekly as operating constraints, not celebrated as effort.

## What not to do

Do not restart the enclosure refresh, mobile analytics screen, small-farm kit, or reseller packaging before June 15. Do not add new battery variants, mix hardware revisions or reporting intervals within a comparison, push trial checks into weekends, make a production-cell commitment, begin an unscoped certification path, or promise growers a 90-day result. Do not use the lab result, lack of crop loss, or parallel activity as proof of a decision.

## June 15 decision

Leadership should decide which approaches deserve funded, representative 90-day field trials, which should stop, and whether any approach is suitable only for a defined environment—for example, solar may prove conditional rather than universal. It should also decide whether the paused roadmap remains paused, based on the staffing required to complete those trials and sustain support.

June 15 should not be forced into a final product selection if the evidence is still immature. Final selection must wait for at least one completed representative 90-day cycle using comparable methods, including reliability and alert-latency results. A production commitment to the higher-density cell must additionally wait for certification scope, landed economics, and credible supply timing. A commercial claim that visits do or do not matter must wait for measured grower labor and renewal or expansion evidence. Until then, preserve current service and describe all findings as provisional.
