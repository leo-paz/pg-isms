# Decision memo

**Date:** May 11, 2026  
**To:** CanopyTrace leadership and board  
**Subject:** Focus the next five weeks on a comparable battery program and sustainable grower service

## Recommendation

Make completing a representative 90-day crop cycle without a battery-change visit the company’s sole development priority through June 15. Do not declare a technical winner yet. The current results are promising but incomparable: the trials use different revisions and reporting intervals, none has completed a representative field cycle, and each option has an unresolved downside.

Run one bounded evidence sprint centered on a common test protocol. Use the same sensor revision, reporting interval, battery-life measurement, alert-latency measurement, and representative placement rules for every retained candidate. Treat sleep-mode firmware as the primary near-term investigation because it already has field data and can expose the apparent battery/latency tradeoff, but limit deployment to a controlled subset. Do not broaden it until leadership defines an acceptable alert-delivery range and the field data show it stays within that range. Continue existing cell and solar observations only when they require no weekend checks or material new work; otherwise pause them.

The objective of June 15 is a disciplined operating decision, not a premature claim that the 90-day goal has been met.

## Actions now

The CTO should use the existing written decision rights to name one fixed battery-program team and keep its membership stable through June 15. Its first deliverables should be a common protocol, an inventory of which current results remain comparable, and a dated measurement dashboard. Track battery survival, battery-change visits per deployed sensor, alert latency, trial-support hours, and failures by greenhouse conditions. Record sensor revision and reporting interval with every observation.

Product and field staff should contact a practical sample of current growers to learn what visits cost them in labor or disruption and whether visit frequency affects renewal or expansion. Separate observed behavior from stated preference. No crop loss and no missed response window establish that service has held so far; they do not establish that growers are indifferent.

Protect current customers before expanding trials. The named field-support owner should control visit scheduling and may decline trial activity that jeopardizes contracted response. Leadership should assign and train a backup for routine coverage during normal hours. If adequate backup cannot be established from current capacity, reduce trial scope rather than add hours. Existing certified irrigation and alarm systems reduce the consequence of slower sensor alerts, but they do not make the change irrelevant.

## Do not do through June 15

Pause the cosmetic enclosure refresh, mobile analytics screen, small-farm kit, and reseller packaging. Do not start a fundraise, acquisition process, broad deployment, production cell order, certification work, or solar rollout on the evidence available. Do not use the 108-day laboratory result as proof of field performance, the two sunny sites as proof of general solar performance, or 18 days of firmware data as proof of a crop-cycle result.

Stop engineer reassignment among these efforts. Do not compensate for uncertainty with parallel work or weekend checks. Any emergency customer work remains in scope, but ordinary experimentation must fit within normal weekday capacity.

## Sustainable operating cadence

Plan weekly capacity before committing trial work, reserving enough field capacity to maintain current response performance. Hold one short weekly evidence review with the decision owners; cancel work that exceeds the capacity envelope instead of moving people. Give the four engineers who averaged 49 hours compensating reductions immediately so their schedules return to normal, and remove routine weekend monitoring. Use automated or next-business-day collection where operationally acceptable; otherwise shrink the trial.

Review team pulse, hours, support response, visit backlog, and reassignments each week. A worsening service measure or renewed overtime is a trigger to reduce experimental load, not evidence that the team must push harder.

## June 15 decision and later evidence

On June 15, leadership should choose one primary approach for a representative 90-day field cycle, with at most one explicitly bounded fallback. The choice should weigh standardized early field evidence, alert latency, grower burden, support capacity, and known implementation constraints. For the cell, require a scoped certification path, delivered-cost impact, and supply-risk assessment. For solar, require performance across shaded as well as sunny placements.

Leadership should also decide which paused roadmap item, if any, deserves capacity after the battery program is sustainably staffed; restarting all four would recreate the current failure mode.

Final technical adoption must wait for a complete representative 90-day field cycle using the common protocol. Renewal and expansion effects may require still later evidence as customer decisions occur. Until those results exist, describe all conclusions as provisional and preserve the ability to change course.
