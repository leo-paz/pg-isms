# Decision memo

**Date:** May 11, 2026  
**To:** CanopyTrace leadership and board  
**Subject:** Focus the next five weeks on reliable evidence and sustainable field service

## Recommendation

CanopyTrace should make the 90-day crop-cycle objective its only active product-development priority through June 15. Pause the cosmetic enclosure refresh, mobile analytics screen, small-farm kit, and reseller packaging. None addresses the stated objective, and keeping all seven efforts active has produced frequent reassignments, long hours, and deteriorating team sentiment without resolving the battery problem.

Designate the sleep-mode profile as the primary field-learning track for these five weeks because it is software-based, already has some field exposure, and is comparatively reversible. This is a test priority, not a product decision: its 36% bench improvement would not by itself carry the observed day-57 median to 90 days, and the alert-delay increase may be unacceptable even though the sensors do not control irrigation or safety systems.

Pause further solar expansion and new-cell hardware trials during this period. Preserve the existing solar observations, and perform only bounded desk work on the cell: obtain a certification scope, total cost implications, supplier commitments, and the earliest realistic deployment schedule. Do not place a production order or declare any approach the winner.

## Work through June 15

Run one documented, representative firmware trial using a single sensor revision, fixed reporting interval, predefined greenhouse and row conditions, and consistent measures. Record battery trajectory, alert latency, missed alerts, visits, and support load. If the existing devices cannot be normalized without disrupting growers, collect comparable data only where feasible and state the limitation rather than blending unlike results. Set explicit stop conditions for material service degradation or unacceptable alert performance.

In parallel, interview a practical cross-section of the 19 paying growers and begin recording the labor and operational burden of battery visits, along with renewal or expansion concerns. The absence of crop loss does not establish that visits are harmless; equally, 94 visits do not establish churn impact. Ask rather than infer. Do not change contracts, pricing, or customer promises based on this short study.

Maintain ordinary support and all contracted response windows. The named field-support owner should retain operational responsibility, but leadership should assign and train at least one backup from the existing team and publish weekday coverage. Weekend checks should occur only when required to preserve current service or satisfy a predefined trial stop condition, with equivalent weekday time removed. Leadership should review workload weekly, cap each person's work at the current regular schedule, and reduce trial scope if the work does not fit. No result is important enough to require another 49-hour average.

Use the existing written decision rights. One accountable technical owner should run the trial; the product lead should own grower research; the field-support owner should define feasible service coverage. Hold one short weekly evidence review. Reassign people only through that forum for a documented service risk or failed stop condition, not to advance paused roadmap work.

## Do not do before June 15

Do not generalize the 108-day lab cell result to deployed performance, treat sunny solar results as representative, or roll sleep mode out fleet-wide. Do not promise a 90-day cycle, start a fundraise or transaction process, add roadmap work, or compensate for uncertainty with overtime. There is no reported safety, continuity, financing, or transaction emergency requiring those moves.

## June 15 decisions

Leadership should decide whether the firmware profile has earned continued representative testing, whether alert latency requires a different configuration, and whether the cell's certification, economics, and lead time justify making it the next primary track. It should also decide whether solar is a condition-specific option worth a later standardized test, and which paused roadmap item, if any, resumes without diluting the battery objective.

The June meeting cannot honestly select a proven 90-day solution. Five more weeks will not produce a representative completed 90-day field cycle. Before a fleet-wide choice or customer claim, leadership must wait for at least one standardized, representative 90-day field result on the intended hardware and reporting configuration, including visit and alert performance. Renewal, expansion, and grower-labor conclusions must likewise wait for measured evidence. The board should receive the June decision, open uncertainties, workload trend, and the date of the next evidence gate.
