# Decision memo — May 11, 2026

**Decision:** For the five weeks ending June 15, CanopyTrace should make one outcome the company priority: select one lead and at most one backup capable of entering a representative 90-day field validation for a full-cycle deployment, using comparable evidence and explicit user, alert-latency, cost, supply, and certification gates. CEO owns the operating decision; CTO owns technical evidence; product lead owns grower evidence. Checks are May 15, May 22, May 29, June 5, and June 12; the board review is June 15.

**Route and state.** This is primarily an attention, evidence, and sustainable-execution problem, so `operating-with-focus-and-morale` is the primary route. It is not presently a survival, financing, acquisition, organization-authority, safety, or customer-continuity route: runway is 22 months; there is no live transaction; decision rights and field ownership are written; and no incident is open. The sensors provide planning data rather than certified irrigation or alarm control. Those facts do not make alert latency or service continuity irrelevant; any contrary contract, safety, legal, or field fact should be escalated immediately by the CEO to a qualified owner.

## Evidence ledger

**Observed:** Across 412 sensors and two completed cycles, median first change was day 57 and growers requested 94 visits; no crop loss or missed response window was recorded. The sleep profile improved simulated life 36%, but 18 field days coincide with median alert delivery worsening from 20 to 95 seconds. The denser cell lasted 108 lab days, costs $18 more per device, has a quoted 12-week lead, and has unscoped certification. Solar reached 72 days in two sunny houses, while four of ten shaded-row units crossed low battery before day 50. Trials are not comparable. Twenty-one engineering reassignments occurred in eight weeks; four engineers averaged 49 hours, including weekends; one specialist covers all visits; pulse fell from 7.3 to 5.0 while normal support response held.

**Claimed, not established:** the lab cell is the answer; absence of crop loss means visits do not matter; keeping every effort active maximizes learning. None establishes representative durability, customer value, or learning speed.

**Unknowns and tests:** By May 15, the CTO will freeze a protocol using one sensor revision and reporting interval, or prespecify stratification where that is impossible, plus an acceptable alert-latency/service gate derived with product and field owners from contracts and grower workflow. Crossing that gate stops expansion of the relevant profile. By May 22, the CTO will deliver a written certification scope, verified supply terms, and unit-economics range for the cell; infeasible certification timing, supply, or economics removes it from lead status. By May 29, the product lead will seek structured input from all 19 growers, separately analyzing growers with and without visits, recording labor, disruption, trust, renewal/expansion effect, and willingness to accept latency or price tradeoffs. Before interviews begin, the CEO will approve a decision threshold; a majority of responding visit-exposed growers reporting material recurring burden or purchase impact supports keeping the objective high priority. A weak or biased response remains unknown, not evidence of indifference.

The proposed mechanism is that stable ownership plus comparable trials will reveal which approach can eliminate change visits without unacceptable tradeoffs. Rivals are environment, sensor revision, reporting interval, visit practice, or a weak customer need—not battery technology alone. No honest 90-day field conclusion is possible by June 15.

## What to do now—and stop doing

The CTO should bound technical work to a single evidence program: comparable bench characterization and grower-agreed field cohorts for the sleep profile and denser cell, with stable owners and no mid-cycle reassignment absent a service escalation. Do not expand sleep-mode exposure until its latency gate is set. Freeze solar-cap development as a universal solution; preserve its telemetry and treat a sunny-only segment as a later adaptation, because current shaded evidence conflicts with a general deployment claim.

Effective May 11, cancel the cosmetic enclosure refresh; defer the mobile analytics screen, small-farm kit, and reseller packaging through June 15. The product lead owns clean handoff notes so restarting does not require reconstruction. Do not place a production cell order, begin unscoped certification, promise a 90-day result, or declare a technical winner. The CEO owns these dispositions and may reopen one only for an acute continuity, legal, or contractual need—not preference or visible busyness.

## Protected execution and workload

Beginning May 12, the CTO will publish stable assignments and remove the four roadmap streams rather than stacking trial work on top. Cap each engineer at 40 hours per week, with no planned weekend checks. Protect three meeting-free half-days weekly for trial analysis/build work; hold one short weekly evidence review; move status reporting to the shared protocol record. Notifications and executive availability are bounded outside a named service escalation.

The field-support owner retains current grower continuity and response commitments. By May 15, the CEO must name and train a qualified backup, document each active deployment and escalation path, and establish a rota. Weekend work is reserved for a contracted response or genuine continuity issue, followed by time back the next workweek. If qualified backup cannot be established, reduce trial enrollment rather than rely on the sole specialist. The CEO checks hours, weekend work, missed service, reversals, and pulse weekly. Any renewed 49-hour pattern, two consecutive unplanned weekends, missed response window, health concern, or material error triggers immediate workload reduction and backup review; acute health or safety issues go to qualified help.

Morale is an operating signal here: the observed decline accompanies switching, long hours, weekends, and an unclear finish line, but does not prove the goal is wrong. Stable ownership, visible weekly evidence, grower contact, private problem-solving, normal hours, and a dated decision are the intervention. Raw hours and public pressure are not determination measures.

## June 15 decision and later gates

The goal premise is that avoiding a battery-change visit over a 90-day crop cycle creates sufficient grower and company value without unacceptable service, cost, supply, or certification tradeoffs. The current tactic is a controlled comparison, not simultaneous productization.

On June 15, the CEO decides, with CTO and product lead evidence:

- **Continue:** name one lead and at most one backup for a representative 90-day field cycle if protocol compliance and early trajectory are credible, the alert/service gate holds, and dependencies are viable.
- **Adapt:** if the premise remains plausible but one gate misses, change one material variable—profile, cell, environment segment, or combination—and precommit a new bounded test. Sunny-only solar is one preserved option, not a general conclusion.
- **Reopen-goal:** only if grower evidence materially weakens the value premise and subsequent discriminating evidence confirms it, or opportunity cost, capability, or harm changes the objective. CEO owns this branch.
- **Stop:** stop an approach that breaches a hard service, safety, legal, certification, supply, or economic boundary; stop the objective only after representative evidence invalidates its premise or a superior preserved option meets a precommitted rule.

Leadership must wait for at least one comparable, representative 90-day field cycle before claiming the objective is met. Renewal and expansion effects may require later contract decisions and must be measured when they occur. Preserve raw trial data, solar segmentation evidence, supplier optionality, and deferred-project notes. The June 15 output is a disciplined validation choice, not a launch claim.

This record's routing, evidence classes, cadence, and decision branches are cross-source synthesis. All prompt facts—especially contracts, latency needs, supplier terms, certification obligations, customer burden, and team health—require current verification by their named owners.
