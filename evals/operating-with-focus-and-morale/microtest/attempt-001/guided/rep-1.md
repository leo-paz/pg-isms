# Decision memo: CanopyTrace battery-cycle priority

**Date:** May 11, 2026  
**Decision:** What to prioritize through the June 15 board meeting  
**Accountable owner:** CEO; CTO owns technical evidence, product lead owns grower evidence, field-support owner owns service continuity  
**Primary route:** `operating-with-focus-and-morale`. Runway is 22 months, there is no live financing or buyer process, decision rights are written, and no authority, safety, or continuity crisis is reported. Acute incidents, contractual breaches, or a material runway change would route first to their qualified owners.  
**Checks:** May 15, May 22, May 29, June 5, and June 12; leadership decision June 15. The product conclusion comes only after a representative 90-day field cycle.

## Recommendation

Make the single company priority: **put one technically plausible configuration into a predeclared, representative 90-day field validation that aims for zero battery-change visits across the tested deployment, without unacceptable alert delay, missed contracted response, or extended hours.** The June 15 outcome is not “prove 90 days”; five weeks cannot supply that evidence. It is to select one primary configuration, freeze a comparable protocol, start or schedule the valid cycle, and measure whether visits matter to growers.

The dated baseline is 412 sensors across two completed cycles, median first change on day 57, and 94 requested visits. There was no recorded crop loss or missed contracted response. The target needs a deployment definition, cohort size, environmental strata, acceptable alert latency, and statistical pass rule. CTO must propose these by May 15 from the 19-grower fleet; CEO approves them May 18 with product and field review. Inventing a cohort threshold here would overstate the facts.

The proposed mechanism is that energy storage or consumption is the binding cause of visits. Rivals are sensor revision, reporting interval, shade, deployment conditions, or a visit problem too unimportant to justify the cost. The falsifier for the current tactic is failure under the frozen protocol or breach of its latency, service, workload, certification, or economic gate. The broader 90-day goal is not falsified by one incomparable or partial trial.

## Evidence ledger and gates

**Observed:** the fleet outcomes above; 36% projected improvement in a 30-device bench simulation; 18 field days with median alert delivery worsening from 20 to 95 seconds; a cell lasting 108 lab days with an $18 device increment, 12-week quoted lead time, and unscoped certification; solar lasting 72 days in two sunny houses while four of ten shaded units crossed low battery before day 50; 21 engineering reassignments in eight weeks; four engineers averaging 49 hours; unplanned weekend checks; one field specialist covering all visits; pulse falling from 7.3 to 5.0; and support response staying in its prior-quarter range.

**Claimed, not established:** the cell is “the answer”; absence of crop loss means growers do not care; keeping seven initiatives active is fastest learning. The approaches are not comparable because revisions and reporting intervals differ, and none has completed a representative 90-day field cycle.

**Consequential unknowns:**

- CTO, by May 15: normalize trial records and issue a protocol covering revision, reporting interval, environment, cohort/power, alert-latency limit, measurement, and rollback. A protocol that cannot distinguish configurations means no winner may be declared.
- CTO, by May 22: obtain a documented certification scope, field-unit availability, landed cost, and supply risk for the dense cell. If certification or availability prevents a timely valid cycle, preserve it as a later option rather than the primary tactic.
- CTO and field-support owner, by May 22: determine whether solar can serve the predeclared representative conditions. Failure in required shaded conditions blocks a fleet-wide solar claim; it may support a sunny-site segment test.
- Product lead, by June 5: invite all 19 growers to a structured review of labor minutes, disruption, satisfaction, renewal/expansion relevance, and acceptable alert latency, and reconcile answers with service and commercial records. Report response count and dissent; do not treat nonresponse as indifference. This evidence changes the economic priority, not the battery physics.
- CEO, weekly: compare assigned hours, weekend work, support performance, and reassignments. Any planned load above 40 hours, uncovered contractual response, or renewed switching pauses new trial work until coverage is restored.

On May 22 the CEO and CTO will choose the one configuration that best clears these gates. If none clears, choose the most reversible discriminating experiment, not a production solution. Existing trials may continue passive, safe data collection, but the two unselected approaches receive no new engineering work through June 15.

## Stop/defer and protected execution

Effective May 11, the CEO defers the cosmetic enclosure refresh, mobile analytics screen, small-farm kit, and reseller packaging until June 15; only contractual support, defect correction, incident response, and preservation work continue. Bound board preparation to one written update and two hours per owner per week. Cancel ad hoc trial reassignments; the CTO alone changes assignments at the weekly evidence review and records why.

Each engineer has four recurring three-hour maker blocks per week, with notifications and routine meetings off. Company meetings are bounded to a 30-minute Monday allocation check and 45-minute Friday evidence review, plus essential customer and incident coordination. Product lead schedules direct grower contact without using the field specialist as the default interviewer.

Cap planned work at 40 hours weekly through June 15. Schedule no weekend trial checks unless an existing service obligation or genuine continuity incident requires them. By May 15, the CEO must assign and train qualified backup field coverage and document the handoff, customer history, contractual response window, and escalation path. Until backup is qualified, the CEO owns escalation and removes discretionary trial work; deep-work blocks never override current-grower service. The field-support owner receives two protected non-call periods weekly. Any exceptional response ends when continuity is restored and is followed by equivalent recovery time.

Morale is an observed pulse decline plus overload and switching, not proof that the goal is wrong. Test whether stable ownership, visible weekly evidence, direct grower contact, an honest 90-day finish line, and recovery improve the signal. CEO reviews workload and pulse privately each Friday; hours and public pressure are not determination scores.

## June 15 decision and later evidence

Leadership should **continue** the selected field cycle if the protocol is representative, prerequisites are met, grower service is intact, and workload safeguards hold. It should **adapt** one material variable—profile, cell, solar segment, or deployment scope—if the goal remains valuable but the chosen tactic misses a precommitted feasibility gate. It should **reopen the goal** only if grower/commercial evidence shows visits have little material effect and a better constraint is evidenced, or repeated representative tests across plausible tactics undermine 90-day feasibility. It should **stop** a tactic for an unacceptable latency or customer-continuity effect, an unresolvable certification boundary, or an economic/workload boundary leadership precommits on May 18.

June 15 can authorize one bounded 90-day test and preserve the other designs, supplier quote, and collected data. It cannot authorize fleet rollout or claim success from lab projection, sunny-only results, or partial field survival. Leadership must wait for the frozen cohort's day-90 result, visit count, latency and support record, environmental breakdown, total unit/service economics, and grower labor plus renewal/expansion evidence. On June 15 the CTO must date the honest day-90 review from actual cohort activation; certification or the 12-week supply lead may make that later than September 13. The CEO owns the final `continue`, `adapt`, `reopen-goal`, or `stop` decision at that review.
