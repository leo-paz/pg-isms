# Decision memo — May 11, 2026

**Decision and route.** Through June 15, CanopyTrace should make one outcome its priority: produce a comparable, field-grounded basis for choosing the next representative 90-day battery trial, while maintaining current grower service and normal hours. This is an `operating-with-focus-and-morale` constraint: runway is 22 months, there is no live financing or sale process, decision rights exist, and there is no open safety or continuity incident. The CEO should name the accountable decision-right holder from the existing matrix by May 12. The CTO owns technical evidence, the product lead owns grower-value evidence, and the field-support owner retains service continuity. Checks are May 18 and June 1; leadership decides on June 15.

## What is true, and what is not

**Observed:** Across 412 sensors and two completed cycles, median first change was day 57 and growers requested 94 visits; no crop was lost and no contracted response window was missed. The firmware simulation projected 36% improvement, but only 18 field days exist and median alert delivery slowed from 20 to 95 seconds. The denser cell lasted 108 lab days, costs $18 more, has a quoted 12-week lead, and unscoped certification. Solar reached 72 days in two sunny houses, but four of ten shaded-row units crossed low battery before day 50. Trials are not comparable. There were 21 engineering reassignments in eight weeks; four engineers averaged 49 hours, weekends were unplanned, one specialist covers every visit, and pulse fell from 7.3 to 5.0 while support held steady.

**Claimed, not established:** the lab cell is “the answer”; absence of crop loss means visits do not matter; maximum parallelism is fastest learning. These claims do not establish field durability, customer value, or learning speed.

**Consequential unknowns and tests:**

- By May 18, the CTO will publish a common protocol—same revision and reporting interval, greenhouse/shade strata, battery and alert measures, sample-size rationale, and pre-registered pass/fail gates derived from product commitments. Any approach exceeding the agreed alert-latency or delivery-reliability limit is adapted or dropped; no safety significance should be inferred because certified systems, not these sensors, control irrigation and alarms.
- By June 1, the product lead will contact all 19 paying growers and report responses separately from nonresponses: labor/time per visit, workflow disruption, perceived value, and whether a visit would affect renewal or expansion. Service logs will add company travel and labor. If visits have no material customer or service-economic consequence, leadership reopens the objective rather than treating 90 days as intrinsically valuable.
- By May 18, an owner designated by the CTO will document certification scope, sample availability, delivered cost, and supplier timing for the denser cell. Unresolved certification or unavailable samples keeps it at feasibility stage; 108 lab days alone cannot advance it as the chosen solution.

## One priority and bounded tactics

The baseline is day 57 median first change and 94 requested visits; the company objective remains a full 90-day cycle without a battery-change visit. The June 15 target is narrower: complete 35 days of comparable evidence where possible, identify decisive failures, and approve at most the smallest set of candidates justified for a representative 90-day field cycle. The proposed mechanism is that fewer battery changes reduce grower and service burden. Rivals are that visits are immaterial, alert tradeoffs cost more value, or environment and reporting settings—not the power source—drive failures. A falsifier for this five-week cycle is that the protocol cannot distinguish candidates or customer/economic evidence does not support the objective. June data may gate a trial; it cannot prove 90-day performance.

Firmware and solar may receive only the work required by the common protocol. The cell receives certification, supply, cost, and sample feasibility work—not a production commitment. Preserve solar as a possible sunny-house segment rather than generalizing from two sites. Record all configuration differences and failures; do not harmonize them after seeing results.

Effective May 12, **defer** the cosmetic enclosure refresh, mobile analytics screen, small-farm kit, and reseller packaging until the June 15 decision owner explicitly restarts one. **Cancel** ad hoc engineer switching. **Bound** internal status to one 30-minute evidence review weekly. Do not add a conference, media, investor, or board-deck workstream; the decision owner prepares a short evidence snapshot from the same ledger.

## Execution, service, and morale safeguards

Each engineer gets four recurring three-hour maker blocks weekly, with trial ownership held stable through June 15. Work is scheduled within a 40-hour week; there are no planned weekend trial checks. The field-support owner will name and train a backup by May 18, publish the existing contracted escalation path and handoff record, and rotate any genuinely required after-hours response with equivalent time removed that week. Grower contacts, ordinary support, incidents, payroll, legal, and fiduciary duties override focus blocks. If coverage cannot be staffed within these bounds, reduce trial scope rather than extend hours.

Track weekly: hours and weekend work (operations owner), reassignments (CTO), support-window performance and visits (field owner), grower contacts (product lead), protocol results (CTO), and pulse plus recovery concerns (CEO). The workload limits are zero planned weekends, no engineer above 40 scheduled hours, and no unowned grower coverage. A breach triggers workload removal and backup coverage within two business days. Pulse is a signal, not a verdict; test whether stable ownership, visible evidence, user contact, and recovery reverse the decline. Health concerns go to qualified help, not a productivity intervention.

## June 15 decision and later evidence

Leadership should `continue` by funding one representative 90-day field trial only if a candidate passes the pre-registered alert/reliability gate, shows a credible battery trajectory, has feasible cost/supply/certification, and the workload safeguards hold. `Adapt` if the 90-day premise remains valuable but a tactic misses: change one material variable—profile, segment, reporting interval, or hardware—and run a bounded test. `Reopen-goal` if grower and service evidence shows visits are immaterial, or repeated comparable tests undermine feasible 90-day operation. `Stop` an approach immediately for a hard certification, service-continuity, safety, or pre-registered reliability failure; stop the objective only after the decision owner records premise failure or a superior use of the same capacity.

Preserve the unchosen approaches and deferred roadmap artifacts as documented options, not active projects. Leadership must wait for a representative completed 90-day field cycle before claiming success; renewal/expansion effects require observation at their actual decision dates, and the denser cell requires current certification and supply evidence. Product requirements, contracts, certification, employment limits, and supplier facts must be revalidated by qualified owners.
