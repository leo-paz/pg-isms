# CanopyTrace decision memo

**Date:** May 11, 2026  
**Decision:** Set one battery-reliability evidence program through June 15; do not select or deploy a winning approach yet.  
**Horizon:** Five-week operating cycle, followed by a representative 90-day field validation.  
**Primary route:** `operating-with-focus-and-morale`. CanopyTrace is not in a survival, financing, acquisition, authority, safety, or continuity crisis on the facts provided.  
**Accountable owner:** CEO. Technical evidence: CTO. Grower evidence: product lead. Service continuity: field-support owner.  
**Checks:** May 18, May 25, June 1, June 8; decision review June 15.

## Recommendation now

Make the sole company-changing priority: **by June 15, produce comparable evidence sufficient to choose one lead battery tactic for a representative, opt-in 90-day field validation, while maintaining current grower service and a sustainable workload.** The company objective remains a full 90-day crop cycle without a battery-change visit. The next five weeks cannot prove that outcome: no candidate has completed a representative 90-day field cycle, and the existing trials differ in revision and reporting interval.

Freeze productization of all three approaches. Run only the minimum work needed to compare them on a common sensor revision, reporting interval, environment categories, battery threshold, alert-delivery measure, and test protocol. Preserve the current supported configuration and require an explicit rollback plan for any field unit.

## Evidence ledger

**Observed:** Across 412 sensors in two completed cycles, median first change was day 57 and growers requested 94 visits; no crop was lost and no contracted response window was missed. Sleep mode improved projected life 36% in a 30-device simulation, but 18 field days show median alert delivery moving from 20 to 95 seconds. The denser cell lasted 108 lab days, adds $18, has a quoted 12-week lead time, and has unscoped certification work. Solar caps reached 72 days in two sunny houses, while four of ten shaded-row units crossed low battery before day 50. There were 21 engineer reassignments in eight weeks; four engineers averaged 49 hours, weekend checks occurred, and pulse fell from 7.3 to 5.0. Ordinary support performance held.

**Claimed, not established:** The lab result proves the new cell is the answer; absence of crop loss means growers do not care; keeping every effort active is fastest learning.

**Unknowns and owners:**

- Product lead, by May 25: interview a defined cross-section of the 19 paying growers and inspect service records to quantify visit labor, disruption, renewal/expansion relevance, and acceptable planning-data alert latency. Report response counts and contrary cases, not anecdotes. If visits are immaterial, leadership must reassess the value and economics of the 90-day objective; if material, use the measured burden in selection.
- CTO, by May 18: publish the common protocol and reconcile existing data by revision and interval; by June 8, report normalized bench and limited field-screen results, uncertainty, and failures. Noncomparable results cannot select a winner.
- Operations/finance owner designated by the CEO, by May 25: verify cell pricing, supply commitment, lead-time risk, gross-margin effect, and certification scope. An unbounded certification path or unacceptable unit economics blocks the cell from lead selection.
- Field-support owner, by May 18: document visit load, weekday coverage, trial burden, and escalation capacity. Any trial that breaks contracted response or leaves no backup is paused.

## Stop/defer choices through June 15

Effective May 12, the CEO defers the cosmetic enclosure refresh, mobile analytics screen, small-farm kit, and reseller packaging. Only defect, contractual, safety, security, or current-customer continuity work proceeds. The CEO also cancels further ad hoc engineer reassignment and bounds technical work to the shared protocol. Solar remains a possible sunny-site tactic, not a universal solution; denser-cell procurement beyond reversible samples is deferred; sleep-mode rollout beyond a small opt-in, rollback-ready screen is deferred until latency acceptability is measured.

## Protected execution and workload

Remove deferred work before reserving time. Each engineer gets four weekday blocks of at least three contiguous hours for the evidence program; meetings are consolidated into two bounded windows weekly. The product lead holds two grower-contact blocks weekly. Trial notifications go to one channel and are checked at scheduled weekday times.

Cap engineers at 40 scheduled hours per week through June 15; no planned nights or weekends. By May 15, the field-support owner cross-trains a named primary backup and the CEO names a second escalation backup. The field owner retains current-grower continuity authority; trial work yields to contracted support. After-hours response is limited to existing incident/contract obligations, with a written handoff and next-business-day queue for everything else. If a genuine exceptional push is authorized, the CEO records its reason, coverage, recovery time, and end no later than June 15. Check fatigue, errors, reversals, weekend work, response performance, and pulse weekly; a missed response window, repeated unplanned weekend work, or rising errors triggers workload reduction and CEO review within one business day.

Low morale is an operating signal, not evidence against the goal. The leading mechanisms to test are switching, invisible progress, single-person field coverage, and recovery loss. Restore ownership and visible weekly evidence; do not use hours or public pressure as determination scores.

## June 15 decision and later gates

On June 15, the CEO decides with the CTO, product lead, and field owner:

- **Continue:** choose one lead tactic and begin a representative, opt-in 90-day field validation only if common-protocol evidence favors it, grower latency/service gates hold, economics and certification have bounded paths, and workload safeguards held.
- **Adapt:** if the 90-day premise remains plausible but the lead tactic misses a gate, change one material variable—such as segment, reporting interval, or power approach—and authorize one bounded test. Solar may qualify only for evidenced sunny conditions.
- **Reopen-goal:** do not do this merely because the five-week screen is inconclusive. Reopen after two representative, discriminating field validations across plausible tactics fail, or credible grower/economic evidence shows avoiding visits does not justify the cost and opportunity burden. The CEO owns this decision.
- **Stop:** stop a tactic immediately for a hard certification, safety, customer-continuity, or contractual failure; stop the objective only if its premise is invalidated or a survival boundary later supersedes it.

Leadership must wait for the full representative 90-day cycle before claiming the technical objective is met. Review field outcomes after day 90, including first-change distribution, visits, alert latency, support load, grower impact, failures by environment, and confounders. Preserve the current serviceable configuration and reversible candidate documentation until that decision.

**Lineage and caveats:** The routing, evidence classes, record structure, and branch thresholds are cross-source taxonomy synthesis, not a claimed Paul Graham recipe. Supplier, certification, contractual, employment, health, and field facts require current verification by their qualified owners.
