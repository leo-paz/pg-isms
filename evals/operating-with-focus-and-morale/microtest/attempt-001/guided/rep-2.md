# Decision memo — May 11, 2026

**Decision:** Make a comparable field-validation loop for the 90-day battery objective CanopyTrace's sole product-development priority through June 15. The CTO is accountable; the CEO owns resource protection and the June decision. Checkpoints are May 18, May 25, June 1, June 8, and June 15.

## State and evidence

This is an attention-allocation and sustainable-execution problem, not a survival, financing, acquisition, organization-design, or incident response state. Runway is 22 months; no transaction is active; decision rights and field support ownership exist; no safety or continuity incident is open. Current-grower service, payroll, legal/fiduciary duties, and incident response remain protected. The sensors provide planning data, but that does not make slower alerts automatically acceptable.

**Observed:** 412 sensors had a day-57 median first change and generated 94 visits over two cycles; no crop was lost and contracted response windows were met. The sleep profile improved simulated life 36%, but 18 field days coincided with median alert delivery rising from 20 to 95 seconds. The new cell lasted 108 lab days, costs $18 more, has a quoted 12-week lead time, and has unscoped certification. Solar performance differs sharply between sunny and shaded placements. Trials are not comparable. There were 21 engineering reassignments in eight weeks; four engineers averaged 49 hours, weekends occurred, pulse fell from 7.3 to 5.0, and ordinary support performance held.

**Claims, not facts:** the new cell is “the answer”; absence of crop loss means visits do not matter; and keeping seven efforts active is fastest. Each exceeds the evidence.

**Consequential unknowns:**

- By May 18, the CTO will publish one protocol: common sensor revision and reporting interval, representative placement strata, control arm, endpoints, exclusions, sample-size rationale, alert-latency limit, and 90-day completion rule. A qualified certification reviewer will scope the new cell by June 8. If comparability, safe/contractual use, supply, or certification cannot be established, that tactic cannot become the primary arm.
- By June 5, the product lead will review visit records and interview all reachable growers, seeking coverage across high- and low-visit accounts, to measure grower labor, disruption, and stated renewal/expansion effect. The result may change urgency or segment choice; it cannot by itself prove future retention.
- By May 18, the CEO and field owner will record actual visit load, weekend triggers, backup capability, and response coverage. Any uncovered response obligation blocks a new trial expansion.

## One priority and five-week scope

The outcome by June 15 is **a stable, representative cohort running under one protocol, with decision-grade early evidence and no service or workload breach, so leadership can commit to one primary 90-day validation path**. The baseline is no completed comparable 90-day field cycle. The final target remains a representative deployment completing 90 days without a battery-change visit; June 15 is an allocation gate, not proof of that target.

By May 18, the CTO will gate the three tactics and select at most two feasible candidate arms plus a control. Sleep mode is eligible only with a precommitted alert-latency threshold grounded in contracts and grower workflow. Solar must be stratified by light exposure and cannot be generalized from sunny sites. The higher-density cell is eligible for field allocation only if available units are comparable and a credible certification, supply, and unit-economics path exists; otherwise preserve it as a later option. Launch or normalize the cohort as soon as the protocol and coverage gates pass, then freeze owners and configuration.

The proposed mechanism is reduced power draw or increased/harvested energy. Rivals include sensor revision, reporting interval, light exposure, battery variability, and measurement artifacts. A candidate is falsified by an early battery threshold crossing, unacceptable alert performance, infeasible certification/supply economics, or failure concentrated in its intended placement segment. No historical trial should be pooled as if comparable.

## Stop, defer, and protect

Effective May 12, the CEO defers the enclosure refresh, mobile analytics screen, small-farm kit, and reseller packaging until June 15. No conferences, media, optional investor work, or new internal initiatives enter the cycle. Essential support continues under the field owner. The product lead owns grower discovery; the CTO owns the protocol and technical cohort. Reassignments require CEO approval and only for an incident or continuity need.

Do not promise 90-day performance, select a production design, order at scale, start certification work beyond the bounded scope, or infer retention from crop outcomes during these five weeks.

Cap each person's planned work at 40 hours weekly, with no planned weekends. Remove deferred work rather than stacking focus blocks on existing load. Schedule four recurring contiguous engineering blocks weekly, one grower-contact block, and one 30-minute evidence review. The CEO must designate and train a field-coverage backup by May 18; the field owner remains escalation owner, with the existing contracted response window as the outer limit and a written handoff log. Trial alerts go to the CTO's named backup outside working hours; genuine incidents use the existing escalation path. After any unavoidable callout, cancel equivalent planned work and provide recovery time.

Morale is an operating signal, not evidence against the objective. The observed decline is consistent with switching, weekends, an unclear finish line, or other unknown causes. The CEO will privately check workload and blockers weekly; pulse is tracked, but progress is judged by comparable technical and grower evidence, not hours or confidence.

## June 15 decision

The CEO, advised by the CTO, product lead, and field owner, will:

- **Continue** one primary tactic through a full representative 90-day cycle if its early field trajectory passes the frozen battery and alert thresholds and service/workload safeguards hold.
- **Adapt** one material variable—segment, reporting interval, placement, or configuration—if the 90-day premise remains plausible but the tactic misses a precommitted threshold; set a new bounded test.
- **Stop a tactic** if it crosses the latency, battery, service, certification, supply, cost, or workload boundary.
- **Reopen the objective** only after repeated comparable tests across plausible tactics/segments undermine it, or measured grower value and opportunity cost show another objective dominates. The CEO owns that later decision; one five-week miss is insufficient.

Preserve the runner-up tactic and current service process as options. Leadership must wait for a completed representative 90-day field cycle before claiming the objective is met or rolling out broadly, and for observed renewal/expansion behavior over its natural cycle before claiming commercial impact. On June 15, record results, confounders, safeguard status, selected branch, owner, budget, and the next dated full-cycle review.

**Caveats and lineage:** Current technical, contractual, certification, employment, and grower facts require qualified revalidation. The routing, evidence classes, cadence, and branch thresholds are operating synthesis; this memo makes no claim that they are a source author's prescribed recipe.
