# Decision memo

**Date:** May 11, 2026  
**To:** CanopyTrace leadership and board  
**Subject:** Focus the next five weeks on a comparable battery decision and sustainable grower support

## Recommendation

CanopyTrace should make the 90-day deployment objective its single product-development priority through June 15, while maintaining current grower service. It should freeze the four unrelated roadmap efforts, stop rotating engineers among projects, and run one bounded evidence program that makes the three battery approaches comparable. This is a five-week decision sprint, not a five-week promise to select or ship a solution.

The company should not declare a technical winner now. The higher-density cell's 108-day lab run is promising but does not establish field performance, certification feasibility, supply availability, or acceptable economics. The firmware result is early and includes a material alert-latency tradeoff; even its projected 36% improvement cannot be treated as a representative 90-day result. Solar performance varies sharply by light conditions. None has completed the representative field cycle the objective requires.

This is important, but it is not an emergency that justifies sustained overtime. No crop has been lost, contracted response windows have been met, the sensors do not control irrigation or safety alarms, and the company has 22 months of runway with no transaction deadline. Conversely, the absence of crop loss does not prove visits are harmless: renewal, expansion, and grower labor effects have not been measured. Leadership should treat both claims as unknowns.

## Actions now

The CEO should use the existing decision rights to announce a June 15 focus period and freeze new work on the cosmetic enclosure, mobile analytics screen, small-farm kit, and reseller packaging. Preserve existing work; do not polish, launch, or add scope. Exceptions should require an actual current-grower continuity need, not roadmap preference.

Create one battery program with stable assignments through June 15:

- A technical owner should define a common comparison protocol: the same sensor revision where feasible, the same reporting interval, explicit greenhouse and row conditions, and common measures for battery state, alert-delivery latency, visit demand, failures, and operator time. Existing results should be labeled non-comparable where they differ rather than pooled.
- Make firmware the primary near-term field-learning track because field data already exist and it does not carry the cell's stated procurement lead time. Continue only a controlled cohort, with predefined latency and battery thresholds and no broad deployment. Do not assume that planning-data alerts can slow from 20 to 95 seconds without consequence; product should establish acceptable response needs with growers.
- Limit the higher-density cell work to a time-boxed feasibility package: scope certification, obtain a credible schedule and total per-device cost implications, and clarify what the 12-week supplier lead time means for validation and availability. Do not order a rollout quantity or redesign around it yet.
- Stop expanding the solar trial. Continue collecting scheduled data from existing units only when that does not require extra hours or service risk. Segment sunny and shaded results; the four early low-battery thresholds in shaded rows make a universal claim unjustified.
- The product lead should conduct structured conversations with a representative selection of the 19 growers and review available service records to estimate who performs work during visits, disruption, perceived value of 90 days, acceptable alert latency, and whether visit frequency affects renewal or expansion intent. Report observations separately from inferred commercial impact; the company lacks outcome data today.

Current growers come first. The named field-support owner should retain service authority and contracted response windows should not change. Establish at least one weekday-trained backup if an existing team member can competently fill that role. If competent backup cannot be created in five weeks, leadership must reduce trial activity rather than rely on the field specialist's weekends. No new trial should create an unsupported service obligation.

## Working model through June 15

Set a normal-hours capacity budget for every person and schedule trial checks during working hours. End routine weekend trial checks immediately; genuine current-grower support obligations remain covered under the established service process. Any work that exceeds capacity is removed from the sprint, not absorbed at night or on weekends.

Keep assignments stable. The 21 reassignments in eight weeks are themselves a constraint on learning and morale. The CTO owns protocol and technical evidence, the product lead owns grower evidence, the field-support owner owns service protection, and the CEO resolves priority conflicts under the existing rights. A short weekly review should cover service performance, staff hours, pulse trend, protocol deviations, and evidence against the common measures. It should not reopen the frozen roadmap each week.

The team should publish a one-page scorecard. It must distinguish measured field results, laboratory results, projections, and unknowns. Include confidence and caveats, not just point estimates. If service response deteriorates, hours rise, or a trial requires unscheduled weekend intervention, pause the responsible trial and restore service capacity.

## June 15 decisions

Leadership should decide three things on June 15:

1. Whether the 90-day objective remains the highest-value company objective, informed by grower evidence and the operational cost of 94 visits—not by crop loss alone.
2. Which single approach, or explicitly defined combination, advances into a representative 90-day field validation; which alternatives are stopped or parked; and what cost, latency, reliability, and service thresholds constitute success.
3. Whether current field-support coverage and engineering capacity can sustain that validation within normal hours. If not, reduce scope or fund qualified coverage; do not plan around continued overtime.

Leadership should not authorize general rollout, promise 90-day life, or represent any approach as proven on June 15. A shipment decision must wait for at least one representative 90-day field cycle using controlled revisions and reporting intervals across relevant operating conditions. The cell path must also wait for certification scope and supply evidence; solar claims must wait for adequate shaded-condition evidence; firmware adoption must wait for validated alert-latency acceptability. Commercial conclusions should wait for actual renewal, expansion, labor, or other credible customer evidence. June 15 is the date to choose the next disciplined bet—not to manufacture certainty the current data cannot support.
