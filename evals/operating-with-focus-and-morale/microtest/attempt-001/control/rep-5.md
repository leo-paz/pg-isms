# Decision memo

**To:** CanopyTrace leadership  
**Date:** May 11, 2026  
**Subject:** Focus the next five weeks on comparable evidence and sustainable field operations

## Recommendation

For the next five weeks, make the 90-day battery objective the company’s sole development priority and run one controlled, representative field experiment comparing the three battery approaches with the current product. Pause the cosmetic enclosure refresh, mobile analytics screen, small-farm kit, and reseller packaging. Do not select a permanent technical solution before June 15, and do not treat any current result as decisive.

This is a focus decision, not yet a technology decision. The baseline is plainly short of the company objective: among 412 sensors, the median first change was day 57 and growers requested 94 visits. But the company does not know the commercial or labor cost of those visits, and no current approach has completed a comparable 90-day field cycle. Concentrating the team will improve the evidence available to the board while ending the reassignment and weekend pattern that is already degrading team pulse.

## Actions now

Appoint one accountable experiment lead under the existing written decision rights. By May 15, that lead should publish a short protocol that fixes the sensor revision, reporting interval, battery-life definition, deployment conditions, and data collection method across all arms. Include the current configuration as a control. Sample across sunny and shaded conditions representative of the installed base, rather than extrapolating from a bench simulation, a laboratory cell test, or two sunny greenhouses. The protocol should state in advance what would count as a low-battery event, a visit, an alert delay, and missing data.

The experiment must measure more than days to battery threshold. Track alert-delivery latency, grower-requested and company-initiated visits, staff time per visit, and any missed contracted response window or customer-continuity issue. Begin a lightweight customer-impact study with the 19 growers: ask consistently about grower labor, disruption, satisfaction, renewal intent, and expansion relevance. This will not establish causation in five weeks, but it will replace the claim that “no crop loss” means visits do not matter with actual evidence. The sensors supply planning data and certified systems separately control irrigation and safety alarms, so the observed latency change is not presented as a safety failure; it may still affect product usefulness and must be tested.

Immediately start parallel desk work that does not require choosing the higher-density cell: obtain a scoped certification plan, total installed cost implications, supplier capacity and lead-time confirmation, and a schedule showing when qualified cells could reach deployments. The $18 unit cost and quoted 12-week lead time are material, but their business consequences cannot be inferred from the information available.

Preserve current service. Keep the named field-support owner as the single coordinator, but give that person scheduled backup from a cross-trained teammate during normal working hours. Publish a visit rota and escalation contact. Reserve explicit weekly capacity for ordinary support and battery visits before allocating capacity to trials. Existing response performance is stable; do not jeopardize it to accelerate experimentation.

## What not to do before June 15

Do not declare the higher-density cell the winner because it reached 108 days in a lab. It lacks representative field evidence, certification scope, and a near-term supply path. Do not conclude that growers are indifferent because no crop was lost; renewal, expansion, labor, and inconvenience have not been measured. Do not broadly deploy the sleep-mode profile while its field data show median alert delivery slowing from 20 to 95 seconds. Do not generalize solar performance from sunny sites while shaded-row failures remain unresolved.

Do not continue all seven workstreams as active learning. Pause the four roadmap efforts cleanly: record their current state, owner, dependencies, and safe restart point, then stop assigning engineering time to them. Do not begin a fundraise, acquisition process, full product repricing, fleetwide retrofit, certification commitment, or supplier purchase merely to create urgency; none is required by the facts or necessary for the June meeting.

## Operating model through June 15

Set fixed five-week staffing and prohibit casual reassignment. Use a weekly planning meeting to allocate normal-hour capacity among experiment setup and monitoring, data analysis, customer-impact interviews, supplier/certification discovery, and protected support coverage. Every new request must displace named work and be approved through the existing decision rights; it cannot simply be added.

End unplanned weekend trial checks. Configure checks for business hours where possible, schedule any genuinely time-bound observation as part of the normal weekly workload, and compensate by reducing weekday allocation rather than adding hours. The field-support backup handles conflicts. Hold a brief twice-weekly experiment review and one weekly workload review using hours, support load, reassignments, and the team pulse. Leadership should intervene if assignments exceed ordinary hours or support coverage becomes thin. The goal is not to maximize activity by June 15; it is to produce trustworthy evidence without further exhausting a ten-person team.

## June 15 decision

Leadership should decide whether to continue, narrow, or stop each technical path; whether the 90-day objective remains the right near-term company objective; and which paused roadmap item, if any, restarts with a named owner and explicit capacity. The decision packet should compare early field results on battery trajectory, latency, environmental performance, visits, support burden, grower feedback, cost, supply, and certification feasibility. Apply predeclared criteria: credible path to a representative 90-day cycle, acceptable planning-data responsiveness, reliable performance across relevant conditions, operational feasibility, and justified economics.

June 15 will arrive before any newly standardized deployment can complete a full 90-day cycle. Therefore leadership may select a leading path or discontinue a clearly inferior one, but it should not authorize a fleetwide final solution unless evidence already justifies that action. Final technical selection must wait for a representative, comparable 90-day field cycle and, for the new cell, scoped certification and credible supply evidence. Any claim about retention, expansion, or grower labor should remain provisional until customer-impact data are collected over enough time to support it.

Material caveat: five weeks can improve comparability and expose weak paths, but it cannot prove durability over 90 days or quantify long-term commercial effects. The appropriate June outcome may be a narrower experiment and a later final decision—not artificial certainty.
