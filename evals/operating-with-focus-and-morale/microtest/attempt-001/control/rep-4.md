# Decision memo

**Date:** May 11, 2026  
**To:** CanopyTrace leadership and board  
**Subject:** Five-week operating plan for battery-cycle reliability

## Recommendation

For the next five weeks, make preserving current-grower service while producing decision-grade battery evidence the company’s only active product-development priority. Treat June 15 as a portfolio gate, not as a deadline to declare a technical winner. The available evidence does not support the CTO’s, product lead’s, or CEO’s conclusion: a 108-day lab run does not establish field performance; no crop loss does not establish that visits are commercially harmless; and seven concurrent efforts are producing switching and overtime, not comparable learning.

Continue the sleep-profile work as the primary field investigation because it already has 18 days of field data and can generate useful evidence during this window. Limit the higher-density cell to a tightly bounded feasibility track covering certification scope, supplier timing, and economics. Pause additional solar-cap development, while recording data from already-installed units during ordinary working hours if that creates no extra grower visit. This is an operating choice for five weeks, not a conclusion that sleep mode will meet the 90-day objective.

## Actions now

By May 15, name one battery-program owner using the existing written decision rights. That owner should issue one common test protocol: the same sensor revision, reporting interval, definitions, and logging across treatment and comparison devices. It should measure time to low-battery threshold and first change, visit incidence, missing or delayed alerts, alert-latency distribution, greenhouse and row conditions, and staff time. Predefine rollback conditions for unacceptable alert performance or support burden. Existing trials can remain as directional evidence, but their heterogeneous results must not be pooled or presented as a controlled comparison.

Keep the existing sleep-profile cohort running if it can be monitored safely and within contracted service expectations. Add a matched comparison only to the extent current inventory, grower consent, and normal-hour staffing permit; do not disrupt customer installations merely to improve the experiment. Before expanding the cohort, product and field owners must determine what alert delay growers consider operationally acceptable and check any applicable commitments. The change from a 20-second to a 95-second median is not a demonstrated safety problem because separate certified systems handle irrigation and safety alarms, but it may still reduce the planning product’s usefulness.

Time-box the cell track to documents and vendor work that can be completed without waiting for the quoted 12-week supply lead: obtain a written certification-work scope, sample and production timing, the conditions behind the 108-day lab result, and the per-device commercial effect of the additional $18. Do not place a production order or start an unscoped certification program during this window.

The product lead should interview a practical cross-section of the 19 paying growers, without promising a solution. Record battery-visit labor, disruption, perceived severity, acceptable alert latency, and whether the issue affects stated renewal or expansion intent. Interviews can reveal needs, but they cannot establish actual renewal causality. Begin tracking future visit, renewal, and expansion outcomes consistently.

## What not to do before June 15

Pause active engineering and launch work on the cosmetic enclosure refresh, mobile analytics screen, small-farm kit, and reseller packaging. Preserve notes and restart points, but accept no “small” side tasks from those efforts. Do not start another solar deployment, broadly roll out sleep mode, combine approaches without a separate test, declare the cell selected, or market a 90-day claim. Do not use ordinary support performance to justify more experiments, and do not use the absence of crop loss to dismiss a burden that has not been measured.

## Team operating model

Freeze staffing for the five-week window: a stable battery group, a clearly staffed current-grower service function, and a small, capped cell-feasibility assignment. No person should be reassigned among these streams without the program owner recording which work stops as a result. Use one short weekly review of protocol adherence, customer service, evidence, hours, and decisions; avoid daily executive redirection.

Return the four engineers averaging 49 hours to normal weekly hours immediately and eliminate planned weekend trial checks. Schedule trial inspections and data reviews during weekdays. Actual contracted customer issues retain priority over experiments. Because one field specialist covering every visit is a continuity and burnout risk, designate and train at least one backup from current staff and publish daytime coverage through June 15. The field specialist should not personally absorb both routine service and experimental monitoring. If normal-hour capacity is insufficient, reduce the experiment’s size or pace—not service coverage and not rest. Track weekly hours, weekend interventions, grower response performance, visit load, and the team pulse.

## June 15 decisions and later evidence

On June 15, leadership should choose one primary path for a representative 90-day field cycle and, only if normal-hour capacity remains, one explicitly capped fallback. The decision should consider comparable field survival and alert data, grower feedback, service burden, cell certification and supply facts, incremental economics, and team workload. Leadership should also decide whether any paused roadmap item merits restart; no more than one should restart unless named capacity exists after service and the battery cycle are staffed.

Sleep mode should advance only if its alert behavior is acceptable and its field trajectory justifies completing the cycle. The denser cell should become primary only if certification, supply, and economic questions are sufficiently scoped to support a field test; its lab result alone is insufficient. Solar should remain paused unless the company can define a credible environment-specific segment and test shaded-row failure rather than generalize from two sunny greenhouses.

CanopyTrace must wait for a completed, representative 90-day field cycle using consistent hardware and reporting before claiming that the company objective is met. It should also wait for actual renewal and expansion observations before claiming visits affect—or do not affect—commercial outcomes. June 15 evidence can justify the next focused test, not certainty that current data cannot provide.
