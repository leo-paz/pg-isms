# Operating decision — July 15 to August 5, 2026

## Decision

**One company-changing priority:** make a new teacher’s first session with the core deck reliably start without staff help. This is the constraint most directly supported by current user evidence and, if removed, could turn interest into repeatable school demand.

**One current tactic:** run a three-week, observed quick-start-insert experiment: create the smallest reordered instruction insert, print test copies, use them in the 12 already-booked new-teacher sessions, and revise only from observed setup failures.

This is not a promise to redesign the whole product. It is a focused test of whether instruction order is the immediate obstacle.

## What we know, think, and do not know

**Facts:** Only 5 of 14 new teachers completed setup without help; 7 of the 9 who stalled independently said the quick-start order was unclear. Four of the 5 successful teachers requested quotes. A test insert can be printed in two business days, and 12 sessions are booked for July 22, 24, and 28. Six approvals recently took two to three days rather than the prior norm of four working hours. Morale is 3/10, two teacher calls and two peer critiques were skipped, two print decisions were reversed, and Lena has worked 67-hour weeks with no day off.

**Interpretations:** Instruction order is the best-supported near-term bottleneck. Successful setup may increase commercial intent, but the quote requests show association, not causation. The approval slowdown likely relates to Lena’s overload because its timing coincides with her workload increase, but that cause is not proven. Low morale is likely being worsened by excess hours, missed customer contact, reversals, and lost team rituals; “visible hours” is not evidence of progress.

**Unknowns:** We do not yet know whether a reordered insert fixes setup, what other failure modes exist, whether quote requests become orders, or whether the 12 sessions resemble a larger market. We have no evidence that an expo, AI-literacy deck, or catalog redesign will solve the observed setup problem.

## Decision gates

- **Continue the insert tactic after July 22** if at least 75% of that day’s teachers set up unaided and no new repeated blocker appears. Because the sample is small, treat this as a practical signal, not proof.
- **Change the insert tactic** if fewer than 75% succeed or at least two teachers stall at the same step: revise that step once and retest July 24. If the printer timing prevents a revision, use clearly marked paper prototypes; do not infer failure from logistics.
- **Stop this tactic and choose a different setup intervention** if two distinct insert versions fail to improve unaided setup, or if observation shows the blocker is not instruction order. Preserve the goal; do not keep polishing an unsupported solution.
- **Continue the priority through August 5** if the 12-session evidence trends materially above the 5/14 baseline, with a working target of at least 10/12 unaided setups and no repeated blocker among more than two teachers. Quote requests are a secondary signal, not the setup measure.
- **Reconsider the goal on August 5** if setup becomes reliable but schools show no continuing interest, or if evidence identifies a different dominant barrier. Do not reconsider it merely because a competitor announces something.

Success also requires operating health: approvals return near the four-working-hour norm, scheduled teacher calls and critiques occur, no print decision is reversed without new evidence, weekly hours stay within the ceiling, and morale stops declining. If those indicators worsen for two weekly checks, reduce concurrent work further; extending hours is not an allowed remedy.
