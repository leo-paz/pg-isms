# Operating decision — July 15 to August 5, 2026

**One company-changing priority:** make a new teacher’s first Mosaic Minute session reliably self-starting. For these three weeks, the company’s central result is that teachers can set up the current core deck without staff help and show enough intent to request a quote.

**One current tactic:** run a rapid, observed quick-start-insert test through the 12 already-booked new-teacher sessions, changing only the instruction sequence between test versions and keeping the core deck unchanged.

## What we know, what it means, and what we do not know

**Facts:** Five of 14 new teachers set up unaided; nine stalled, and seven of those nine independently identified the quick-start order as unclear. Four of the five unaided teachers requested quotes. No revised insert has been tested, and test inserts can be printed in two business days. Twelve sessions are booked for July 22, 24, and 28. Six approvals have recently taken two to three days, versus a prior norm of four working hours, after Lena’s workload increased. Morale is 3/10; teacher calls, print decisions, and peer critiques have become less reliable while Lena has worked 67-hour weeks.

**Interpretation:** First-session setup is the strongest evidenced constraint in front of us. Removing it could expose more teachers to the same quote intent seen among successful starters. That is a promising causal hypothesis, not a forecast: the sample is small, quote requests are not orders, and the revised insert may not solve the problem. The approval slowdown and morale decline appear temporally associated with overload and lost operating rhythm, but the facts do not prove a single cause.

**Unknowns:** We do not yet know the setup rate with a revised insert, whether the seven comments represent all causes of stalling, whether quote intent persists in a larger group, or whether quotes become purchases. We also lack evidence that the expo, an AI-literacy deck, or a catalog redesign is the best use of cash and time.

These are explicit “not now” decisions, freeing the company to work on the single tactic.

## Decision gates

By August 5:

- **Continue this tactic** if at least 9 of the 12 booked teachers set up unaided and the remaining stalls identify a clear, addressable instruction issue; print the best-performing insert for the next limited batch while continuing measurement.
- **Change tactics, keep the goal** if fewer than 9 set up unaided but observations still locate the blockage in onboarding; test a different onboarding mechanism rather than endlessly polishing the same insert.
- **Reconsider the goal** if setup succeeds but teachers do not proceed toward quotes or describe a more consequential barrier, or if direct conversations produce repeated evidence of a different urgent need. Choose the next goal from that evidence, not competitor activity.
- **Stop the insert test immediately** if a version causes materially more confusion than the current sequence, produces misleading classroom instructions, or cannot be run consistently; revert to the prior version and diagnose before another session.

Separately, if approval times, missed calls, reversals, critique completion, Lena’s workload, and morale show no improvement by July 31, reduce concurrent work again and revisit operating roles/capacity. Do not answer weak execution by adding hours or reviving the three deferred initiatives.
