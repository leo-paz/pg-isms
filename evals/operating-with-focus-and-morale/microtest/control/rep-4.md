# Operating decision — July 15 to August 5, 2026

## One priority and one tactic

**Company-changing priority:** Make a new teacher's first core-deck session work without help, so that genuine purchase interest is not lost at setup.

**Current tactic:** Run one controlled test of a revised quick-start sequence in the 12 already-booked new-teacher sessions. Do not change the core deck or add another product initiative during these three weeks.

This is a focus decision, not a claim that instructions are certainly the whole problem.

## Evidence classification

**Facts:** Only 5 of 14 new teachers completed setup without help; 9 stalled at the instruction sequence, and 7 of those independently called the quick-start order unclear. Four of the 5 successful teachers requested a school quote. No revised insert has been tested. A test insert can be made in two business days, and 12 sessions are booked for July 22, 24, and 28. Six recent approvals took two to three days after Lena's workload rose, versus a prior norm of four working hours. Morale is 3/10, two teacher calls and two critiques were skipped, two print decisions were reversed, and Lena has worked 67-hour weeks with no full day off.

**Interpretations:** The instruction sequence is the strongest evidenced near-term bottleneck. Founder overload is a plausible contributor to slow approvals, reversals, lost user contact, and morale, but the facts do not establish it as the sole cause. The expo lead forecast, the need for a three-week AI response, and the need for a catalog redesign are assertions without supporting demand evidence here. Visible hours are not evidence of useful progress.

**Unknowns:** Whether a revised insert will cause better setup; whether 26 total observed teachers are representative; whether quote requests become orders; the eventual return of the expo; whether buyers want an AI deck; whether bookstores need anything beyond the current price sheet; and which factors besides workload drove the morale decline.

Lena makes the product-content decision within the normal four-working-hour window. Eli sends the approved file for the two-business-day test run. Freeze that test version once printed; no mid-test print reversal.

Lena attends at least one session on each date as a silent observer and joins the short teacher debrief. She does not coach the revised group. Preserve all scheduled teacher calls.

**July 29–31:** Mina and Lena review individual-session notes and the current-versus-revised results before discussing impressions. Report counts and denominators, not percentages alone, because the sample is small. Separate observed behavior from teacher comments and team inference.

**August 3–5:** Make and log the next decision using the gates below. Share the evidence and decision with the team, then restore or revise the next three-week plan.

## Precommitted decision gates

- **Continue the tactic:** If the revised insert has a higher unassisted-setup count than the concurrent current-insert group, with no repeated new confusion, use it for the next small print run and verify again in subsequent sessions.
- **Change tactics but keep the priority:** If the revised insert is no better, or the same sequence stall persists, retire that version and use the observed failure points to test a materially different onboarding aid. Do not respond by adding features to the deck.
- **Reconsider the priority:** If teachers can set up successfully but show no quote/follow-up interest and their debriefs consistently identify weak core value rather than onboarding friction, investigate product value and buyer demand before further instruction work. Quote requests remain a leading signal, not proof of sales.
- **Stop early:** Stop using the revised insert immediately if it contains a material error or repeatedly prevents a session from proceeding. Otherwise finish the small test; do not stop because of one unfavorable observation.
- **Reconsider the frozen initiatives only with new evidence:** Reopen the expo only with credible event economics before any future refundable decision; the AI deck only after repeated teacher or buyer demand; and the catalog redesign only if bookstores cannot progress with the current price sheet. A competitor announcement or internal enthusiasm alone is insufficient.

Success on August 5 means a completed, interpretable test; restored decision speed and user contact; no continuity miss; Lena operating within the workload boundary; and morale measured rather than assumed. It does not mean the unknowns have disappeared.
