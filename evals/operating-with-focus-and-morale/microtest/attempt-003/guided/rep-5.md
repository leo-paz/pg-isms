# Decision memo — May 11, 2026

**Decision and route.** Through June 15, CanopyTrace should make one company priority: produce decision-grade evidence for the single tactic that will enter a representative 90-day field cycle, without missing a contracted response window or extending current hours. The CEO is accountable; the CTO owns technical evidence, the product lead owns grower evidence, and the existing field-support owner owns continuity. This is an `operating-with-focus-and-morale` constraint: 22 months of runway, no live financing or buyer process, written decision rights, and no open safety or continuity incident rule out the higher-precedence routes on current facts. Certified systems control irrigation and alarms, but alert delay and certification remain material product questions.

**What is true.** Observed: 412 sensors had a day-57 median first change and generated 94 visits across two cycles; service records show neither crop loss nor missed response windows. No approach has a comparable representative 90-day field result. The cell lasted 108 lab days but adds $18, has a quoted 12-week lead, and has unscoped certification. Sleep mode improved a bench projection 36%, while 18 field days show median alert delivery worsening from 20 to 95 seconds. Solar reached 72 days in two sunny sites, but four of ten shaded units crossed low battery before day 50. There were 21 engineering reassignments, four engineers averaged 49 hours, weekends were unplanned, and pulse fell from 7.3 to 5.0.

Claimed, not proven: the cell is “the answer”; absence of crop loss means visits do not matter; maximum parallel work accelerates learning. Unknown are visit labor and renewal/expansion effects, acceptable alert latency, cell field performance and economics, certification scope, supplier reliability, and whether the pulse decline is caused by switching, workload, or something else.

**Priority and evidence loop.** Make the higher-density cell the leading *test tactic*, not the product answer: it is the only current result above 90 days, while its field validity and feasibility remain unknown. By May 15, the CTO will freeze a common protocol—same sensor revision, reporting interval, environments, discharge metric, and failure definitions—and state which existing results cannot be compared. Start a representative cell field cohort if hardware exists; otherwise document availability and the earliest honest start. Preserve scheduled readings from existing sleep and solar trials, but assign no new engineering to them before June 15.

By May 22, the product lead will audit actual company labor/cost for the 94 visits and interview a documented cross-section of current growers about labor, planning impact, and acceptable latency. The economic gate is evidence-supported avoided service and grower value at least equal to the incremental deployed cost; opinions alone do not pass it. The technical gate is a preregistered discharge trajectory consistent with 90 days, no degradation in contracted service, and a scoped certification path. A rival explanation is that lab conditions, reporting differences, or site sunlight—not the tactic—caused the apparent gains. Incomparability or failure of any gate blocks commitment.

**Stop/defer now.** Effective May 11, the CEO defers the enclosure refresh, mobile analytics, small-farm kit, and reseller packaging until the June 15 decision. Cancel ad hoc engineer reassignment. Do not pool mismatched trials, order production volume, certify, broadly deploy, promise 90 days, or infer customer indifference from no crop loss. Time-box supplier and certification discovery; it supports the one decision rather than becoming another program.

**Protected execution.** Each engineer gets four recurring three-hour maker blocks weekly; routine meetings and trial checks fit outside them. Cap scheduled work at 40 hours weekly, with no planned weekends. By May 15, the CEO assigns and trains a field backup, publishes a visit rotation and handoff log, and gives the support owner authority to interrupt maker time for contracted response or an incident. Any after-hours exception is recovered within the same week and reviewed next business day. Maintain two grower-contact blocks weekly. Check hours, weekend calls, response windows, reassignments, errors, and pulse every Friday. A missed window, sustained health symptom, or repeated error triggers immediate workload reduction and qualified escalation.

**Morale, cadence, and June 15 branches.** Treat the pulse decline as a signal, not a verdict. The five-week test is whether stable ownership, visible evidence, backup coverage, and recovery improve pulse and eliminate unplanned weekends while service holds. The goal premise is that eliminating battery visits through a full crop cycle creates worthwhile value; the cell is only the current tactic.

On May 22 and June 5, owners record observed results, confounders, and safeguard status. On June 15, leadership uses existing decision rights to: `continue` with the cell into one representative 90-day cycle only if all gates and safeguards hold; `adapt` to exactly one bounded sleep-mode or site-specific solar test if the premise remains plausible and that tactic has stronger comparable evidence; `reopen-goal` only after at least two discriminating representative tactic tests undermine the 90-day premise or customer/economic evidence changes the objective; or `stop` on a certification, safety, service, or economic hard boundary. Preserve the other approaches as documented options, not active work.

June 15 cannot establish 90-day field success or renewal causality. Broad rollout must wait for the representative 90-day cycle; renewal/expansion conclusions must wait for the relevant customer decision window. Revalidate certification, supplier, cost, workload, and customer facts with qualified owners. Evidence classes, routing, branch names, and this record structure are cross-source synthesis, not a claimed recipe from any named essay.
