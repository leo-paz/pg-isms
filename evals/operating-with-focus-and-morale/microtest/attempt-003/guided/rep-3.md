# Decision memo — CanopyTrace

**Date:** May 11, 2026  
**Decision:** Concentrate the next five weeks on launching one decision-quality path to a representative 90-day, no-visit deployment.  
**Owner:** CTO; CEO owns tradeoffs and capacity. **Checks:** May 18, May 29, June 12. **Board decision:** June 15.

## State and evidence

This is an `operating-with-focus-and-morale` state: runway is 22 months; there is no live financing, sale, authority, safety, or continuity crisis. Existing grower service, contracted response windows, payroll, and legal/fiduciary duties remain protected. Sensors provide planning data, but slower alerts still require customer and contract verification.

**Observed:** 412 sensors reached median first change on day 57 and generated 94 visits over two cycles; no crop was lost or response window missed. The higher-density cell lasted 108 lab days but adds $18, has a 12-week lead, and unscoped certification. Firmware has a 36% simulated improvement and only 18 field days, with median alert delivery worsening from 20 to 95 seconds. Solar performance varies sharply by light. Work has fragmented across seven efforts, with 21 reassignments; four engineers averaged 49 hours, weekend checks occurred, one specialist covers all visits, and pulse fell to 5.0 while support held steady.

**Claimed, not proved:** the cell is “the answer”; growers do not care; maximal parallelism accelerates learning. Absence of crop loss does not establish visit burden, retention, or willingness to pay.

**Unknowns:** The product lead will interview at least 10 of the 19 growers by June 5 and record visit labor, disruption, renewal/expansion effect, and acceptable alert latency. Four or more reporting material renewal, expansion, or labor impact makes no-visit performance a validated commercial priority; fewer than four triggers a goal review, not automatic abandonment. By May 18, the CTO will put all candidates on one sensor revision, reporting interval, environment segmentation, and measurement plan. By June 8, the CTO will obtain written cell certification scope, supply timing, and unit-economics impact. Leadership must set acceptable margin and certification bounds by May 18, before seeing results.

## Priority and exclusions

The sole outcome is: **by June 15, start a representative field validation capable of determining whether one deployment can reach day 90 without a battery visit, while maintaining existing service.** The cell is the lead hypothesis because it alone has exceeded 90 days anywhere, but that is not field proof. Its mechanism is greater capacity; rivals are environmental load, revision/reporting differences, and operational behavior. The falsifier is failure to meet predeclared certification, supply, economics, or comparable-test gates.

Effective May 11: defer the enclosure refresh, mobile analytics screen, small-farm kit, reseller packaging, and new media/conference work. Bound firmware to measurement and alert-latency characterization only; make no production rollout. Defer new solar engineering; existing units may log passively without weekend work. The CEO cancels further reassignment except for an incident or contracted service need.

## Protected execution and morale

The CTO schedules four contiguous engineering blocks weekly and one weekly grower-evidence review. Meetings are limited to one 30-minute operating review plus essential service coordination; notifications are off during maker blocks. The field-support owner continues service, and by May 15 the CEO names and trains a backup, with a written handoff and escalation to the CEO inside existing contractual windows.

Cap scheduled work at 40 hours per person, with no planned weekends. Any trial needing after-hours observation must use automation or a rotated, compensated window that replaces weekday hours. The current exceptional pattern ends May 15. Missed sleep, errors, reversals, health concerns, or coverage failure triggers immediate recovery and backup—not more hours. Morale is treated as a signal of fragmentation and unsustainable coverage; restore visible progress, ownership, user contact, and recovery rather than demanding enthusiasm.

## June 15 decision and later evidence

**Continue** the cell path if standardized evidence remains consistent with 90 days and certification, supply, economics, service, and workload gates hold. **Adapt** to a bounded firmware test if the cell misses a gate but customer evidence supports the goal and alert latency is acceptable. **Reopen the goal** if customer evidence shows little material burden or two discriminating, representative approaches later miss. **Stop** an approach for a safety/legal boundary, customer harm, or a predeclared feasibility failure.

June 15 cannot honestly certify 90-day field endurance: no representative trial can mature by then. The board should approve one lead validation, its bounded fallback, staffing envelope, and later decision date based on the trial start. Preserve the deferred roadmap and passive solar data as options. Final product choice must wait for a complete representative 90-day field cycle plus measured renewal, expansion, and grower-labor evidence.
