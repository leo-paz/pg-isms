# Decision memo — May 11, 2026

**Decision and route.** Through June 15, CanopyTrace’s single priority is to create trustworthy, comparable evidence for one path to a representative 90-day deployment with no battery-change visit, while preserving grower service and normalizing workload. CEO owns the operating decision; CTO owns technical evidence; product lead owns grower evidence. This is a focus-and-morale constraint, not a runway, financing, acquisition, authority, safety, or continuity crisis: runway is 22 months, decision rights and field ownership exist, and no incident is open. Checkpoints are May 15, May 22, May 29, June 5, and June 12; final review is June 15.

**Evidence ledger.** Observed: 412 sensors had median first change on day 57 and generated 94 visits across two cycles; service lost no crop and missed no contracted response window. Firmware improved projected life 36% on a 30-device simulation, but 18 field days coincided with median alert delivery slowing from 20 to 95 seconds. The cell lasted 108 lab days, costs $18 more, has a quoted 12-week lead time, and unscoped certification. Solar reached 72 days in two sunny houses, while four of ten shaded units went low before day 50. Trials are not comparable. There were 21 engineer reassignments, four engineers averaged 49 hours, weekend checks occurred, and pulse fell from 7.3 to 5.0; support response remained stable.

Claimed, not proven: the cell is “the answer”; growers do not care; maximum parallelism learns fastest. Unknowns are renewal, expansion, and labor effects (product lead: interview and service-record review by May 22); acceptable alert latency by grower workflow (product lead, same date); comparable performance by environment and revision (CTO: common protocol and dataset, initial read May 29); cell certification scope and landed economics (CTO, June 5); and feasible field coverage (CEO, May 15). Each finding changes candidate selection, pass criteria, or staffing—not the evidence classification.

**Priority and test.** The baseline is no candidate completing a representative comparable 90-day field cycle; the ultimate target is the stated full-cycle outcome. By May 15, the CTO will freeze a common sensor revision, reporting interval, environments, cohort definition, alert-latency guardrail, and precommitted pass/fail rule. Until June 15, all three candidates may appear only inside that one controlled comparison; no candidate enters productization. The proposed mechanism is longer effective battery life; rivals include environment, revision, reporting frequency, and avoidable visit practice. Short-run readings can select the next tactic, but only a completed representative 90-day field cycle can validate the objective.

**Stop/defer now.** Effective May 12, the CEO defers the cosmetic enclosure refresh, mobile analytics screen, small-farm kit, and reseller packaging through June 15. Cancel unplanned trial switching and weekend checks. Bound internal status to one 30-minute weekly review. Do not announce a winner, order production cells, begin certification, or generalize sunny-house solar results. Essential support, contracted response, payroll, legal/fiduciary work, and incident handling continue.

**Protected execution and morale.** The CEO removes assignments before protecting four recurring contiguous engineering blocks per week; hours may not exceed 40 per person without a genuine incident and explicit time-off recovery. No routine nights or weekends. By May 15, appoint and train a backup for the field specialist, with a shared visit log, escalation contact, and contracted response window; the specialist remains continuity owner. Direct grower contact occurs twice weekly under the product lead. Any service or alert-latency breach overrides experiment work. Pulse, hours, reassignments, support response, visit load, and decision waits are reviewed weekly. The morale hypothesis is fragmented ownership plus unsustainable coverage, not lack of commitment; test it through fewer handoffs, visible evidence, backup coverage, and recovery.

**June 15 decision.** Continue only if safeguards hold and the controlled comparison yields a credible leading candidate. Adapt one variable—candidate, segment, configuration, or protocol—if the goal remains plausible but the threshold misses. Reopen the 90-day goal only after two discriminating, representative candidate tests fail or grower-value evidence shows the outcome is inferior to another need. Stop a tactic immediately for unacceptable service/latency, certification, supply, cost, or workload bounds. On June 15 leadership should select one candidate (or explicitly declare evidence insufficient), preserve the runner-up, and fund one representative 90-day field cycle with a dated end and coverage plan. It must wait for that completed cycle—and renewal, expansion, labor, latency, and visit evidence—before claiming product success.

**Lineage and caveats.** The operating structure and combined thresholds are synthesis, not a claimed external recipe. Technical, certification, employment, contractual, and customer-impact facts require current qualified validation; no unavailable source citation is invented.
