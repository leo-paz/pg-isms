# CanopyTrace focus and morale decision memo

**Date:** May 11, 2026  
**Decision:** How to reach a credible 90-day deployment while restoring sustainable execution.  
**Primary route:** `operating-with-focus-and-morale`; no survival, financing, acquisition, organization-authority, safety, or continuity trigger is reported.  
**Owner:** CTO for the evidence program; CEO for scope and workload. Checks: May 18, May 29, and June 8; board decision: June 15. Final product conclusion cannot occur before a representative standardized field trial completes.

## Evidence ledger

**Observed:** Nineteen growers pay for the product. Across 412 sensors and two completed cycles, median first change was day 57 and 94 visits occurred, with no crop loss or missed contracted response. The three trials are not comparable: revisions and reporting intervals differ. Sleep mode has a 36% simulated improvement and 18 field days, with median alert delivery worsening from 20 to 95 seconds. The dense cell lasted 108 laboratory days but adds $18, has a 12-week lead time, and unscoped certification. Solar performed differently in sunny and shaded placements. There were 21 engineering reassignments in eight weeks; four engineers averaged 49 hours, team pulse fell to 5.0, and ordinary support remained stable.

**Claimed, not proved:** the cell is the answer; growers do not care about visits; maximum parallelism learns fastest.

**Unknown:** Product lead owns five grower interviews plus visit/labor records by May 29; determine whether visits affect labor, trust, renewal, or expansion, without treating absence of crop loss as indifference. CTO owns a version-controlled comparison protocol by May 18 and comparable bench/field interim evidence by June 8. Operations owns cell certification scope, supply risk, and unit-economics impact by June 8. CEO owns a workload and pulse check weekly. These results govern candidate selection, scope, or reopening the objective.

## One priority

Establish one credible path to a full 90-day, no-battery-change deployment: baseline median day 57 and 94 visits; target a representative standardized field cohort completing 90 days without a battery-change visit, while contracted service holds and planning-data timeliness remains acceptable to affected growers. The mechanism is lower energy use or additional energy capacity; rivals include reporting configuration, environment, revision differences, and placement. June 15 is an honest candidate-selection checkpoint, not proof of the 90-day outcome. A representative trial missing the target, breaching an agreed grower-use threshold, or creating unacceptable certification, supply, or economic constraints falsifies that tactic.

## Stop and defer now

CEO defers the enclosure refresh, mobile analytics screen, small-farm kit, and reseller packaging through June 15. Cancel new variants and engineer switching. Preserve existing trial data by freezing revisions and measurement definitions; do not expand three parallel programs. Bound each approach to comparable gate evidence, with no claim of victory from simulation, laboratory duration, or a favorable segment alone.

## Protected execution and morale

CTO schedules recurring contiguous engineering blocks and limits meetings to the evidence program and essential operations. Product lead schedules weekly direct grower contact. Field support continues under the named specialist, but the CEO must appoint and train a backup by May 18, document handoff and escalation, and rotate any necessary after-hours coverage. Cap the four engineers at 40 scheduled hours weekly through June 15; no planned weekends. Incidents and contracted grower response override focus blocks, with compensating recovery and CEO review. No work is added without removing equal capacity.

Treat the pulse decline and weekend objection as operating signals, not proof the goal is wrong. Weekly private checks track pulse, reassignments, support response, errors, and recovery; hours are a safeguard, never a seriousness score. Visible progress, grower evidence, stable ownership, and a finite June 15 checkpoint are the morale intervention.

## June 15 decision and later branches

Leadership should select one primary candidate for a representative 90-day field trial only if standardized evidence, grower-use requirements, and feasibility gates discriminate it. Preserve the runner-up's records and supplier option without active parallel development.

- **Continue:** one candidate passes the gates; launch the representative trial with unchanged measurement and support safeguards.
- **Adapt:** no universal candidate passes but a bounded segment or configuration remains plausible; change one material variable and set a new dated test.
- **Reopen-goal:** two discriminating representative tests across plausible tactics or segments miss 90 days, or grower evidence shows visit reduction lacks value relative to cost and opportunity cost.
- **Stop:** a tactic crosses safety, legal, certification, continuity, or precommitted economic bounds; stop the objective only if its premise is invalidated or a superior preserved option meets the agreed rule.

The board must wait for a completed representative 90-day field cycle before declaring success. Current facts about certification, customer value, economics, and acceptable alert latency require revalidation by qualified owners.
