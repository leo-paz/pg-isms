# CanopyTrace focus and morale decision memo

**Dated:** May 11, 2026  
**Decision:** concentrate the company on proving a credible, supportable path to a full-cycle deployment.  
**Horizon:** operating reset through June 15; final evidence requires a representative 90-day field cycle.  
**Route:** `operating-with-focus-and-morale` is primary. Runway, financing, acquisition, authority, safety, and continuity do not currently route this elsewhere. The CEO owns the reset; the CTO owns technical evidence; the product lead owns grower evidence. Checkpoints are May 18, May 25, June 1, June 8, and June 15.

## Decision now

Choose one outcome: a representative deployment reaches day 90 without a battery-change visit and without materially degrading the planning-data service growers rely on. The baseline is median day 57 and 94 grower-requested visits across 412 sensors in two completed cycles; the target is day 90 in a comparable field cohort. The proposed mechanism is better energy performance. Rivals include reporting settings, hardware revision, sunlight, and operating conditions. No present result establishes a winner.

Through June 15, create one comparable test protocol: same sensor revision, reporting interval, low-battery definition, greenhouse-condition strata, and measures for visit frequency and alert latency. Continue only the minimum bounded work on the three approaches needed to compare them. Do not represent June 15 as the technical finish line.

## Evidence ledger

**Observed:** the deployment and service baseline; 36% simulated sleep-mode improvement with 18 field days and median alerts slowing from 20 to 95 seconds; 108 lab days for the cell, plus $18 quoted cost and 12-week quoted lead time; solar's sunny and shaded results; 21 reassignments; four engineers at 49 hours; pulse decline; ordinary support performance; and no recorded crop loss or missed contracted response window.

**Claimed:** the cell is proved, visits do not matter, and maximum parallelism speeds learning.

**Unknown:** representativeness and 90-day field performance of every approach; acceptable planning-data latency; certification scope; supplier reliability; grower labor and renewal effects; and whether switching caused delay or low morale. By May 18, the CTO must publish the common protocol and comparability gaps. By May 25, the CTO must deliver cell certification scope and landed-cost/lead-time bounds; material infeasibility removes it from primary contention. By June 8, the product lead must document conversations with all reachable growers and service records; any stated renewal/expansion risk or repeated material labor burden preserves commercial urgency, while its absence reopens relative priority but does not prove indifference.

## Stop and protect

Defer the enclosure refresh, mobile analytics screen, small-farm kit, and reseller packaging effective May 11; their owners preserve notes and restart conditions. Cancel cross-project engineer reassignments. Bound internal status to one weekly evidence review. Defer optional events, media, and investor work. Preserve contracted support, field visits, incident handling, payroll, legal/fiduciary duties, and current grower communication.

The CEO must name and train field-support backup by May 13 and establish a weekday roster, escalation destination, response window, and shared handoff log. Schedule contiguous engineering blocks and product-led grower contact. Plan no weekend trial checks; automation or the roster covers them. Reduce planned work to a sustainable five-day envelope rather than stacking focus onto 49-hour weeks. Any genuine incident uses backup coverage and prompt compensatory recovery. The CEO reviews hours, unplanned calls, service misses, and pulse weekly; worsening fatigue, errors, or health symptoms triggers immediate workload reduction and qualified help where appropriate.

## Morale, cadence, and June 15 decision

The observed morale signal is the pulse fall and objection to weekends, alongside churn and sustained hours—not proof that the goal is wrong. The tactic is standardized comparison with stable ownership; the goal premise is that eliminating crop-cycle battery visits creates grower and operating value. Restore agency through fixed owners, visible weekly evidence, user contact, and a credible end to the five-week push.

On June 15, leadership should **continue** if safeguards hold and at least one approach has a credible standardized path to 90 days; select one primary and at most one backup. **Adapt** one variable if the premise remains plausible but a tactic misses its technical, latency, cost, or environmental threshold. **Reopen the goal** if grower evidence questions value or repeated representative approaches fail. **Stop** an approach for service harm, infeasible certification/supply economics, or no credible 90-day path. Preserve the current supported deployment and accumulated trial data. Do not claim success or make an irreversible rollout decision until a representative 90-day field cohort completes; the CTO owns that later dated review once the protocol fixes the cohort start date.

Material caveat: no approach is directly comparable yet, and absence of crop loss does not measure labor or buying behavior. These operating thresholds are cross-source synthesis, not a claim about any essay author's recipe; current technical, contractual, certification, workload, and health facts require appropriate revalidation.
