# Decision memo

**Date:** May 11, 2026  
**To:** CanopyTrace leadership  
**Subject:** Focus the next five weeks on a comparable battery-cycle test and sustainable grower service

## Recommendation

Treat the 90-day deployment objective as the company’s single operating priority through June 15, but do not declare a technical winner yet. The evidence is not comparable or mature enough: no approach has completed a representative 90-day field cycle, trials differ in hardware revision and reporting interval, the cell result is laboratory-only, sleep mode has only 18 field days and materially slower alerts, and solar performance varies by light conditions.

For the next five weeks, run one controlled learning program under a single owner and protocol. Standardize sensor revision, reporting interval, greenhouse-condition recording, battery metrics, alert-delivery measurement, and service logging. Use an unchanged configuration as a control. Continue only the smallest sleep-mode field cohort that can answer whether its battery improvement persists in representative conditions and whether 95-second median alert delivery is acceptable to growers. Do not broaden it until affected growers understand the change and leadership confirms it does not conflict with product or contractual expectations. The sensors are not irrigation or safety controls, which lowers immediate safety risk, but does not establish that the latency is acceptable.

In parallel, do bounded desk work on the other approaches: obtain a certification scope, landed cost implications, and credible supply timing for the higher-density cell; document the environmental conditions under which solar is likely to fail or succeed. These are decision inputs, not active engineering workstreams.

Start measuring the customer consequence of battery visits. For every visit, record grower labor or disruption, reason, timing, and any renewal or expansion concern; interview a representative set of current growers without claiming that either crop-loss absence or visit volume proves customer indifference. Ninety-four requested visits show operational burden, while zero crop loss and no missed response windows show service has contained the harm so far. Neither answers willingness to renew or pay.

## What to stop

Pause the cosmetic enclosure refresh, mobile analytics screen, small-farm kit, and reseller packaging through June 15. Do not keep three engineering approaches fully active, change trial configurations midstream, order production quantities of the new cell, begin unscoped certification, or promise a 90-day result. Do not use the 108-day lab run as proof, the lack of crop loss as proof of customer value, or five additional weeks as a substitute for a full crop-cycle test.

## Operating cadence and service protection

End engineer reassignment except for an explicit incident or a weekly leadership-approved change. Assign a stable, small trial team; everyone else supports current growers or paused-work documentation only. Cap work at existing normal hours: eliminate weekend trial checks by designing weekday check schedules, remote monitoring where already available, and clear thresholds for intervention. If the test cannot run within that capacity, reduce cohort size rather than extending hours.

The named field-support owner should publish weekly visit capacity, scheduled visits, and escalation coverage. Cross-train at least one current teammate for basic coverage during ordinary working hours, while keeping decision rights with the named owner. Customer incidents and contracted response windows take precedence over experimental checks. Review service load, trial data quality, hours, and team pulse once weekly. Leadership should treat renewed overtime or deteriorating response as a reason to narrow the trial, not as evidence of commitment.

## June 15 decisions

Leadership should decide whether the standardized evidence justifies continuing sleep mode as the lead candidate, funding certification and supply diligence for the cell, limiting solar to a defined light-exposure segment, or stopping any option. It should also confirm one product priority, staffing and coverage limits, acceptable alert latency, and the customer metric that will accompany battery life.

June 15 is a portfolio checkpoint, not necessarily a final architecture choice. A production decision should wait for a representative 90-day field cycle using comparable configurations, including battery survival distribution rather than only a median, alert performance, visit demand, environmental segmentation, failure modes, certification scope, unit economics, supply risk, and evidence of grower impact. If those data remain inconclusive, leadership should continue the narrowest useful test rather than restart all workstreams.

This plan preserves runway and service while acknowledging the principal caveat: current evidence cannot show which option will meet the objective or whether eliminating visits materially changes retention or expansion.
