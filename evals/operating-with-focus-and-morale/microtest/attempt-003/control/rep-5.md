# Decision memo

**Date:** May 11, 2026  
**To:** CanopyTrace leadership  
**Subject:** Focus the next five weeks on a credible battery decision

## Recommendation

Make the 90-day deployment objective the company's single product-development priority through June 15, but do not declare a technical winner yet. The available tests are neither representative nor comparable. Create one battery-life program with one accountable leader, common test conditions, and a June 15 decision gate. Pause active development of the enclosure refresh, mobile analytics screen, small-farm kit, and reseller packaging. Keep current grower support fully staffed.

Treat the higher-density cell as a candidate requiring immediate feasibility work, not as the answer. Its 108-day laboratory result is promising, but cost, supply timing, certification, and field performance remain unresolved. Continue collecting data from already-running sleep-mode and solar trials where this requires little intervention, but stop adding variants or bespoke checks. The purpose of the next five weeks is to make the next experiment and business decision sound, not to force a premature 90-day conclusion.

## What to do now

By May 15, define a shared protocol: one sensor revision, reporting interval, battery-life endpoint, greenhouse mix, and measures for alert latency, visits, failures, and support load. Preserve raw results from the current mismatched trials, but label them directional rather than comparable. Establish written thresholds for advancing each approach.

In parallel, scope the cell's certification path, confirm sample availability and the supplier's lead-time terms, and model the $18 device cost against current economics. For sleep mode, continue the controlled field observation and determine whether 95-second median alert delivery is acceptable to growers and consistent with product commitments before broader deployment. For solar, document performance by light conditions; the shaded-row failures make a general solution claim unsupported.

Contact the 19 paying growers with a short, consistent research script and review existing service records. Measure grower labor, disruption, renewal or expansion implications, and tolerance for cost or slower alerts. Absence of crop loss or missed response windows does not establish that visits have no commercial cost. Do not change deployed behavior merely to accelerate a trial without grower agreement and a rollback plan.

## What not to do

Do not launch or broadly retrofit any of the three approaches, promise 90-day life, place a production cell order, or start unscoped certification. Do not restart the four paused roadmap efforts before June 15. Avoid further engineer switching: exceptions should be limited to current-customer continuity or a newly identified material risk. Do not interpret planning-data status as permission to ignore latency or reliability.

## Operating through June 15

Return the four engineers averaging 49 hours to the company's normal schedule and end unplanned weekend trial checks. The work reduction comes from pausing seven competing streams, not from asking the team to compress them. Publish stable assignments for the five-week period and review progress once weekly against the common protocol.

Protect the field specialist by naming and training a suitable backup, scheduling any necessary after-hours coverage in advance, and giving equivalent time back within the same week. Trial monitoring should use automated or next-business-day review unless an existing customer commitment requires faster action. The named support owner retains authority to interrupt experimental work when current-grower service is at risk.

## June 15 decisions

Leadership should decide whether the 90-day objective remains the highest-value company objective, using the new customer evidence; which single approach, if any, merits a representative 90-day field validation; and what staffing and acceptance thresholds govern that validation. It should resume at most one paused roadmap effort only if capacity remains at normal hours.

June 15 cannot honestly establish full-cycle field durability. A production or broad-rollout decision must wait for a representative 90-day field cycle on consistent hardware and reporting. The cell additionally requires certification scope, supply confidence, and acceptable economics; sleep mode requires sufficient latency and reliability evidence; solar requires evidence across sunny and shaded conditions. If no candidate clears the preset gate, leadership should explicitly continue discovery or revise the objective rather than select the least-incomplete result.

These recommendations assume no undisclosed safety, contractual, or continuity issue; any such issue should override the experiment plan.
