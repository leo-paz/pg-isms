# Decision memo

**To:** CanopyTrace leadership  
**Date:** May 11, 2026  
**Subject:** Focus the next five weeks on a credible 90-day path and sustainable service

## Recommendation

Make one company priority through June 15: produce decision-quality evidence about the 90-day battery objective while maintaining current grower service at normal working hours. Do not declare a technical winner now. The three trials are not comparable, and none has demonstrated a representative 90-day field cycle. The 108-day cell result is promising but is a lab result with unresolved certification, cost, and supply questions. The firmware evidence is short and shows materially slower alerts. Solar performance varies sharply by greenhouse conditions.

Pause the cosmetic enclosure refresh, mobile analytics screen, small-farm kit, and reseller packaging. Preserve their work and document restart points, but assign no engineering capacity to them before June 15. Twenty-one reassignments in eight weeks are impeding learning and contributing to an unsustainable workload. A narrower portfolio is warranted by the stated objective, 94 requested visits, falling team pulse, and weekend work—not by an emergency. There is no current safety, crop-loss, response-window, runway, fundraising, or acquisition crisis.

## Work through June 15

Name one accountable technical lead and use the existing written decision rights. Establish a single test protocol using the same sensor revision, reporting interval, battery thresholds, greenhouse-condition categories, and measures. Record battery trajectory, alert latency, failures, and service effort. Existing results should remain labeled as directional rather than pooled.

Make firmware the primary active engineering experiment for these five weeks because it can generate additional field evidence without waiting for a supplier or certification work. This is not a selection for release. Use a bounded, representative field cohort and a comparison cohort, and test whether power savings can be retained while addressing the increase from 20-second to 95-second median alert delivery. Leadership must not assume that either latency is acceptable merely because certified systems separately control irrigation and safety.

Keep the other options alive with tightly limited work. Time-box the higher-density cell to obtaining a certification scope, credible unit economics, supplier timing and dependency risks, and a plan for representative field validation. Continue collecting data from already installed solar caps during ordinary hours, but add no new solar deployment until the shaded-row failure pattern is understood. Do not start three new full trials.

In parallel, conduct structured interviews with a useful cross-section of the 19 growers, including customers who did and did not request visits. Measure grower labor, disruption, perceived reliability, and any likely effect on renewal or expansion. The absence of crop loss does not establish that visits have no customer cost; the company currently has no evidence either way.

Protect capacity explicitly. Return the four engineers to normal hours, prohibit routine weekend trial checks, and plan work to that constraint. Publish a five-week staffing plan, limit work in progress, and make changes only through the accountable lead. Cross-train a backup for the field specialist and schedule coverage, visits, and trial checks during weekdays. Preserve contracted response windows and current support; if service demand exceeds the staffed plan, pause experiment work before adding hours. Review workload, service performance, experiment quality, and team pulse weekly. The field specialist should help design coverage but should not be left solely responsible for solving a management capacity problem.

## June 15 decision

Leadership should decide which single approach earns the next full, representative 90-day field validation, with a second approach retained only if the evidence supports a specific hedge and it has separately budgeted ownership. The decision should use comparable early field evidence, latency behavior, customer findings, certification scope, economics, supplier risk, environmental fit, and team/service capacity. It may also decide that the objective or test design needs revision if customer evidence changes the value of avoiding visits.

Leadership should not approve broad rollout or claim the 90-day objective is achieved on June 15. It must wait for at least one complete representative 90-day field cycle. A cell decision must additionally wait for certification feasibility and supply evidence; firmware release must wait for acceptable alert performance; solar adoption must wait for representative shaded-condition results. Any later rollout should also confirm that current-grower service and normal-hour staffing remain viable.

The main caveat is timing: five weeks can improve comparability and narrow choices, but cannot supply the missing full-cycle proof. This plan trades superficial parallel progress for a defensible decision and a team capable of executing it.
