# Decision memo — May 11, 2026

**To:** CanopyTrace leadership  
**Subject:** Operating focus through the June 15 board meeting

## Recommendation

From now through June 15, make the 90-day deployment objective the only product and engineering priority. Pause the enclosure refresh, mobile analytics screen, small-farm kit, and reseller packaging. Preserve ordinary support and all contracted response commitments. Treat these five weeks as a disciplined evidence-and-recovery period, not as a deadline to name a winning battery approach. The June 15 deliverable should be a defensible next investment decision and a sustainable operating plan.

## Why

The current deployment clearly misses the stated objective: among 412 sensors, the median first change was day 57 and growers requested 94 visits. However, the evidence cannot rank the three approaches. The trials differ in hardware revision, reporting interval, environment, duration, and laboratory versus field conditions. The cell's 108-day laboratory run does not resolve certification, supply, cost, or field performance. Sleep mode has only 18 field days and materially slower alerts. Solar results split sharply by light conditions. Likewise, no crop loss does not show that visits are commercially irrelevant; their effects on grower labor, renewal, and expansion have not been measured.

There is no open safety or continuity incident, and separate certified systems control irrigation and alarms. That permits deliberate testing. It does not justify ignoring service obligations or the emerging operating problem: frequent reassignment, four engineers at 49-hour weeks, weekend checks, a single field specialist, and a falling team pulse.

## Work through June 15

1. **Normalize the experiment.** In the first week, the CTO and product lead should establish one written comparison protocol: consistent sensor revision and reporting interval, representative operating conditions, and predefined measures for days to threshold/change, 90-day completion, alert latency, visits, and failures. Continue the existing sleep-mode field work only with a documented rollback rule and a leadership-approved latency bound; the prompt provides no acceptable bound. For solar, collect existing-trial data by sunny versus shaded placement during scheduled work, but do not expand deployment. For the cell, time-box work to certification scope, delivered-cost analysis, and supplier confirmation; do not treat another lab run as field validation.

2. **Measure customer impact without disrupting service.** Conduct structured conversations with the 19 growers or the relevant subset that can be reached during normal hours. Record who performs or accommodates changes, labor and disruption, acceptable visit frequency, latency expectations, and whether battery performance affects renewal or expansion. Keep observed facts separate from predictions. Continue promised visits and support at their existing service level.

3. **Reset the operating system.** Use the existing written decision rights to assign one accountable owner per workstream and prohibit ad hoc reassignment except for a contracted-service or genuine safety/continuity issue. Plan all work within normal weekly capacity. Cancel unplanned weekend trial checks; if a noncritical planning sensor cannot wait, pause that trial rather than extend hours. Leadership should designate and train backup field coverage during regular hours, while reserving the named field-support owner's capacity for grower commitments. Review trial progress, service load, hours, and team pulse once weekly, and remove scope whenever capacity is exceeded.

## What not to do

Do not roll out any approach fleet-wide, place a bulk cell commitment, begin unscoped certification work, or promise growers a 90-day result. Do not restart the four paused roadmap efforts before June 15. Do not use the absence of crop loss as customer validation, the 108-day lab result as proof, or simultaneous activity as a substitute for comparable learning. Do not solve schedule pressure with overtime.

## Decisions on June 15

Leadership should review the normalized early data, customer findings, cost and certification scope, service performance, workload, and pulse trend. It should then choose one primary technical path for the next representative field cycle, retain at most one explicitly bounded fallback, fund clear ownership and field capacity, and stop the rest. It should also decide whether the 90-day objective remains the highest-value company objective and whether capacity supports restarting no more than one paused roadmap effort.

Leadership must still wait for a representative completed 90-day field cycle before declaring a technical winner or fleet rollout. A cell decision must also await certification requirements, credible delivered supply, and economics; solar must prove performance across relevant light conditions; sleep mode must meet an explicit alert-latency standard. Claims about retention or expansion must wait for measured customer outcomes. Five weeks can improve the decision, but cannot supply evidence that has not yet matured.
