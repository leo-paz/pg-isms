# Common Chord operating order: July 15-August 24, 2026

## Decision now

Through August 24, Common Chord will run four choirs, protect current service, execute the contracted showcase at the already-promised scope, and concentrate discretionary effort on learning whether visible rehearsal progress improves attendance and renewal.

The August 20 fifth-choir launch is cancelled/postponed; Maya communicates this by July 16 before taking deposits or making commitments. The full rebrand and employer-membership pitch are deferred until after the renewal readout. Decline the produced livestream unless, by July 17, the sponsor funds its full external cost and supplies an experienced crew requiring no more than two staff hours total; even then, label it showcase documentation, not evidence of renewal impact. Do not add public-production complexity to the showcase.

These are focus decisions, not conclusions that the deferred ideas are bad. Demand and renewal effects for all four ideas are unknown.

## Non-negotiable service floor

- Current rehearsals, payroll, venue bills, incident coverage, the 24-hour member-response standard, and contracted August 29 showcase obligations remain staffed.
- No one works more than 48 hours in a week through August 24. Every person gets one uninterrupted day off in each seven-day period. Maya and Ellis publish coverage and days off by July 16; Maya sheds expansion/rebrand work first. If coverage cannot satisfy both limits and the service floor, cut optional showcase polish, not rest or incident coverage.
- Rowan sets a twice-daily member-inbox rotation with one named backup. Ellis assigns a second check of every song sheet before release.
- Written decision rights resume immediately. Maya does not approve routine rehearsal adjustments. Ellis decides rehearsal standards and conductor assignments; Rowan decides renewal communications; Maya retains pricing and location decisions. Unresolved cross-functional issues get a 15-minute owner meeting, not an approval queue.

## Single operating board

Maya owns capacity and contracted-showcase tracking. Ellis owns the rehearsal test and quality checks. Rowan owns member research, renewal operations, and the dashboard. Each item has one owner, due date, and red/amber/green state. The team meets Mondays for 25 minutes to choose work and Thursdays for 15 minutes to review evidence. No new initiative enters before August 24 unless it protects the service floor or resolves an acute safety, legal, or financial issue.

Dashboard, updated each Thursday:

1. Attendance by choir and rehearsal; intervention/control status.
2. One-question post-rehearsal response: “I can tell how I am improving” (1-5), response count, and median.
3. Renewed / eligible members from August 10, by prior attendance band and initial test assignment.
4. Member replies over 24 hours; song-sheet corrections; incidents.
5. Weekly hours, days off, and anonymous morale pulse.
6. Showcase obligations due/complete and committed spend.

## Dated execution

**July 15-17 — stabilize and instrument.** Ellis freezes avoidable repertoire/process changes and documents the minimum tactic: a written goal at rehearsal start and a closing recording that lets members hear progress. Recordings are shared only through the existing private member channel, with advance notice and a no-recording accommodation; they are not marketing assets. Rowan establishes the last two rehearsals as baseline for attendance and the progress question, confirms the 138-member renewal list, and tags the 52 low-attendance members. Maya removes herself from the nine waiting rehearsal adjustments; Ellis clears them by July 17. Maya produces a showcase obligations list separating contractual requirements from optional polish.

**July 18-19 — assign the test.** Ellis pairs the four choirs as closely as possible on recent attendance and size, then uses a documented coin flip within each pair: two use the progress tactic for rehearsals July 20-31; two continue normal rehearsal. More than one conductor must deliver the tactic. Conductors use the same goal/recording checklist. This is a small, imperfect field test: conductor effects, contamination, and sample size limit causal claims.

**July 20-31 — run, do not decorate.** Rowan contacts all 52 low-attendance members with the same short script: acknowledge attendance, ask the primary reason (progress, schedule, price, fatigue/other), and explain renewal dates without pressure. Log reason, intended renewal, and requested accommodation. Do not invent discounts or promise schedule changes. Ellis observes one rehearsal in each arm for fidelity and burden. Maya works only on runway/service oversight and required showcase work.

**July 24 checkpoint.** If anyone lacks a day off, exceeds the hour cap trajectory, response breaches recur, or another song-sheet error occurs, remove optional showcase work and reduce experiment administration before the next rehearsal. Keep the member-facing tactic only if it is operationally safe.

**July 31 decision.** Compare intervention versus control change from baseline in attendance and the progress score; review conductor burden and errors. Treat a repeatable improvement across both intervention choirs, with roughly an 8-percentage-point attendance advantage or a clear progress-score gain and no workload/quality harm, as a directional signal—not proof.

- If that signal exists, Ellis rolls the tactic to all four choirs August 1-24.
- If results are flat, inconsistent between conductors, or burdensome, stop mandatory recordings. Keep only any low-burden written goal that members rate useful. Use the call data to choose one bounded response to the leading reason (for example, clearer make-up options if scheduling dominates). Do not start another company-wide initiative.
- If service or rest is red, stop the test entirely and stabilize operations.

**August 1-7 — prepare renewal.** Rowan publishes renewal communications that accurately describe the current four-choir experience and August 10-24 deadline. No claims that progress tactics “drive retention.” Rowan sends personal, factual follow-ups to low-attendance members based on their logged reason. Ellis completes conductor coverage through August 24. Maya confirms only contractual showcase commitments.

**August 10 renewal opens.** Rowan reports daily counts internally and sends the same scheduled reminders to all eligible members. No mid-window pricing, brand, or fifth-location changes. Any member-risk pattern is handled within Rowan’s authority; urgent service failures supersede experiment purity.

**August 17 checkpoint.** Compare renewal to the full eligible base, by attendance band and original randomized arm. Because renewal is incomplete and the test is small, use this to target truthful reminders, not declare a winner. Recheck hours, morale, inbox latency, errors, and showcase readiness.

**August 24 close and decision record.** Rowan locks counts and reports renewal rate, attendance, progress scores, reasons from the 52-member outreach, workload, errors, and test limitations. Maya, Ellis, and Rowan record: what is known, unknown, and the next reversible test. A fifth choir, rebrand, employer plan, or expanded media production may enter planning only after this review, with a named owner, capacity budget, member-demand evidence, and a success/stop threshold. The August 29 showcase then proceeds at contractual scope.
