# Common Chord renewal operating order

**Effective:** July 15-August 24, 2026  
**Objective:** renew the four current choirs by making member progress visible while delivering existing promises safely and restoring sustainable execution.

## Decision now

Until the August 24 close, Common Chord will run one improvement effort: a controlled test of written song goals plus private closing rehearsal recordings, followed by a rules-based rollout. It will not open the fifth choir on August 20, rebrand, pitch employer memberships, or produce a livestream. Those ideas have no demand or renewal evidence and would consume the capacity needed for current members.

The August 29 showcase remains on, with its contracted venue, accompanists, and performer communications honored. Its scope is frozen at what is already promised. The sponsor may be told the livestream can be reconsidered after August 24; sponsorship does not override the freeze. Any unavoidable contract decision goes into the risk log, not into an expanded event.

## Non-negotiable operating guardrails

- Written decision rights resume immediately: Maya decides pricing/new locations; Ellis decides rehearsal standards/conductor assignments; Rowan decides renewals/member communication. Maya does not pre-clear routine rehearsal changes. Disputes go into a 15-minute Monday owner meeting; the named owner decides afterward.
- Current rehearsals, payroll, venue bills, 24-hour member replies, contracted showcase work, and incident coverage are protected first. Everything else is stopped or parked.
- From July 16, every person has at least one uninterrupted day off in each seven-day period. No one is scheduled above 45 hours weekly through August 24. If protected work does not fit, Maya removes parked work or buys bounded administrative help; the team does not silently add hours.
- Ellis assigns a named backup for each rehearsal and incident role. Rowan reviews the reply queue at 10 a.m. and 3 p.m. each workday. A second person checks every song sheet before release. Recordings require advance member notice and consent, private access only, no public reuse, and deletion by September 30 unless members expressly request retention.

## Unknowns that remain explicit

The prior 61%-to-79% result is encouraging but confounded by one conductor, time, and no control. The crossover can improve confidence but will still be small and may carry period effects. It will not prove the tactic causes renewal. Demand and economics for a fifth choir, livestream, rebrand, and employer memberships remain unmeasured. On August 24 the team records those as unknowns—not as momentum, credibility, or failure—and chooses any next test only after current-member delivery and recovery are secure.
