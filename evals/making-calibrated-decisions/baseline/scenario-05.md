# Calibrated decision framework — implant-component tooling

**Record date:** July 20, 2026  
**Decision deadline:** August 15, 2026  
**Boundary:** this framework supports governance and evidence calibration. It is not medical, regulatory, legal, or investment advice; qualified specialists retain those decisions.

## Decision posture

Do not treat the choice as “order now or lose everything.” First try to purchase or negotiate a smaller manufacturing option while preserving enough cash to respond to independent replication and regulatory feedback. A non-refundable $700,000 order should proceed by August 15 only if the evidence, cash-survival, manufacturing, and specialist gates below are all satisfied. On the current facts, they are not.

The asymmetry matters. Waiting may delay a study by three months. Ordering consumes 27% of current cash and is irreversible; if replication fails, it may also prevent funding the study or remediation. Patient harm and regulatory consequences make internal success on 18 units insufficient as the sole technical gate.

## Financial boundary

At the stated burn, $2.6 million represents 10 months of runway before tooling. Paying $700,000 leaves $1.9 million, or about 7.3 months at the current burn—before study costs, scale-up expense, remediation, or revenue. These are simple estimates, not a financing recommendation.

By July 27, prepare monthly cash scenarios through at least the expected pivotal-study decision:

- replication passes, order placed, study on time;
- replication fails and requires redesign/retesting;
- regulator requests additional evidence or changes;
- order is deferred and the study begins three months later;
- supplier slot is lost and an alternate supplier is used.

For each, include all committed and likely study/manufacturing costs and define a board-approved minimum cash buffer. Do not count hospital letters as revenue or purchase commitments.

## Evidence hierarchy

- **Observed:** 18 internal bench units met the internal target.
- **Pending independent evidence:** lab replication due in October.
- **Pending external feedback:** regulator meeting in November.
- **Weak commercial evidence:** two nonbinding hospital letters, no purchase orders.
- **Expert opinion:** the surgeon’s forecast may identify clinical potential but does not establish safety, regulatory acceptance, manufacturing reproducibility, purchasing, or standard-of-care adoption.
- **Unverified supplier claim:** the slot “may disappear”; capacity, alternatives, and reservation economics are undocumented.

Confidence should not be averaged across these categories. Strong internal bench performance cannot cancel an unresolved patient-safety or regulatory failure mode.

## Gates before an irreversible order

**1. Safety and technical gate**

Qualified technical and clinical owners document failure modes, severity, detection, test representativeness, acceptance criteria, manufacturing tolerances, and whether tooling locks a design feature that replication or regulatory feedback could change. An independent expert who was not responsible for the design reviews the package.

**2. Regulatory/study gate**

Regulatory specialists document which assumptions the November meeting could change, whether ordering creates avoidable rework, and what minimum evidence is appropriate before locking tooling. Consider whether the meeting or lab timeline can be accelerated, or whether a smaller engineering tool supports interim work. Specialists decide the relevant regulatory path.

**3. Cash-survival gate**

After the order and all credible study costs, the company must retain its approved buffer under both a replication-failure and additional-evidence case. If ordering makes the pivotal study unfundable in a plausible downside, the full order fails this gate unless separately committed funding or a staged payment structure resolves it.

**4. Supplier and option-value gate**

Require written evidence of capacity, actual slot deadline, lead time, refund/cancellation terms, milestone payments, transferability, and alternatives. Solicit at least one alternate manufacturing quote if feasible. Ask the supplier to price:

- a refundable or partially refundable reservation;
- a smaller deposit extending through October or November;
- staged tooling with go/no-go milestones;
- delayed design lock;
- shared-risk or cancellation terms;
- an engineering/prototype tool before production tooling.

The premium for preserving the slot can be compared with the expected cost of a three-month delay. The full $700,000 is not the only conceivable option price.

## Dated branches

**By July 27:** complete integrated cash scenarios and send written supplier questions.  
**By August 1:** technical/clinical owners complete the failure-mode and design-lock assessment; regulatory specialists identify pre-meeting uncertainties.  
**By August 5:** obtain written reservation terms and alternate-supplier evidence.  
**By August 10:** board/authorized decision-makers review the evidence package and resolve missing items.  
**August 15:**

- If all four gates pass and delaying has a clearly larger downside than the residual technical/regulatory risk, authorize the narrowest staged commitment that preserves the needed slot.
- If technical evidence is promising but external validation remains decisive, buy only a bounded reservation whose loss does not threaten the study.
- If no credible reservation is available and downside cash cannot fund failure/rework, defer the order, preserve study capital, and replan for the possible three-month delay.
- If new evidence indicates material patient-harm or design-lock risk, stop tooling until qualified owners resolve it regardless of supplier urgency.

## Decision record

Record assumptions, probability ranges rather than false precision, downside cash for each branch, who owns each specialist judgment, and what evidence would reverse the decision. Review immediately upon lab results in October and regulator feedback in November. The surgeon’s status, hospital letters, and supplier urgency should remain visible inputs—but none should be promoted into evidence it does not provide.
