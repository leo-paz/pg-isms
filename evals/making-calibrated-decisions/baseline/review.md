The baseline is valid red evidence. Every case has a material gap under strict, whole-criterion scoring even though the responses often provide sensible substantive advice.

Recurring failures:

- **Workflow routing is implicit rather than exact.** None of the responses names all required primary and specialist workflows, so they do not demonstrate when calibration owns the record and when a specialist owns execution.
- **Unknowns are listed without a complete accountability tuple.** Responses commonly omit one or more of owner, artifact/current source, due date, observable threshold, and decision effect for each consequential unknown.
- **Branch sets are incomplete or overlapping.** Dated gates appear often, but the required proceed/adapt/defer/reject-or-preserve alternatives are not consistently mutually exclusive and do not consistently include costs of delay, protected options, switch conditions, and exact handoffs.
- **Source predictions are not fully resolvable.** The celebrity case lacks an unambiguous 30-day outcome and explicit credibility update for each named partner; the historical-source case lacks a retirement/update rule.
- **High-harm gates remain underspecified.** The medical-device response correctly raises caution and computes cash impact, but still permits an August order without making the October independent replication and explicit qualified legal evidence mandatory.
- **Routine release calibration lacks explicit ownership and precommitment.** The tooltip plan preserves a control and resists early noise, but omits the exact specialist route, named owner, firm success threshold, and the full four-way branch handoff.

Strongest baseline behaviors were arithmetic and evidence classification, reversible testing, control preservation, and refusal to treat prestige, unsigned contracts, nonbinding letters, or hoped-for financing as established facts. The material red gap is the absence of the complete workflow, accountability, and branch discipline demanded by the rubric.
