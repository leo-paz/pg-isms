# Decision memo

**To:** ArchiveFox leadership  
**From:** Adviser  
**Date:** May 4, 2026  
**Subject:** Six-week implementation decision-rights trial

## Decision

ArchiveFox should immediately run a six-week, live but reversible delegation trial. The Head of Implementation should own routine go-live sequencing and data-mapping exceptions when the proposed action matches a previously accepted pattern and creates no new customer, product, security, legal, or commercial obligation. The CEO should retain novel or unsupported customer promises, service credits above $8,000, and exceptions that alter contract, security, data-retention, or product commitments. Existing security and contract matters should continue to go promptly to the security adviser or counsel; advice does not itself authorize a customer promise.

This addresses the demonstrated constraint without committing to a new executive layer. In the first quarter, 20 of 48 requests waited more than two business days, although 15 of those 20 were routine choices matching accepted patterns. Nine delayed a go-live, and unclear ownership produced contradictory dates in six cases. After decisions arrived, 17 of the 20 were completed without overtime or added staff. That points first to decision latency and ambiguity, not established delivery-capacity failure. The shadow exercise is supportive, not conclusive: the Head and CEO agreed in 16 of 18 cases, while the two disagreements identify useful boundaries—an above-$8,000 credit and an unsupported retention promise.

## What to defer or reject now

- **Defer hiring a COO.** ArchiveFox has no role charter or costed capacity model, and the evidence provided does not establish a COO-sized workload or a delivery-capacity shortage. A hire now could add another approver without removing the bottleneck. Reconsider only after the trial produces a defined, persistent body of work that cannot sensibly sit with current owners.
- **Reject a standing weekly approval council for this trial.** Routine exceptions need one accountable owner and a short clock, not a committee or a weekly wait. Cross-functional input may be requested when needed, but the named decision owner remains accountable.
- **Stop using the twice-daily CEO Slack queue as the operating mechanism.** It did not reduce waits and cannot solve the CEO's expected unavailability. Slack may notify people; the implementation tracker must hold the request, owner, deadline, evidence, decision, and customer-facing consequence.
- **Do not broaden delegation to novel promises during the trial.** No employee should infer permission from silence, urgency, or a missed deadline. Existing customer commitments remain in force unless an authorized decision explicitly changes them.

## Six-week operating model: May 4–June 14

### 1. Publish one page of decision rights before live delegation

The CEO and Head of Implementation should classify the known exception patterns and sign a one-page matrix by the end of May 5:

1. **Head of Implementation decides:** sequencing and mapping choices that match a documented prior pattern, stay within supported capabilities and existing commitments, and do not change price, credits, contract, security, retention, or product obligations.
2. **CEO decides:** novel or unsupported customer promises; credits above $8,000; any exception with a material commercial commitment not already covered by an approved pattern; and any case whose category is genuinely unclear.
3. **Adviser/counsel route:** new security or contract terms go directly to the existing specialist, with the CEO retaining any business approval needed after advice.

For service credits at or below $8,000, the CEO should either document an already accepted pattern that the Head may use or retain the decision. The shadow exercise alone is not enough to assume a general credit policy.

Customer Success may supply facts and communicate the authorized outcome, but it does not decide implementation exceptions under this trial. This removes the ambiguity behind the contradictory dates.

### 2. Use a visible clock and a single record

Every request enters the implementation tracker with: customer, requested decision and reason, category, proposed action, cited precedent if any, named owner, time received, decision deadline, go-live/customer-date effect, specialist input, final decision, and who will communicate it. No decision is complete until the record and customer-facing owner are clear.

The Head should triage each request within four business hours. A delegated request should be decided within one business day. A reserved request should reach the CEO or specialist immediately and have a named next-update time within one business day. Anything threatening a confirmed go-live before that clock expires is flagged at intake for same-day attention.

If a clock is missed, the tracker automatically marks the item overdue and alerts the CEO, Head of Implementation, and Customer Success owner. A missed clock does **not** authorize a new promise. Customer Success communicates the last confirmed date, what remains undecided, and the next update time; it does not issue competing dates. At the May 5 kickoff, the CEO must name a temporary acting approver for reserved business decisions during known absences or explicitly remain the blocker. Without that coverage, ArchiveFox cannot credibly promise a reliable escalation clock while the CEO is unavailable.

### 3. Audit without re-creating preapproval

The CEO should review a small sample of completed delegated decisions twice weekly, after they are made, plus every case the Head marked ambiguous. Review should test whether the cited precedent and boundary were applied correctly. Corrections update the matrix prospectively; they should not silently reverse a customer communication. Any suspected unauthorized legal, security, retention, or product promise pauses that category and goes to the appropriate specialist immediately.

Leadership should hold a 20-minute weekly operating review, not an approval council. Review the metrics, overdue cases, boundary questions, and customer continuity risks; assign one owner to each matrix change.

### 4. Measure the constraint and safety

Track weekly: request volume and category; time to triage and decision; number waiting over two business days; go-lives delayed by decision latency; contradictory dates; CEO reversals or category corrections; credits and new obligations approved; customer escalations attributable to the trial; utilization, post-decision backlog, overtime, and added staffing. Record the revenue timing affected, but distinguish delayed revenue from revenue actually lost.

## Decision after six weeks

On June 15, the CEO, Head of Implementation, and Customer Success lead should choose among three outcomes using the tracker:

- **Institutionalize and cautiously expand** if routine decision waits and owner/date contradictions clearly decline, go-lives are not being held for routine calls, workload remains manageable, and there are no unauthorized security, contract, retention, product, or material commercial promises.
- **Keep but narrow** if speed improves while errors cluster in a specific category; return that category to the CEO or specialist and retain safe delegated categories.
- **Pause and redesign** if delegation causes material customer harm, repeated boundary violations, or reversals that show the matrix is unreliable.

Only then should leadership assess a COO. The case requires a written role charter, a costed capacity model, and evidence of recurring executive work that cannot be resolved through clearer decision rights. A council should be considered only if the trial reveals frequent decisions with genuinely shared, inseparable authority—not merely poor routing.

The caveats are material: the shadow sample is small, agreement with the CEO is not proof of decision quality, one delayed request lacks a usable record, and six weeks will not expose every rare risk. The trial therefore preserves specialist escalation, keeps novel promises with the CEO, protects existing commitments, and makes expansion conditional on live evidence.
