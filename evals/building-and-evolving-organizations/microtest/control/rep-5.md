# Decision memo

**Date:** May 4, 2026  
**To:** ArchiveFox leadership  
**Subject:** Six-week delegation trial for implementation exceptions

## Decision

Starting May 5, delegate a narrow class of implementation decisions to the Head of Implementation for six weeks. The delegated class should be limited to go-live sequencing and data-mapping choices that match a documented precedent already accepted by ArchiveFox. Implementation should become the single decision owner for those cases; Customer Success should provide customer context but should not be a second, implicit approver.

The CEO should retain decisions involving service credits, new or changed contract or security terms, unsupported data-retention or other unusual customer promises, and any sequencing or mapping choice without a clear precedent. New security and contract terms should continue to go promptly to the existing security adviser or counsel, with the CEO retaining any required business decision.

This is a controlled transfer of routine authority, not broad delegation. The evidence supports it: 15 of the 20 delayed requests were routine choices matching prior patterns, the shadow exercise produced 16 agreements in 18 cases, and most work proceeded without overtime or added staff once a decision arrived. At the same time, the two shadow disagreements—an over-$8,000 credit and an unsupported retention promise—show that commercial and commitment boundaries matter and should remain outside the initial delegation.

## What not to do now

Do not hire a COO now on the evidence available. There is no role charter or costed capacity model, and the recorded bottleneck is concentrated in decision rights rather than demonstrated lack of executive capacity. Reconsidering the role later is reasonable, but anticipated growth to 25 people is not by itself a sufficient hiring case.

Do not create a weekly cross-functional approval council. It would blur the ownership that needs to become explicit and would be too slow for implementation decisions that can delay go-lives. Also stop treating the twice-daily CEO Slack queue as the remedy; it did not reduce waits in March and depends on an executive expected to be unavailable roughly ten hours each week.

Do not delegate credits, novel terms, or unusual promises during this trial, and do not change existing customer commitments merely because an exception is awaiting review.

## Six-week operating model: May 5–June 15

In the first three business days, the CEO and Head of Implementation should publish a one-page authority table. It should define the delegated categories above, name the Head of Implementation as the accountable decision maker, list the retained and specialist-routed categories, and state that ambiguity means escalation rather than inferred authority. Attach a short precedent register for accepted sequencing and mapping patterns; a request qualifies as routine only when the decision record links to one of those precedents.

Use one shared exception log as the system of record. Each entry should include the customer, request time, requested decision, category, owner, linked precedent if any, customer commitment at risk, route and time of escalation, decision and rationale, decision time, and any resulting date change. Implementation owns log completeness. Customer Success owns accurate communication of the current committed date, but may not create a new date or promise while a decision is pending. This should eliminate the assumption that the other function will decide.

Apply the following clock:

- Within four business hours, Implementation classifies and assigns every request.
- The Head of Implementation decides a qualifying routine request within one business day of receipt.
- Anything outside the boundary is routed within four business hours to the CEO, security adviser, or counsel as appropriate. The CEO should provide a decision or a dated next-update commitment within one business day.
- Before a known period of unavailability, the CEO should name an acting approver for retained business decisions or explicitly identify when decisions will resume. If neither occurs, the existing customer commitment remains in force; no one fills the gap with an unsupported promise.

Hold a 15-minute daily operational review between Implementation and Customer Success for open items and customer communications. It is a status and routing check, not an approval council. Once a week, the CEO should review completed delegated decisions after the fact for boundary adherence and record any disagreement; that review must not become a new prerequisite for action. Any suspected boundary breach, customer-impacting error, or contradictory date should be escalated immediately and reviewed within one business day. Leadership may temporarily narrow delegation after such an event while preserving a clear owner for unaffected routine cases.

Maintain customer continuity by keeping one customer-facing owner, honoring the last confirmed commitment until a change is approved, communicating delays without speculating, and recording any revised date in the shared log before it is conveyed. Existing specialist routes remain intact.

## Decision after six weeks

On June 16, leadership should review the log case by case and compare the trial with the January–March baseline. Continue the delegated authority if routine decisions are materially faster, ownership conflicts and decision-wait go-live delays have fallen, no unauthorized commercial or technical promise occurred, and there is no material customer harm attributable to a delegated decision. Also check whether the post-decision backlog, overtime, and utilization indicate that faster decisions merely moved the constraint downstream; the current 69% utilization and 1.2-day backlog are reassuring observations, not proof of future capacity.

If results are safe but mixed, keep the narrow delegation and revise the precedent register, clock, or routing language. If boundary violations or customer harm occur, narrow or suspend the affected category rather than automatically restoring CEO approval of every routine case. Expand authority only one category at a time and only after the same evidence review.

Revisit a COO hire only if leadership first produces a role charter, a costed capacity model, and evidence of a sustained executive gap that clearer decision rights cannot solve. Consider a recurring council only if the log shows a material class of genuinely cross-functional decisions that cannot be assigned to one owner.

## Caveats

The shadow exercise covered only 18 cases and did not expose customers to the decisions; it therefore tests agreement better than real-world impact. One delayed request has no usable record, and the available data do not establish the financial effect of every delay. The three-week deferral of $42,000 in ARR demonstrates that waiting can matter, but it should not be generalized into a total cost estimate. These limits argue for a measured, logged trial with hard boundaries—not for preserving the current bottleneck.
