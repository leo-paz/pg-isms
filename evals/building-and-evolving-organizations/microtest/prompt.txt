On May 4, 2026, you are advising ArchiveFox, a 16-person B2B SaaS company that manages collections workflows for 31 museums. It has $1.7 million ARR and 20 months of runway. The CEO still approves every implementation exception: go-live sequencing, data-mapping deviations, service credits, and any unusual customer promise.

From January 1 through March 31, the implementation tracker recorded 48 exception requests. Twenty waited more than two business days for a decision (median 3.4 days); nine delayed a go-live, and six produced contradictory dates because Implementation and Customer Success each thought the other would decide. Fifteen of the twenty delayed requests were routine sequencing or mapping choices matching patterns accepted before. Four involved new security or contract terms and were promptly sent to the existing security adviser or counsel; one has no usable record. Once the CEO answered, the team completed 17 of the 20 without overtime or added staff. One delay moved the start of $42,000 in annual recurring revenue by three weeks.

In an April 6–17 shadow exercise, the Head of Implementation wrote down a decision before the CEO in 18 cases. The CEO later chose the same action in 16. The two differences were a credit above $8,000 and an unsupported data-retention promise. No customer was affected by the exercise. Implementation utilization averaged 69%, and post-decision backlog stayed near 1.2 days.

The CEO says delegation could make promises spiral. The Head of Implementation says routine calls are manageable but the limits are unclear. Customer Success wants a reliable escalation clock. An investor recommends hiring a COO now and adding a weekly cross-functional approval council because the company may reach 25 people next year; there is no role charter or costed capacity model. A twice-daily CEO Slack queue tried in March did not reduce waits, and the CEO expects to remain unavailable about ten hours each week.

Write a concise decision memo, dated May 4, 2026 and no longer than 1,500 words. Recommend what ArchiveFox should do now, what it should explicitly defer or reject, how to operate for the next six weeks, and how leadership will decide what happens afterward. Preserve customer continuity, state material caveats, and do not assume facts not provided.
