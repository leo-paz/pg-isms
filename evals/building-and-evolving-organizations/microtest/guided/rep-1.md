# Decision memo: implementation exception authority

**Date:** May 4, 2026  
**To:** ArchiveFox leadership  
**Decision horizon:** May 5–June 15, 2026  
**Primary branch:** `decision-rights`  
**Decision:** For six weeks, give the Head of Implementation authority over routine, precedented sequencing and data-mapping exceptions within signed customer commitments. Retain CEO preapproval for service credits and unusual commercial promises, and retain qualified security-adviser or counsel review for security, privacy, retention, or contract changes. Do not hire a COO or form an approval council now.

## Why this is the decision

This is a current decision-queue and interface problem. From January 1–March 31, 20 of 48 exception requests waited more than two business days (median 3.4); nine delayed go-lives, and six generated contradictory dates. Fifteen of those 20 delayed requests were routine sequencing or mapping choices consistent with prior acceptances. Once the CEO answered, 17 of 20 were completed without overtime or added staff. One wait deferred the start of $42,000 ARR by three weeks. A twice-daily CEO Slack queue did not fix the waits, and the CEO expects ten unavailable hours weekly.

The April shadow exercise supports a narrow transfer: the Head of Implementation independently matched the CEO in 16 of 18 cases. The two differences identify boundaries, not a case for broad authority: a credit above $8,000 and an unsupported data-retention promise. Utilization of 69% and a post-decision backlog near 1.2 days do not currently support a capacity hire.

A rival explanation is poor intake, incomplete records, or cross-functional confusion rather than CEO approval alone; one delayed request has no usable record. The pilot therefore fixes the interface and records outcomes as well as transferring authority. The bottleneck would be falsified if complete requests still wait or go-lives still slip after eligible decisions no longer require the CEO.

## Evidence and gates

- **Observed:** The dated tracker results, shadow decisions, utilization, backlog, prior Slack-queue result, CEO availability expectation, company size, ARR, runway, and customer count supplied above. Enterprise customer continuity and the existing qualified-review paths remain protected.
- **Claimed:** The CEO's fear of spiraling promises, the Head's confidence and uncertainty about limits, Customer Success's need for a clock, and the investor's COO/council recommendation and 25-person forecast.
- **Unknown:** The full mix and quality of all 48 requests; hidden rework or customer dissatisfaction; sustainable decision load; total credits; and whether a qualified internal CEO backup exists. The Head owns a complete decision register through June 15; Customer Success records contradictory or changed customer communications; Finance records credits and revenue timing; the CEO names a backup or documents a coverage plan by May 5. These artifacts determine whether to retain, narrow, extend, or stop the transfer.

This is capital-light software, so the narrow transfer is reversible, but enterprise security, contractual commitments, and customer promises have higher error costs. No speed target overrides qualified review or the signed contract. No promise may be made while an excluded request is pending.

## Six-week operating contract

The **Head of Implementation** is accountable end to end for timely implementation exception decisions and a single go-live date. The Head may decide a request only when it:

1. concerns sequencing or data mapping;
2. stays within the signed contract and supported product behavior;
3. matches a cited, documented prior acceptance; and
4. creates no new security, privacy, retention, legal, pricing, credit, or other customer commitment.

Everything else is excluded. All service credits remain with the CEO during this test. New or changed contract terms go to counsel; security, privacy, or retention questions go to the existing security adviser and/or counsel. The CEO makes the commercial decision after required advice. The CEO also retains any unsupported or unusual promise. Customer Success owns customer communication, not exception approval, and communicates only the one date recorded by Implementation.

Every request goes into one decision register with request time, customer, requested outcome, contract/support check, precedent link, owner, due time, consultation, decision, customer message, and later outcome. The Head accepts and decides an eligible complete request by the end of the next business day. If eligibility is unclear, the Head escalates within four business hours rather than guessing. The CEO supplies a decision or a named acting decision owner by the end of the next business day; qualified review may take longer, but its owner and expected response must be recorded and no commitment is made meanwhile. The named CEO backup covers absence under the same written limits. If no qualified backup exists, the CEO records protected coverage windows on May 5 rather than silently leaving the queue.

On May 5, the Head briefs Implementation and Customer Success and publishes the one-page decision matrix and customer holding language. On May 18 and June 1, the Head, CEO, and Customer Success lead review a 20-minute exception sample and the measures below. This is retrospective audit, not an approval council; work does not wait for the meeting. Reversals are corrected through the single customer-continuity owner, the Head, with Customer Success delivering the corrected message. Security or legal evidence remains with its qualified owner.

## Measures and June 15 decision

The Head reports weekly: request counts and completeness; percent of eligible requests decided within one business day; escalation time; decision-wait go-live delays; contradictory dates; CEO reversals and rework; post-decision backlog; utilization; credits; and any unsupported, contractual, security, privacy, or retention commitment. Review the short interval cautiously because volume is unknown.

- **Keep:** Continue the current pilot scope if at least 90% of complete eligible requests are decided within one business day, there are zero contradictory dates and zero unauthorized promises or bypassed reviews, no more than one go-live is delayed solely awaiting a decision, CEO reversal/rework is no more than 10% of delegated decisions, and post-decision backlog stays at or below two business days.
- **Revise:** If speed and continuity improve but exactly one boundary, intake, or workload assumption misses without customer, legal, security, or privacy harm, change that one variable and extend once through June 29. The Head owns the revised matrix and a new numeric threshold.
- **Expand:** If all keep thresholds pass, make routine sequencing/mapping authority permanent. Then—and only then—the CEO and Finance may run a separately logged, two-week test allowing the Head to approve standard service credits up to $8,000 total per customer in any rolling 30 days, within existing contract terms; credits above that or split requests remain CEO decisions. Use the same continuity and zero-harm gates.
- **Rollback or stop:** Immediately suspend delegated authority for the affected domain upon any unsupported contract, retention, security, or privacy promise; bypass of required qualified review; material customer harm; or evidence manipulation. Preserve the register, return that domain to CEO preapproval, and route legal/security consequences to the qualified owner. Also stop if fewer than 70% of eligible requests meet the clock for two consecutive weeks or more than 20% are reversed; those results would not support this mechanism.

The CEO owns the June 15 decision, using the Head's register, Customer Success communications log, and Finance record. The preserved option is the prior CEO-approval model, restored by domain rather than through a broad reorganization.

## Explicit deferrals and hiring switch

Reject the weekly cross-functional approval council: it adds another queue without evidence that meetings solve the current mechanism. Defer a COO search: the title, role charter, full cash/runway cost, ramp, interfaces, and capacity case are unknown, and forecast headcount is not a present bottleneck. Do not treat growth to 25 people as an automatic trigger.

Reconsider added capacity only after decision rights and intake are working and four consecutive weeks show both utilization above 85% and post-decision backlog above three business days, with prioritization unable to remove the gap. At that point the CEO must first cost and freeze the actual role's outcomes, decisions, hands-on work, interfaces, span, and 30/60/90-day evidence; the evidence may support implementation capacity rather than a COO. Separately, reconsider an operating-leader role only if the register shows a sustained residual set of cross-functional executive outcomes with no accountable owner. Until either switch condition is met, preserve runway and keep the organization flat.

This memo uses the supplied decision-rights and bounded-test framework as synthesis. It relies on no external company facts or legal conclusions; current contract, security, privacy, and legal questions remain with the named qualified reviewers.
