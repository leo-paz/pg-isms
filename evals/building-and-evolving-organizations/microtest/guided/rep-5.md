# Decision memo: implementation exception authority

**Date:** May 4, 2026  
**To:** ArchiveFox leadership  
**Decision horizon:** May 4-June 15, 2026  
**Primary branch:** `decision-rights`  
**Decision:** Run a six-week, reversible delegation test for routine implementation sequencing and data-mapping exceptions. The Head of Implementation will own the implementation outcome and these decisions within written limits. Preserve CEO or qualified-reviewer approval for service credits, unsupported promises, and new security or contract terms. Do not hire a COO or create a weekly approval council now.

## Why this is the current bottleneck

The January 1-March 31 tracker shows a recurring decision queue, not a demonstrated staffing shortage: 20 of 48 requests waited more than two business days (median 3.4 days), nine delayed go-live, and six generated contradictory dates. Fifteen of those 20 delayed requests were routine sequencing or mapping choices matching accepted patterns. After the CEO decided, 17 were completed without overtime or added staff; utilization averaged 69%, and post-decision backlog remained near 1.2 days. One delay moved the start of $42,000 ARR by three weeks. A twice-daily CEO Slack queue did not reduce waits, and the CEO expects roughly ten unavailable hours weekly.

The proposed mechanism is that CEO preapproval and ambiguous Implementation/Customer Success authority create avoidable waiting. A rival is hidden delivery complexity or rework. The present utilization, post-decision backlog, and completion evidence weaken—but do not eliminate—that rival. The April 6-17 shadow exercise adds bounded support: the Head's choice matched the CEO's in 16 of 18 cases; the differences were precisely higher-risk categories, a credit above $8,000 and an unsupported retention promise. The small, consequence-free sample does not justify broader commercial or contractual authority.

## Evidence ledger and gates

- **Observed:** the dated tracker counts, latency, go-live effects, contradictory dates, decision categories, downstream completion, and the one ARR timing event; the April shadow results; 69% utilization and 1.2-day post-decision backlog; and the failed March Slack queue. These establish repeated latency and a plausible authority mechanism.
- **Claimed:** the CEO's concern that promises could spiral and forecast of ten unavailable hours per week; the Head's confidence; Customer Success's request; the investor's 25-person forecast and COO/council recommendation. These do not establish a role or committee need.
- **Unknown:** error and rework after delegated decisions, sustainable workload, customer impact beyond recorded go-live delays, the best service-credit boundary, the cause of the missing record, availability of an internal backup, and future operating needs at 25 people. The decision log owner is the Head of Implementation; Customer Success supplies date and customer-impact evidence; the CEO reviews reversals and workload; security adviser or counsel reviews their domains. These are due at the May 25 interim review and June 15 final review.

ArchiveFox is a 16-person, enterprise B2B SaaS company serving 31 museums, with reported $1.7 million ARR and 20 months of runway. No default-dead conclusion is supported by the facts provided. Routine sequencing and mapping are reversible; security, contract, retention, unusual promises, and credits have higher error cost. Existing qualified review must remain in place.

## Operating design for the next six weeks

**May 4-8: install the boundary.** The CEO and Head of Implementation will publish a one-page authority matrix using only documented, previously accepted sequencing and mapping patterns. The Head may choose among those patterns without CEO preapproval. Anything without a matching documented precedent is outside scope.

The following remain outside delegated authority: every service credit until a separate written limit is evidenced; any new security, privacy, data-retention, or contract term; unsupported product or delivery promises; and any exception that changes customer economics or creates an irreversible commitment. New security or privacy issues go to the existing security adviser; contract terms go to counsel; credits and unusual commercial promises go to the CEO. No one may treat silence as approval.

**Ownership and interfaces.** The Head of Implementation is accountable end-to-end for implementation plan, mapping, and go-live readiness. Customer Success supplies customer constraints and owns customer communications, but does not independently set or contradict implementation dates. A single dated decision log is the system of record; it records requester, customer, category, precedent, owner, timestamps, decision, affected date, consultation, reversal, rework, and customer impact. Customer Success must use its logged date in external communication.

Requests must be acknowledged within four business hours. The Head decides in-scope requests within one business day. Out-of-scope requests are routed the same business day to the named reviewer, who provides a decision or explicit next update within one business day. By May 8, the CEO must name a documented absence backup for nonlegal escalations with the same written limits; adviser and counsel coverage remains unchanged. If no authorized decision arrives, Customer Success communicates that review is pending and makes no new promise.

The Head designates a named implementation backup and conducts a 30-minute handoff on the matrix and log by May 8. This preserves continuity during absence and creates a succession artifact. The CEO audits outcomes twice weekly rather than preapproving in-scope choices. A reversal changes the relevant precedent prospectively; it does not create a second decision owner.

**Safeguards.** Preserve all customer commitments already made. Do not silently move a go-live; Customer Success gives the customer a dated update when a decision affects timing. Security, privacy, contract, and unsupported-retention questions retain human qualified review and evidence. Any disputed category stays outside delegation until written. The unrecorded prior case is not evidence for a precedent.

## Measures and June 15 decision

The Head reports weekly volume, share decided within one business day, requests over two business days, go-live delays caused solely by decision waiting, contradictory dates, reversals, rework or overtime, customer complaints, unauthorized promises, qualified-review bypasses, utilization, and post-decision backlog. The May 25 review may clarify one boundary but may not add credit, contract, retention, or security authority.

Use these precommitted outcomes, requiring at least 12 in-scope delegated cases by June 15; otherwise extend measurement at the same scope rather than infer success:

- **Keep:** at least 90% of in-scope decisions occur within one business day; none wait over two business days; zero contradictory dates or queue-caused go-live delays; no more than one CEO reversal; post-decision backlog stays at or below two business days; and there is no unauthorized promise, qualified-review bypass, material customer harm, or overtime attributable to the test. Make this narrow authority permanent.
- **Revise once:** 75-89% meet the one-day clock, or there is one interface failure or more than one nonharmful reversal, while all protection and continuity limits pass. Change only the implicated boundary or interface, document it, and run through June 29 for a final decision.
- **Expand:** only after the Keep thresholds pass, add newly documented sequencing or mapping precedents to the same matrix. Do not expand into credits, security, contract, retention, or unusual promises without a separate shadow sample, explicit limit, and qualified review.
- **Add capacity:** do not open a role unless decision latency meets target yet post-decision backlog exceeds two business days or overtime recurs for three consecutive weeks, and a costed capacity model shows the smallest role's relief exceeds cash, runway, recruiting, ramp, management, and handoff costs. That evidence would support a scoped capacity role, not automatically a COO.
- **Rollback or stop:** immediately remove the implicated category and restore CEO or qualified preapproval after any unauthorized customer promise, security/privacy/contract bypass, material customer harm, or two consequential reversals. Preserve the log, notify affected owners, and have the CEO and relevant qualified reviewer own remediation.

## Explicit deferrals and next ownership

Reject the weekly approval council: it adds another queue and split ownership without evidence that meetings solve this latency. Defer a COO search: there is no role charter, costed capacity model, or observed executive-capability gap, and future headcount is only a forecast. Also defer credit delegation; the one observed disagreement supplies a boundary, not a safe dollar policy.

On June 15, the CEO decides among the precommitted outcomes using the Head's log, with Customer Success attesting to continuity and advisers attesting to protected-domain handling. The preserved option is the current CEO/qualified-review path. The switch condition for reconsidering a COO or other role is a sustained residual capability or capacity gap after the decision-rights test, supported by a frozen outcome charter and full-cost model.

**Lineage and caveat:** The branch labels, evidence classification, safeguards, and test design are taxonomy/runtime synthesis, not a claimed historical recipe. No external source-derived mechanism or current law is asserted here. The shadow sample is small, the missing record limits completeness, and the financial figures and future staffing forecast were not independently verified; counsel and the security adviser remain the current owners of legal and security judgments.
