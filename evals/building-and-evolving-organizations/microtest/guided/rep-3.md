# Decision memo: implementation exception authority

**Date:** May 4, 2026  
**To:** ArchiveFox leadership  
**Decision horizon:** May 4-June 15, 2026  
**Primary branch:** `decision-rights`

## Decision

Run a six-week, reversible delegation test. The Head of Implementation will own decisions on routine go-live sequencing and data mapping only when the choice exactly matches a documented pattern previously accepted by ArchiveFox and changes no contract, price, service credit, security/privacy term, retention commitment, or unsupported customer promise. The CEO will retain all other exception authority for now. The Head of Implementation is accountable for a correct, on-time implementation outcome; Customer Success owns customer communication but may communicate a changed date only from the decision log.

Do not hire a COO now. Defer any executive search until a sustained capacity or capability gap remains after decision latency and interface ambiguity are addressed, and until leadership has a role charter and costed capacity model. Reject a weekly approval council: the observed problem needs one decision owner and a clock, not another multi-owner queue. Stop using the twice-daily Slack queue as the control; it did not improve waits.

The CEO owns the final June 15 decision. The Head of Implementation owns the test and record; Customer Success owns continuity updates; the existing security adviser and counsel retain independent review of their current domains. Reviews are May 18, June 1, and June 15.

## State, evidence, and diagnosis

ArchiveFox is a 16-person enterprise-software company serving 31 museums, with $1.7 million ARR and a reported 20 months of runway. These facts do not establish that survival is safe: burn, obligations, gross margin, and financing needs are unknown. Nothing here changes the survival plan. Routine implementation decisions are reversible; security, privacy, contract, credit, retention, and unusual customer promises have materially higher error costs and remain protected.

**Observed from the dated tracker and shadow exercise:** From January 1-March 31, 20 of 48 exception requests waited over two business days (3.4-day median), nine delayed a go-live, and six produced contradictory dates. Fifteen of the delayed 20 resembled previously accepted routine choices. Four new security or contract matters reached the existing specialists promptly; one request lacks a usable record. After CEO decisions, 17 of 20 completed without overtime or added staff. One delay moved the start of $42,000 ARR by three weeks. In April, the Head of Implementation independently matched 16 of 18 CEO choices; the disagreements involved a credit above $8,000 and an unsupported retention promise. Utilization averaged 69%, and post-decision backlog stayed near 1.2 days. The March Slack queue failed to reduce waits. The CEO expects about ten unavailable hours weekly.

**Claimed, not established:** the CEO's concern that promises will spiral; the Head's confidence that routine calls are manageable; Customer Success's need for a reliable clock; and the investor's forecast, COO recommendation, and council proposal.

**Decision-relevant unknowns:** hidden rework or customer dissatisfaction after delegated calls; whether the accepted-pattern list is complete; sustainable workload; the cause of the unusable record; contract-specific limits; and the cost and need for a COO. By May 6, the Head will produce the pattern list and investigate the missing record; the CEO will accept or narrow the list. Through June 15, the Head will measure rework, load, and outcomes, with at least ten business days of follow-up before a case counts as mature. Customer Success will log complaints and conflicting communications. Counsel and the security adviser will determine requirements in their domains rather than leadership inferring them. By June 15, the CEO will own a costed capacity model only if residual load appears. Fewer than ten mature eligible cases means evidence is insufficient to expand, so the pilot continues at its current boundary.

The leading mechanism is centralized routine approval compounded by ambiguous Implementation/Customer Success authority. A rival is insufficient implementation capacity or judgment. Current utilization, low post-decision backlog, completion after decisions without added staff, and 16/18 shadow agreement weigh against that rival but do not eliminate hidden rework. The smallest intervention costs audit time, documentation, CEO review, and boundary training, while preserving current staffing and reversibility. Its falsifier is unchanged waiting time after delegation or material rework, customer harm, or boundary breaches.

## Operating rules for the next six weeks

By May 6, the Head will create one versioned exception log and a whitelist of accepted routine sequencing and mapping patterns, including the prior decision cited for each. The CEO will approve the boundary once, not each listed decision. The CEO must also name a written absence backup with identical limits; the Head must name an implementation backup. No backup may waive a retained control.

Starting May 7:

| Request | Owner and clock | Safeguard/default |
|---|---|---|
| Exact-match routine sequencing or mapping | Head of Implementation decides by the end of the next business day; implementation and Customer Success are notified from the log that day | If precedent is absent or ambiguous, it is not eligible |
| New sequencing/mapping deviation, any service credit, or unusual promise | CEO; Head escalates the same business day, with decision due within two business days | No credit or new promise is made while pending; existing written terms and last confirmed customer date remain in force |
| Security, privacy, or retention issue | Existing security adviser, and counsel where contractual/legal review is implicated; internal routing the same business day | No unsupported representation or commitment; qualified clearance is written in the log |
| New contract term | Counsel, with CEO commercial approval after advice; internal routing the same business day | No term is promised or accepted while pending |

If a retained decision misses its clock, Customer Success sends the customer a factual holding update by the next business day without inventing a date or concession. The absence backup becomes the internal destination. Specialist response times are not known; the internal routing clock is not a promise that counsel or the adviser will decide within it.

Every entry records intake time, eligibility rationale, owner, consultations, decision time, customer date communicated, precedent, later rework, complaint, backlog effect, overtime, and any boundary issue. Customer Success reads the log rather than maintaining a separate date. The CEO reviews completed delegated entries weekly after the decision, without turning review back into preapproval. A suspected security, legal, privacy, or material customer-continuity issue pauses the affected category, preserves the record, and goes immediately to the qualified owner.

## June 15 decision rules

The Head supplies the evidence; the CEO selects one result and records the next owner:

- **Keep at current scope:** at least 90% of eligible requests are decided within one business day, none waits over two; there are zero decision-queue go-live delays, contradictory dates, control breaches, or pilot-attributable overtime; mature-case CEO agreement is at least 90% with no material customer harm; and average post-decision backlog is no more than 1.5 business days. The Head remains owner and monitoring continues monthly.
- **Expand the proven mechanism:** all keep thresholds hold for four consecutive weeks and at least ten mature eligible cases. The CEO may make the current whitelist permanent and authorize the Head to propose one additional named set of repeat sequencing/mapping patterns for a separately logged test. Credits, contracts, security/privacy, retention, and unusual promises do not expand.
- **Revise once:** latency improves and the evidence supports delegation, but one non-harm boundary, interface, or workload threshold misses. Change only that variable, name its owner, and run a final two-week test ending June 29.
- **Add capacity study, not an automatic hire:** delegated decisions meet their clock, yet utilization exceeds 85% and post-decision backlog exceeds two business days for four consecutive weeks because of documented execution load rather than rework or handoff delay. The CEO then commissions a costed role charter; these facts alone do not establish that the role should be a COO.
- **Rollback or stop:** any unauthorized credit or contract/security/privacy/retention promise, material customer harm caused by delegated authority, missing decision evidence, or repeated contradictory date restores preapproval for the affected category immediately. The CEO owns restoration; counsel, the adviser, or Customer Success owns consequences in its domain.

This preserves the option to hire later without paying recruiting, ramp, management, runway, handoff, and role-ambiguity costs before their benefit is evidenced. The decision method and branch labels are operating synthesis from ArchiveFox's supplied facts, not an external company analogy or legal conclusion; current specialist review remains authoritative.
