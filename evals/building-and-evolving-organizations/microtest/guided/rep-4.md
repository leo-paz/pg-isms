# Decision memo: implementation exception authority

**Date:** May 4, 2026  
**To:** ArchiveFox leadership  
**Decision horizon:** May 5–June 15, 2026; intermediate review May 25; final decision June 16  
**Primary branch:** `decision-rights`  
**Accountable owner:** Head of Implementation for implementation outcomes; CEO for the retained-risk boundary and June 16 decision

## Decision

ArchiveFox should run a six-week, reversible delegation pilot for routine implementation sequencing and data-mapping decisions that match a documented, previously accepted pattern. The Head of Implementation will decide those cases without CEO preapproval and remain accountable for the customer’s go-live outcome. The CEO should retain approval of service credits, unsupported or unusual customer promises, and exceptions that create new security, privacy, contract, data-retention, or other legal terms. Security and contract issues continue to the existing security adviser or counsel; the pilot does not weaken those reviews.

Do not hire a COO now. Defer any COO search until a persistent capability or capacity gap remains after this decision queue is corrected and leadership has a costed role charter and capacity model. Reject the weekly cross-functional approval council for this problem: the evidence points to ambiguous authority and a centralized queue, while another approval forum would add handoffs and diffuse ownership. Stop relying on the twice-daily CEO Slack queue as the remedy; it did not reduce waits in March. Preserve Slack only as notification, not as the decision system.

## State, gates, and evidence

ArchiveFox is a 16-person, capital-light B2B SaaS company serving 31 museums, with $1.7 million ARR and a reported 20 months of runway. No evidence supplied shows survival is the binding issue, so this memo makes no new cash commitment. Enterprise implementation makes customer promises, security, contracts, continuity, and switching costs material. Routine sequencing and mapping decisions are reversible enough for a bounded test; unsupported promises and new security or legal terms are not.

**Observed (dated records or exercise):** From January 1–March 31, 48 exceptions were requested; 20 waited more than two business days (3.4-day median), nine delayed go-live, and six produced contradictory dates. Fifteen delayed cases matched prior routine sequencing or mapping patterns. Four new security or contract matters were promptly routed to existing specialists; one case lacks a usable record. After CEO decisions, 17 of 20 were completed without overtime or added staff. One delay shifted the start of $42,000 ARR by three weeks; this is timing impact, not evidence that the ARR was lost. In the April 6–17 shadow exercise, the Head independently matched the CEO in 16 of 18 cases; the differences were a credit above $8,000 and an unsupported data-retention promise. No customer was exposed. Utilization averaged 69%, and post-decision backlog remained near 1.2 days. The March Slack queue did not reduce waits.

**Claimed:** The CEO believes delegation could make promises spiral; the Head believes routine calls are manageable but boundaries are unclear; Customer Success wants a reliable escalation clock. The investor’s forecast of 25 people next year and recommendation for a COO and council are advice, not demonstrated needs.

**Unknown:** The shadow sample is small and tested agreement, not customer outcomes. Sustainable decision load, hidden rework, the correctness of prior patterns, credit frequency and economics, and the cause of the unusable record are unverified. The COO’s full cost, remit, ramp, and benefit are also unknown.

The evidence owner for pilot performance is the Head of Implementation, using a shared exception register due June 15. Customer Success owns a weekly reconciliation of promised dates against that register. The CEO owns an audit of every delegated decision at the May 25 and June 16 reviews. The owner of the unusable record must be assigned by the CEO on May 5; its cause and corrective action are due May 12. Leadership will treat any absent record as a control failure, not as a favorable outcome.

## Bottleneck and operating contract

The current outcome at risk is timely, internally consistent go-live execution. The proposed mechanism is that founder preapproval of repeatable calls creates avoidable queue time, compounded by unclear ownership between Implementation and Customer Success. A rival explanation is insufficient implementation capacity or more complex cases. The 69% utilization, 1.2-day post-decision backlog, completion of 17 delayed cases without overtime, and 15 routine delayed cases make capacity less likely on current evidence, but the pilot will measure it. The bottleneck is falsified if delegated cases still wait, backlog rises materially, or rework rather than approval latency explains delays.

From May 5:

- **Head of Implementation:** owns each customer implementation through go-live and may decide sequencing or mapping exceptions only when the register links to a specific prior accepted pattern and the choice creates no new contractual, security, privacy, retention, financial-credit, or unsupported customer commitment. Decision deadline: one business day from a complete request.
- **Customer Success:** supplies the customer need and contract-relevant context, but does not decide implementation exceptions or give a date dependent on an undecided exception. It records a single provisional date and receives notice of the decision the same business day.
- **CEO:** decides all service credits and unusual promises and resolves any case outside the delegated boundary. Escalation occurs immediately; target response is one business day and hard escalation is two business days. Until approval, the default is no new promise or credit and no deviation from the existing agreement—not an invented customer commitment.
- **Security adviser or counsel:** continues to own qualified review of new security or contract terms. No deadline in this memo overrides responsible review.
- **Backup and succession:** By May 5, the Head names one existing-team absence backup, and the CEO records a backup or safe-hold rule for retained decisions. The backup uses identical limits. If either is missing, delegated authority pauses during the owner’s absence.

The shared register is the audit artifact and single source for request time, completeness, customer, domain, proposed action, linked precedent, owner, consultation, decision time, promised date, escalation, rework, customer effect, and final outcome. The Head reviews it daily; the CEO and Customer Success review it weekly. The full pilot cost—leadership review time, documentation time, rework, customer impact, and any overtime—must be logged. No headcount, title, committee, or compensation change is authorized.

## Six-week test and safeguards

Baseline is the January–March record above. Measures are delegated-decision latency; requests over two business days; contradictory owners or dates; go-live delays attributable to decision latency; unauthorized promises; rework within ten business days; customer complaints or credits attributable to a delegated choice; backlog; utilization; overtime; and completeness of records. Customer continuity is protected by one end-to-end owner, unchanged specialist review, same-day notification, a safe-hold default, weekly audit, and immediate rollback for material harm.

On June 16, leadership applies these rules:

- **Expand:** With at least 12 eligible cases, at least 90% are decided within one business day, none exceeds two; there are zero contradictory owners/dates, unauthorized promises, pilot-caused customer harm, or specialist-review bypasses; no go-live is delayed by delegated-decision latency; records are complete; backlog stays at or below two business days; and no overtime is caused by the pilot. Make routine sequencing/mapping authority permanent. The next bounded scope may be a written class of standard service credits, but only after the CEO derives and documents a cap and conditions from actual economics and observed cases; do not infer an $8,000 safe limit from one disagreement.
- **Keep:** All harm and continuity limits pass but fewer than 12 eligible cases occur. Keep the same scope—do not broaden it—until 12 cases or July 13, then apply the same rules.
- **Revise once:** The mechanism reduces median latency from 3.4 days but misses one non-harm threshold, or one boundary/interface ambiguity occurs without customer harm. Change only the implicated boundary or interface, document it, and run one final four-week test owned by the Head.
- **Rollback or stop:** Immediately restore CEO preapproval for the affected domain if there is any unauthorized security, contract, privacy, retention, or financial promise; material customer harm; specialist-review bypass; two delegated decisions causing avoidable rework or go-live delay; a repeated ownership contradiction; or backlog above three business days for two consecutive weekly reviews. Preserve the evidence and route legal/security consequences to the qualified adviser or counsel.

Do not open a COO role after the pilot merely because growth is forecast. The switch condition is a sustained residual capability or capacity gap after authority and interface causes are addressed, with the gap’s value exceeding a documented full cost. The CEO would then own a role charter covering outcomes, decisions, hands-on work, interfaces, initial span, cost, ramp, 30/60/90-day evidence, and expansion threshold.

This record’s routing, branch, evidence ledger, safeguards, and test design are organizational taxonomy synthesis. It relies on no external historical recipe or unverified current law. Any current legal, employment, security, privacy, or contract conclusion remains with the existing qualified reviewer.
