## 4. Current bottleneck

1. **Outcome at risk:** On-time campaign starts, customer continuity, and the September 30 renewal.
2. **Baseline and severity:** 8 of 18 campaigns were more than two business days late in the April 1-June 30 interval; 6 were attributed by dated reviews to the founder queue. Four customers complained and one $180,000 renewal is at risk.
3. **Current path:** The operations lead owns schedules and report handoff, but selected mission changes wait for the founder. Flight-safety and security leads independently own high-harm controls.
4. **Proposed mechanism:** Routine, bounded requests are delayed because outcome ownership and approval authority are split between the operations lead and founder.
5. **Causal rivals:** Weather caused 2 late starts; hidden rework, an incomplete boundary, or unsustainable operations-lead workload could also limit improvement. Pilot shortage is not supported by this interval.
6. **Prior attempt:** Seven inside-plan scheduling decisions were delegated from May 20 through June 15; 6 cleared the same day without a recorded safety or access-control event. The test did not cover vendors and left weather-window language ambiguous.
7. **Current versus speculative work:** Campaign-change handling is current and essential. Series A optics and the need for a COO are claimed, future concerns.
8. **Benefit of relief:** Removing six queue-caused delays could improve start reliability, reduce the founder's claimed 9-hour weekly load, and protect customer continuity. Recovery of the renewal is possible but not assumed.
9. **Full cost and risk:** Delegation adds drafting, review, audit, escalation, backup, and operations-lead workload, plus the risk of an unclear boundary. A committee would add a recurring handoff and meeting without addressing a missing decision right. A COO would add the recruiter's claimed cash and equity cost, recruiting and ramp time, management and role-interface costs, and an unverified runway effect.
10. **Falsifier and conclusion:** If correctly delegated requests clear quickly but queue-caused late starts persist, or if late starts are instead explained by weather, rework, or capacity, the proposed mechanism is incomplete. The observed queue frequency and the prior bounded result justify a reversible decision-rights test, not a layer, committee, or hire.

## 5. Selected change and deliberate non-changes

### Written authority matrix due July 22

The operations lead drafts one matrix for each change type. Every row must state the customer outcome, permitted boundary, required consultation, notification, escalation destination and response deadline, audit artifact, and backup.

Once all required reviewers sign the matrix, the operations lead may approve:

1. A same-site time-window change only when it remains inside the already approved flight plan and the written, pre-reviewed weather and operating boundary.
2. A route adjustment only when it remains inside a route envelope pre-approved by the flight-safety lead and does not require a new safety, security, customer-data, legal, vendor, or contractual exception.

For either type, the operations lead records the request, decision, applicable matrix row, time, and notifications before execution. Any ambiguity is an escalation, not implied authority. The founder must approve or reject a properly escalated non-safety request by the end of the next business day. Flight-safety and security stops take effect immediately and need no founder confirmation.

## 6. Bounded test and dated decisions

### Test design

- **Hypothesis:** Giving the operations lead bounded authority for inside-plan time-window changes and pre-approved-envelope route adjustments will remove founder-queue delay without weakening safety, security, accountability, or customer continuity.
- **Rival:** Weather, rework, unclear interfaces, or operations-lead capacity—not founder approval—will remain the limiting mechanism.
- **Affected unit:** Every contracted campaign with an eligible change request from go-live through September 1.
- **Artifacts:** Signed authority matrix; request and decision log; campaign-start and cause log; founder and operations-lead time logs; rework and complaint log; safety and access incident records; backup documentation; August 18 finance stress test.
- **Measures:** Count and percentage resolved the same business day; number unresolved after one business day; campaign starts more than two business days late by cause; founder and operations-lead hours; boundary errors; rework; complaints; schedule or report-handoff misses; safety incidents or near misses; access-control breaches; stop-authority overrides.
- **Safeguards:** Signed pre-review; least authority necessary; pre-execution logging; independent stop rights; same-day rollback; preserved records; no vendor substitution; no informal backup; no automatic scope expansion.
- **Confounders:** Weather and request mix are logged separately. Small volume is reported as counts and percentages and does not turn zero incidents into proof of safety.
- **Preserved options:** Restore founder approval immediately; revise one boundary; add the smallest justified operations capacity later; or write a COO charter only if a distinct executive gap is subsequently evidenced.

### Final decision on September 2

If the hard safeguards passed, zero campaign starts were more than two business days late because of the founder queue, at least 90% of eligible requests were resolved the same business day, the founder spent no more than 3 hours per week on these requests for at least 3 consecutive complete weeks, and no capacity predicate fired, keep the delegated rights at the tested scope. Any vendor authority or broader route/weather authority requires a separate evidenced mechanism, written boundary, and qualified review.

The unresolved September 30 renewal remains a separate customer-continuity fact after this test. The founder records the customer's response by September 2 but does not treat it as a renewal outcome. A weekly launch committee becomes eligible for consideration only if the logs show a distinct repeated cross-owner coordination failure after decision latency is removed. A COO becomes eligible for consideration only if a distinct, current executive capability or sustained residual capacity gap remains, a role charter is written, and verified full cost is justified by the outcome.
