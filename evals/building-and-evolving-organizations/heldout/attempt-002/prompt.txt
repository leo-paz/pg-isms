It is July 15, 2026. You advise Northstar Relay, a 31-person Series A company whose general-availability enterprise SaaS helps support teams reproduce software incidents. It has 42 paying customers. The June 30 finance reconciliation shows $7.8 million of unrestricted cash, average net burn of $370,000 over April-June, and no debt payment due in the next 12 months. The approved operating plan therefore has roughly 21 months of cash at that burn.

The immediate decision concerns Leah, the VP of Engineering. Engineering grew from 9 people on January 6 to 18 on June 30. Leah's direct reports grew from 7 to 14; there are no people managers. Three senior engineers lead technical work for individual squads but have neither performance-management responsibility nor a management title. Leah remains accountable for engineering delivery, reliability, hiring, and development of the team. She also joins two design-partner calls each month to hear technical product feedback.

Dated operating records show the following:

- A calendar and work-log audit for June 1-28 attributes 46% of Leah's recorded time to coding and code review, 22% to incidents and release support, 9% to one-to-ones and coaching, 5% to recruiting, and 18% to roadmap and cross-functional work. Her January role memo, written when she had seven reports, expected her to remain the principal reviewer for the hardest changes; it has not been revised.
- The Q2 one-to-one log shows 19 of 40 scheduled meetings occurred on their original date, 11 were moved at least twice, and the remainder were cancelled or had no note. On June 30, 6 of Leah's 14 reports had no current growth goal and 5 review drafts were 19-33 days late.
- Four release retrospectives dated April 8 through June 27 record cross-squad dependency conflicts discovered during integration. They added 9, 11, 12, and 16 calendar days respectively. No artifact named an owner for tracking dependencies across squads.
- The applicant system shows that 11 candidates passed the engineering screen between May 1 and June 21. The median wait to Leah's interview was 13 calendar days; 4 candidates withdrew during that wait. Nine interview invitations were rescheduled after conflicts appeared on Leah's calendar. No candidate evidence establishes whether a Director of Engineering or Staff Engineer would remove the recorded delays.
- On May 22 and June 18, customer-facing releases had to be rolled back. The incident records show that Leah was the only person with both the release permission and practiced knowledge to complete the rollback; 19 and 23 customers experienced 67 and 54 minutes of degraded service.
- Since May 6, the company has reassigned routine code reviews to the three senior engineers, removed two weekly status meetings, given interview scheduling to the recruiting coordinator, and cut active roadmap projects from seven to five. The June records above were collected after those changes.

The explanations conflict. Leah says the real gap is senior technical depth and asks to open a Staff Engineer role so she can stop rescuing hard projects. The COO says companies at this stage are supposed to add a Director of Engineering and cites an investor's advice. The CEO wonders whether an executive coach and a week off would solve a personal energy problem. In a June 24 pulse survey, 8 of 14 respondents said they often did not know which cross-squad dependency mattered most, and 5 said they wanted more useful career feedback. Those responses are attributable self-reports, not proof of cause. It is not yet known whether the senior engineers want or can sustain broader coordination work, whether Leah can change her own time allocation, how much hidden rework exists, or whether a durable full-time capacity gap remains after clearer ownership and interfaces.

The company can fund one engineering hire under the approved plan, but no requisition has to open this month. The next enterprise release is scheduled for September 18. By July 18, the CEO wants a decision: open one of the proposed roles now, make a bounded change to Leah's role first, or do something else. Recommend the concrete action for the next eight weeks and the September decision that follows. Ground it in the dated record, distinguish records from interpretations and unresolved questions, preserve a single owner for engineering outcomes and customer continuity, and state directly observable conditions for continuing, revising, adding capacity, or stopping the change.
