## 4. Current bottleneck conclusion

The outcome at risk is predictable engineering delivery and reliability without sacrificing team development, recruiting latency, or customer continuity. The supplied interval shows four cross-squad conflicts adding 9-16 days, repeated missed or moved one-to-ones, incomplete growth and review work, a 13-day candidate wait with four withdrawals, and two rollbacks for which Leah was the only practiced and permitted operator. Leah currently owns all engineering outcomes and the decision path concentrates hard review, incident support, people work, and cross-functional work on her.

The primary hypothesis is that Leah's unrevised principal-reviewer role and concentrated decision/continuity interfaces no longer fit 18 engineers and 14 reports. A rival is a genuine senior technical capability gap; other rivals are a durable people-management capacity gap, hidden rework, roadmap volatility, and personal energy. Stage, headcount, investor advice, and titles are not causal evidence.

The company already moved routine reviews, removed meetings, delegated scheduling, and cut projects. The repeated post-change June evidence justifies a bounded role change rather than `keep-flat`. It does not justify `role-or-hire` now because the role mechanism, candidate evidence, full cost, ramp, and residual capacity gap are unknown. The falsifier for the primary hypothesis is that clear review boundaries, dependency ownership, protected management time, and a qualified rollback backup are implemented as designed but Leah still must repeatedly rescue hard technical work or the recorded outcomes do not improve for a mechanism-specific reason.
