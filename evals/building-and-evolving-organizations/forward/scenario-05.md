## 4. Current bottleneck record

1. **Outcome at risk:** Clinics receive promises, go-live states, and ticket dispositions that do not reliably represent a completed customer outcome; renewal stewardship is deteriorating.
2. **Baseline and interval:** In Q2 2026, 11 custom workflows were promised without review, seven clinics were marked live with unresolved data issues, 19 closed tickets reopened within seven days, and reported renewal risk moved from 8% to 21%, a 13-percentage-point increase. The denominators, distribution among pods, overlap among clinics, event dates, and downstream harm are unknown and must be frozen by 2026-07-21. The evidence establishes a recurring quarter-level pattern, not a per-week frequency.
3. **Current owner and path:** Three pods nominally own implementation through renewal, while sales, implementation, and support receive separate local signals. Actual pod-lead names, authority limits, backup coverage, and escalation performance are unknown on 2026-07-15 and must be entered in the customer-outcome register by 2026-07-16.
4. **Proposed mechanism:** Local metrics make signed value, nominal timeliness, and closure count individually rewarding while cross-functional help and unfinished customer work reduce local performance. **Rivals:** workload/capacity, product gaps, data tooling, ambiguous definitions or decision rights, customer-driven scope change, and reporting artifacts.
5. **Prior attempts:** No completed attempt is supplied. Values training, another committee, and individual rankings are proposals, not evidence that a smaller intervention failed.
6. **Current and essential:** Selling feasible work, implementing clean clinic data, resolving support issues, and preserving renewals are current essential work.
7. **Relief and falsifier:** Relief means fewer unreviewed promises, false go-lives, and seven-day reopens without stopping legitimate selling, implementation, or support. The mechanism is falsified or materially weakened if the documented plan does not create the stated pressures, if the same failures persist after a trustworthy shared-outcome exposure, or if root-cause evidence attributes at least half of a category to a rival unaffected by incentives.
8. **Full cost:** No new headcount is authorized. Cash and runway impact are unknown until the compensation memo. Expected costs are management and analyst time, feasibility-review latency, remediation work on the 11/seven/19 events, possible short-term reduction in signing, go-live, and ticket volume, payroll/legal review, employee uncertainty, cross-pod comparison bias, new logs and handoffs, and customer communication. Risks include gaming the shared scorecard, hiding risk, avoiding difficult clinics, diffused accountability, inequitable pay effects, and loss of trust if terms change retroactively. Reversibility is high for shadow scoring, moderate for workflow rules, and lower for compensation. Continuity costs are bounded through named backups and company-wide guardrails. Succession cost is the time to document pod accounts, decisions, and open risks.

**Conclusion:** The observed failure pattern and stated local metrics justify an `incentive-or-culture` test. They do not justify training, a committee, rankings, another layer, or hiring. The smallest responsible intervention is one-pod shared-outcome testing, while the three customer-protection interfaces apply to every pod because known customer work cannot ethically serve as an untreated experiment.

### Rejected branches and alternatives

- **Mandatory values training:** Rejected. Interviewees reportedly understand customer outcomes; no evidence shows a knowledge-of-values gap. Training does not remove the metric penalty for cross-functional help.
- **Fourth approval committee:** Rejected. It would diffuse the pod's accountable owner and add latency. A two-business-day, named feasibility interface is sufficient to test the observed promise problem.
- **Individual rankings across functions:** Rejected. They would make unlike work falsely comparable, intensify local optimization, and create incentives to avoid invisible remediation or difficult clinics.
- **`role-or-hire`:** Rejected now. No sustained residual capacity or capability gap has been established after priority, interface, and incentive causes are addressed.
- **`decision-rights` or `process-or-control` as primary:** Rejected. Decision limits and minimum controls are necessary safeguards, but the main evidenced mechanism is the reward/status signal. They remain bounded, owner-specific, and expiring with the test unless independently justified.
- **`keep-flat`:** The team shape is preserved, but unchanged incentives are not acceptable because a material recurring failure pattern is already reported.

## 6. Test and decision

### Hypothesis, rival, unit, measures, and safeguards

- **Hypothesis:** When the pilot pod is evaluated on a shared clinic outcome and remediation/cross-functional help is not treated as failure against a local count, its members will make fewer unreviewed promises, false go-live declarations, and premature closures while maintaining bounded operating throughput.
- **Rivals:** Capacity, product gaps, data tooling, ambiguous definitions/authority, customer scope change, and reporting artifacts explain the failures better than incentives.
- **Affected unit:** One selected pod for incentive exposure; all three pods for customer-continuity guardrails. The other two pods provide descriptive comparison only because assignment is not random.
- **Primary weekly measures:** (1) percentage of custom promises reviewed before commitment; (2) count and percentage of go-lives with a disqualifying unresolved data condition; (3) count and rate of same-issue tickets reopened within seven days; (4) frozen-cohort renewal risk and account-level recovery steps; (5) cross-functional remediation/help hours recorded without individual ranking.
- **Balance measures:** Signed annual value and count, median feasibility-review time, total and on-time go-lives, ticket arrival/closure/backlog, time to durable resolution, workload/after-hours exceptions, and customer complaints. Final-four-week signing, go-live, and durable-resolution volume must each remain at least 80% of the pilot pod's verified Q2 weekly baseline unless documented demand was lower; if demand was lower, available demand and conversion are reported and the throughput gate is `insufficient evidence`, not a pass.
- **Artifacts:** Customer-outcome register, standard-workflow catalog, custom-promise log, go-live reconciliation checklist, ticket-reopen report, frozen renewal-risk report, weekly shared scorecard, workload record, confidential pulse/dissent record, compensation-mechanics memo, qualified-review memo, correction log, and dated decision notes.
- **Confounders:** Nonrandom pilot selection, clinic mix, renewal timing, ticket arrival, implementation cohort size, planned releases, staff leave, and remediation of the existing 11/seven/19 events. Each weekly scorecard records these rather than adjusting them away informally.
- **Safeguards:** No retroactive pay change; written lawful-plan gate; no individual rankings; no weakening of clinic-data, privacy, security, or customer-continuity review; preserved source records and metric corrections; confidential dissent; no retaliation; accessibility/accommodation review; explicit customer owner and backup; same-day independent routing of high-harm issues; prior lawful compensation plan preserved for rollback.
