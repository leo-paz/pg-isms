Case ID: scenario-05
Reviewer ID: organization-baseline-generator-001

Treat this as an incentive and operating-system failure, not primarily a culture problem. The pods are nominally accountable for renewal, but each function is rewarded for a local result that can damage the customer outcome.

Weeks 1–2:

- Pause new unreviewed custom-workflow commitments.
- Review the 11 custom promises, seven unresolved go-lives, and 19 reopened tickets. Assign an owner, customer-risk level, corrective action, and deadline to each.
- Create a renewal-risk recovery list, prioritizing clinics affected by multiple failures.
- Define shared pod outcomes: implementation quality, time to stable adoption, unresolved critical issues, ticket reopen rate, and renewal health.
- Preserve necessary functional measures, but stop using signed value, nominal go-live, or raw closure volume as sufficient indicators of success.

Weeks 3–4:

- Redesign compensation prospectively so each function has meaningful exposure to shared customer outcomes.
- For sales, include commitment quality and downstream success alongside signed value.
- For implementation, define go-live using acceptance criteria that include data integrity and resolution of critical issues.
- For support, measure durable resolution, severity-adjusted responsiveness, and reopen rates rather than closures alone.
- Establish a lightweight pre-contract review for genuinely nonstandard workflows, owned by the relevant pod with defined response deadlines. Do not add a general-purpose fourth committee.
- Give pod leads authority to trade local throughput for customer health without penalizing team members for assisting another function.

Weeks 5–6:

- Pilot the revised measures and review process in the three existing pods.
- Hold weekly customer-health reviews focused on decisions and recovery, not blame.
- Examine whether targets create new gaming risks or conflicts. Adjust definitions and thresholds before finalizing compensation.
- Coach managers to evaluate cross-functional contribution explicitly.

Weeks 7–8:

- Finalize compensation changes with clear effective dates and written examples.
- Review progress on the affected clinics and compare custom commitments, clean go-lives, reopen rates, and renewal risk with the starting baseline.
- Remove any temporary recovery process that adds delay without improving outcomes.
- Publish durable pod decision rights and escalation rules.

Do not introduce individual rankings across every function; they would reinforce local optimization and discourage cooperation. Values training may support communication later, but it should not substitute for correcting incentives, authority, and quality definitions.
