Reviewer ID: organization-baseline-generator-001

The primary decision is immediate cash preservation and solvency, not organizational design. With $900,000 in cash, $310,000 of fixed monthly obligations, and only $55,000 of dependable monthly gross profit, the company has an approximate monthly cash deficit of $255,000—about three and a half months before considering one-time costs. That does not bridge a five-month revenue delay.

For tomorrow’s board plan:

- Produce a 13-week cash forecast showing payroll, fixed obligations, gross profit, unavoidable certification costs, and payment dates.
- Identify actions that ensure payroll in nine days and extend survival beyond the earliest plausible revenue date, with additional contingency.
- Freeze hiring and discretionary spending immediately.
- Divide obligations into essential, deferrable, renegotiable, and removable categories. Contact major counterparties promptly about payment timing or revised commitments.
- Prepare specific cost-reduction scenarios with dates, cash saved, operational consequences, and decision deadlines. Protect work essential to certification and eventual revenue.
- Pursue financing and other cash sources in parallel, but do not base the survival plan on unsigned funding.
- Define weekly cash checkpoints and trigger dates for progressively stronger reductions if financing or certification milestones slip.
- Communicate quickly and factually with the team once decisions are made, including what changes, why, and when the next update will occur.

Do not add a chief of staff, business units, or a culture committee now. Reporting lines and coordination already work, and those initiatives consume cash and attention without addressing the existential constraint. Organizational work should be limited to assigning one owner for the cash plan, one owner for certification, clear spending authority, and a small crisis cadence with explicit decisions and deadlines.
