# Decision and closing plan

## Decision

Do not sign Fund Y within 48 hours and do not postpone the GMV correction. The company should disclose the corrected 31% growth figure to both funds immediately, before accepting either offer, and give each the same reconciled explanation and revised deck. Hiding the error until after signing would turn a fixable reporting mistake into a trust problem at exactly the moment the company is choosing a long-term board partner.

Subject to a rapid review of the complete documents, reference checks, and any required company approvals, the preferred counterparty should be Fund X. Y's higher valuation and possible $1 million bonus are attractive, but the company does not need rescue capital and therefore should not trade durable governance quality for speed. Both funds receive one board seat, while Y also receives an observer seat. More importantly, the reference pattern differs sharply: X is described as patient through missed quarters, while Y is associated with aggressive follow-on signaling and pressure for a fast sale. Those behaviors could affect future fundraising flexibility and the timing of an exit. If the full-document review reveals unacceptable terms from X or Y resolves the governance and conduct concerns convincingly, the company can revisit the choice before acceptance; the recommendation is not to sign either current paper unread.

## July 20: correct the record and organize the process

The CEO, controller, finance lead, and counsel should meet immediately to document the duplicated feed, establish how it occurred, verify that 31% is the correct figure, and determine whether any other metrics depended on the same feed. Preserve the original deck and calculation trail. Prepare a revised slide, a short factual explanation, and a table showing the old and corrected figures. Do not minimize the change or speculate about intent.

Send the correction to X and Y at the same time. State that the error was discovered on July 19, resulted from a duplicated data feed, and changes reported year-over-year GMV growth from 42% to 31%. Attach the revised deck, offer access to the reconciliation, and ask each fund to confirm in writing that it received the correction and whether its offer remains open on the stated terms. Pause use of the incorrect deck everywhere else and brief the board on the mistake and response.

Also on July 20, appoint one internal deal lead and open a shared issue list. Ask company counsel to compare every term in both documents, not just valuation, check size, board seats, and liquidation preference. The comparison should cover financing conditions, governance and voting rights, protective provisions, information rights, pro rata rights, option-pool treatment, founder restrictions, expense reimbursement, confidentiality, closing conditions, and any language that could function like exclusivity. Counsel should identify what approvals and signatures are required and control document versions. Tax, accounting, or personal financial questions should go to the appropriate qualified advisers.

## July 21-22: diligence the investors and normalize the economics

Management should conduct focused follow-up calls with the cited founders. For X, test what "patient support" meant in concrete decisions: bridge financing, leadership changes, down rounds, budgets, and exit timing. For Y, ask for specific examples of follow-on signaling, reserve decisions, sale pressure, board conduct, and treatment of confidential information. Request additional references selected by the company, including founders whose companies underperformed or needed more capital. Summarize facts separately from impressions and share them with the board.

Finance should produce a simple ownership comparison on a fully diluted basis using the capitalization table and the actual option-pool language. On the headline figures alone, X implies a $100 million post-money value and Y a $129 million post-money value before resolving how the bonus is documented. The team should confirm whether Y's $1 million is additional purchase price, a company payment, conditional funding, or something else, and whether it changes ownership or closing risk. No decision should count it until the written mechanics are understood.

## July 23-25: negotiate, approve, and sign one term sheet

After both funds respond to the correction, give each a clear, equal deadline for any revised paper. Tell X it is preferred if the complete terms remain acceptable, and seek enough time for an orderly definitive-document process through August 15. Ask Y to remove the observer seat and address the company's concerns about follow-on support and exit pressure through narrower governance rights and clear expectations; do not use accusations or disclose confidential reference comments. The company should not manufacture competing bids, misstate its intentions, or grant exclusivity casually.

By July 24, counsel should deliver a red-flag comparison and the deal team should recommend one path to the board. The approval memo should include corrected performance data, dilution under consistent assumptions, governance differences, reference findings, open conditions, and the downside if the chosen fund later declines to follow on. The board should have time to question management and counsel. Any conflicted director or interested person should be identified and handled through the company's normal process.

On July 25, accept and countersign only the selected term sheet after counsel confirms the final text matches the approved version and the required company approval has been obtained. If X withdraws after the correction or its documents contain material hidden disadvantages, Y is not an automatic fallback: repeat the same approval test on Y's final terms. If neither passes, let the offers expire. Profitability and the absence of rescue need make that a credible outcome.

## July 26-August 15: move from term sheet to a controlled closing

Set a closing calendar with owners for investor diligence, financial and capitalization verification, definitive financing documents, disclosure schedules, board materials, signatures, and funds flow. Hold brief daily deal-team check-ins and twice-weekly executive updates. Route all investor requests through the internal deal lead, log what is provided, and ensure corrected GMV materials replace the old deck in the data room.

Finance should reconcile the capitalization table, historical issuances, option grants, cash, debt, and key marketplace metrics. Management should prepare candid disclosures about the duplicated feed and the remediation adopted: named metric owners, source-level checks, and review before external reporting. Counsel should lead negotiation of the definitive documents and explain departures from the approved term sheet to the board. The company should not sign ancillary documents or announce the financing until the applicable review and approvals are complete.

Target final board approval and signature readiness by August 13, leaving August 14-15 for final conditions, signature collection, and confirmation of funds. Closing should occur only when the documents, approvals, updated ownership schedule, disclosures, and funds-flow instructions all agree. If an unresolved issue materially changes economics, control, investor obligations, or the accuracy of company disclosures, pause and return it to the board rather than forcing an August 15 close.
