# Financing readiness and process plan through November 30, 2026

The company is not ready to market an $18 million round on software seed benchmarks or promise pilot output by next summer. The financing question is how much capital, time, and contingency are required to convert a validated lab cell into a financed pilot milestone. The August 15 review and supplier quotes are therefore the first decision gate.

## Starting position and financing objective

At the current $610,000 monthly spend, $8.4 million represents roughly 13.8 months of cash before pilot spending or changes in burn. That is not comfortable relative to a 12-to-18-month pilot timeline. Set an internal minimum cash threshold and decision dates now, while the company still has choices.

The objective should be an evidence-backed milestone: fund construction, commissioning, and demonstrated operation of the pilot line, with corporate runway and contingency. Until August 15, use a range. The $9 million-to-$13 million pilot estimate does not establish an $18 million raise; the company must also model operating burn, payment timing, contingency, and the completion cash buffer. Build scenarios before choosing the ask.

## Readiness work through August 15

Assign owners to the data room and integrated milestone model. Prepare an investor package covering cash and burn, lab validation evidence, the pilot objective, cost and schedule ranges, the exact limitations of both automaker letters, major assumptions, evidence expected August 15, and the process calendar.

Build lower-cost/shorter, base, and higher-cost/longer cash scenarios. Show monthly cash, ordinary burn, phased pilot requirements, contingency, proceeds, and closing cash through the milestone. Stress delay and higher supplier costs. Separate facts from assumptions and identify which August 15 findings change them. Determine a credible raise range and minimum viable first close.

Rewrite the narrative. The automaker letters show evaluation interest, not demand, revenue, backlog, or bankable offtake. Describe them as nonbinding, with no deposits or purchase commitments, and do not convert them into forecast revenue. Pilot output by next summer should remain an internal scenario unless the review supports it. Show investors a dated milestone map with dependencies and ranges.

## August 15 decision gate

Within several business days of receiving the review and quotes, reconcile them into the model and hold a board-level financing decision meeting. Approve: the pilot milestone definition; cost and schedule ranges; uses of funds; contingency policy; target raise range; minimum close; acceptable syndicate composition; and the point at which the company would phase, resize, or pause the project.

If the evidence materially worsens the cost or schedule, disclose that result and revise the plan before launching broadly. Do not preserve the $18 million headline merely for consistency. If material questions remain, run a short, time-boxed clarification phase and begin only limited investor education. Readiness means that management can answer why the amount is sufficient, what achievement it buys, what happens in the downside case, and when more capital may be needed.

## Investor selection

Prioritize investors by fit with the milestone and ability to support follow-on capital, not by the highest implied valuation alone. The specialist climate fund should be the leading institutional diligence candidate because it has already engaged and is more likely to understand pre-revenue industrial risk. Test its experience with hardware scale-up, reserves for later rounds, decision speed, ownership requirements, governance expectations, and willingness to lead or syndicate.

Keep the generalist growth fund informed through brief milestone updates, but do not design the current process around it; its stated revenue requirement is incompatible with the company’s immediate need. It may become relevant after the pilot or if its mandate changes.

Treat the strategic manufacturer as a distinct track. Joint development could provide technical resources, credibility, or non-equity support, but exclusivity may restrict future customers, partners, financing options, or negotiating leverage. Management should define commercial red lines before substantive negotiation: scope, field, geography, duration, performance obligations, termination, and what value the company must receive for any restriction. Qualified advisers should review any actual terms; this plan does not recommend a legal structure. Do not give the strategic party access or timing that undermines the institutional process, and do not count speculative partnership value as funded cash.

Build a focused list of additional climate, industrial, deep-tech, and strategic-capital investors that can finance pre-revenue pilot milestones. Score each on stage fit, check size, relevant track record, follow-on capacity, process speed, strategic conflicts, governance fit, and references from portfolio founders. Seek a lead capable of validating the round, plus co-investors whose capital and incentives remain useful through commercialization.

## Process from late August to November 30

From August 18 through August 28, finalize materials, investor scoring, references, diligence responsibilities, and a controlled data-room index. Prepare consistent answers to the hardest questions: why now, why this milestone, why this amount, what the letters prove, what they do not prove, and what happens if the pilot is late or over budget.

Launch concentrated outreach around August 31. Contact the strongest candidates in a tight window so meetings and decisions remain comparable. Ask each investor early about mandate fit, check size, ability to lead, diligence requirements, decision committee dates, and realistic timetable. By mid-September, advance only investors that accept the pre-revenue pilot thesis. Provide the same core factual updates to all serious parties and track questions, document access, references, concerns, and next actions in one process log.

Use late September and October for management meetings, technical diligence coordinated around the independent work, customer-reference discussions that preserve the limitations of the evaluation letters, site or supplier diligence where appropriate, and syndicate formation. Weekly, update the cash forecast, runway threshold, probability-weighted pipeline, and fallback triggers. Avoid serial one-off negotiations that let the process drift.

Set an internal target to identify a credible lead and core terms by late October, leaving November for confirmatory diligence and documentation. Continue parallel contingency planning: a smaller staged financing tied to defined evidence, a bridge from existing supporters, pilot phasing, or a partnership contribution. These are alternatives to evaluate, not commitments, and none should depend on unsigned strategic discussions.

By November 15, the board should compare executable financing paths using milestone sufficiency, dilution, restrictions, governance, closing certainty, investor quality, follow-on capacity, and downside resilience. By November 30, aim to have a financed plan or an approved fallback that preserves runway. Success is not announcing an ambitious round; it is securing enough credible capital and aligned support to reach the next value-creating milestone without overstating demand, schedule, or technical readiness.
