# Financing decision and process through September 30, 2026

The company should turn the current collection of conversations into a time-bounded financing decision, not continue taking meetings until an offer happens to appear. The immediate question is not simply “Can we raise?” It is whether a Series A now is the best way to fund a specific operating plan, on acceptable terms, relative to the credible alternative of continuing without one.

## Starting position

As of July 20, the company collects $180,000 per month and spends $270,000, so its current net cash burn is about $90,000 per month. With $2.1 million of cash and no debt, static runway is roughly 23 months. That estimate is only a baseline: hiring salespeople and completing integrations would increase spending, while continued revenue growth would reduce burn. The company is not under immediate payment pressure, because no obligation is late.

The commercial evidence is encouraging but needs to be made decision-ready. Monthly collections increased from $120,000 to $180,000 over nine months, ARR is $1.7 million, and average net-new ARR was $90,000. The financing materials should reconcile collections, ARR, net-new ARR, churn, and cash so that management and investors can see what is recurring, what is growing, and what converts into cash.

## Define the financing before marketing it

By July 24, the founders should approve a one-page financing brief containing:

- The use of funds: named sales roles or capacity, the two enterprise integrations, associated implementation resources, and a contingency reserve.
- A base operating case and a financed case, each shown monthly through at least the expected point of sustainable cash generation or the next financing decision.
- The minimum, target, and maximum raise. These amounts should come from the monthly plan, not from investor appetite. The minimum must fully fund a coherent plan; the target should fund the planned expansion with adequate buffer; the maximum should have identified productive uses.
- The milestones the round is intended to buy, with dates and owners. These should include integration completion, sales-capacity deployment, revenue or ARR outcomes, and a cash/runway floor.
- Acceptable deal boundaries, including dilution, governance, board composition, protective provisions, liquidation economics, and investor control rights. Exact limits should be agreed privately before term sheets arrive.

If the founders cannot specify the roles, integration budgets, milestones, and required buffer by July 24, they should not launch a Series A process yet. They should spend the following week finishing the operating plan.

## Assign ownership and protect the business

The CEO should remain the decision owner but stop being the sole process operator. One founder or a designated internal lead should own the fundraising calendar, investor tracker, document requests, follow-ups, and weekly reporting. The CEO should reserve fixed meeting blocks and cap fundraising work outside them. Six hours a week may be sustainable during preparation, but a live process will require concentrated availability; therefore product decisions need an explicit backup owner and escalation path.

The founders should hold a 30-minute financing review twice each week. The tracker should record each investor’s stage, last contact, next action, decision maker, relevant experience, concerns, requested material, and probability-adjusted status. “Interested” is not a stage. Useful stages are introduced, first meeting, diligence requested, partner meeting, term-sheet discussion, passed, or deferred.

## Build a qualified investor list

By July 27, score each investor against written criteria: ability to lead the required round, enterprise software and analytics experience, willingness to support the two integrations and sales build, reputation with founders, speed and clarity of decision making, follow-on capacity, governance approach, and conflicts with competitors. References should be taken from founders of both successful and difficult portfolio companies before accepting a term sheet.

The existing eight conversations are only a starting set. The three investors that requested data should receive the same prepared package at the same time and be asked for a dated next step. The two that said to reconnect next quarter should be treated as deferred, not active, unless they commit to the current schedule. The three introduction-only investors need a prompt qualification meeting or should be removed from the active process. Any additional investors should meet the same criteria and enter during a short, coordinated outreach window.

## Run a synchronized process

From July 20 through August 2, prepare the financial model, capitalization table, customer and revenue analyses, product roadmap, integration plans, hiring plan, security and legal materials, and a concise presentation. Create one controlled data room and a standard response log. Management should also identify any facts likely to slow diligence and resolve or disclose them early.

Launch qualified outreach during August 3–7. Schedule first meetings close together, send consistent information, and ask every investor to work toward the same September decision timetable. By August 14, decide which parties have earned access to deeper diligence. By August 28, aim to have completed substantive diligence and partner-level meetings with the strongest candidates. Each Friday, compare actual progress with explicit gates: number of qualified leads, diligence engagements, partner meetings, open issues, and credible paths to a term sheet.

Set September 11 as the target for written proposals or clear term-sheet timing. Compare offers as complete packages, including price, dilution, control, board structure, liquidation terms, investor quality, certainty, and closing conditions. Do not let a high valuation obscure restrictive governance or weak partnership fit. Use counsel for documents, but keep economic and control decisions with the founders and board.

By September 18, choose a preferred financing path and a backup bidder if one exists. Use the remaining period for confirmatory diligence and documentation. September 30 is the decision date: sign an acceptable financing with a defined closing path, explicitly extend the process for a short period because a specific offer is close, or stop.

## Establish the plan B now

The fallback should be approved by July 31, before fundraising results influence judgment. Starting from approximately $90,000 monthly burn, management should specify a cash floor that triggers action and a monthly budget that preserves the core company. The plan should rank the proposed sales hires and integrations, identify what can be sequenced or delayed, and connect every discretionary commitment to observed revenue performance.

If the process has no credible lead or partner-level momentum by August 28, freeze expansion commitments that depend on new capital and shift to the base plan. If no acceptable term sheet exists by September 30, end the open-ended process, tell investors the company is operating to milestones, and set objective conditions for reconsidering financing. Those conditions might include completion of an integration, sustained collection growth, a defined ARR milestone, or reaching a later cash decision point.

This structure replaces vague interest with evidence and deadlines. It preserves the option to raise while the company has meaningful runway, prevents fundraising from indefinitely consuming product leadership, and makes “raise,” “extend,” or “continue independently” a deliberate capital-allocation decision.
