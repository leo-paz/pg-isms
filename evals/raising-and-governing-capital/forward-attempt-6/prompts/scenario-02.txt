On July 20, 2026, a default-alive developer-tools company is comparing two unsigned Series A term sheets. Offer A invests $5 million at a $40 million pre-money valuation, with a 1x non-participating liquidation preference, broad-based weighted-average anti-dilution, a three-seat board with two founder seats and one investor seat, and an option-pool increase funded post-money. Offer B invests $7 million at a $55 million pre-money valuation, with a 1x participating preference, full-ratchet anti-dilution, an option-pool increase funded pre-money, a five-seat board with two founder seats, two investor seats, and one mutually approved independent, plus investor vetoes over budget, hiring or firing executives, new debt, and any sale. Both expire July 27. The company has $4.5 million cash, burns $150,000 monthly, and the board says either amount funds the same 24-month milestone. Founder references for A are consistently positive; two references for B report pressure to replace CEOs after missed plans. The CEO prefers B because ‘the $55 million headline means less dilution.’ Compare the offers and produce a decision without giving legal, tax, or investment advice.
