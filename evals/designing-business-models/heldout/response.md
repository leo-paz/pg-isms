## Board decision memo — July 14, 2026

### Recommendation

Select the **direct cancer-center route** as Morrow's primary economic hypothesis, preserve the **manufacturer license** as the single fallback, and close the episode route for now. This is a decision to test and conditionally prepare a route, not to launch, promote, deliver, or permit clinical reliance on the investigational device.

Use the existing non-dilutive funding and the separately capped evidence budget. **Do not raise institutional equity now.** The direct route has positive modeled economics and keeps the customer relationship, safety feedback, data access, and product roadmap closer to Morrow, but there is no payment evidence and the stated reachable market supports a durable company more clearly than a venture-return case. The company is funded through March 2027, so it need not sell equity to resolve a question that can be tested by October.

The license is the fallback because it removes much of the manufacturing and commercial build if direct demand or Morrow's capability case fails. Its current outline is not acceptable as a deal: it is nonbinding, creates one-partner concentration, begins with a $350,000 Morrow-funded transfer, and couples five-year exclusivity to a year-five forecast that is not a commitment. The episode route has attractive theoretical scale, but its nine-month procurement/evidence cycle misses the board's evidence window, and its core value claim—attributable avoided acute care—has not been established.

### Product and decision state

- **Product and company state:** This is regulated diagnostic hardware with disposable cartridges, cloud and integration needs, and potentially severe clinical consequences. It is investigational and pre-clearance. Morrow is not default-dead: non-dilutive funding covers the regulatory, human-factors, and prospective-study work through March 2027.
- **What is known:** In a supervised 48-patient feasibility study, 93% of paired results were within the prespecified laboratory tolerance and 81% of participants completed a test without staff help. Patients and oncology nurses use and benefit from the concept. Three centers want access discussions.
- **What is not known:** No center, oncology group, or manufacturer has made a binding payment commitment. The feasibility study is not proof of severe-neutropenia sensitivity, unsupervised routine use, clinical outcomes, procurement, deployment, renewal, retention, or expansion.
- **Binding constraint:** First, present regulatory permission and the January clinical gate; second, identification of an actual payer. A six-month center sale and a nine-month episode sale both have longer latency than the October evidence window. Commercial contracts are reversible; clinical harm from a false or invalid result may not be. That asymmetry makes the clinical gate controlling.
- **Facts to verify before exposure or capital:** the current regulatory pathway and what pre-clearance discussions or conditional commitments are permitted; trial and clearance timing; human-factors performance; all-in reader/cartridge COGS including tooling, inventory, scrap, shelf life, working capital, quality systems, reliability, warranty, liability, cloud, and supplier qualification; security and integration cost; the 500-center and 1,200-group counts; budget owners and procurement cycles; concentration by health system; routine cartridge utilization; and the partner's manufacturing, service, data, audit, and minimum-guarantee capacity.

### Actors, evidence, and economics by route

#### 1. Direct cancer center — select as primary

The beneficiary is the chemotherapy patient; the users are the patient and oncology nurse; the buyer is center procurement or the oncology service line; the payer is the center's operating budget; and the channel is a direct, roughly six-month enterprise sale. The value event is a usable home result delivered for clinician review. The alternative is the center's current laboratory and symptom-escalation workflow. The relevant budget, effect on acute-care use, and willingness to pay remain unverified.

The proposed package is one $900 reader plus cartridges at $28 each. At the stated 180 patients and eight cartridges each, a center uses 1,440 cartridges:

- Year-one revenue: $900 + (1,440 × $28) = **$41,220**.
- Product COGS: $420 + (1,440 × $9) = **$13,380**.
- Product gross profit: **$27,840**, or **67.5%** of revenue.
- After the stated $12,000 sale, $4,000 training/integration, and $3,000 support costs, year-one center contribution is **$8,840**, or **21.4%**. This excludes central regulatory, quality, cloud costs not already in cartridge COGS, working capital, warranty, and corporate overhead.
- In a later cartridge-only year, revenue is **$40,320** and contribution after stated support is $27,360 − $3,000 = **$24,360**, or **60.4%**, before the omitted costs above.
- The first-year break-even point on the stated costs is about **975 cartridges**, or **122 patients** at eight cartridges each: ($12,000 + $4,000 + $3,000 − $480 reader gross profit) ÷ $19 cartridge gross profit.

If all 500 claimed centers were reachable and behaved like the model, first-wave revenue would be **$20.61 million** and first-wave contribution **$4.42 million**; later annual cartridge revenue would be **$20.16 million** and contribution after stated support **$12.18 million**. Those are arithmetic ceilings on unverified assumptions, not a forecast. Reader replacement, utilization retention, center renewal, center expansion, health-system concentration, and switching behavior have no evidence yet. Integration and workflow learning create switching costs, but Morrow must not count those as retention.

#### 2. Episode payment — reject now

The beneficiary and users are again patient and oncology nurse; the buyer and payer are a risk-bearing oncology group; the channel is a roughly nine-month outcomes-and-procurement sale. The value event is an attributable improvement over the group's current care pathway, most likely avoided acute care or better episode performance. Attribution, budget authority, actual use, payment, deployment, renewal, retention, and expansion are all unproven.

At 180 annual episodes, revenue is 180 × $480 = **$86,400**. Direct episode cost is 180 × $152 = **$27,360**, leaving **$59,040**, or **68.3%**, before year-one acquisition, implementation, and adjudication. Subtracting the stated $40,000 leaves **$19,040**, or **22.0%**, in year one. It takes about **122 episodes** to recover that $40,000 at a $328 per-episode contribution. At the unverified 1,200-group ceiling, annual revenue could be $103.68 million, but that apparent venture scale rests on the least-established value claim and the longest evidence latency. Morrow should not fund or sell an outcomes contract until attribution and a risk-bearing payer are demonstrated.

#### 3. Manufacturer license — preserve as fallback only

The manufacturer would be the buyer/licensee, payer, manufacturing channel, and commercial channel; patients and clinical teams remain beneficiaries and users. The outline supplies no actual payment, deployment, renewal, or performance evidence. Morrow would spend **$350,000** on design transfer and receive **$500,000** on clearance, a nominal net **$150,000** before internal oversight and tax. A 250,000-cartridge minimum in year three would yield **$1.25 million** of annual royalty at $5 per cartridge if it is an enforceable annual payment obligation. The partner's year-five 1.2 million-unit forecast would yield **$6 million**, but it is explicitly not a commitment. Timing and minimums in years four and five are not specified.

Licensing avoids a sales force, manufacturing build, and much working-capital exposure, but places 100% of commercial execution in one partner. Five-year oncology exclusivity, transfer dependence, and weak data access could make switching slow and expensive. Preserve the option only by requiring, before acceptance, partner funding or reimbursement of accepted design-transfer work; the $500,000 clearance payment; an enforceable royalty floor of at least **$1.25 million annually beginning in year three**; field-limited, performance-conditioned exclusivity; launch and supply diligence; quality and incident duties; audit rights; timely access to safety, usage, and customer data; and termination, step-in, and technology/data-reversion rights. The existing nonbinding forecast cannot substitute for those terms.

### Company boundary and capital posture

Under the direct route, Morrow should retain the center contract, clinical-safety feedback loop, product specification, and roadmap. Patients and centers retain their legal data rights; Morrow should contract only for the minimum operational and safety data access required for monitoring, regulatory duties, and product improvement. Morrow should use documented interfaces and qualify replaceable specialist manufacturers and key suppliers rather than vertically integrate merely because the product is important. Acceptance tests, lot traceability, reliability telemetry, and explicit service levels must distinguish supplier failure from architecture or workflow failure. Integration, support, quality, inventory, supplier redundancy, and field monitoring must be costed before scaling.

Under the license fallback, the partner may own manufacturing and channel execution, but Morrow must retain auditability, required safety data, enforceable standards, and a switching path. The partner's maturity, capacity, quality record, replaceability, and ability to service centers require diligence. No exclusivity should survive missed minimums, launch dates, supply levels, safety reporting, or data obligations.

The **durable-company path** is direct center sales funded with grants, customer commitments, and operating cash as milestones permit. On the supplied assumptions it can become contribution-positive, requires a focused quality/supply/support organization, and fits the CEO's clinical-responsibility goal. Its downside is slow enterprise sales, hardware working capital, regulated support, and a market ceiling that has not shown venture returns. Plausible eventual outcomes are a profitable independent diagnostics company or a later strategic license/acquisition; neither should be priced into today's decision.

The **venture path** would require evidence of a much larger repeatable market—additional center segments, indications, geographies, or materially higher justified utilization—plus a team and supply system able to absorb acceleration. Reopen institutional equity only after: (1) the January clinical gate and current permission pathway are passed; (2) at least **10 post-clearance centers** are live at full price, at least **80%** renew, and average cartridge use is at least **75% of the modeled 1,440 per center**; (3) fully loaded center acquisition payback is no more than **12 months** and mature contribution remains at least **50%** after all omitted costs; and (4) a verified bottom-up market supports at least **$100 million in annual revenue** and a 24-month plan shows equity buys at least a **3× increase in safe deployment capacity** rather than covering negative unit economics. Until then, equity would finance uncertainty rather than acceleration. The capital owner should preserve the durable path, with no institutional dilution now.

### One bounded commercial-evidence test

**Test:** By July 31, counsel and the regulatory lead must approve one truthful, non-promotional conditional center-access offer. From August 1 through October 16, the CEO and customer-learning lead present exactly that offer to **eight qualified high-volume centers**: the three existing interested centers and five independently recruited centers. Each receives the same post-clearance first-year package: one reader plus a commitment to 1,440 cartridges at the listed prices, total **$41,220**, with a **$4,122 deposit** placed in third-party escrow. The deposit is credited at lawful deployment, returned automatically if Morrow fails the regulatory/January conditions or cannot supply, and otherwise forfeited for customer convenience. No discounts, free devices, outcome guarantees, paid site participation, or custom clinical claims are allowed. Morrow does not recognize or access escrowed funds pre-clearance.

The agreement is expressly conditioned on confirmation of the current regulatory pathway and permissions, required clearance, completion of human-factors validation and the 120-patient study, and the January 15 gate. It delivers no device, directs no care, enrolls no patient, changes no current workflow, and permits no clinical reliance. Materials state the device is investigational and describe the 48-patient feasibility results with their supervised-study limitations. Clinician review, monitoring, incident response, and stop rules remain mandatory in any later lawful deployment. Counsel may veto the protocol; if it cannot approve it by July 31, Morrow makes no substituted soft-interest test and reports **zero legally usable commitments**.

The evidence budget is capped exactly at **$75,000**: $18,000 legal/regulatory review; $16,000 for eight workflow, security, integration, and procurement scopes; $15,000 loaded internal test labor; $12,000 recruitment and travel; $4,000 escrow/contract administration; $5,000 analysis; and $5,000 contingency. Sites receive none of this budget.

The primary metric, frozen before outreach, is the number of executed agreements with funded deposits by **October 23**. Record time to procurement decision, source (existing versus independently recruited), named budget owner, forecast cartridge use, integration scope, and verbatim rejection reason. Predeclare these interpretations:

- **Proceed / keep price:** **4–8 of 8** centers sign and fund deposits at full price, totaling at least **$16,488**, with at least one signer among the five independently recruited centers. The November board keeps direct center as primary and authorizes post-gate readiness work, not deployment.
- **Revise / segment or change package:** **2–3 of 8** sign and fund, or 4 or more sign but none is independently recruited. The board keeps the license fallback alive, authorizes one bounded redesign around the dominant documented budget, integration, volume, or procurement objection, and requires another paid-commitment gate before commercial build. Existing signers are not grandfathered into discounts because none were offered.
- **Stop direct / activate fallback diligence:** **0–1 of 8** signs and funds, including the counsel-veto case. Stop spending on the direct sales build and move to the license diligence requirements above. Interest, praise, committee meetings, unsigned letters, and requested free pilots count as zero. Do not preserve a free clinical deployment; a separately funded research relationship may continue only under the clinical protocol and cannot be treated as commercial evidence.

Pre-clearance timing, annual budget cycles, warm-lead selection, procurement rules, and the feasibility study's limits are real confounders. The independent-center requirement reduces but does not eliminate selection bias. The test therefore resolves whether the present full-price route has enough commitment to justify the next step; it does not prove market size, renewal, clinical effect, or venture scale.

### Decision rights and next owners

The board owns the November 6 route and capital decision. The CEO owns execution of the bounded economic test and may not override counsel or the clinical/regulatory lead. Counsel owns legality of pre-clearance exposure. The clinical/regulatory lead owns the January 15 determination: sensitivity of at least **95%** for severe neutropenia, invalid results no higher than **5%**, and **no unresolved serious device-related event**. Failure of any element stops clinical reliance and commercial deployment regardless of commercial demand; neither the direct route nor the license fallback can bypass that gate.

After a **proceed** result, the CEO owns conditional center readiness, engineering owns the verified integration/supply cost, and the clinical/regulatory lead retains release authority; after lawful paid deployment, shipping owns rollout and growth owns repeatable acquisition. After a **revise** result, business-model and customer-learning owners run the single approved package revision while the CEO preserves the license option. After a **stop** result, the CEO and partner-boundary owner run manufacturer diligence against the minimum, data, quality, and reversion conditions, while the capital owner keeps institutional equity closed. If the January gate fails under any branch, the clinical/regulatory lead owns root-cause work and a new board-reviewed evidence plan; commercial owners stop.
