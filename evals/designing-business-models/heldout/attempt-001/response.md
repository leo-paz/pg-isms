## Board recommendation

Make the first berth a **$6.4 million installed sale, built by the incumbent EPC**, with BlueBerth retaining the controls IP, raw event-level operating-data access, and the direct support relationship. Do not put construction inside BlueBerth. Offer service in parallel by August 1 to learn the customer's preference, but rank it second and proceed only if financing becomes available for construction rather than only after field proof. Keep a non-exclusive, data-complete EPC license as the third route.

This order buys the same indispensable evidence—an approved vessel deployment followed by 120 consecutive days at at least 98.5% availability and zero reportable safety incidents—while taking the least balance-sheet risk. If more than one route clears, the order is: **installed sale, service SPV, qualifying EPC license, stop**. A site in the repair band pauses all three routes until repaired or moved; a site in the stop band ends this berth.

## Business-model record

**Product, state, and constraint.** This is capital-heavy, regulated, high-harm energy hardware plus control software. BlueBerth is pre-revenue apart from engineering studies; 2,000 lab cycles are useful technical evidence but there has been no vessel use, paid deployment, renewal, or retention. The binding first-berth constraint is not theoretical unit economics; it is permission, construction funding, and a long-latency field reliability result. An arc-flash or vessel-control error has severe consequences, so pricing cannot waive the interconnection agreement, current state electrical approval, accepted port fire-and-evacuation plan, vessel-class signoff, monitoring, accountable human operation, remote isolation, rollback, and incident-response/reporting requirements.

Cash is $15.5 million and current net burn is $440,000 per month, or **35.2 months of simple runway**. The grant is reimbursement after milestones and is therefore excluded until cash is received. The VC indication and the lender indication are not cash. Ring-fencing the sale's stated $1.4 million peak working-capital exposure leaves $14.1 million, or **32.0 months at the current burn**, before crediting sale receipts or grant reimbursement.

**First segment and actors.** Sound Crossing's operations team receives uptime and fuel-savings value; dispatchers are the users; its CFO is the purchase approver; and Sound Crossing is the payer under either direct route. The Port owns the berth, the utility controls interconnection, and BlueBerth's controls generate the learning data. This is a direct enterprise sale with regulatory and site stakeholders, not a scalable channel yet. The customer's existing operating alternative and quantified fuel-savings budget were not supplied and must not be invented. Its $180,000 non-refundable engineering payment demonstrates willingness to fund engineering, not willingness to buy or use the system. The three unpaid, non-binding letters are leads, not demand evidence. The first site would be 100% of revenue; only a signed second site reduces forecast concentration to 58%.

The service operating economics are attractive, but they do not cure the construction-financing circularity. For illustration only, if $3.57M of debt amortized over the 10-year customer term at 9%, annual debt service would be about $556k and operating DSCR would be about **2.35-2.67** across the accepted price range. The lender must supply the actual term, amortization, reserves, covenants, draw conditions, and contingency treatment; interest rate and leverage alone are insufficient.

**Venture shape and capital fit.** None of the supplied routes supports the founders' conventional-VC condition. Across all 48 reachable berths, service yields at most **$71.28M of annual recurring gross profit** at $0.34, or $62.64M at $0.32. Installed-sale support yields $5.28M recurring gross profit at 48 berths; the $1.85M equipment gross profit is transactional, not recurring. Licensing yields $5.04M recurring gross profit at 48 berths, and the incumbent alone can deliver only 32 berths in eight years at four per year. Reaching $100M through service with 48 berths would require $2.083M gross profit per berth, equivalent to about **$0.406/kWh** at the supplied $0.175/kWh direct operating cost and 9 GWh—above the customer's ceiling—or about 68 identical berths at $0.34.

Therefore choose the founders' durable-company path: target 20-30% annual growth, use customer payments for sold systems, and use project finance only after it is construction-available and the reliability gate is proven. Stage hiring behind paid sites. The $20M VC expression is non-binding, with no terms or dilution supplied, and the market math does not justify accepting conventional venture equity. The venture path's evidence cannot arrive before regulated field deployment plus the 120-day run; scaling earlier would add dilution, project exposure, and safety risk. No exit evidence is supplied for either path, so no exit value should be underwritten.

**Construction boundary.** Keep EPC construction outside BlueBerth. The claimed saving is 12% of $1.25M, or **$150k per site**, and begins only after the third simultaneous site. At three sites that is $450k of savings against $1.8M of annual hires, before the $900k tooling bill. First-year simple break-even is 18 sites ($2.7M / $150k); subsequent annual break-even is 12 sites ($1.8M / $150k), while only one site is funded. Internalization also delays the first evidence by six months: **$2.64M of current burn**, plus $900k of tools and about $900k for six months of the new team, or roughly **$4.44M of pre-evidence cash cost** before site materials. Use a milestone contract, BlueBerth-owned interface specification, acceptance tests, data access, and a documented replacement path. Revisit internal construction only when signed backlog supports at least 12 sites per year, at least three simultaneous sites are real, quality and coordination failures are attributable to the EPC rather than BlueBerth's design/interface, and a fresh build-versus-buy model covers management and delay costs.

## Bounded decision test

Authorize one test through **October 15, 2026**, capped at **$250,000**, with no long-lead equipment, construction hires, geographic exclusivity, or site commitment before the gates clear:

1. **Commercial/legal—CEO, $60k cap:** By August 1 put both contracts in front of Sound Crossing. The sale is $6.4M on the stated 30/30/30/10 milestones with $180k annual support. The service offer starts at $0.34/kWh, may move only within $0.32-$0.34, and requires at least 9 GWh/year for 10 years, at least a $750k termination payment, and BlueBerth's customer-contract and raw-data ownership.
2. **Site, safety, and permissions—VP Engineering, $90k cap:** Obtain the binding utility price and schedule by September 30 and document the current approval path, named accountable operator, monitoring/event log, remote isolation and human abort procedure, incident response, fire-and-evacuation acceptance, and class signoff. This work is diligence and design only, not an equipment order.
3. **Finance and partner boundary—CFO, $100k cap:** Seek a conditional lender commitment stating leverage on total all-in cost, construction-stage draw availability, interest no higher than 10%, DSCR at least 1.35, complete amortization/reserve/covenant terms, and BlueBerth equity no higher than $1.8M. In parallel, counter the incumbent EPC at no less than $500k at commissioning and $150k annually, no geographic exclusivity, and raw event-level data; obtain scopes and schedules, but do not spend $320k qualifying both alternates.

Interpret the interconnection result first: **at or below $750k and 12 months is pass; $750,001-$1.1M or 13-18 months is repair/alternate-site; anything worse is stop for this berth.** An approval, fire-plan, class, or safety-path failure also stops energization until cured.

Then apply these precommitted branches:

- **Installed sale / pay:** An executed $6.4M contract and the $1.92M deposit actually wired, with the $1.4M exposure preserved, is payment evidence. CEO closes; CFO controls milestone cash and grant reimbursements; VP Engineering owns permissions, safety, instrumentation, and the reliability run; the EPC owns construction. Do not count authorization or an unsigned contract as payment.
- **Service / revise:** Use this only if the sale is rejected and Sound Crossing executes the bounded take-or-pay contract **and** a lender commits to construction-stage funding meeting every board threshold, including the all-in equity cap. CEO owns the customer contract; CFO owns SPV close and draw conditions; the EPC remains construction owner; VP Engineering owns the safety and reliability gates. A contract without construction funding, or post-reliability-only debt, does not clear.
- **License / partner:** Use this only if neither direct route clears and the incumbent accepts the cash, no-exclusivity, and raw-data terms. CEO/legal owns the boundary contract; VP Engineering owns the interface, acceptance test, and event schema; the EPC owns the customer and construction. If it insists on exclusivity or summaries, repair once or stop; do not trade away the switching path merely to get a deployment.
- **Repair or alternate site:** For the middle interconnection band, the business-development lead owns a written utility/customer cost-and-schedule cure or a funded alternate-site package; CFO freezes project capital and Engineering places no long-lead orders. Reapply the same thresholds rather than relabeling a miss as a pass.
- **Stop / preserve:** If the site is beyond the stop threshold, Sound Crossing rejects both direct offers after a site pass, or no route meets its data and capital gates, CEO ends work on this berth. CFO preserves cash; `learning-from-users` records whether the cause was price, financing, permission, schedule, or control terms. Maintain the three letters at no deployment cost, but do not treat them as a free-product strategy or demand.

Pricing interpretation is also fixed in advance: keep a $6.4M sale or $0.34 service acceptance; change service price only within $0.32-$0.34 with all other protections intact; do not infer a new segment from one customer; grandfather only the signed first-customer service price for its contract term; stop rather than go below $0.32 or increase sale working-capital exposure above $1.4M.

## Evidence that permits scaling or changing course

The first deployment is not validated at commissioning. Require 120 consecutive days at **at least 98.5% availability, zero reportable safety incidents, complete raw event-level data, and all permissions current**. Any reportable incident triggers isolation, required reporting, independent root-cause review, and a new board release decision. Availability below 98.5% sends the product to Engineering for repair and restarts the consecutive-day clock; it is not a pricing failure. For the sale, the final $640k remains tied to this run.

After that gate, require the $180k support renewal or continued compliant take-or-pay performance, documented uptime/fuel-savings effects without unsupported causal claims, and a signed second site before scaling the sales motion; that second signature is the first concrete reduction of concentration from 100% to 58%. Reconsider an SPV-first model only when construction-stage project debt keeps total BlueBerth equity within $1.8M and at least one completed site proves the operating inputs. Reconsider licensing first only when partner terms preserve raw data, switching, and control-software value. Reconsider internal construction only at the backlog and failure-attribution gate above. Until one of those facts changes, the installed-sale/EPC-construction decision stands.
