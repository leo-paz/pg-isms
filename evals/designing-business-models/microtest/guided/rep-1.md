Primary owner: Designing business models

Ordered Handoffs: (1) managing runway and survival must first set a spending switch date if the company is default-dead; (2) learning from users collects owner, technician, nonrenewal, and courier evidence under the economic tests below; (3) engineering for leverage instruments site costs and creates a replaceable courier interface; (4) shipping and iterating implements offers, billing, and rollout controls; (5) acquiring and growing users runs the direct and wholesaler cohorts after the offers are fixed; (6) competing and positioning validates claims and partner dependence; (7) communicating clearly turns the validated outcome into owner-facing sales material; (8) building and evolving organizations owns staffing if dispatch ever passes the build gate; and (9) raising and governing capital is revisited only if a gated build cannot be funded from operations. If in-house dispatch becomes a multi-sided courier product, marketplace acquisition and platform governance must also own courier liquidity, incentives, trust, and participant policy before launch.

## Decision now

For the next 90 days, keep the company a capital-light, B2B subscription business. Test a base-plus-usage price by pharmacy volume, continue direct owner-led selling, and offer the wholesaler only a nonexclusive, performance-gated proof. Keep the SLA-backed courier API, add observability and a replaceable interface, and do not authorize the $600,000 dispatch build.

The evidence supports that direction, but not yet a permanent catalog or a causal performance claim. All 18 pilots paid $500 per month and 12 renewed, a 67% pilot-to-renewal rate. Renewed sites improved from 86% to 95% on-time delivery, a nine-point observed change. The six nonrenewals, site mix, duration after renewal, and other operational changes are unknown, so “95% at renewed sites” is usable evidence, while “the product causes a nine-point improvement” is not yet justified.

## Business-model record

Product and state: this is capital-light B2B route-optimization software with enterprise-like deployment and integration work. Prescription delivery makes it high-consequence and potentially subject to healthcare, privacy, pharmacy, courier, and consumer rules that must be confirmed for each market. The product has paid use and an initial renewal signal, but repeatable pricing, segment margins, and channel economics are not established.

The binding constraints are distorted unit economics above 2,500 monthly deliveries, limited evidence from 18 sites, and the risk that a channel or dispatch commitment consumes an 11-person company. Pricing tests and a short channel proof are reversible within weeks. Two-year exclusivity and a six-month, $600,000 build are slow and costly to reverse. Before day 10, the CEO must also document cash on hand, monthly burn, default-alive/default-dead status, and a no-new-spend date; if survival binds, that plan supersedes the discretionary budgets below.

Actors and segments:

| Segment | Beneficiary / user | Buyer and payer | Current evidence | Alternative and likely budget | Main unknowns |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Up to 2,500 deliveries per month | Patients benefit; pharmacy technicians operate the product | The pharmacy owner approves and the pharmacy pays | Some portion of 18 sites paid $500; segment-specific renewal is unknown | Manual planning, courier tools, or the status quo; budget source is pharmacy operations/delivery | Segment count, technician adoption, delivery labor saved, failed-delivery cost, deployment time, support load, and renewal by volume |
| Above 2,500 deliveries per month | Same beneficiary and user | Same owner and payer | These sites generate 60% of compute and support cost, but their count, revenue share, and renewals are unknown | Manual dispatch, courier software, internal processes; larger delivery operations may have more budget, but that must be tested | Fully loaded cost per delivery, API-call rate, support causes, willingness to pay for scale/SLA, concentration, and expansion |
| Wholesaler-referred pharmacies | Same end users and buyers; the wholesaler is an acquisition channel, not yet the payer | Pharmacy remains payer unless the wholesaler makes a minimum purchase commitment | “Access to 600” is reach, not qualified pipeline or paid demand | Direct sales and other pharmacy networks | Introduction quality, attribution, cannibalization, sales cycle, deployment rate, channel support, integration scope, and who owns the customer relationship |

The value event is a paid, live pharmacy deployment that measurably improves reliable delivery without shifting unsafe work to technicians, couriers, or patients. Track actual use, first invoice collection, deployment, second and third invoice payment, renewal or annual commitment, retention, expansion, and reasons for churn separately. Do not count praise, demonstrations, introductions, unsigned letters, free use, or an undeployed contract as demand.

For every site, calculate monthly contribution as collected revenue minus discounts, refunds, channel share, courier API charges, compute, delivery-linked services, onboarding labor amortized over 12 months, and attributable support labor. Report both gross margin before sales expense and contribution after acquisition expense. Tag every cost by site and volume band. The current fact that larger pharmacies create 60% of compute and support cost is not enough to set a surcharge until their count and fully loaded dollars are known.

## Pricing and direct-sales test

Use completed or API-processed deliveries as the value metric, with a location base fee to cover access, ordinary support, and product value. Quote the following provisional catalog to new customers from day 21 through day 75:

- Standard, up to 2,500 monthly deliveries: $750 per location per month.
- Scale, above 2,500 monthly deliveries: $1,500 per location per month plus $0.15 for each delivery above 2,500.
- When the startup incurs the courier API charge, show the actual $0.40 per API-processed delivery as a separate pass-through line rather than hiding it in the subscription. Confirm the accounting treatment before presenting it as revenue.
- Standard integrations are included only if they fit the documented connector. Custom work is separately scoped, with a $2,500 minimum and 50% paid before work starts. Annual prepayment may receive at most a 10% discount; do not stack discounts.

These are test prices, not conclusions. They deliberately expose whether an owner will pay for the software value independently of the courier’s transaction cost. Before quoting, replace any package whose forecast gross margin is below its floor with a price calculated from actual direct cost. Target at least 65% site-level gross margin for direct sales; never quote a price that forecasts less than 50% without the CEO explicitly labeling it a time-bounded learning subsidy.

Honor all current contracts. Keep the 12 renewed sites at their contracted base through the 90-day test. By day 60, offer them the applicable new tier at the next lawful renewal, with a 20% first-year transition discount in exchange for a 12-month commitment and permission to use de-identified outcome evidence. Where contracts allow, introduce the API pass-through only with advance notice and consent; there should be no surprise midterm increase.

The CEO owns price and the head of sales owns execution. Recruit 20 qualified, owner-confirmed opportunities split evenly across the two volume bands. Deliver at least 12 written quotes, six per band, and seek at least three paid, live deployments per band. A “yes” requires a signed order, collected first invoice, and production deployment within 30 days. Cap the test at two engineer-weeks of nonreusable work and $10,000 of external expense. No new prospect gets an open-ended free production pilot.

Precommit the interpretation:

- **Pay / keep:** for a segment, at least three of six quoted owners pay and deploy, at least 80% pay a second invoice or sign an annual commitment, and measured gross margin is at least 65%. Keep that segment’s offer.
- **Revise / change:** two of six pay and at least three nonbuyers independently identify the same price metric, package boundary, or budget obstacle. Make one bounded revision and retest; generic interest does not qualify.
- **Segment:** acceptance differs by more than 20 percentage points between volume bands, or either band misses 65% margin while the other passes. Maintain separate packages rather than averaging away the result.
- **Grandfather:** a current customer is healthy and willing to renew but cannot absorb the new tier at the next renewal. Offer the 20% one-year transition discount; do not promise indefinite $500 pricing.
- **Preserve free:** at most two prospects may receive a 14-day, synthetic- or historical-data sandbox solely to resolve technician usability. It carries no production SLA and does not count as willingness to pay.
- **Stop:** pause acquisition in a segment if no more than one of six owners pays after one revised offer, or if the accepted price cannot produce at least 50% gross margin. Return to user evidence rather than adding sales volume.

Sales should be owner-led because technicians use but do not approve the purchase. The technician champion joins discovery and deployment, but the call to action is an owner-approved paid start. Use only truthful cohort language: “renewed sites moved from 86% to 95% on-time during the observed period.” Capture baseline volume, staffing, courier changes, delivery radius, weather, and service mix so those confounders can be separated. The day-90 effects gate is no severe safety or privacy incident attributable to the product, no site-level on-time decline greater than three percentage points without an explained external cause and corrective plan, and at least 80% of new paid sites still live and paying. Failure pauses scaling even if sales conversion passes.

## Wholesaler channel test

Reject the proposal as written. A 25% share of all revenue, two-year exclusivity, and unpriced custom integration transfers acquisition, concentration, and technical risk to the startup in exchange for uncommitted access. At the test Standard price, the share alone is $187.50 per location every month before API, support, or integration cost.

By day 15, counter with a 90-day, nonexclusive proof covering no more than 20 pharmacies:

- 15% of collected subscription base revenue for the first 12 months of a referred account, then zero; no share of API pass-through, implementation fees, taxes, refunds, or preexisting/direct accounts.
- At least 30 named, qualification-matched introductions, 15 completed owner meetings, eight paid deployments, and first invoices collected by day 90.
- A written attribution window, no double commissions, direct access to pharmacy owners and technicians, and startup ownership of product telemetry and its customer contracts. Each pharmacy retains its data and export rights.
- No exclusivity during the proof. Any later exclusivity must be narrow by region and segment, last no more than six months at a time, terminate automatically if agreed quarterly paid-deployment minimums are missed, and never prevent service to existing accounts.
- No custom integration until ten pharmacies sign contingent paid orders. The wholesaler prepays external costs; startup exposure is capped at two engineer-weeks and $15,000. The integration uses a documented, removable interface and must not give the wholesaler control of pricing, roadmap, or renewals.

Compare wholesaler and direct cohorts on qualified-to-meeting, meeting-to-paid, time to deployment, collected revenue, churn, support hours, gross margin after revenue share, acquisition payback, and cannibalization. Extend the channel only if it produces at least eight paid deployments, at least 55% first-year gross margin after the share and integration amortization, no more than two support hours per site per month after onboarding, no material safety or privacy issue, and no worse than a 12-month acquisition-payback forecast. If the wholesaler insists on 25%, it must instead guarantee enough prepaid revenue and fund integration so the same margin and payback thresholds hold; two-year exclusivity remains off the table.

The selected channel branch is direct sales plus this nonexclusive proof. Integrate the channel only after every threshold passes; revise once if introductions are qualified but conversion misses; preserve the relationship without integration if timing is the only failure; stop if the wholesaler will not accept measurable obligations or customer/data protections. The preserved option is a later performance-based regional arrangement, not blanket exclusivity.

## Courier make-or-partner boundary

Partner for now. The current API is variable at $0.40 per delivery and comes with an SLA. The proposed build consumes $600,000 before six months of maintenance, infrastructure, security, compliance, incident response, and opportunity cost. Even with zero in-house operating cost, $600,000 equals 1.5 million partner deliveries; spread over 24 post-launch months, that is 62,500 deliveries per month merely to recover development cost. “Access to 600 pharmacies” cannot be used in that calculation until pharmacies are contracted, deployed, and producing volume.

The company must still reduce supplier dependence. The CTO owns a day-1-to-60 boundary test capped at two engineer-weeks and $15,000:

1. Instrument API calls by site for cost, p95 latency, contract-SLA attainment, failure rate, retries, manual interventions, and effect on delivery outcomes. Correlation IDs must distinguish partner failure from the startup’s adapter, data, or workflow failure.
2. Document customer, route, and derived-data ownership; security and privacy duties; export rights; termination assistance; price-change terms; subprocessor use; incident notification; and the right to use another supplier. Legal and security owners must confirm current obligations before live data enters any alternative.
3. Put the partner behind a versioned internal interface, preserve route export, and run a rollback drill. Obtain written terms and technical evaluations from at least two alternatives. Test only synthetic or de-identified shadow traffic until permission, assurance, and contracts support live use.
4. Ask the current partner for volume tiers, stronger incident remedies, and portability. Record coordination labor and blocked roadmap or sales opportunities as costs rather than calling dispatch “core.”

Precommit the boundary branches at day 60 and confirm them at day 90:

- **Partner:** retain the current provider if it meets its contracted SLA, API-attributable route failures remain below 1%, no unresolved severe incident exists, and no required customer/data right is blocked.
- **Repair:** if misses are chiefly caused by the startup’s adapter, bad inputs, or weak observability, repair those components and rerun the test before blaming the supplier.
- **Dual-source:** qualify a second provider if the current supplier misses its SLA twice in 90 days, switching can be completed within 30 days, and the alternate’s incremental total cost is no more than $0.15 per delivery for comparable assurance. Route no patient-impacting traffic without monitoring, human override, and rollback.
- **Integrate/build:** authorize a full plan only if at least 70% of forecast volume is contract-backed, partner-routed volume will exceed 125,000 deliveries per month, a complete 24-month comparison including development, internal labor, cloud, assurance, maintenance, and six-month delay shows both at least $300,000 and 30% savings with payback within 18 months after launch, and partner repair or dual-sourcing cannot meet a documented capability, control, or safety requirement. A board-approved funding and staffing plan must show that core route optimization and support will not be starved.
- **Stop:** if no partner can meet the safety, privacy, or reliability floor and the build gate does not pass, stop offering the dispatch-dependent feature rather than exposing pharmacies and patients to unmanaged failure.

The preserved option is an implementation-ready dispatch specification plus the internal interface, not speculative code. The cost of delaying a build is measurable: $0.40 per partner delivery plus verified coordination cost and any documented lost deals. The benefit of delay is avoiding $600,000, six months, and management diversion before volume and requirements are known.

## 90-day operating calendar and final gates

**Days 1–15 — establish truth.** The CEO sets runway constraints and decision rights. Finance/operations produces site-level unit economics. Learning from users interviews the owner and lead technician at all 18 pilot sites, including all six nonrenewals, and samples courier operators. Engineering instruments cost, usage, failures, and support. Security/legal confirms current permission, privacy, pharmacy, courier, human-review, monitoring, rollback, incident-response, and data-retention obligations. Keep a manual route override and accountable incident owner throughout.

**Days 16–30 — launch bounded offers.** Publish the provisional packages and billing rules, train sales on owner-led qualification, begin the 20-opportunity direct cohort, issue the wholesaler counterproposal, and start the courier interface and supplier review. No performance claim goes beyond observed evidence, no free production use is counted as demand, and no custom channel work starts without the signed-order and funding gates.

**Days 31–60 — collect commitments, not enthusiasm.** Require invoices and live deployment for pricing evidence. Review direct and channel cohort economics weekly. At day 45, stop any offer forecast below 50% gross margin. At day 60, choose the preliminary courier branch, notify existing customers of any next-renewal transition, and end the wholesaler discussion if it will not accept a nonexclusive measured proof.

**Days 61–90 — retest and decide.** Make at most one pricing or packaging revision per segment, complete second-invoice and effects checks, calculate channel economics including integration amortization, and run the supplier rollback or shadow test. The CEO records one branch for each decision with the measured numerator, denominator, margin, confounders, and owner.

At day 90, scale only a package that passes payment, margin, retention, and user-effects gates. Scale the wholesaler channel only if its separate deployment, margin, support, and payback gates pass. Continue the courier partnership unless every build gate passes; current facts do not meet them. Pricing implementation then hands to product, successful cohort growth to acquisition, interface work to engineering, and any later funded dispatch organization to capital and organization owners. Missing evidence means extend the bounded test, not infer success from the 600-pharmacy headline or the 95% renewed-site result.
