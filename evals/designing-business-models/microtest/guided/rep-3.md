## Decision now

For the next 90 days, keep dispatch partnered, reject the wholesaler proposal as written, and test a metered two-tier subscription through both a direct-sales control cohort and a small, non-exclusive wholesaler cohort. Grandfather the 12 renewed pharmacies at $500 for 90 days while measuring their economics; do not approve the $600,000 build or two-year exclusivity.

The input evidence is promising but not yet repeatable: 18 pharmacies paid for a ten-week pilot, 12 renewed (67%) and six did not, and renewed sites improved on-time delivery from 86% to 95%. That is evidence of payment, deployment, renewal, and an outcome at the renewed sites, but not proof that the software caused the improvement or that the churned sites saw it. The strongest immediate problem is capture: high-volume pharmacies generate 60% of compute and support cost while paying the same as smaller sites.

All figures in this plan come from the founders' July 14, 2026 brief. Vendor terms, current pharmacy/privacy rules, and cost assumptions must be revalidated before exposure.

## Pricing and direct-sales test

The CEO and head of sales should recruit 16 new owner decisions, stratified before outreach: eight at or below 2,500 monthly deliveries and eight above it. Each receives the applicable published package, no discount above 10%, a three-month paid term, and the same ROI review. Pharmacy technicians join workflow validation, but an owner's signed paid order is the acceptance event. In parallel, interview all 12 renewal owners, all six nonrenewal owners, at least eight technician users split across the two volume bands, and four courier operators. Nonresponse is recorded rather than replaced with praise from users.

At the sale, record proposed price, accepted price, sales hours, implementation promise, expected volume, current on-time rate, manual exception time, courier mix, owner-stated ROI, and objection. At 30 and 60 days after activation, record payment status, active usage, on-time rate, exceptions, support and compute cost, and any site or courier change. Discounted wins remain in the cohort and are not counted as full-price acceptance.

Precommitted pricing branches at day 90:

- **Keep:** at least six of eight small-site owners and five of eight high-volume owners accept at no more than a 10% discount, at least eight total sites are paid and live, each volume band models at least 70% fully loaded gross margin, and no material safety or compliance failure occurs. Publish the packages and give the 12 grandfathered sites 60 days' notice before migration.
- **Segment:** owner acceptance clears those thresholds but either volume band's gross margin is below 70%, or support hours differ by more than 2:1 between bands. Change included volumes, support entitlements, or overage price for that band; preserve the other band's package.
- **Change:** only three to five owners in either band accept, but at least half of decliners quantify positive ROI at a lower price or different value metric. Run one bounded revised paid offer; do not treat a free trial as validation.
- **Grandfather:** a renewed customer would otherwise churn solely because of migration and remains above 70% margin at $500. Preserve $500 only through its next renewal, with its current scope and an explicit end date; no new customer gets the legacy package.
- **Stop:** fewer than three of eight owners in a band accept and fewer than four can document annual value at least twice the annual price, or the band cannot reach 70% margin without an unaccepted price. Stop selling that package or segment instead of buying growth.

## Sales and wholesaler channel

Direct owner-led selling is the benchmark because it reveals the economic buyer, sales effort, deployment friction, and reasons for churn. The motion is: technician workflow discovery, owner ROI review, paid order, standard onboarding, then 30/60-day operating review. Report direct customer-acquisition cost using founder and staff time at fully loaded rates; do not call it zero.

The wholesaler's 600-pharmacy access is reach, not demand. At the current $500 price, a 25% share leaves only $375 before compute, support, integration, or dispatch costs. Two-year exclusivity would remove the direct control cohort and surrender customer access before conversion, retention, and channel economics are known.

Counter with a 90-day, non-exclusive pilot limited to 20 named pharmacies:

- The wholesaler may receive 25% of cash-collected subscription revenue from accounts it directly sources for their first 12 months, then 10%; usage pass-through, taxes, refunds, and integration fees are excluded.
- The wholesaler prepays the greater of $25,000 or 125% of the fully scoped custom-integration cost. Scope is capped, acceptance criteria are written, and new work requires a new statement of work.
- The startup owns the direct customer relationship, product and de-identified aggregate learnings; each pharmacy controls its patient data. Both parties get only the permissions needed for service. The wholesaler cannot block data export, direct support, or termination.
- No exclusivity is granted in the 90-day period. Any later exclusivity must be limited to the named region and wholesaler-sourced independent pharmacies, terminate automatically on missed minimums, and include at least 75 paid live pharmacies and $250,000 in annual cash revenue net to the startup. Those figures are negotiation guards, not current forecasts.

The channel passes its day-90 gate only if the wholesaler produces 20 qualified owner introductions, 12 signed paid orders, and 10 paid live sites; startup deployment stays at or below four staff hours per standard site; post-share gross margin is at least 65% in each volume band; channel customer-acquisition payback is at most six months; and direct customer and product-data access remain intact. If conversion clears but margin fails, revise the share or package. If margin clears but deployment fails, repair onboarding before scaling. If either the integration prepayment or non-exclusivity guard is rejected, stop this deal while preserving a future channel relationship. Do not grant exclusivity at day 90 even if the pilot passes; a full renewal cycle must first confirm retention.

## Make-or-partner decision

Continue with the SLA-backed API and cap discovery at $15,000 plus two engineer-weeks. The CTO should not begin the $600,000 production build. Ignoring all owned operating cost, $600,000 equals 1.5 million dispatches at $0.40 each. On a 24-month decision horizon with six months to build and 18 months of subsequent savings, break-even requires about 83,333 dispatches every month after launch. Owned infrastructure, support, compliance, courier connectivity, on-call coverage, and residual partner use only raise that threshold. The current brief supplies no contracted volume or control advantage that clears it.

During days 1–30, engineering must separate supplier failures from the startup's routing, integration, pharmacy workflow, and courier failures; measure API volume, latency, error rates, SLA credits, incident severity, and manual recovery time; review data ownership, price-change, termination, and portability terms; and obtain one qualified alternate supplier quote. Build a narrow internal adapter, replayable logs with patient data minimized, export, manual fallback, and a tested supplier switch procedure. These are architecture assets, not a dispatch product.

During days 31–60, run a paper design and failure exercise for partner, dual-source, and owned options. The owned estimate must include six-month opportunity cost, ongoing engineering and on-call labor, compliance and security, courier integrations, insurance, incident response, uptime, support, and expected cost per dispatch. Interview the 16 prospective owners to learn whether owned dispatch changes purchase or retention; a feature request without paid commitment is not value evidence.

At day 90, apply these boundary branches:

- **Partner:** keep the current supplier if trailing-60-day availability meets its contracted SLA, there is no unresolved severity-one safety incident, the adapter can switch suppliers, and contracted 18-month volume is below 100,000 monthly dispatches. Renegotiate volume pricing rather than build.
- **Repair:** if the partner misses a remediable service or contract requirement, invoke SLA remedies and set a 30-day cure plan. Do not attribute a routing, pharmacy-process, or local integration defect to the supplier.
- **Dual-source:** qualify the alternate if the current supplier has two severity-one incidents in 60 days, misses SLA in two consecutive months, announces a price increase above 25%, or cannot meet a required customer/data-control term, while neither incident nor economics yet justifies ownership.
- **Integrate/build:** return for board approval only if at least 100,000 monthly dispatches are contractually committed for 18 months, at least 70% of that volume is portable, the fully loaded owned case has a conservative payback within 24 months and remains cheaper under a 50% volume downside, and at least five paid owners make the missing control a purchase or renewal condition. Release still requires current legal permission, safety review, human fallback, incident response, load testing, and an SLA at least as strong as the partner's.
- **Stop:** cancel owned-dispatch work if the build gate fails or the capability does not affect paid purchase, retention, safety, or supplier leverage. Retain the adapter and measurements.

Rejected now are indefinite flat $500 pricing, free pilots, the offered two-year exclusivity, an unfunded custom integration, and the $600,000 dispatch build. Delay costs differ: waiting 90 days on pricing prolongs some margin leakage, so meter immediately; waiting on exclusivity costs only unproven reach; waiting on the build retains a $0.40 variable charge but avoids $600,000, six months of distraction, and reliability risk. At day 90, product owns an accepted package rollout, growth owns only channels that pass economics, engineering owns partner/repair/dual-source actions, and the board owns any later capital-heavy build.
