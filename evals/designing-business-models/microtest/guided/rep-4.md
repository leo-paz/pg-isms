## Decision now

For the next 90 days, keep pharmacies as the payer, replace the unmetered flat price for new sales with a metered offer, continue founder-led direct selling, and test the wholesaler only through a non-exclusive, performance-gated pilot. Do not authorize the $600,000 dispatch build. Continue with the SLA-backed API while measuring its full economics, negotiating volume terms, and qualifying one alternative.

This is deliberately provisional. Twelve of 18 paid pilots renewing is useful willingness-to-pay evidence, but it is only 66.7% site renewal after ten weeks, not durable retention. The nine-point on-time improvement occurred at renewed sites; it cannot be represented as a causal result for all customers until the six non-renewers and differences in volume, courier, geography, and implementation are analyzed.

## Business-model record

**Product and state.** This is capital-light, workflow-critical B2B software with enterprise/integration and regulated, potentially high-harm characteristics because prescription delivery can affect patients. The product is past free-interest validation: 18 sites paid $500 per month, deployed for ten weeks, and 12 renewed. It is not yet at repeatable economics or channel scale. The binding uncertainty is contribution margin and price acceptance by volume segment, followed by whether the wholesaler can produce paid deployments. The tests are reversible within 90 days; a two-year exclusive contract and a six-month internal build are costly to reverse. Pricing evidence can arrive in 30–60 days, while durable retention and patient effects take longer.

Before day 15, verify cash runway; monthly deliveries at each of the 18 sites; whether the $0.40 fee applies to every delivery; compute, support, implementation, and sales hours by site; invoice collection; technician activation; renewal term; expansion; courier mix; late-delivery definitions; exceptions and patient complaints; security and pharmacy/courier permissions; and the six owners' stated and observed reasons for not renewing. Separate product failure from courier failure and never infer willingness to pay from compliments, a demo, an introduction, or an unsigned order.

| Segment/path | Beneficiary, user, buyer, payer, channel | Value event and alternative | Evidence and unknowns | Economic shape |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pharmacies at or below 2,500 monthly deliveries | Patients and the pharmacy benefit; technicians use; owners buy and the pharmacy pays; initially direct | More prescriptions delivered on time with fewer manual routing interventions; alternatives are manual planning, courier tools, or another routing vendor | Paid pilot and some renewal are real; segment-specific renewal, deployment, technician use, outcome, budget, and expansion are unknown | Monthly subscription plus metered deliveries; API, compute, support, onboarding, and founder sales must be attributed; likely lower support exposure than the large segment, but this must be measured |
| Pharmacies above 2,500 monthly deliveries | Same actors; direct or wholesaler-sourced | Same event, potentially at greater scale | These sites produce 60% of compute and support cost, but their count, revenue share, renewal, route density, and incremental value are unknown | The current $500 flat price structurally undercharges this segment. If every delivery incurs $0.40, API cost alone exceeds $1,000 above the threshold before compute or support |
| Wholesaler path | Pharmacy remains buyer and payer; wholesaler is a lead/distribution channel, not proof of demand | Lower acquisition friction and trusted access versus founder-led outreach | “Access to 600” is not an activation, paid deployment, or retention commitment | Revenue share, integration, support, concentration, sales effort, and exclusivity all reduce margin and option value |

The budget owner is the pharmacy owner; the budget category and approval ceiling are unknown and must be recorded in owner calls. Revenue should be recognized/collected monthly from the pharmacy, with implementation billed separately. Courier charges should remain separately identifiable. Switching risk includes workflow retraining, integrations, delivery history, and provider dependence. The startup must retain the customer contract, route/job schema, outcome logs, billing data, and the right to export and change dispatch suppliers, subject to lawful patient-data permissions.

**Predeclared interpretations.** A price counts as accepted only when an authorized owner signs, pays the first invoice, and the site deploys within 30 days. A renewal counts only when payment continues after the initial term. Technician activity without owner payment is workflow evidence, not buyer evidence. A wholesaler introduction is a lead, not revenue. Analyze outcomes and churn by volume, baseline on-time rate, courier, geography, route density, seasonality, implementation quality, and technician adoption. If non-renewers had worse deployment or couriers, diagnose that rather than relabeling all churn as price resistance. Claims during the test may say “95% at renewed pilot sites,” not “95% for pharmacies.”

## Days 1–30: establish economics and put bounded offers in market

1. By day 10, create a site-level ledger for all 18 pilots: collected revenue; deliveries; the $0.40 API charge; compute; support and implementation hours valued at a loaded hourly rate; sales effort; gross contribution; renewal; and outcome/safety measures. Allocate shared costs by a declared rule and show results both with and without founder labor. The finance/operations owner and engineering lead sign off on the ledger.
2. By day 15, Learning from Users completes structured interviews with the owner and lead technician at all six non-renewers and a matched six renewers. Ask about the last purchase decision, budget, alternatives, operational failures, and price, but prefer invoices, usage, and deployment behavior over stated enthusiasm.
3. Instrument completed deliveries, retries, manual overrides, latency, courier/SLA incidents, on-time outcomes, support time, compute, and API cost. Maintain human override, rollback, incident response, access controls, and current legal/security review before adding pharmacies or changing dispatch behavior. Do not expose patient data to the wholesaler merely to accelerate a pilot.
4. Quote new direct prospects one standard offer: **$750 per pharmacy per month, plus $1.00 per completed delivery, plus a $1,500 one-time implementation fee**. Sites above 2,500 deliveries add a **$750 monthly Scale support fee**. Courier transportation charges, if any, remain separate. The offer is monthly for the first 90 days and includes an export/right-to-switch clause. It is a price test, not a claim that the final price is optimal.
5. Apply an economic floor before accepting a quote: expected contribution margin after API, compute, attributed support, and revenue share must be at least 60%. If the ledger shows the $1.00 usage rate cannot meet that floor, set the rate to at least `(API cost per delivery + variable compute/support per delivery) / 0.40`; the base and Scale fees must cover site-fixed service costs. Do not hide negative unit economics inside a flat “enterprise” price.

Keep the 12 renewed sites at $500 through day 90 so the test does not destroy the retention cohort. Show them a shadow invoice and, on day 60, ask the owner to choose the new metered plan beginning after day 90 or a six-month transition plan. No customer is grandfathered indefinitely.

The direct pricing test needs 12 qualified owner decisions, six in each volume segment. Cap company exposure at 12 implementations, $18,000 of waived implementation fees in total, and two weeks of engineering work; discounts require CEO approval and are logged. Review on days 30, 60, and 85.

Pricing branches are precommitted:

- **Keep/pay:** in a segment, at least four of six owners sign, pay, and at least three deploy within 30 days; projected contribution margin is at least 60%; no critical safety incident occurs; and median on-time performance improves at least five percentage points without an increase of more than two percentage points in manual exceptions or patient complaints.
- **Change/revise:** two or three of six pay, or the margin/effects gate narrowly misses. Change one element only—base fee, usage rate, Scale fee, or implementation scope—and run six more owner decisions; do not interpret a discounted acceptance as acceptance of the original price.
- **Segment:** acceptance differs by at least 25 percentage points or contribution margin differs by at least ten points between volume groups. Maintain separate packages and sales qualification rather than averaging them.
- **Grandfather:** a renewed customer rejects the new plan but remains healthy at the old price. Offer a documented transition of no more than six months only if its contribution is non-negative and it supplies the data needed to decide the segment.
- **Preserve-free:** no production routing is free. A maximum 14-day, non-production data validation may be used solely to observe technician fit; it cannot count as price acceptance.
- **Stop:** zero or one of six in a segment pays an economically viable offer after one revision, or contribution margin remains below 40%. Stop selling that segment. Stop the entire offer if neither segment clears the paid-deployment and patient-effects gates.

## Days 15–75: compare direct sales with a protected channel test

Continue founder-led direct sales to owners, using a technician as the operational champion and involving the incumbent courier before deployment. Prioritize pharmacies with 1,000–5,000 monthly deliveries so both economic segments are represented. Track owner meetings, signed-and-paid conversion, days to deployment, implementation hours, sales hours, 30/60-day active payment, outcome change, and contribution margin by cohort. No scalable paid acquisition begins before one segment clears the pricing gate.

Reject the wholesaler proposal as written. A 25% share of all revenue, custom work, and two years of exclusivity combines margin loss, fixed cost, concentration, and blocked alternatives before the channel has produced one paying site. Counter with a 90-day, non-exclusive pilot covering 30 named, opt-in pharmacies:

- The wholesaler supplies 30 warm owner introductions by day 30. Revenue share is 25% only of collected platform and Scale subscription revenue from attributable accounts; exclude taxes, refunds, implementation, courier charges, and API/usage pass-through. Pay no share on the existing 18 sites.
- The wholesaler funds the custom integration through a non-refundable fee. Startup engineering exposure is capped at two engineer-weeks; anything larger pauses the pilot. The integration must use the normal interface, preserve exportability, and pass security, permission, monitoring, and rollback review.
- Customer contracts, pharmacy relationships, product data, and pricing remain with the startup. The wholesaler receives only data necessary for its channel role.
- No exclusivity is granted in the pilot. After six months, limited exclusivity could be reconsidered only for named territory/accounts with minimums of 15 paid live sites by day 90, 40 by month six, and 75 by month 12; a miss automatically converts the arrangement to non-exclusive. There is no company-wide or two-year lock without a minimum revenue guarantee that covers foregone direct contribution and integration cost.

The channel passes the day-85 gate only if it produces at least 20 introductions, 12 owner meetings, eight qualified opportunities, five signed-and-paid pharmacies, and four deployments; projected contribution margin after the share is at least 60%; sales-and-integration acquisition cost pays back within 12 months; deployment time is no worse than 25% above direct; and support hours per site are no worse than the matched direct cohort. If it passes, expand non-exclusively. If leads are strong but integration or economics miss, revise scope/share. If introductions or paid deployments miss, continue direct and stop custom work. Access, publicity, or an exclusivity promise never substitutes for these results.

## Days 1–90: partner on dispatch while preserving a build option

The simple build comparison already argues against immediate integration. At $0.40 per delivery, $600,000 equals **1.5 million deliveries**. To recover that fixed cost in the six-month build window would require **250,000 deliveries per month**, ignoring the API fees still paid during construction, internal cloud cost, maintenance, on-call coverage, security/compliance, hiring, defects, and the opportunity cost of diverting an 11-person company. The partner also supplies an SLA, so an internal system must be compared with the SLA and operating burden, not with variable price alone.

By day 30, the CTO must produce a boundary ledger with current and forecast monthly volume, actual provider invoices, SLA performance, incidents, rate limits, roadmap blocks, integration/support hours, and a credible internal run-cost estimate. The finance owner calculates post-launch payback as `600,000 / (monthly deliveries × (0.40 - internal variable cost per delivery))`; if internal variable cost is at least $0.40, the build never saves variable cost. The model must include fees paid during the six-month build and at least two years of maintenance and on-call cost.

By day 45, negotiate volume tiers, audit/incident rights, data export, termination assistance, service credits, and price notice with the current partner. In parallel, qualify one alternate provider against a fixed test corpus in a sandbox; do not send live patient data without proper permission and review. Keep an internal provider-neutral job interface, immutable request/result logs, explicit error ownership, and a manual fallback. These measures distinguish bad pharmacy inputs, startup orchestration defects, and supplier failures and make replacement possible.

Boundary branches at day 85 are:

- **Partner (selected default):** retain the current API if it meets its SLA, economics produce an acceptable priced offer, and no material control or patient-safety requirement is blocked.
- **Repair:** after a material breach with a credible root cause and cure date, enforce the SLA and retest for 30 days.
- **Dual-source:** qualify and route bounded traffic to the alternate if there are two material SLA breaches in 30 days, unacceptable concentration, or the current provider will not grant export/termination protections.
- **Integrate:** authorize an internal build only if committed volume and measured internal run cost produce payback within 18 months; two qualified suppliers cannot meet a documented control, quality, or customer-capture requirement; a named team can own 24/7 operations, security, and compliance; and spending $600,000 leaves at least 18 months of base-case cash runway. The board must approve the opportunity cost.
- **Stop:** do not build if payback exceeds 24 months, the capability/safety gate fails, or the priced customer segment cannot support the cost. Stop serving a use case rather than silently accepting unsafe dispatch.

## Day 90 decision

The CEO presents one cohort table and selects branches, not a blended narrative. Expansion requires paid deployment, at least 80% of newly deployed sites still active and paid at day 60, a median improvement of at least five on-time percentage points, no serious patient-safety event, and at least 60% projected contribution margin. The chosen plan records rejected alternatives, owner, review date, and switch condition.

Absent contrary test results, the day-90 plan is: keep metered pricing for the segment that clears the gates; transition the original 12 with bounded grandfathering; scale direct sales first; continue only a non-exclusive, performance-qualified wholesaler channel; and partner or dual-source dispatch rather than build it. This preserves the valuable options—the customer relationship, provider-neutral data/interface, direct channel, and ability to build later—while avoiding two premature irreversible commitments.
