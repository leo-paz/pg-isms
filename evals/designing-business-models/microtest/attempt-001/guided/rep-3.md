# 90-day business-model decision plan

**Primary owner:** Designing business models. The immediate decisions are capture and packaging, channel economics, and the company boundary around dispatch. If a day-1 cash audit shows the company is default-dead or has less time than these tests require, managing runway and survival takes primary ownership and sets the switch date first.

**Handoffs:** (1) Learning from users verifies owner willingness to pay, nonrenewal causes, technician workflow, and patient effects under the economic test below; (2) shipping and iterating products implements metering, billing, packages, and rollback; (3) acquiring and growing users runs the direct and wholesaler cohorts only after their economics are specified; (4) engineering for leverage owns cost instrumentation, the dispatch adapter, logs, and supplier portability; (5) competing and positioning verifies whether the 86%-to-95% result is differentiated and defensible; (6) raising and governing capital assesses financing only if the dispatch build later passes its economic and capability gates.

## Business-model record

### Product, state, and decision conditions

| Field | Record |
|---|---|
| Product type | Capital-light subscription software with enterprise/integration work and a high-harm, regulated delivery context. It is not presently a marketplace: courier firms are suppliers/integrations, not participant liquidity that the startup must create. |
| Company/product state | Eleven people; 18 pharmacies completed a ten-week paid pilot at $500/month, 12 renewed and 6 did not. This is real payment, deployment, and first-renewal evidence, but not yet stable retention or expansion evidence. Survival state and cash runway are unknown and must be established on day 1. |
| Binding constraint | The current constraint is not product use alone; it is whether price and channel revenue cover delivery-variable, compute, implementation, and support costs by segment. Two-year exclusivity and a $600,000 build would sharply reduce optionality before that is known. |
| Evidence latency | Offer acceptance can be seen in 2-4 weeks; paid deployment and a second invoice need roughly 6-10 weeks. Durable retention, expansion, and a six-month internal build cannot be proven inside 90 days. |
| Reversibility/error cost | A new-account price test is reversible; changing existing customers is moderately reversible; a short non-exclusive channel pilot is reversible; two-year exclusivity, a custom one-off integration, or committing $600,000 and six months are costly to reverse. A routing failure can delay medicine, so production supplier tests require human override and rollback. |
| Facts to verify by day 10 | Cash runway and time-to-zero; deliveries per account; which deliveries actually incur the $0.40 fee; compute, support, implementation, sales, assurance, and compliance cost per account; gross margin by site; renewal duration and cancellation rights; why each of six sites did not renew; effect and workflow data at all 18 sites; owner budget and alternatives; courier contract, SLA performance, data portability, volume discounts, and termination terms; current procurement, security, privacy, pharmacy, courier, and patient-data requirements in every operating jurisdiction. |

The 86%-to-95% on-time change is a nine-percentage-point observed association at renewed sites. It must be described that way, not as a causal guarantee: the six nonrenewers, route mix, courier changes, seasonality, staffing, baseline volume, and measurement method are unresolved confounders.

### Pricing and unit economics

For each site, calculate monthly collected revenue less courier API fees, compute, support labor, implementation amortization, assurance/compliance cost, payment cost, and any channel share. Show both contribution margin and fully loaded gross margin. The current price creates a crucial bound: if every delivery incurs $0.40, $500 is consumed by API fees at 1,250 deliveries before any other cost. On the wholesaler path, $500 leaves $375 after its 25% share, equal to only 937.5 deliveries of API fees. Whether that fee applies to every optimized delivery is therefore a day-3 fact, not an assumption.

| Segment/path | Willingness-to-pay and value metric | Initial offer and revenue timing | Sales, implementation, cost, switching, expansion |
|---|---|---|---|
| Existing 12 renewed sites | Payment and renewal establish willingness to pay $500, not more. Meter monthly deliveries and service burden before repricing. | Grandfather $500 for the 90-day test, with no surprise mid-contract increase. Invite at least four sites, including every available high-volume site, to sign a future two-part-price amendment only after seeing the quote and usage report. | Record owner calls, sales hours, deployment hours, support tickets, compute, and API charges. Preserve export and cancellation terms. Expansion is additional locations or delivery volume, but counts only when paid. |
| New direct small and large sites | Use monthly deliveries as the auditable cost-aligned metric; retain a platform fee for workflow value. Do not price on claimed patient outcomes until attribution is credible. | Quote one bounded test package: **$500 platform fee plus $0.80 per delivery routed, monthly in advance for the base and monthly in arrears for usage, with a 30-day opt-out.** Include at most eight implementation hours; quote excess implementation separately. This is a test price, not a conclusion. | Owners receive the quote; technicians validate use. No discount above 10% without recording it as rejection of the tested price. Every proposal contains data export, human override, and service terms. Large sites may expand, but sales pauses when projected account contribution is negative. |
| Wholesaler-sourced sites | Payment by introduced pharmacies is the evidence; an introduction is not. Use the same customer price during the comparison so channel economics are visible. | Counteroffer a 90-day, non-exclusive, named-account pilot. Pay 25% only on collected first-year revenue for genuinely sourced accounts, with no share on existing/direct accounts and no automatic renewal. Require a nonrefundable $25,000 integration fee or reduce scope to the existing reusable API. | Cap internal exposure at two engineer-weeks and $15,000 of other cost. Wholesaler supplies 20 named introductions and permission to contact owners. The pharmacy still receives normal deployment, support, security review, and switching rights. |

Predeclared pricing interpretations at day 90:

- **Keep:** at least 50% of qualified owners offered the direct test price accept without more than a 10% discount, at least 80% of acceptors deploy within 30 days, every deployed account pays its second amount due, and forecast direct gross margin is at least 60% with no negative-contribution account.
- **Segment:** one volume band clears those gates and the other does not, or support/compute differs by at least 2x after controlling for deliveries. Keep the clearing band and create a separately costed high-volume/complexity package; do not average away the loss.
- **Change:** acceptance is below 50% but interviews show a specific package, metric, or budget mismatch, or margin is 40%-59%. Test one revised structure for another 30 days; discounts count as evidence against the original price.
- **Grandfather:** preserve the 12 renewers' $500 terms through the promised period if a new package clears, then offer migration with notice. Their renewal and measured margin—not complaints alone—determine migration pacing.
- **Stop:** pause a segment or usage pattern if projected contribution remains negative, gross margin is below 40%, safety/permission gates fail, or owners will pay only below the cost floor.

For nonrenewals, precommit the interpretation before interviews: price rejection requires the owner to say the product was worth using but not at $500 or to accept a lower-priced bounded offer; product/workflow rejection means technicians did not achieve repeat use or the owner saw no credible value; deployment failure means required integration or training never completed. A discount request, praise, or continued free use is not payment.

### Company boundary: courier dispatch

| Boundary field | Record |
|---|---|
| Customer/data/standard ownership | The startup must own the pharmacy relationship, product experience, routing inputs/outputs, observability, and contractually permitted export. The supplier can execute dispatch without owning customer identity or making the product's data nonportable. Use the minimum patient data required and verify current permissions and retention requirements. |
| Coordination and dependency cost | Measure API latency, failure and retry rates, manual interventions, support incidents, missed-delivery attribution, roadmap blocks, and vendor-response time. The partner's existing SLA reduces operating risk but its actual attainment must be audited. |
| Internal capability and fixed cost | The proposed build is $600,000 and six months before adding ongoing infrastructure, maps/services, dispatch operations, on-call coverage, security, compliance, insurance, maintenance, and opportunity cost. The team has not demonstrated these capabilities. |
| Partner maturity and replaceability | An API and SLA are positive maturity evidence; financial durability, incident record, pricing power, termination rights, data export, and alternate suppliers are unknown. Qualify a second supplier rather than assuming replaceability. |
| Interface and switching path | Put the present provider behind a versioned internal adapter; retain normalized route/dispatch events, test fixtures, replay, and export; implement provider-specific code only at the edge. Build a sandbox integration to a second provider and a runbook for failover. |
| Failure attribution | For every incident, log whether the route plan, startup adapter, supplier API, courier execution, pharmacy input, or patient availability failed. Compare against the contracted SLA. Repair architecture defects internally; enforce or replace suppliers for supplier defects. |

The selected 90-day branch is **partner and qualify a dual source**, not the $600,000 build. Even with an impossible zero internal variable cost, the build needs 1.5 million deliveries merely to equal $0.40 API spend before ongoing cost or time value. At a 24-month payback that is 62,500 deliveries per month; with internal variable cost `c`, the threshold is `600,000 / (24 x (0.40 - c))`, plus the omitted ongoing and risk costs. Current committed volume is unknown.

Run a bounded boundary test through day 75: the CTO owns it; cap it at one engineer for four weeks and $20,000 total cash; instrument at least 10,000 production deliveries if normal authorized traffic reaches that number (otherwise use all normal traffic, never manufacture patient exposure); solicit two alternate suppliers; exercise one sandbox/replay integration; and obtain the current partner's volume-price, export, incident, and termination terms. No unproven provider receives live patient traffic. Guards are current legal and contractual permission, security review, minimum necessary data, human dispatch override, monitoring, rollback, incident response, and accountable pharmacy approval.

At day 75, apply these branches:

- **Partner:** keep the current supplier if SLA attainment is at least 99.9%, supplier-attributable failures affect fewer than 0.1% of deliveries, its effective fee plus internal coordination cost beats qualified alternatives, and no customer/data-control gate fails.
- **Dual-source:** add the qualified alternate if it passes security and replay tests, switching can occur within one business day, and expected avoided downtime or negotiated savings repays integration within 12 months.
- **Repair:** fix the internal adapter if more than half of measured technical failures are startup-attributable; do not call that supplier failure.
- **Integrate/build:** authorize a full internal design phase only if signed customer commitments produce volume above the fully loaded 24-month break-even threshold, partner dependence causes more than 0.5 percentage points of on-time failure or blocks paid requirements worth at least $300,000 annually, the team has named dispatch/security/on-call owners, and the $600,000 outlay leaves the survival-plan minimum runway intact. Financing interest is not cash.
- **Stop:** halt an alternate or internal path if permission, assurance, rollback, or patient-effect gates fail. Do not expose customers to a boundary experiment to rescue sunk cost.

The preserved option is an internal dispatch build later; the switch condition is the integrate/build gate above. The cost of delaying it 90 days is at most measured API spend and partner incidents during the test, while the benefit is avoiding a six-month commitment before volume and failure attribution are known.

## Sales and channel decision

Do not accept the wholesaler offer as written. Twenty-five percent of revenue may be viable only after pricing absorbs it; two-year exclusivity would surrender the direct option before the channel proves paid deployments; and a one-off integration creates fixed cost and switching dependence. The counteroffer above preserves access while making the wholesaler fund or narrow the integration.

Run matched direct and wholesaler cohorts. By day 15, select 20 qualified direct prospects and require 20 named wholesaler introductions, stratified by delivery band. By day 45, target at least 12 owner meetings, six written offers, and four paid acceptances in each path. By day 75, require deployment and a second amount due where timing permits. Track collected revenue, owner-to-paid conversion, days to deployment, sales and integration hours, support load, delivery volume, churn/opt-out, margin after revenue share, and patient/technician effects.

At day 90:

- Expand the channel only if it produces at least four paid deployments, at least 80% deploy within 30 days, forecast gross margin after the share is at least 50%, channel acquisition payback is at most six months, safety gates pass, and conversion is at least 1.5x the matched direct cohort. Permit no more than six months of narrow exclusivity, and only for named accounts or a defined region, after those gates pass and with minimum paid-volume and termination clauses.
- Revise the channel if paid conversion clears but margin is 40%-49%, usually by reducing the share, limiting its duration, raising the channel price, or obtaining a guaranteed minimum. The pharmacy payer and customer/data relationship remain explicit.
- Continue direct only if the channel misses paid-deployment, margin, or payback gates. Preserve the wholesaler as a referral source without exclusivity.
- Stop the channel if it demands two-year exclusivity before proof, refuses named-account attribution or customer access, will not fund/narrow the custom work, or creates unsafe deployment pressure.

## 90-day operating calendar and final decision gate

**Days 1-15 — establish the floor.** The CEO/CFO establishes runway and the per-account cost ledger. Product instruments deliveries, costs, interventions, and source attribution. Learning-from-users conducts owner and technician interviews across all 18 sites, deliberately including all six nonrenewers, and verifies alternatives and budget without treating praise as payment. Security/operations verify current law, permission, procurement, switching, monitoring, rollback, human review, and incident response before further exposure. Sales issues no scalable-channel or discounted offer yet.

**Days 16-45 — put bounded offers in market.** Sales sends the direct two-part-price offers and the wholesaler runs only the non-exclusive named-account pilot. Product implements metering and billing; implementation and support hours are capped and logged. Engineering instruments the current supplier, requests commercial terms, and begins the portable adapter and alternate-provider replay. Weekly review checks negative-contribution and patient-harm stop conditions.

**Days 46-75 — require behavior beyond signatures.** Deploy accepted offers, collect usage charges and second amounts due, and measure opt-outs. Compare matched direct/channel cohorts and small/large economics. Finish supplier qualification and classify incidents. Do not equate an undeployed contract, wholesaler introduction, or financing proposal with economic evidence.

**Days 76-90 — choose and hand off.** Apply the predeclared pricing, channel, and boundary branches without averaging segments. The recommended decision absent contrary evidence is: keep existing customers grandfathered; use delivery-metered pricing for new sales only where margin gates clear; reject the wholesaler's current exclusivity and custom-work terms, continuing only the gated pilot; and retain the SLA-backed partner behind a portable interface while qualifying an alternate. Reject for now flat $500 pricing at every volume, outcome pricing, immediate channel exclusivity, an unfunded custom integration, and the $600,000 in-house build.

For each selected branch, preserve export, direct sales, a second supplier, and the later build option. Sales/growth owns a clearing direct or channel cohort; product owns a clearing package rollout; engineering owns partner, dual-source, or repair results; learning owns any failed willingness-to-pay or effect interpretation; survival owns any runway breach; and capital/governance receives the internal-build case only after committed volume, capability, payback, and runway gates all clear.
