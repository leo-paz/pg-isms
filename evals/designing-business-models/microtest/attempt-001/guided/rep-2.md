## Business-Model Record

### Product, state, and constraints

This is capital-light, workflow-critical B2B software with an enterprise/integration component and high-harm, regulated user effects: late or failed prescription delivery can affect patients. The company is 11 people and has early paid evidence, not repeatable economics: 18 pharmacies paid $500 per month for a ten-week pilot, 12 renewed and 6 did not. Pilot-to-renewal retention is 67%; current renewed-cohort MRR is $6,000 before any discounts or revenue share. Renewed sites improved on-time delivery from 86% to 95%, a 9 percentage-point association, but there is no evidence here that the product caused all of it or that nonrenewers had the same effect.

The binding 90-day constraints are weak willingness-to-pay evidence above $500, unmeasured fully loaded account margin, cost concentration in large pharmacies, and dependence on both a courier API and a proposed exclusive channel. Pricing and partner tests are reversible; a two-year exclusive deal, custom integration, or six-month $600,000 build is slow and costly to reverse. A patient-impacting dispatch failure has high error cost, so live provider changes require permission, assurance, monitoring, human fallback, incident response, and rollback.

Facts to verify by day 14 are: delivery volume and current API usage by account; compute, support, implementation, sales, assurance, and compliance cost by account; discounts and actual cash collection; renewal term and expansion; why each of the 6 pharmacies left; effect and baseline for all 18 sites; late-delivery attribution; pharmacy budget owner and alternative costs; procurement, security, data ownership, switching, and deployment requirements; partner SLA performance and credits; current pharmacy, prescription-delivery, patient-data, courier, and subcontracting requirements in every operating jurisdiction. Finance owns cost and cash data, product owns outcome instrumentation, sales owns buyer evidence, engineering owns API evidence, and qualified counsel owns current legal verification as of the test date.

Payment means collected cash under a signed production agreement, not praise, an introduction, a pilot promise, or an undeployed contract. Paid deployment means technicians use the product on live routes after required procurement, security, training, and integration. Renewal means collected payment for a new term after the initial commitment. Retention is reported by pharmacy and by delivery volume; expansion means added sites, deliveries, or contracted recurring revenue without a compensating discount. Before the test, interpret a refusal tied to price after verified value as a willingness-to-pay signal; refusal caused by missing integration, security, or poor outcomes is a product/deployment signal; discount-only acceptance is evidence at the discounted price; nonuse after signing is not validated demand; churn with good measured outcomes is a capture or buyer-fit problem, while churn with weak outcomes is a value problem.

### Pricing and unit economics

Use completed monthly deliveries as the provisional value metric because it scales with customer activity and the $0.40 supplier cost. Do not immediately reprice the 12 renewers. Grandfather their $500 price through day 90 in exchange for cost metering and structured review, and disclose the future package test plainly.

For new production offers during days 22–60, test one transparent package: a $750 monthly minimum including 500 completed deliveries, then $1.50 per additional completed delivery, with onboarding separately quoted at verified cost and no more than a three-month initial term. At 2,500 deliveries this produces $3,750 revenue against at least $1,000 of stated API cost, leaving 73% before compute, support, implementation, sales, assurance, and compliance. That is a hypothesis, not a margin claim. Do not offer annual discounts until a fully loaded contribution margin is known. Cap any test discount at 15%, record it separately, and never exchange a discount for exclusivity.

By day 14, calculate monthly account contribution as collected revenue minus courier API, compute, support labor, implementation amortized over the committed term, third-party services, and assurance/compliance cost. Also report sales effort and acquisition cost below contribution rather than hiding them. The initial acceptance gate is at least 65% gross margin before acquisition cost and at least 50% contribution after the wholesaler share, using observed—not list—revenue and current costs. Concentration is reported as the largest account and largest five accounts' shares of revenue, delivery volume, support, and contribution. Switching risk includes data export, route history, courier credentials, staff retraining, and contract termination.

Run the bounded offer test with 12 qualified new pharmacies: 6 at or below 2,500 deliveries and 6 above it. Sales owns it; maximum exposure is 60 hours of sales/implementation time, $10,000 of unbilled integration work, and no bespoke feature. Require a signed three-month production order and first collected payment by day 60. Acceptance is at least 3 of 6 in each segment at no more than the 15% discount, live deployment within 21 days, and projected account gross margin of at least 65%. Rejection is 0 or 1 paid acceptance in a segment after at least 5 qualified owners receive the offer. Two acceptances is inconclusive and permits one revised test, not a rollout. Review confounders—source, volume, missing integration, procurement delay, outcome baseline, geography, and courier—on days 30 and 60.

Precommitted pricing branches at day 60 are: **keep** the package in a segment that clears acceptance, deployment, and margin gates; **change** once if owners validate value but price objections dominate and margin remains at least 65%; **segment** if the two volume cohorts differ by at least 25 percentage points in paid acceptance or 15 margin points; **grandfather** the 12 renewers for one additional contracted term if a sharp migration would obscure retention, while adding no new flat-price accounts; **stop** selling a segment if it cannot clear the margin floor without an unvalidated price or if patient outcomes fail the safety gate. Payer branches are **pay** when the owner signs, deploys, and pays; **revise** for a different owner-controlled budget only after interviews show that owner is not the budget holder; **preserve-free** only for at most two instrumented design partners with no production promise and an end date; **stop** when neither the owner nor a verified alternative payer makes an equivalent commitment after the bounded retest.

### Sales and wholesaler channel

Direct founder-led selling remains the control motion for 90 days. Target pharmacies by monthly delivery band, require owner participation before technical work, and record discovery hours, sales cycle, deployment hours, collected revenue, and 30-day live use. Do not scale acquisition while price and contribution are unresolved.

Reject the wholesaler's offered bundle now: 25% revenue share plus two-year exclusivity plus custom integration combines uncertain acquisition economics, lock-in, engineering exposure, and channel concentration. Counter with a nonexclusive, terminable 90-day referral test: at least 20 named introductions, no custom product work, one documented standard interface, the wholesaler funding any unique integration, and a 15% share of net collected subscription revenue from referred accounts for their first 12 months only. No share applies to preexisting accounts, taxes, pass-through courier charges, or revenue after month 12. Customer contracts, pricing, data rights, support relationship, and renewal remain with the startup.

Acquiring-and-growing-users owns the cohort. By day 75 the channel must yield at least 20 owner-authorized introductions, 10 qualified opportunities, 6 signed production accounts, and 4 live, paying accounts; fully loaded acquisition payback must be no more than 6 months and contribution after share at least 50%. Maximum startup exposure is $15,000 and 80 staff hours. Compare the cohort with contemporaneous direct leads on paid conversion, days to deployment, support, margin, retention proxy, and outcome. Confounders include brand trust, lead selection, territory, pharmacy size, sales assistance, and integration readiness.

Channel branches at day 75 are: proceed to a nonexclusive one-year agreement only if all gates clear; revise only the referral share or introduction obligation if one misses narrowly and direct economics remain sound; keep direct sales if channel payback or contribution misses; stop if the wholesaler requires exclusivity, customer/data ownership, unbounded integration, or control of pricing. Two-year exclusivity is reconsidered only after at least two comparable channel cohorts, never on projected access to 600 pharmacies. This preserves the wholesaler option without surrendering other channels.

### Courier dispatch boundary

Choose **partner, with a replaceable interface and qualified backup**, for this 90-day period. The current partner supplies a mature API for $0.40 per delivery and an SLA; the proposed in-house system requires $600,000 and six months before learning whether it can safely replace that service. The apparent fee break-even is 1.5 million deliveries ($600,000 divided by $0.40), but that ignores build delay, 30% contingency, ongoing dispatch operations, monitoring, incident response, compliance, uptime, and the fact that an internal system cannot eliminate all per-delivery cost. The unproven 600-pharmacy channel cannot justify irreversible capacity.

The startup should own the pharmacy relationship, product data permitted by contract and law, outcome definitions, provider-neutral route request/result schema, monitoring, and export path. The specialist should own courier connectivity and dispatch operations under SLA. Engineering must document credential portability, data return/deletion, termination, SLA credits, rate limits, and a switch path. By day 30, request volume tiers and stronger credits from the incumbent and qualify one alternate provider. Cap the provider adapter, telemetry, and shadow qualification at four engineer-weeks and $25,000.

By day 60, run at least 1,000 replayed or simulated routes through the alternate. Only after current legal/contractual permission, security review, and a no-harm check may it handle at most 10% of live routes at two pharmacies, with technician confirmation, real-time monitoring, an incumbent fallback, incident response, and one-click rollback. Attribute failures with request/response IDs and timestamps: malformed requests, internal timeouts, or bad route rules are architecture failures; accepted valid requests that miss the provider contract or SLA are supplier failures. Compare route acceptance, dispatch latency, on-time rate, failure/retry rate, support minutes, and cost per completed delivery. Stop live exposure immediately for a serious patient-impact incident or an on-time decline greater than 2 percentage points versus the same sites' four-week incumbent baseline.

Boundary branches at day 75 are: **partner** if the incumbent meets SLA and the build gate is absent; **dual-source** if the alternate completes the test with at least 99.9% successful API transactions, no more than a 2-point on-time decline, no serious incident, and a fully loaded cost no higher than $0.40 per delivery; **repair** the internal architecture if failures originate there, then rerun simulation; **stop** an alternate that fails permission, security, safety, or reliability; **integrate/build** only when all of the following are evidenced: at least 175,000 monthly deliveries contractually committed for 12 months, validated ongoing internal operating cost no more than $0.15 per delivery, expected net savings at least $43,333 per month, a 30%-contingency investment of $780,000 paying back within 18 months after launch, sufficient staff to operate the service without displacing the core roadmap, current legal and assurance approval, and post-investment runway of at least 18 months. Raising-and-governing-capital and survival owners must verify that last gate; projected wholesaler volume does not count.

## 90-day decision calendar

- **Days 0–14:** Instrument account cost and outcome cohorts; reconcile collected revenue; interview all 6 nonrenewers and a stratified set of 6 renewers; verify procurement, security, data, switching, and current regulatory requirements; calculate margins by volume band. Preserve direct sales but make no new $500 flat offers.
- **Days 15–30:** Launch the 12-account price test, state the grandfather policy, negotiate the bounded wholesaler counteroffer, measure incumbent SLA and failure attribution, request volume pricing, and select an alternate for simulation.
- **Days 31–60:** Collect first payments and deploy price-test accounts; run standard-interface channel work only if the wholesaler accepts the guards; complete at least 1,000 alternate-provider simulations. At day 60 execute the payer and pricing branches using the declared thresholds.
- **Days 61–75:** Measure 30-day use, outcomes, support, and contribution; run only the guarded live provider test; compare direct and referred cohorts. At day 75 execute channel and boundary branches.
- **Days 76–90:** Shipping-and-iterating-products implements the winning billing package; growth documents the repeatable segment and motion; engineering finishes provider portability and monitoring; finance publishes account/cohort contribution. The founders approve only decisions that met the precommitted gates.

The selected working branches are usage-linked pricing test, direct sales plus a guarded nonexclusive referral test, and partner/dual-source rather than build. Rejected now are permanent $500 flat pricing, the wholesaler's bundled exclusivity, and the $600,000 build. Preserved options are one-term grandfathering, a later performance-based wholesaler agreement, and eventual in-house dispatch after committed scale and capability exist. Delay costs are continued losses on large accounts, roughly $500 per month of price shortfall against API cost alone at 2,500 deliveries under the stated usage assumption, and ongoing supplier dependence; rushing costs are avoidable churn, channel lock-in, six months of roadmap displacement, and patient-impact risk. At day 90, retain only a segment whose paid retention/effect evidence is credible, gross margin is at least 65%, and on-time performance has not fallen more than 2 points or produced a serious incident; otherwise the next owner is learning-from-users for value failure, business-models for capture or margin failure, growth for a cleared channel, engineering for architecture failure, or building-and-evolving-organizations plus capital/survival owners for a future build gate.
