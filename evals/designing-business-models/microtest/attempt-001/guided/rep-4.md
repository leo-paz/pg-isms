# 90-day business-model decision plan

**Primary owner:** designing-business-models.

**Handoffs (in order):** (1) `managing-runway-and-survival` first only if a day-3 cash review shows that the company is default-dead or cannot safely fund the 90-day tests; (2) `learning-from-users` to run owner, technician, courier, renewal, and nonrenewal evidence collection; (3) `acquiring-and-growing-users` to execute the qualified direct and wholesaler cohorts; (4) `shipping-and-iterating-products` to implement offers, billing, entitlements, and rollout; (5) `engineering-for-leverage` to add cost metering, isolate the dispatch interface, and instrument reliability; (6) `competing-and-positioning` to address wholesaler and courier-platform dependence; (7) `communicating-clearly` to constrain outcome claims; and (8) `raising-and-governing-capital` only if the selected boundary later creates a justified financing need.

## 90-day execution and tests

### Days 0–14: establish economics and safeguards

- CEO owns the business-model record and freezes both the offered wholesaler contract and the dispatch build. Finance/operations produces site-level cohort P&Ls and confirms runway by day 3. CTO meters delivery/API/compute/support cost and separates optimization from dispatch by day 10. Customer success, under `learning-from-users`, interviews the owner and technician at all 18 sites and the lead courier at a minimum of 12, explicitly covering the six nonrenewals.
- Reconstruct on-time performance for all 18 sites and predeclare the common numerator, denominator, baseline, and incident definitions. No site enters a new commercial cohort without current permission, security, rollback, human review, and incident-response checks appropriate to prescription delivery.
- Classify service work: configuration that can become a repeatable product stays; recurring manual route intervention or uncapped custom support is priced as service or stopped. Publish the two standard packages internally, metering and invoicing the courier charge separately.

### Days 15–45: run the bounded pricing and direct-sales test

Founder-led sales will quote the package above, without discretionary discounts, to **16 qualified new pharmacies: eight Standard and eight Scale**. Qualification requires the stated volume band, owner attendance, technician champion, courier readiness, baseline delivery data, and authority to begin within 21 days. Test budget is capped at **$12,000** of loaded sales, onboarding, and engineering time; exposure is limited to these 16 sites and the standard adapter.

For each segment, precommit:

- **Keep:** at least 5/8 sign at list price, pay onboarding plus the first invoice within 15 days, and at least 4/8 deploy within 21 days; projected software gross margin meets 70% Standard or 65% Scale.
- **Change once:** 3–4/8 pay and the recorded objections cluster around the same package boundary rather than lack of value; revise one term or package, not both price and qualification, and test against four additional same-segment prospects.
- **Segment:** acceptance differs by at least 25 percentage points between bands or measured support/compute differs by at least 2x per delivery; maintain separate prices and service limits.
- **Grandfather:** preserve the existing 12 only through their current committed term or the 90-day migration window; report them separately from new-price acceptance.
- **Stop:** 0–2/8 pay, or deployed sites cannot meet the relevant margin/effects/safety gates. Do not explain rejection away with compliments, LOIs, discounts, or wholesaler interest.

At day 45, the direct motion proceeds only if at least eight total prospects paid at list price, six deployed, median sales cycle is no more than 30 days, standard implementation stayed within its hour cap for at least 80% of sites, and no unresolved high-severity safety/privacy incident exists. Otherwise narrow the segment or package before adding volume; do not hire a sales team.

### Days 15–75: counter with a channel experiment, not exclusivity

Reject the wholesaler offer as written. Counter with a **90-day, nonexclusive referral pilot** covering **30 named pharmacies**, direct startup-pharmacy contracts, no most-favored-nation clause, no future exclusivity, no custom product fork, and a **15% share of collected software subscription revenue for the first 12 months of each referred account**. The wholesaler prepays any approved standard connector work above the 80-hour cap. Limit company test cost to **$20,000**, including loaded integration and channel time.

Precommit the branches at day 75:

- **Integrate/continue:** the wholesaler accepts the guards and produces at least 20 owner-authorized introductions, 12 qualified meetings, six list-price paid accounts, and four deployments; channel contribution margin after share is at least 60%, CAC payback is at most six months, and median deployment is at most 21 days.
- **Repair once:** at least three pay but a single measured integration or qualification defect blocks the gate; allow one 30-day correction with the same economics and no exclusivity.
- **Partner without integration:** leads convert but connector usage is unnecessary; retain referral terms and remove custom work.
- **Stop:** the wholesaler requires 25%, exclusivity, uncontrolled custom work/data rights, or misses the payment gate. Preserve founder-led direct sales and the ability to test other wholesalers.

The channel is not allowed to hide discounts, combine its cohort with direct conversion, or receive credit for an introduction without collected payment and deployment. The 25% share could be reconsidered only after observed cohort economics show the same margin and payback gates; access to 600 pharmacies alone is not such evidence.

### Days 0–90: partner for dispatch while creating a real switch option

Do **not** authorize the $600,000 in-house dispatch build in this window. On purchase price alone, the build equals **1.5 million deliveries** at $0.40 each. It would need **62,500 deliveries per month for 24 months** merely to equal $600,000, before internal variable cost, maintenance, reliability coverage, compliance, delay, or cost of capital. The current evidence supplies neither that durable volume nor proof that owning dispatch improves customer capture or quality enough to offset the burden.

The CTO instead owns a **$25,000-capped partner/dual-source test**: validate invoice-to-delivery counts, exercise the incumbent SLA and fallback, negotiate volume tiers, complete a non-production adapter against one alternate by day 45, and run synthetic failure, retry, export, rollback, privacy, and incident drills by day 60. No live prescription traffic moves to the alternate without accountable pharmacy approval and assurance review.

At day 90, apply these precommitted boundary branches:

- **Partner:** remain with the incumbent if SLA, safety, portability, and economics pass and switching is demonstrated.
- **Dual-source:** qualify limited live volume only if concentration risk is material and the alternate independently passes the same safety, permission, reliability, and total-cost gates.
- **Repair:** require a dated remediation plan and service credits for a correctable incumbent SLA/interface failure; switch if it misses the date.
- **Integrate/build:** commission a separate staged plan only after three consecutive months of measured dispatch volume and a downside model show a **24-month-or-shorter payback** using `(build cost + 24 months of internal fixed operating cost) / (partner price per delivery - internal variable cost per delivery)`, at **50% of forecast volume**, while a staffed team demonstrates SLA parity, 24/7 incident response, compliance, security, rollback, and customer/data-control benefit. Board approval and runway after the $600,000 outlay are additional gates.
- **Stop dispatch scope:** if neither provider can meet permission, safety, or reliability requirements, keep route optimization and require pharmacies to contract dispatch independently rather than internalizing unsafe operations.

| Decision | Selected branch now | Retention/effects gate | Rejected alternative and preserved option | Switch condition, delay cost, and next owner |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pricing | Test segmented, itemized Standard and Scale offers; grandfather only committed incumbents. | At least 80% of deployed test sites remain paid and active at day 60 after deployment, on-time performance does not fall more than 2 percentage points from each site's verified post-pilot level, and no unresolved high-severity patient/privacy incident exists. | Sales/channel | Founder-led direct is primary; offer the wholesaler only the guarded nonexclusive pilot. | Count collected list-price payment, paid deployment, cohort retention, channel margin, and safe user effects—not access, introductions, or praise. | Reject two-year exclusivity and speculative custom work. Preserve the wholesaler as a measurable referral source and preserve other/direct channels. | Continue, repair once, or stop at day 75 using the numeric channel gates. | Make-or-partner | Partner now, add an alternate interface, and defer building dispatch. | Incumbent/alternate must meet SLA, failure-drill, permission, privacy, rollback, human-review, incident-response, and total-cost gates. | Reject a six-month, $600,000 build on current evidence. Preserve a staged internal build if measured downside economics and capability gates later pass. | Use the formula and 50%-volume downside at three consecutive months of scale. Delay in interface work preserves a single-supplier risk; premature building delays the validated optimization product and consumes at least $600,000. The founders should end day 90 with three decisions based on collected payment and deployed economics: a kept, revised, segmented, or stopped price; a direct motion plus a proven nonexclusive channel or no wholesaler; and a partner/dual-source dispatch boundary unless the much higher build gate is actually met.
