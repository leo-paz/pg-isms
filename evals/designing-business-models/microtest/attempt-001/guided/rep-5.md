## Business-model record

### Product, stage, and constraints

This is capital-light B2B operations software with enterprise/integration characteristics and a high-harm, regulated delivery context. It is at paid-repeatability, not scale: 18 pharmacies paid $500 per month for ten weeks, 12 renewed and 6 did not, for a 67% first-renewal rate. At renewed sites, on-time delivery rose from 86% to 95%, a nine-percentage-point association. That result is not yet causal or representative because results for nonrenewers, site mix, route mix, seasonality, courier changes, and measurement definitions are unknown.

The binding decisions are capture and cost-to-serve by volume, whether a reseller channel can work without surrendering the market, and whether courier dispatch is an architecture dependency or a capability worth owning. A price or channel test is reversible within one contract cycle; two-year exclusivity and a six-month, $600,000 build are costly to reverse. Pricing evidence can arrive with signed offers in 2-4 weeks and paid deployment in 4-8 weeks; renewal evidence takes at least the existing ten-week cycle. A full in-house build cannot produce reliable evidence inside 90 days.

By day 7, the CEO and finance owner must establish cash runway, monthly burn, whether $600,000 is actually available, and a dated survival switch if needed. By day 10, the product and finance owners must establish, for every pilot and renewal: monthly deliveries, routes actually sent through the paid API, collected revenue, discounts, compute, support hours at loaded cost, onboarding and sales hours, vendor fees, SLA incidents, patient complaints, renewal date, and stated renewal or nonrenewal reason. They must also verify current pharmacy, privacy, courier, chain-of-custody, accessibility, and incident-response obligations with accountable counsel or compliance ownership before expanding exposure.

No claim should say the product caused the 95% result until comparable cohorts or a credible within-site design support it. Pharmacy staff retain a manual override; the company logs route changes and late or failed deliveries, minimizes patient data, confirms permissions and contracts, attributes incidents, and maintains rollback and human escalation. A safety, privacy, or permission failure stops rollout regardless of revenue.

### Economics and proposed offers

The payer is already evidenced: the pharmacy, approved by its owner. The evidence supports willingness to pay $500 for a ten-week trial and, for 12 sites, at least one renewal at $500. It does not support willingness to pay the prices needed for high-volume or API-heavy use.

For new direct offers during the 90 days, use monthly deliveries as the platform metric and API-dispatched deliveries as a separate, visible cost metric:

| Segment | Ninety-day offer | Delivery, sales, support, and margin rules |
|---|---|---|
| Standard, no more than 2,500 deliveries/month | $500 platform fee per month, billed in advance, plus $0.90 for each delivery actually sent through the partner dispatch API, billed monthly in arrears. | Owner-led sale with a technician champion; no more than two sales calls, four onboarding staff-hours, and two support hours in the first month without escalation. Direct fully loaded gross margin, after API, compute, onboarding amortized over 12 months, and support, must be at least 60%. |
| High volume, more than 2,500 deliveries/month | $1,250 platform fee per month plus $0.10 for every delivery above 2,500 and $0.90 for every delivery sent through the partner dispatch API. Platform fee is in advance and usage is in arrears. | No more than three sales calls, eight onboarding staff-hours, and four first-month support hours without escalation. Fully loaded gross margin must be at least 60%, and no single site may have negative contribution margin. If support, rather than compute, drives the overage, diagnose the workflow before raising the usage price again. |
| Existing 12 renewed sites | Keep the contracted $500 platform price for the 90-day test and show a nonbinding shadow invoice under the new metric. Offer migration at the next renewal, not retroactively. | This protects the observed renewal cohort while exposing economic differences. A maximum 10% platform-fee discount is allowed only for annual prepayment; API usage is not discounted. Every discount is tagged so discounted acceptance is not mistaken for full-price willingness to pay. |

If the $0.40 API fee is incurred on every delivery rather than only on dispatched deliveries, the $0.90 usage charge applies to every delivery and quotes must say so plainly. If dispatch is optional, sell it as an optional SLA-backed package and do not charge it on routes that do not use the API. The finance owner reconciles metered usage to vendor invoices monthly. Expansion comes from more delivery volume or additional pharmacy locations, not from adding technician seats. Revenue is recognized from collected subscription and usage charges; a signed but unpaid or undeployed contract is not acceptance.

Predeclared interpretations are:

- **Payment:** accept only an owner-approved, signed, paid offer at the quoted metric, followed by production deployment. Compliments, introductions, free trials, and the wholesaler's 600-account claim do not count.
- **Revise:** revise price or package when owners report price as the explicit objection, operational effect remains at or above the gate, and a revised package can still meet the margin floor. Do not infer price rejection from silence.
- **Preserve free:** allow only a 14-day, nonproduction demonstration with synthetic or minimized data when required to validate integration. Do not preserve an ongoing free production tier for a product with API and support cost.
- **Stop:** stop selling a segment if fewer than 3 of 10 qualified owners will pay any offer that meets the margin floor, or if deployment cannot meet the effects and safety gates.
- **Churn:** classify each nonrenewal as price, insufficient effect, missing workflow, implementation failure, support failure, courier change, business closure, or other owner-stated reason. The six historical nonrenewals cannot all be labeled price churn without evidence.

### Wholesaler channel economics

Do not accept the current offer. Counter with a nonexclusive, 90-day, 30-pharmacy co-sell test. The wholesaler must name the 30 pharmacies by day 20, secure owner introductions, and permit the startup to contract and bill pharmacies directly. Pay 15% of collected platform fees for 12 months on accounts first sourced by the wholesaler; pay no share on taxes, refunds, or the $0.90 pass-through-and-service usage charge. Cap pre-commitment custom work at two engineer-weeks and $20,000 of loaded cost, using a documented interface rather than a wholesaler-specific fork.

If the wholesaler insists on 25%, require all of the following before reconsidering: the share applies only to collected platform fees and ends after 12 months; a nonrefundable $50,000 integration payment; at least 100 paid, production pharmacies within 12 months; and any exclusivity is limited to the named region and wholesaler-sourced accounts, begins only after the 100-site threshold, and terminates if quarterly paid-site minimums are missed. Do not grant two-year exclusivity merely for access.

Continue the channel after day 90 only if at least 20 qualified owner meetings, 10 signed and paid pharmacies, 8 production deployments within 30 days of signature, and projected fully loaded contribution margin after revenue share of at least 50% are achieved; sales plus integration cost must be recoverable from contribution within 12 months. Also require no material privacy or safety incident and no evidence that channel accounts displace direct opportunities. If those gates fail, end the pilot or revise its mechanics without exclusivity. Because ten-week renewals may fall just beyond the review, long-term expansion still requires at least 70% first renewal for the channel cohort before scaling beyond 30 sites.

### Courier dispatch: partner now, preserve the build option

Continue partnering for the next 90 days and create a switching path; do not authorize the $600,000 build. Ignoring maintenance and the six-month delay, the build equals 1.5 million deliveries at $0.40 each. It would require 250,000 deliveries per month merely to equal the vendor spend during the six build months, or 62,500 per month to recover the fixed build cost over 24 operating months, before internal variable cost, maintenance, assurance, downtime, and opportunity cost. Current committed volume is unknown, so “core” is not evidence for integration.

In days 1-30, instrument each dispatch through a vendor-neutral adapter and attribute failures to pharmacy input, the startup's optimizer/adapter, courier execution, or vendor response. Record latency, API errors, SLA breaches and credits, manual interventions, data export, termination rights, price-change rights, security evidence, and internal support cost. Ask the current provider to repair contractual or service gaps and obtain one qualified alternate supplier.

In days 31-60, replay at least 1,000 de-identified historical dispatches against the alternate and route 5% of eligible live volume at three consenting pharmacies through it, with manual override and rollback. Cap this boundary test at four engineer-weeks and $25,000 in incremental spend. In days 61-90, increase the alternate to 10% only if on-time performance is at least 93%, critical error rate is no worse than the incumbent, and there is no safety, privacy, or permission failure. This tests replaceability without taking on dispatch operations.

The boundary branches are precommitted:

- **Integrate/build:** open a build plan only when contracted demand is at least 150,000 API-dispatched deliveries per month for 12 months, credible internal variable plus maintenance cost is no more than $0.15 per delivery at that volume, the delivered system can match the SLA and safety controls, and 24-month savings after the $600,000 build are at least $300,000. Cash must already be available, and runway after reserving $600,000 plus a 20% contingency must remain at least 18 months.
- **Dual-source:** keep 5-10% with an alternate when both suppliers meet the effect and safety gates and the redundancy cost is less than the expected outage/dependency loss. This is the preferred option if switching works.
- **Partner:** keep the incumbent when it meets the SLA, fully loaded partner economics beat the build gate, data is portable, and the alternate remains viable.
- **Repair:** negotiate credits, price tiers, termination, data rights, and incident response when the capability is sound but vendor terms or service cause the failure.
- **Stop:** remove the dispatch package and retain route optimization if neither supplier can meet permission, safety, reliability, or margin thresholds. Do not hide a supplier failure as an optimizer failure, or an adapter defect as a supplier failure.

At the final review, the retention/effects gate is at least 70% first renewal among accounts that have completed ten weeks, on-time delivery of at least 93%, no increase in failed-delivery or patient-complaint rate, and no material safety or privacy incident. The selected starting branches are **segment** new pricing, **grandfather** the current 12 for 90 days, **direct plus bounded nonexclusive channel test**, and **partner with a dual-source test**. Rejected alternatives are one flat $500 price for all volumes, the wholesaler's present two-year exclusive deal, and an immediate in-house dispatch build.

The preserved options are a later price revision based on explicit rejection evidence, a performance-earned wholesaler relationship, a portable alternate API, and an in-house build after contracted volume and capability justify it. Delay costs are limited to 90 days of continued $0.40 API fees and forgone channel reach; that is preferable to premature exclusivity or a $600,000 commitment. Results hand off as follows: learning-from-users owns cause-coded renewal evidence; shipping owns validated packaging; growth owns cohorts only after payment and deployment gates; engineering owns adapter reliability; survival owns any runway breach; organization and capital owners act only if the build switch condition later opens.
