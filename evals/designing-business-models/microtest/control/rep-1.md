# 90-day decision plan

The next 90 days should answer three questions with evidence: which pharmacy segment has attractive unit economics, whether the wholesaler can acquire those customers without taking control of the company’s route to market, and whether owning dispatch would create enough economic or strategic value to justify its cost and risk. Do not commit to two-year exclusivity or a $600,000 build during this period.

## Decisions to make now

Treat the ten-week pilot as promising but not conclusive. A 67% renewal rate and a nine-point improvement in on-time delivery at renewed sites establish a credible value proposition, but the six non-renewals may reveal weaknesses in willingness to pay, implementation, or customer fit. The buyer is the owner, while technicians, couriers, and patients influence retention; sales and product discovery must cover all four perspectives.

Replace the flat $500 pilot price with a production price that reflects both value and cost. A practical test menu is:

- Essential: $600 per location per month, including up to 1,500 deliveries.
- Growth: $900 per month, including up to 2,500 deliveries.
- High-volume: $900 per month plus $0.20-$0.30 for each delivery above 2,500, with priority support and reporting.
- Paid implementation: $500-$1,500 depending on integration work, waived only in a tightly defined experiment.

These are test prices, not permanent commitments. They preserve an accessible entry point while preventing high-volume pharmacies from consuming 60% of compute and support at the same price as small sites. Quote each prospect a single recommended tier, rather than presenting every negotiation as a menu. Grandfather the 12 renewed sites for 60 days, show them their delivery and service data, and then offer an annual agreement with a modest prepayment discount.

Continue to partner for courier execution. The proposed build cost equals the current API fees on 1.5 million deliveries, before maintenance, operations staffing, insurance exposure, service failures, or the value of the partner SLA. At 2,500 deliveries per pharmacy per month, the API costs only $1,000 per site per month; replacing it would require substantial scale and would still not eliminate ongoing costs. Over the next 90 days, validate whether dispatch ownership is actually necessary, negotiate the partner economics, and design portability. Reconsider building only if volume, customer demand, and strategic control clear explicit gates below.

## Days 1-30: establish economics and customer fit

Instrument a pharmacy-level contribution-margin report: monthly deliveries, subscription revenue, partner API fees, compute, support hours, onboarding effort, gross margin, renewal status, and outcome improvement. Separate route-optimization costs from courier API pass-through costs. Calculate margins under the current price and all three proposed tiers.

Interview all 18 pilot owners and lead technicians, including every non-renewal. For renewed sites, also sample couriers and gather patient complaint or satisfaction data. Code the answers into a short list of renewal and churn causes: economic value, workflow adoption, delivery volume, integration, reliability, and decision process. Ask owners for the dollar value of fewer late deliveries, technician time saved, avoided redeliveries, and retained patients. The nine-point performance improvement should become a quantified owner-level business case, not merely a product metric.

Run pricing interviews with at least 15 qualified pharmacies across three delivery-volume bands. Test the proposed tiers using real quotes and request a commitment, deposit, or signed order; stated enthusiasm is weak evidence. Target pharmacies with enough delivery complexity to value optimization but verify that high-volume accounts remain profitable after variable costs.

For the wholesaler, provide a counterproposal: a 90-day, 20-30-pharmacy launch; no broad exclusivity; a 15% revenue share on net subscription revenue from accounts it directly sources and that become paying customers; and a separate paid statement of work for the integration. If some exclusivity is indispensable, limit it to the named region and wholesaler-sourced accounts, make it last no more than six months initially, and condition continuation on minimum activated and retained account targets. The wholesaler should supply introductions, co-marketing, technical access, and a named sales owner.

## Days 31-60: run bounded market tests

Sell 12-20 new paid deployments with a standard sales motion: owner discovery, technician workflow demonstration, baseline measurement, two-week onboarding, and a written success review. Allocate prospects across volume bands and price points, but do not discount without recording the reason. Track conversion from qualified meeting to signed agreement, implementation time, weekly technician usage, on-time rate, support burden, and projected contribution margin.

Assign one founder to direct sales and one accountable owner to the wholesaler test. Use the same qualification and activation definitions for both channels so their economics are comparable. The channel is attractive only if it adds incremental, well-qualified demand rather than charging 25% for customers the startup would have won directly.

Measure each channel on:

- customer acquisition cost, including founder and integration time;
- days from introduction to activation and first payment;
- activation and technician usage after 30 days;
- expected 12-month contribution margin after revenue share and support;
- retention intent and outcome improvement;
- concentration and strategic constraints created by the channel.

In parallel, ask the courier partner for volume discounts, clearer incident remedies, data-export rights, and a nonexclusive agreement. Document an adapter boundary and test that route and delivery records can be exported. Obtain proposals from at least one backup provider. Build only a small technical spike, capped at two engineer-weeks, if needed to estimate dispatch complexity or prove partner portability; do not start the six-month product build.

## Days 61-90: convert evidence into commitments

Select a production price using paid conversion and contribution margin, not interview preference. A tier should be retained if it yields at least 70% gross margin after directly attributable API, compute, and support costs and if qualified prospects convert at a sustainable rate. If high-volume prospects resist the overage, test a higher fixed enterprise tier with a fair-use ceiling rather than returning to unlimited flat pricing. Publish a one-page pricing policy, discount authority, implementation scope, and renewal process.

Review the 12 existing customers before repricing. Offer annual renewal only after documenting their baseline, achieved outcome, usage, costs, and executive sponsor. Aim to retain at least ten of the twelve through the transition; investigate any loss rather than masking it with discounts.

Choose the sales model using cohort economics:

- Expand direct sales if the founder-led motion produces better 12-month contribution margin and a repeatable sales cycle.
- Expand the wholesaler pilot if it delivers at least 20 qualified introductions, 10 activated paid pharmacies, economics no worse than direct sales after the share, and no material reduction in retention or usage.
- Sign a longer channel agreement only with minimum quarterly activations, revenue-share sunset or step-down provisions, ownership of customer and usage data, termination rights, and no exclusivity beyond narrowly defined, performance-conditioned territory. Reject the current offer if the wholesaler will not narrow exclusivity or fund the custom integration.

Keep partnering for dispatch unless all of the following are true: a rolling 12-month forecast shows API spend and other avoidable partner costs comfortably exceeding the fully loaded cost of building and operating dispatch; customers repeatedly identify dispatch control as a purchase or retention requirement; the company can match the SLA and regulatory/security obligations; and the build has a defensible advantage beyond avoiding fees. Require a revised business case that includes ongoing engineering, 24/7 operations, reliability, opportunity cost, and a sensitivity analysis. A reasonable financial hurdle is payback within 24 months under a conservative volume case, not merely the optimistic case. If the gates are unmet, negotiate the primary partner, qualify a backup, and keep engineering focused on routing, integrations, and measurable pharmacy outcomes.

## Day-90 scorecard and owners

The CEO owns pricing and channel decisions; the CTO owns partner resilience and the dispatch business case; customer success owns adoption and outcome measurement; finance or an operations lead owns account-level contribution margin. Review one shared dashboard weekly.

At day 90, the board packet should contain: renewal and churn reasons for all 18 pilots; paid conversion by price and volume band; gross margin by pharmacy; direct-versus-wholesaler acquisition economics; integration cost and launch results; courier partner and backup terms; and a build-versus-partner model under conservative, base, and upside volumes. The intended outcome is a chosen production price, one repeatable primary sales motion, a bounded channel agreement if earned, and a documented decision to continue partnering unless the ownership gates have been cleared.
