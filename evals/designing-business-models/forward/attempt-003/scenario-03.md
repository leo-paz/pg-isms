## Diagnosis

This is enterprise/integration software with stable annual payment and two renewal cycles, so the task is economic segmentation and packaging rather than first-payer discovery. The current $18,000 uniform price ignores at least three observed jobs: weekly audit use with $120,000–$400,000 of reported avoided remediation, monthly use, and archival use. It also ignores cost concentration: four accounts create 68% of compute and support cost. Whether those four are also the twelve weekly accounts is unknown and must not be assumed.

Seats are not an evidenced value metric. Audit deployment and avoided remediation create value at the account level, while audit volume, data volume, compute, implementation, and support appear to drive cost. A $29 self-serve offer does not remove enterprise procurement by assertion. An unlimited plan would hide the known heavy-account cost exposure. Competitor menus are context, not willingness-to-pay evidence.

Before quoting renewals, build an account ledger for all 24 customers: actual users and audit cadence; deployed business units; data/compute volume; remediation avoided and how it was estimated; procurement and security effort; implementation; support; sales and renewal effort; revenue; gross margin; renewal history; expansion; switching constraints; and concentration. Verify current assurance, compliance, and hosting costs. Predeclare that a signed renewal and paid invoice are acceptance, a request for a concession is acceptance only at the conceded economics, nondeployment is not stable success, and churn must be attributed separately to price, package, procurement, product effect, or company change.

## Package and price test

Use annual account contracts and direct renewal conversations for this cycle. Segment by the job and cost-to-serve, not by published competitor labels:

- **Archive:** retention, export, and low-touch document access with strict storage and support limits. A test range of $12,000–$18,000 annually is an assumption. Offer it only if the workflow can actually become low-touch; otherwise the long procurement burden makes this segment a candidate not to serve.
- **Compliance:** monthly operating use, a defined number of audit deployments or business units, standard support, and bounded compute. Test $24,000–$36,000 annually as an assumption.
- **Audit assurance:** frequent audit use, defined implementation and response service, and a measured allowance for audit/data/compute volume. Test $60,000–$90,000 annually as an assumption, with separately priced overages or services rather than “unlimited.” The range is a hypothesis against the reported $120,000–$400,000 effect, not a claim that every account realizes that value.

The leading value metric is an annual account platform fee plus deployed audit programs or business units; compute/data and premium support act as cost guardrails. During discovery, compare that with audit-event pricing and reject whichever customers cannot predict, verify, or relate to value. Charge one-time implementation separately when it is material. Do not use per-seat pricing unless evidence shows seats track both value and cost better than audit deployment.

The owner is the business-model lead with account executives conducting the conversations and finance validating margin. Use upcoming renewals in each observed segment; because the sample is only 24, collect a comparable decision record rather than interpreting one large account as a market. Cap exposure with a one-cycle migration ceiling or grandfathered current price for customers who need budget notice, but display the target package and its limits so the test still elicits a real renewal decision. Review after each segment has at least three renewal decisions or at the cycle end, whichever comes first.

Measure renewal at quoted price, cleared annual payment, deployment, sales and procurement hours, implementation time, support and compute cost, gross margin, discount depth, audit use, reported remediation effect, expansion, and churn. Confounders include audit schedules, regulatory changes, customer mergers, unusually large remediation events, and overlap between usage and account size.

Precommit the pricing branches:

- **keep:** A package renews repeatedly at its quoted economics, deploys, preserves the audit effect, and clears the target gross-margin floor set from the account ledger.
- **change:** Several qualified buyers reject the same metric or limit for a specific reason, while offering a credible alternative commitment. Change that metric or boundary once and retest.
- **segment:** Distinct archive, monthly, and audit-heavy groups show materially different value, effort, or cost. Maintain separate packages and sales treatment.
- **grandfather:** A valuable current customer needs budget lead time and remains economically sound. Use one dated transition cycle, not permanent unlimited rights.
- **stop:** Stop a tier or account profile that cannot cover procurement, implementation, support, and compute cost or that does not deploy and retain the product's effect.

The selected branch for the next renewal cycle is **segment with annual direct sales**, bounded usage, and paid implementation where required. Reject the $29 self-serve and unlimited enterprise proposals for now. Preserve self-serve as a later option only if a genuinely low-touch buyer, activation path, and acquisition model appear. The retention/effects gate is continued deployed audit use and avoided-remediation evidence; product receives the implementation handoff after the renewal data selects the final limits, and growth receives the sales handoff after those economics stabilize.
