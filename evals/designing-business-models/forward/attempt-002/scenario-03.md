## Diagnosis and record

This company has stable paid behavior—24 annual customers and two renewal cycles—so it should tune pricing, packaging, and sales economics. It should not copy a competitor's menu. A $29-per-seat self-serve tier mismatches an enterprise product with long procurement and an audit/remediation value event; seat count has not been shown to track either value or cost. An unlimited plan would hide the fact that four accounts create 68% of compute and support cost.

Assumed actor map to verify before offers: compliance/audit teams are the users and beneficiaries; a compliance or operations executive is the buyer; procurement approves; the distributor pays. The twelve weekly accounts have quantified willingness-to-pay evidence in $120,000–$400,000 of avoided remediation work, although causality and attribution must be checked. Eight monthly accounts show weaker frequency evidence. Four archival accounts show storage/record value but little workflow value. Actual payment and renewal are real; use frequency alone is not a price metric.

Before setting offers, calculate per account: fully loaded compute, implementation, support, assurance/compliance, sales and procurement effort, current $18,000 gross margin, active audited sites or audit workspaces, incidents/remediation avoided, concentration, switching cost, renewal, and expansion. Predeclare a fully loaded gross-margin floor; the test assumption is 75%, to be replaced only by an already approved company target before any quote is sent. Verify who the four cost-heavy accounts are and whether their cost reflects value-bearing audit activity, defects, bespoke support, or anomalous workloads.

## Proposed packages

Use active audited sites/workspaces as the initial value metric because it is observable and closer to the value event than seats; meter compute as a package guard, not the headline value claim. The following are renewal-test prices, not a conclusion:

- **Records — $18,000 annually:** archival and standard retention/export, one named admin, standard support, and no active audit workflow. Keeping the current price avoids subsidizing long procurement with an unproven low-price tier.
- **Audit — $30,000 annually:** up to two active audit workspaces/sites, standard implementation, workflow and remediation features, and defined support and compute allowances; each additional active workspace is $6,000 annually.
- **Assurance — $48,000 annually:** up to four active audit workspaces/sites, priority implementation and support, assurance reporting, and a defined compute envelope; additional active workspaces are $8,000 annually. Exceptional compute caused by product defects is the company's cost, while customer-driven usage beyond the documented envelope triggers a review rather than a surprise bill.

The motion remains annual, direct enterprise sales through buyer, security, procurement, deployment, and renewal. Self-serve is rejected for this cycle because even the smallest accounts have long procurement calls. “Unlimited” is rejected because it breaks cost attribution. A standardized order form and renewal calendar may reduce sales effort without pretending procurement disappeared.

## Bounded renewal test

The revenue lead owns a test on the next eight eligible renewals: all four cost-heavy accounts, two other weekly accounts, one monthly account, and one archival account. Assumption: at least that mix renews in the next cycle; if not, use all accounts actually due, preserve the segment labels, and do not generalize a missing segment. Exposure is limited to these renewals, with no public price-page change. Quote the package indicated by observed workflow and active-site count; do not let the rep choose price account by account.

Acceptance is a signed annual renewal at the quoted package, deployed entitlement, and payment on standard terms without an off-invoice concession. A verbal yes, delayed procurement, or renewal at the old price is not acceptance of the new price. Record every rejection, downgrade, discount request, procurement objection, and churn reason. Discounting of more than 10% counts as rejection of the tested price even if revenue closes. Track founder or executive involvement, remediation timing, audit season, security review, product defects, compute anomalies, and threatened switching as confounders.

At the review date 30 days after the eighth renewal decision:

- Keep a package where at least 75% of offered accounts accept without disqualifying discount, deployment/renewal remains intact, and the segment clears the fully loaded gross-margin floor.
- Change the price or allowance once where rejection consistently names the same price/metric mismatch and counteroffers still clear the margin floor.
- Segment when weekly/high-value and archival customers show materially different acceptance, support, or expansion economics.
- Grandfather an existing account for one cycle when migration evidence is too sparse or abrupt change would create avoidable churn, with a dated re-evaluation and explicit allowance—not an unlimited promise.
- Stop a package if it causes harmful audit outcomes, deceptive value claims, unacceptable churn, or contribution-negative economics.

The selected branch now is segmented annual enterprise offers with bounded usage, not per-seat self-service or unlimited enterprise. Preserve the existing $18,000 contract as a one-cycle grandfathering option, not the default. The switch condition is signed renewal, paid deployment, intact audit outcomes, and segment-level margin. After the test, business models owns the keep/change/segment/grandfather/stop decision; shipping implements entitlements and sales scales only the accepted motion.
