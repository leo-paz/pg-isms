## Venture-type diagnosis

The selected path today is a durable, software-enabled regulatory-reporting company funded from operations and customers—not a services purge and not a venture round. The existing business has $1.6 million revenue, a 22% operating margin (about $352,000 before financing and taxes), 90% renewal, no acute runway problem, and founders who prefer cash distributions. The reachable specialist market is estimated at $14 million annually. Those facts support a valuable durable company; they do not yet support the growth, market size, software repeatability, capital absorption, or exit scale required by a billion-dollar venture outcome.

That is not a permanent declaration that software cannot become the product. The tool's 35% delivery-time reduction is internal efficiency evidence. Clients still buy judgment, implementation, and accountability, so service is currently part of the value rather than proven scaffolding. Eliminate it only after customers pay for and successfully use a standardized product with much less bespoke labor.

The two paths are:

- **Durable path:** pursue more of the $14 million specialist market with a productized service; preserve 90% renewal, raise operating margin, fund measured hiring from retained earnings or customer prepayment, and continue distributions. Evidence can arrive within renewal and delivery cycles. Downside is concentration in a finite niche and continued expert dependence. Plausible eventual outcomes include continuing distributions or a services/regulatory-software strategic sale, but no exit is required.
- **Venture-product path:** prove standalone software willingness to pay, repeatable deployment, software-like gross margins, a reachable market far beyond the current $14 million estimate, fast growth, and an ability to productively absorb a much larger team and capital. It would reduce near-term distributions, add hiring and financing risk, and require an outcome large enough for venture returns. A strategic software acquisition or public-scale company becomes plausible only after those facts exist.

## Company boundary

Keep regulatory judgment, accountable review, customer relationships, data permissions, reporting standards, and the product's core workflow inside the company for now. Standardize them before delegating. The tool can automate repeatable preparation and validation, but humans retain review and rollback authority for material filings. Commodity infrastructure and clearly specified integrations may be bought from replaceable specialists under portability, assurance, service-level, and switching terms. Do not outsource accountability or create a separate software company until customer ownership, data rights, support duties, and the interface between product and expert work are explicit.

Because this is regulated work, verify current law and permissions, keep truthful claims, log human review, monitor errors, and maintain incident and rollback procedures. A faster workflow is not acceptable if reporting accuracy or accountable user outcomes deteriorate.

## Bounded repeatability test

Run one 120-day offer test ending November 11, 2026. The commercial lead owns the offer; the delivery lead owns labor and quality instrumentation. Use ten matched buyers from the existing niche, split before outreach:

- Offer five a standardized software-enabled reporting service at an assumed test price of $60,000 annually, including fixed-scope onboarding, tool access, up to 20 expert onboarding hours, and up to 8 expert review hours per month.
- Offer five mature organizations a software-led plan at an assumed test price of $24,000 annually, including standard onboarding and up to 2 expert review hours per month; any extra implementation or judgment is separately quoted and recorded rather than hidden.

The prices are reversible assumptions chosen to discriminate willingness to pay and service dependence, not claims about the final market-clearing price. Cap exposure at these ten offers, existing product work, 100 onboarding hours for the service arm, and 50 total hours for the software-led arm. Do not hire 30 people or raise equity for the test.

Acceptance means a signed annual contract, payment on standard terms, deployment, and completion of one real reporting cycle within the stated labor envelope. Praise, free pilots, accelerator interest, and a contract that depends on undisclosed founder labor do not count. Rejection includes a refusal at price, a demand for materially bespoke work, or failure to deploy. Track prior relationships, founder selling, reporting complexity, client size, regulatory calendar, defects, discounts, and deferred expert hours as confounders.

Precommitted thresholds:

- Continue the productized durable package if at least three of five buyers pay and deploy, median onboarding and monthly review stay within the package, reporting quality and 90% renewal are not impaired, and cohort contribution margin exceeds the current 22% operating-margin baseline.
- Open the venture-product branch only if at least three of five software-led buyers pay and deploy, at least four complete a reporting cycle within the two-hour review allowance, the cohort has a credible software-like margin after support and assurance, and separate research demonstrates a reachable market and growth path materially beyond the $14 million niche.
- If buyers pay only when judgment and accountability remain substantial, treat service as value: keep it, standardize it, and use the tool for leverage.
- If buyers want the tool but reject the metric or package while making consistent paid counteroffers, revise once and rerun a bounded cohort.
- Stop a plan that produces reporting harm, deceptive claims, nonrepeatable founder labor, or contribution-negative economics.

The preserved option is a later software spinout or venture raise. Its switch condition is the dated standalone-payment, repeatability, margin, market, and user-effects gate above. The accelerator's plan is rejected for now because it assumes the answer before the evidence and would trade a profitable, founder-aligned business for high fixed cost and dilution. The delay cost is 120 days of slower software scaling; the benefit is preserving cash flow and learning whether services are the product. Until the gate passes, use retained earnings and customer funding, keep hiring tied to paid repeatability, and do not take venture equity.
