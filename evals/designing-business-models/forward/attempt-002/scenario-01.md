Handoffs: 1. `shipping-and-iterating-products` implements only the chosen billing and offer mechanics. 2. `acquiring-and-growing-users` resumes scalable channel work after a payer and contribution-positive motion are demonstrated. 3. `learning-from-users` supports interviews and causal checks, but does not choose the payer or price because product value is already evidenced.

## Diagnosis

This is a capture problem, not yet a pricing-optimization or growth problem. The product is capital-light software with strong use evidence—42,000 weekly active restaurant owners, 61% twelve-week retention, and repeated reports of avoided missed payroll—but no payment evidence. Free retention, praise, and engagement establish neither a buyer nor willingness to pay. Ads would prematurely choose advertisers as payer, optimize impressions instead of payroll outcomes, and introduce a channel whose economics have not been tested. Pause the ad launch for six weeks.

Assumptions to label and verify in week 1: there is no acute runway constraint; the app has permission to use the relevant financial data in paid workflows; current fully loaded hosting, support, assurance, and acquisition costs are known; and the payroll effect is not merely seasonal or self-selection. If time-to-zero is binding, `managing-runway-and-survival` must set the cash switch date first.

The beneficiary and current user are independent-restaurant owners. There are three plausible economic actors:

- Restaurant owners could buy direct because the value event is a forecast that prevents a payroll shortfall. Their alternatives are spreadsheets, bank-balance monitoring, delayed vendor payments, or an emergency loan. Their budget and willingness to pay are unknown.
- Accountants could buy a portfolio package if the product reduces advisory labor or strengthens retention across restaurant clients. Their budget may sit in practice software, but actual deployment and client-data permission are unknown.
- Lenders could buy monitoring or decision support if it reduces loss or servicing work. That path has longer procurement, compliance, integration, and evidence latency, so a verbal partnership or unpaid pilot would not be equivalent to paid demand.

## Six-week capture test

Run one bounded, three-leg paid-offer test against matched, eligible accounts. The prices are predeclared test assumptions, not a final menu:

- Offer 40 retained restaurant owners a $79 monthly payroll-forecasting plan, charged at acceptance, with the existing free product preserved outside the cohort.
- Offer 15 accountants a $299 monthly portfolio plan for up to 20 client restaurants, with payment plus deployment of at least three consented clients required.
- Offer 8 lenders a $2,500 paid six-week evaluation with a named operational user, lawful data access, a deployment date, and agreed success measures. Do not count an LOI, bid, or unpaid security review as acceptance.

The business-model lead owns the test. Cap exposure at those 63 solicitations, normal product operating cost, and the minimum billing/deployment work; do not build lender-specific features or launch ads. Week 1 verifies costs, permissions, actor-specific budgets, and baseline payroll outcomes. Weeks 2–3 present the fixed offers without discretionary discounting. Weeks 3–5 collect payment and observe deployment and use. At the end of week 6, compare paid contribution, sales effort, implementation burden, support, evidence latency, retention, concentration, and expansion potential—not raw conversion alone.

Acceptance means money collected and the product deployed by the intended user. Rejection means an explicit refusal at the stated package or failure to pay after two normal follow-ups. A request for a lower price counts as discounting, not acceptance. Churn means a payer cancels or declines continuation after deployment. Precommitted assumption-level thresholds are: at least 6 of 40 restaurants pay, at least 3 of 15 accountants pay and deploy three clients each, or at least 2 of 8 lenders pay and deploy. A leg must also cover its fully loaded variable and support cost on a credible path; otherwise its conversion threshold is insufficient.

Track confounders: existing relationship strength, founder-led selling, discount requests, restaurant seasonality, payroll cadence, accountant pass-through pricing, lender compliance delay, novelty, and whether avoided payroll incidents would have occurred without the app. Maintain a matched free holdout to ensure the paid test does not degrade twelve-week retention or the payroll effect.

## Precommitted decision

- `pay`: select the actor/package that crosses its threshold and has the strongest contribution per unit of sales and support effort; run a larger renewal/expansion test before scaling acquisition.
- `revise`: if buyers consistently identify the same package or value-metric defect and still make a concrete paid countercommitment, revise once and retest a bounded cohort.
- `preserve-free`: if free use remains valuable but no payer leg clears both payment and economics gates, keep the existing free product without ads while testing a materially different beneficiary-funded or partner-funded hypothesis.
- `stop`: stop a leg if it requires deceptive claims, impermissible data use, bespoke work that destroys repeatability, or no paid commitment.

The selected branch today is to test capture and preserve the current free experience, not to monetize attention. Ads, broad acquisition, and fine-grained price optimization remain rejected. The preserved option is a free core or carefully segmented free tier. The switch condition is six-week paid evidence plus an intact retention/payroll-effect gate; the delay cost is six weeks of forgone ad revenue, which is bounded and preferable to locking the company into an untested payer. Each successful result returns to business models for renewal economics, then hands implementation to shipping and channel execution to growth.
