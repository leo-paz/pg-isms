# Raw evaluation output

Case ID: scenario-05

Reviewer ID: business-model-forward-001

Choose a durable, software-enabled specialist company now, not a venture-financed pure-software company and not a consultancy frozen in its current methods. That choice follows the evidence and founder goals: the business is profitable, renews well, has no acute financing need, and clients still pay for judgment, implementation, and accountability. The tool creates 35% delivery leverage, but it has not shown that customers will buy software without those services. A $14 million reachable specialist market can support an excellent cash-generating company, but on the present facts it does not support the return scale implied by a billion-dollar venture outcome. Neither business form is inherently better; they require different evidence, capital, hiring, and founder objectives.

Keep the high-trust regulatory judgment, implementation, accountable client relationship, workflow design, and quality assurance inside the company boundary. Also own the internal tool, reporting standard, relevant permissions, and the data and interfaces needed to switch commodity vendors. Buy or partner for replaceable infrastructure and specialist components where a vendor can meet the required security, reliability, and regulatory obligations. Do not remove services simply to make the company look like software: here they are currently part of the product. At the same time, do not let bespoke work consume every efficiency gain. Standardize the repeatable reporting workflow, constrain exceptions, and use the tool to raise capacity, margin, consistency, or distribution without promising unsupported automation.

Fund this path with retained earnings, customer prepayments or annual commitments, and, if the cash-flow profile warrants it, modest non-dilutive debt with repayments the base business can cover. Equity would trade durable distributions and control for growth that has not been evidenced. Hiring 30 people before proving a wider repeatable market would add a fixed-cost and management burden unrelated to the present bottleneck.

Preserve the product-venture option with a six-month, capped test rather than a company-wide transformation. Give one owner a small cross-functional team and a fixed budget the profitable core can lose without impairing distributions. Package one standardized reporting workflow, expose a separately priced software or software-led offer to 10 qualified existing or adjacent buyers, and keep regulatory claims, human review, data permission, and incident accountability explicit. Acceptance means a signed paid annual deployment, not willingness expressed in an interview. Track implementation hours, founder involvement, support, compute, gross margin, sales cycle, usage, and whether buyers still require the full judgment service.

At the dated review, continue product exploration only if at least 4 of the 10 buyers sign without more than a 10% discretionary discount, at least 3 can deploy with no more than 20 hours of implementation and without founder delivery, and projected software gross margin is at least 70% after support and compliance costs. Even then, do not raise venture capital until there is renewal or expansion evidence, a credible adjacent market materially larger than the current $14 million niche, a hiring and capital plan tied to staged milestones, and the founders affirm that the resulting growth, dilution, governance, and delayed distributions match their goals. If the test misses those gates, stop the standalone push and fold the useful tooling back into the durable service business. The switch condition is new evidence and changed founder intent, not an accelerator's preference.
