# Raw evaluation output

Case ID: scenario-01

Reviewer ID: business-model-forward-001

This is no longer primarily a product-value question: retained use and the repeated payroll outcome are strong evidence that the product creates value. It is a value-capture question. The beneficiary and user appear to be the restaurant owner, but the buyer and payer are unproven; accountants and lenders are hypotheses, not yet channels or customers. The team also has no willingness-to-pay evidence. Free retention is not payment evidence, so optimizing ads now would choose a revenue model before learning whether the product can capture a share of the much larger value it creates. Pause the ad rollout for six weeks and do not make an irreversible promise that the product will remain ad-supported.

In week 1, build the business-model record from current data. Segment the 42,000 weekly users by payroll risk, restaurant size, accounting relationship, lending relationship, and frequency of forecast use. Quantify hosting and support cost, acquisition source, and the observable value event: a forecast that triggers an action which avoids a missed payroll. Verify the 61% retention definition and look for survivorship, seasonality, and users who would have avoided the problem anyway. Interview a deliberately mixed sample of retained owners, churned owners, accountants, and lenders to identify who controls a budget, what alternative they use, what implementation or assurance each payer would require, and whether an intermediary would buy for one restaurant or a portfolio. Those interviews generate offers; stated enthusiasm alone does not select a payer.

In week 2, prepare three bounded, truthful offers around the same core outcome rather than three unrelated feature bundles. For example, offer owners a paid payroll-protection plan at two annualized price points; offer accountants a portfolio plan covering a limited number of restaurant clients; and offer lenders a monitored-borrower pilot with explicit consent, data boundaries, and no claim that the forecast eliminates default risk. The owner offer should be the fastest, most reversible test because current users can decide without first establishing a new distribution relationship. Accountants and lenders remain parallel hypotheses, but the team must record their procurement, integration, support, sales effort, data-permission, and evidence latency rather than treating a larger contract as automatically better.

During weeks 3 and 4, expose each offer to a predeclared, non-overlapping sample so a user is not repeatedly bargained down. Use at least 60 qualified owner accounts split across two price points, 12 accounting firms with relevant client portfolios, and 6 lenders that already serve the segment. Acceptance means a signed order with a charge, a non-refundable deposit, or an equivalently costly paid deployment commitment. A click, wait-list entry, letter of interest, bid, or positive interview does not count. Keep product access stable during the test, record discounts and founder involvement, and track confounders such as an unusually close payroll date or a pre-existing commercial relationship.

In week 5, compare the segments on more than nominal price: expected annual revenue, payment timing, implementation and assurance work, support and compute cost, gross margin, sales cycle, concentration, switching risk, and control of the customer relationship. A lender contract that requires bespoke risk work may be worse than many owner subscriptions; an accountant portfolio may be attractive if it lowers acquisition effort without surrendering customer or data control. Do not scale a channel from meetings or unsigned pilots.

At the end of week 6, hold the calendar review and follow precommitted branches. For the owner test, treat at least 12 paid commitments from 60 exposures, with no more than a 10% discretionary discount, as enough to proceed to a larger paid rollout at the better-performing price; 6-11 means revise the offer once; and 0-5 means preserve free access while investigating whether the payer, package, or value claim is wrong. For accountants, require at least 3 paid portfolio pilots from 12 qualified firms; for lenders, require at least 1 paid, permissioned deployment from 6 plus an acceptable path through compliance and integration. Select the payer only after comparing these commitments with delivery economics. If no segment crosses its threshold, stop monetization scaling rather than defaulting to ads. Ads can later be tested as their own business model, including trust, distraction, privacy, advertiser demand, and revenue per user, but engagement alone is not a reason to choose them.
