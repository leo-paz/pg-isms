# Raw evaluation output

Case ID: scenario-03

Reviewer ID: business-model-forward-001

The company has enough paid and renewal behavior to tune its economics, but not enough evidence to copy a competitor's menu. A $29-per-seat tier does not match the observed value event, which is avoiding costly remediation during audits, and it may not remove the security, procurement, and sales work of an enterprise compliance purchase. An unlimited plan would sever revenue from the four accounts causing 68% of compute and support cost. Keep annual, assisted enterprise sales for the next renewal cycle and design packages around value and cost drivers rather than seats or unlimited usage.

First, reconstruct account economics for all 24 customers. For each account, record audited entities or facilities, audit events, data and document volume, integrations, implementation hours, support incidents, compute, sales and procurement effort, renewal history, and expansion. Separate the four heavy-cost accounts from the weekly-use cohort rather than assuming they are identical. Verify whether avoided remediation is attributable, who owns the budget, and what metric customers can understand and forecast. Audit events, monitored entities, and data volume are candidate value metrics; support level and compute should be allowances or add-ons when they are cost drivers rather than the principal value metric.

Take three packages into renewal discovery. A records package can serve archival customers with a firm annual platform floor, limited active workflows, standard retention, and standard support. A compliance-operations package can include a defined number of entities or audit workflows, integrations, and normal support. A high-scale package should have a higher base price plus explicit usage bands and paid premium support, not unlimited consumption. The current $18,000 price is a useful control. Test ranges rather than pretending precision: roughly $18,000-$24,000 for records, $30,000-$45,000 for operations, and $60,000-$90,000 for high-scale accounts, with the exact quote tied to a documented allowance. Preserve a custom, priced overage path so an unexpectedly large audit does not create either bill shock or unbounded cost.

Before the renewal cycle, review the proposed metric and claims with compliance, finance, and customer-facing teams. Existing customers should receive at least 60 days' notice. Do not silently force a material price change: offer a one-cycle grandfathered bridge when needed, but make the duration, future package, and usage allowance explicit. Any discount should exchange for something valuable such as a longer term, prepayment, narrower support, or a case study; record it rather than hiding it in an unlimited plan.

Run a bounded renewal test across at least 12 accounts: all four heavy-cost accounts, four other weekly users, and four drawn from monthly or archival use. Balance the order in which packages and prices are presented. Acceptance means a signed annual renewal or order form at the proposed package with no more than a 10% discretionary discount. Verbal approval and procurement entry do not count. Also measure sales hours, required implementation, expected compute and support, gross margin, and whether objections concern the metric, the package, the price, or missing product value.

Precommit the decision by cohort. If at least 3 of 4 accounts in a cohort sign at the proposed package and its projected gross margin clears the company's target after support and compute, use that package for the rest of the cohort. If 2 of 4 sign, revise the allowance or metric once without inventing a broad discount. If 0-1 sign, keep or grandfather the current contract for that cohort while diagnosing the rejection; stop the package rather than forcing it through renewal risk. For a heavy account whose fully loaded gross margin remains below target even at the tested high-scale price, narrow support or usage, charge a bespoke premium, or decline the uneconomic renewal.

Do not launch self-serve merely because the lowest-use accounts consume little ongoing support: they still impose long procurement calls, which is evidence against a low-touch motion. A later self-serve experiment should target genuinely new, low-risk buyers with standardized security, onboarding, support, and a sales-acquisition cost that can be repaid by the small contract. Until paid deployments demonstrate that motion, keep it separate from renewal packaging and leave the validated annual direct-sales model intact.
