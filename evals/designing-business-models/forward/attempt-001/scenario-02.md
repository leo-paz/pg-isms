# Raw evaluation output

Case ID: scenario-02

Reviewer ID: business-model-forward-001

Calling the calibration module core does not establish that the startup should manufacture it. The current problem is serious supplier concentration and quality risk, but an internal line is a twelve-month, $2.4 million capability build by a 14-person company, so it cannot solve the next 90 days of missed installations. The initial boundary decision should be to own the performance specification, validation evidence, and switching interface while keeping module fabrication with qualified specialists. The leading path is a repair-plus-dual-source program, with vertical integration preserved as a later option if external supply demonstrably cannot meet the business.

In days 1-10, assign an operations owner and an engineering owner and separate supplier failure from architecture failure. Verify demand by month, backlog, units per installation, gross margin, warranty exposure, working-capital needs, and the contribution and customer cost of each delayed installation. Break the 7% rework down by defect mode and determine whether it comes from the incumbent's process, ambiguous specifications, incoming inspection, or the sensor design. Document who owns tooling, drawings, calibration data, firmware, certification evidence, and change approval. Without annual volume, target yield, internal operating cost, certification impact, and cost of delay, there is no credible make-versus-buy calculation.

Start two reversible actions immediately. First, give the incumbent a 30-day corrective-action plan with weekly lot data, tooling-access and continuity terms, capacity commitments, and proposed service levels. Second, begin the offered six-week qualification with the alternate supplier. Predeclare qualification as at least three representative production lots and 30 modules, meeting the same calibration and reliability specification, at least 98% on-time delivery, and no more than 2% rework, with no unresolved certification or traceability defect. The $50 unit premium is an option premium; compare it with expediting, rework, lost contribution, customer penalties, and concentration risk rather than rejecting it on unit price alone.

In parallel, spend days 1-15 on an interface feasibility gate. Engineering should identify exactly what the proposed $70,000 and ten weeks buy: a controlled mechanical, electrical, firmware, test, and calibration contract that either supplier can implement without product degradation. Proceed through day 75 only if a bench prototype shows equivalent performance, the work remains within the stated budget and schedule, and regulatory or customer validation can be completed without jeopardizing committed installations. Own that standard and the conformance test internally even if specialists continue to build the part. Freeze nonessential interface changes during supplier qualification so design churn is not misread as supplier underperformance.

At days 45-55, review the evidence without waiting for the full 90 days. If the alternate passes qualification, place a bounded production order and split enough volume to test real delivery rather than granting exclusivity. If the incumbent has reduced lead time to four weeks or less and rework to 2% or less for three consecutive lots, retain it as one source under enforceable service and tooling-continuity terms. If only one supplier meets the thresholds, use that supplier temporarily while finishing the interface and qualifying another source. If failures trace to the startup's specification or design, repair the architecture before blaming either supplier.

By day 90, the desired state is two qualified, nonexclusive sources behind a company-owned interface and validation process, with allocation based on delivered quality, lead time, total landed cost, and continuity. Stop or pause the interface if it impairs calibration, certification, or installation commitments; do not force modularity that makes the product worse. Reopen vertical integration only if both external paths fail the same stable specification, the recurring annual cost of delay and quality exceeds the fully loaded make case, and the company has a funded plan for manufacturing leadership, yield ramp, tooling, certification, inventory, facilities, and working capital. Even then, use a staged pilot line or contract-manufacturing bridge before committing the full $2.4 million. The board's label remains an input; demonstrated control benefits and capability economics make the boundary decision.
