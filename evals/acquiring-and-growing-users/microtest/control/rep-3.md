# 60-day plan

## Executive call

Do not accept the reseller's proposed launch as currently framed, and do not hire three salespeople. The company does not have a lead-volume problem yet. It has a conversion and retention problem concentrated around setup and integration.

The funnel also needs careful interpretation. On the stated denominators, only half of setup starters complete the integration (35% / 70%), while roughly 63% of integration completers are still scheduling in week eight (22% / 35%). That makes integration the first constraint to attack, though the two-of-six group retention result says post-integration value must also be proved at the account level. Sending hundreds more groups into the current path would create support load, damage a potentially useful channel, and make it harder to learn.

Use the next 60 days to:

1. recover or conclusively diagnose the four weak pilots;
2. make one narrow onboarding path repeatable for an explicit ideal customer profile;
3. prove stronger usage with existing and recovered cohorts;
4. test the reseller with a few qualified introductions, not a broad launch; and
5. establish price, delivery cost, and ownership before adding sales capacity.

## Scorecard and definitions

Instrument both site-level and group-level cohorts. Every site should have timestamps for invitation, setup start, prerequisites received, integration work started, connection validated, first real schedule, activation, and weekly core use. Procurement time, customer-waiting time, and company-controlled integration time must be separate; otherwise a six-to-eight-week procurement cycle will obscure what the team can fix.

Use these operating definitions:

- **Setup started:** the office manager completes the first required setup action.
- **Integrated:** production data has passed a validation check, not merely connected credentials.
- **Activated:** the site completes its first real schedule and repeats the core workflow during its first seven days after connection.
- **Retained:** the site meets a meaningful core-use threshold in weeks four and eight. Set that threshold by day 7 from the behavior of the two successful groups—for example, share of eligible schedules handled in-product—rather than counting a login or one token schedule.
- **Group retained:** its sponsor commits to continued paid use and at least the agreed pilot-site share remains retained.

The 60-day targets are:

- raise setup start from 70% to at least 85%;
- raise validated integration from 35% to at least 60% of invitees, measured within 30 days after customer prerequisites arrive;
- activate at least 80% of newly integrated sites;
- get at least four of the six current groups to continued paid use or a written renewal/expansion commitment;
- lift week-eight core use among eligible current or recovered pilot sites from 22% to at least 45%; and
- reduce company-controlled integration work to ten business days or less after complete prerequisites, while tracking staff hours per site.

These are decision gates, not numbers to manipulate. Report cohort counts alongside percentages because the sample is small. New groups entering a six-to-eight-week procurement process cannot produce honest week-eight retention inside this plan, so day-60 decisions should use week-eight results from current/recovered pilots and activation or week-four evidence from newer sites.

## Days 1-10: diagnose, instrument, and reset expectations

Pause sales hiring and paid campaign commitments. The CEO tells the reseller that the startup is interested in a 60-day, non-exclusive validation phase, with a full campaign contingent on mutually agreed economics and product-readiness gates.

The CEO, product owner, integration lead, and customer-success owner conduct structured interviews with the sponsor, office-manager champion, and technical/PMS contact at all six groups. Observe at least four office managers attempting the setup rather than relying only on recalled complaints. For every failed or delayed site, classify the first blocking event: unclear instructions, missing credentials, security/procurement, unsupported PMS/version, data-quality failure, lack of owner, or insufficient workflow value.

Compare the two retained groups with the other four. Define a provisional ideal customer profile from observable traits: PMS and version, number of sites, centralized versus local scheduling, urgency of the scheduling problem, executive sponsorship, an office-manager champion, and ability to supply technical access. Do not generalize from praise alone; identify which repeated workflow causes continued use.

Create one live funnel and cohort dashboard. Review it twice weekly. The numerator, denominator, time window, and exclusion rules for every metric should be visible.

By day 10, give each of the four weak groups a written recovery plan with one next milestone, one startup owner, one customer owner, and a date. If a group no longer has the pain, authority, or willingness to proceed, mark it as a loss with a reason instead of keeping it cosmetically active.

## Days 11-30: repair activation and recover pilots

Concentrate engineering and product effort on the dominant integration path used by the retained ICP. Avoid adding breadth for every PMS during this window.

Replace the current setup with a guided launch path:

- a compatibility and procurement preflight before inviting office managers;
- a short role-specific checklist showing required credentials, owner, status, and expected timing;
- automated validation of credentials and imported data where feasible;
- a kickoff success plan naming the first workflow and target go-live date;
- an escalation path with a response-time commitment; and
- an in-product first-schedule walkthrough plus a brief office-manager session.

Run a daily 15-minute integration triage until the four pilots are either live or have an explicit external blocker. The integration lead owns blocker age, not merely tickets closed. Product owns the completion rate and usability of the path. Customer success owns customer follow-through and go-live.

For acquisition, stay narrow. Ask each of the two retained groups for two permissioned introductions to peers that match the ICP. The CEO also runs 10-15 targeted discovery calls using the same qualification checklist. A qualified pilot must have a named sponsor, office-manager champion, compatible technical path, an agreed use case, procurement owner, success criteria, and willingness to pay. A demo is not a success metric.

Use these conversations to test packaging and willingness to pay. Quote the next direct pilots a clear provisional package that separates recurring product value from exceptional integration work. Calculate support and integration labor per site. Do not promise reseller margin until the direct price covers normal delivery and support and preserves the company's chosen contribution-margin floor after commission.

## Days 31-45: prove repeatability on a bounded cohort

Onboard a small cohort only: recovered sites, expansion sites from the two successful groups, and at most two newly qualified groups if procurement permits. Use the same checklist, assets, success definition, and owners for all of them. A team member other than the CEO should run at least two kickoffs while the CEO observes; this tests whether the motion is transferable without prematurely building a sales team.

For each live site, customer success holds a 15-minute weekly adoption review for the first four weeks. The review covers core-use share, failed or abandoned workflows, user-level adoption, and one action due before the next review. Trigger outreach when a newly live site misses its expected core workflow for three business days. Product and engineering receive only recurring, evidence-backed issues; one-off concierge requests are logged with their staff cost so supportive service does not masquerade as scalable retention.

The reseller test is capped at five ICP-matched, permissioned introductions. The reseller must share enough information to qualify each account before a demo. The startup supplies a one-page ICP, disqualification rules, discovery script, and provisional positioning—not a polished mass campaign. Measure introduction-to-meeting, qualified-opportunity, procurement-start, and expected support burden. Access to a list of 400 is not itself pipeline.

A trade-show presence is acceptable only as a small discovery test if the reseller funds it or the parties agree to a strict spend cap and provides prebooked ICP meetings. Do not announce a general launch, accept exclusivity, or commit implementation capacity.

## Days 46-60: retain, price, and make the scale decision

Complete week-eight reviews for the current/recovered cohorts and week-four or activation reviews for newer ones. Ask sponsors for an explicit continue, expand, pause, or stop decision. Conduct a short loss review for every stopped group and a workflow review for every retained one. Summarize which segment, integration path, and workflow actually retained.

Negotiate—but do not necessarily sign—the reseller term sheet. It must cover lead registration and ownership, ICP and qualification responsibility, pricing authority, commission and payment timing, implementation capacity, support boundaries, data sharing, branding approval, no exclusivity during validation, termination, and what happens to customers after termination.

At day 60, choose among three paths:

- **Scale cautiously:** only if the activation and retention gates are met, at least two new paid ICP pilots have entered or completed procurement at the price floor, staff hours per launch are trending down, and the reseller's sampled introductions are qualified. Authorize a later capped reseller wave and hire at most one salesperson first, with another gate after that person reproduces the motion.
- **Activation still fails:** keep sales headcount flat and assign the next cycle to the dominant setup/integration constraint. If added capacity is essential, favor implementation or customer success over sales.
- **Activation improves but retention does not:** stop expanding the channel and revisit the problem, workflow, and ICP. Better onboarding cannot rescue weak recurring value.

## Ownership and cadence

Assign one directly responsible individual to each outcome using the existing nine-person team:

| Outcome | Startup DRI | Accountable deliverable |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ICP, acquisition, pricing, and reseller | CEO | Qualified pipeline, price floor, and conditional reseller terms |
| Setup and activation | Product owner | Funnel instrumentation, guided setup, and activation rate |
| Integration reliability and speed | Engineering/integration lead | Validated connections, blocker age, and company-controlled cycle time |
| Adoption, recovery, and renewal | Customer-success/operations owner | Success plans, weekly usage reviews, and explicit account decisions |
| Metric integrity | Product/analytics-capable team member | Cohort dashboard, definitions, and twice-weekly data audit |

Every customer account also needs three named counterparts: executive sponsor, office-manager workflow champion, and technical/PMS owner. A missing counterpart is a risk visible on the dashboard, not an informal task for the founder to absorb.

Hold a 30-minute operating review twice weekly: funnel movement, aged blockers, cohort usage, customer commitments, staff hours, and decisions required. Hold a day-30 checkpoint to stop low-value work and a day-60 scale review against the gates above. The CEO remains the acquisition owner for this cycle, but product, integration, and retention each have separate ownership; that is the evidence needed before handing a supposedly repeatable motion to sales hires.
