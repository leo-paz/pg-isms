# 60-day plan

Do not accept the reseller's proposed launch on its current terms, and do not hire three salespeople yet. The company has evidence of demand and unusually good founder support, but not a repeatable path from invitation to durable use. Sending hundreds of groups into a funnel where only 35% integrate and 22% remain active would magnify the failure, damage the reseller channel, and give new salespeople a product problem they cannot solve. Keep the reseller interested with a milestone-gated, nonexclusive test while the team repairs activation and retention.

## Define the funnel before changing it

Use cohorts and report both levels of the multi-site funnel; do not mix group retention with site or office-manager retention.

- **Acquired group:** an ICP-qualified group with an executive sponsor, a compatible practice-management system, a named pilot-site manager, authority to provide integration access, and an agreed pilot start date. Leads, trade-show scans, and demos do not count.
- **Activated site:** the integration has completed a valid sync and the office manager has scheduled one real production workflow. A setup start or credential submission is not activation.
- **Retained site:** it completes meaningful scheduling in each of weeks six, seven, and eight. Also report the percentage of a group's invited pilot sites retained.
- **North-star cohort metric:** retained pilot sites divided by invited pilot sites. Its leading measures are setup start, valid integration, time from access approval to integration, and time from integration to first live schedule.

Rebuild the existing six groups' funnel site by site. The current 35% figure needs an explicit denominator, and procurement time must be separated from time the product or team controls. Instrument invitation, setup start, credential/access request, access approval, first successful sync, first schedule, weekly scheduling, failure reason, support time, group, site, practice-management system, and acquisition source.

## Days 1-7: diagnose and contain

The CEO freezes the three sales hires and any broad reseller or trade-show commitment for 60 days.

Interview the sponsor at all six groups and observe office managers from three paths: retained, integrated but inactive, and integration-incomplete. Review support logs and integration timestamps with them. The key questions are where they stopped, what they expected to happen, who had to authorize access, what manual workaround won, and what differs between the two retained groups and the four others. Turn the answers into a ranked loss table by frequency, severity, system, and owner.

At the same time, ask the two retained groups for outcome evidence and for introductions to a few close peers matching their profile. Do not ask for a broad referral blast. The objective is to identify the narrow initial customer profile, not to inflate lead count.

By day 7, publish one dashboard, one activation definition, a baseline by cohort, and the two or three bottlenecks the team will attack. Preserve the high-touch support customers already value.

## Days 8-21: repair activation and create a retention playbook

Put the largest share of product and engineering capacity on the integration path. Fix the highest-frequency blockers first rather than redesigning the whole product. Likely interventions include a system-specific preflight checklist, explicit access owner and due date, visible setup progress, actionable error messages, automatic retry and data validation, and an internal integration health console.

Run every current pilot through a concierge version of the desired experience: a 30-minute kickoff, shared checklist, scheduled integration session, named operator, and one business-day response SLA. Manually perform steps when necessary, but record minutes and failure modes so that the team learns what must be automated. Offer the four stalled groups a reset with a specific fix and date; count reactivation separately from new retention.

Activation ends with first value, not the sync. Immediately after a successful integration, help the manager complete a real schedule and agree on the recurring workflow, success measure, and next check-in. Create health triggers for no first schedule within 24 hours of sync, no second use within seven days, integration errors, and a week-over-week activity drop. The retention owner contacts an at-risk site within one business day.

Founder-led acquisition continues at low volume: roughly ten peer conversations from retained-customer referrals and tightly targeted outreach. Accept at most three new design-partner groups, only if they match the emerging ICP and have a compatible system, sponsor, access path, and realistic procurement date. The CEO should run these conversations personally and document objections, buying steps, and promises; demo optimization is secondary.

## Days 22-45: test repeatability in small cohorts

Onboard sites in waves small enough for the team to observe, for example five to eight sites at a time. Compare each wave with the original cohort on conversion, elapsed time, failure reasons, support hours, and early repeat scheduling. Hold changes constant within a wave when possible so improvement can be attributed rather than guessed.

Expand cautiously inside the two retained groups and continue the four recovery attempts. These are faster and more informative retention tests than acquiring strangers. Ask retained users what operational result they would miss if the product disappeared, and turn verified results into two reference calls or case studies. Praise for support alone is not product retention evidence.

Give the reseller a counterproposal: during these 60 days it may introduce five to ten handpicked ICP groups for discovery and procurement planning, but there is no mass campaign or promised go-live. Negotiate price, commission on collected revenue, lead-acceptance criteria, attribution window, who owns implementation and support, direct access to customer and usage data, brand control, termination rights, and no exclusivity or volume guarantee. Cap trade-show spend and require shared economics. The price floor must cover reseller commission plus measured onboarding and support costs at the company's target contribution margin; the resulting CAC payback must be acceptable before a launch is approved. Access to 400 names is inventory, not validated distribution.

## Days 46-60: make a gated scale decision

At day 60, review cohorts rather than aggregate anecdotes. New reseller prospects may still be in six-to-eight-week procurement, so judge them on qualified progression and activation only when eligible; do not relabel leading indicators as week-eight retention.

Use these initial gates for moving to a controlled reseller cohort:

- At least 85% of invited office managers start setup.
- At least 60% of invited sites complete a valid integration, with a median of no more than 10 business days after access approval; procurement duration is reported separately.
- At least 50% of eligible, integration-complete pilot sites schedule in each of three consecutive weeks, and the mature cohort shows at least 50% week-eight site retention.
- There are at least two groups willing to serve as references based on an operational outcome, not just service quality.
- Median support hours per activated site and integration failure rates are falling across successive waves.
- Agreed reseller pricing produces acceptable contribution margin and payback after commissions, trade-show cost, onboarding, and support.

If the gates pass, authorize a 10-20-group reseller cohort after day 60 with stop-loss checkpoints after each onboarding wave. Consider one sales hire only after the CEO can hand over a documented ICP, qualification rule, sales process, implementation promise, pricing, and conversion baseline. If activation capacity remains the constraint, the first hire should be implementation/customer success or integration engineering, not sales. If the gates fail, keep the channel paused, tell the reseller exactly which milestones remain, and run another focused repair cycle.

## Ownership and operating cadence

Map the closest existing people to these seats; each metric gets one named DRI, not a committee.

| Outcome | DRI | Supporting team | Weekly measure |
|---|---|---|---|
| Qualified acquisition and reseller terms | CEO | Founder/customer lead | Qualified groups with sponsor, access path, and dated next step; source-to-pilot conversion |
| Integration activation | Technical founder or engineering lead | Two engineers | Valid-integration rate, time after access approval, failures by system |
| Setup and first value | Product lead | Product engineer/designer | Setup-start rate, integration-to-first-schedule time, observed friction |
| Retention and recovery | Customer-success/operations lead | Support/customer teammate | Consecutive active weeks, at-risk response time, reactivated sites, support hours |
| Data integrity | One named analyst or engineer | All DRIs | Event coverage, cohort completeness, denominator reconciliation |

For a nine-person company, this is one cross-functional company priority, not a side project: roughly three people on integration reliability, two on setup/first value, two on customer success and retention, one on measurement/operations, and the CEO on customer acquisition and channel negotiation. Rebalance weekly based on the largest verified funnel loss.

Run a 15-minute daily blocker review and a weekly cohort review led by the CEO. The weekly meeting ends with the current constraint, one or two experiments, their owner, expected metric movement, and a stop date. The CEO remains in sales, onboarding observation, and loss interviews until the process is demonstrably repeatable; hiring salespeople before then would outsource the learning the founders still need.
