# Recommendation

Do not commit to the broad reseller launch or hire three salespeople yet. The constraint is not demo volume: only 35% of invited managers finish the integration and only 22% are still scheduling in week eight. More demand would amplify the broken handoff and support load.

Use the next 60 days to establish a narrow ideal-customer profile, repair the path to first value, and prove that retention is repeatable. Keep the reseller interested with a small, non-exclusive readiness cohort, but make the trade-show campaign conditional on evidence and settled economics.

## Targets and measurement

Track the funnel by invitation cohort, group, site, practice-management system, acquisition source, and week. Keep the current invited-manager denominator so improvements are comparable, while also reporting group- and site-level outcomes.

Define the stages before changing the product:

- **Setup started:** the manager completes the first required setup action within three business days of invitation.
- **Integrated:** production data is connected and passes a validation check. Report procurement/credential waiting time separately from work time.
- **Activated:** within seven days of integration, the site publishes or updates a real schedule through the product; a test action does not count.
- **Retained:** the site performs the core scheduling action in each of four consecutive weeks. Continue to report the existing week-eight measure.

The 60-day operating targets are:

- setup start: 70% to at least 85%;
- completed integration: 35% to at least 60% of invited managers;
- median integration time after credentials and approvals are ready: 10 business days or less;
- activation: at least 80% of completed integrations within seven days;
- four-consecutive-week retained usage: at least 50% of activated sites;
- the two currently retained groups remain retained, and at least two of the four stalled groups return to a real weekly workflow;
- no critical integration issue remains without an owner and next action for more than one business day.

These are decision thresholds, not forecasts. With six customers, show raw counts beside every percentage. New groups may still be in procurement at day 60, so do not mislabel leading activation signals as week-eight retention.

## Days 1–10: diagnose and recover

1. Freeze the three sales hires, trade-show spend, and any reseller volume commitment. The CEO tells the reseller that a staged launch proposal will be ready on day 30.
2. Conduct a structured account review of all six groups. Interview the executive buyer, pilot champion, and office manager where possible; inspect event logs and support history. Compare the two retained groups with the four stalled groups across practice-management system/version, sites, procurement path, data readiness, champion strength, time to integrate, support interventions, and the moment the product first replaced an existing scheduling task.
3. Put every stalled account on a recovery plan with a named blocker, owner, next action, and date. Offer a live integration working session and a jointly agreed go-live date. Do not hide product defects behind more training.
4. Instrument the defined stages and produce one cohort dashboard. Add reason codes for abandonment and alerts when an account is idle for 24 hours during setup or seven days after activation.
5. Write an initial ICP and exclusion list. A qualified pilot should have a supported system/version, a committed executive sponsor and office champion, access to the required credentials/data, a real scheduling workflow and volume, and agreement to a weekly pilot review. Prospects without these conditions stay in nurture rather than entering a pilot.

The day-10 output is a ranked bottleneck list. Given the current numbers, integration completion is the first expected priority, but the evidence should determine whether the causes are product defects, unclear responsibilities, credential/procurement delay, or poor-fit accounts.

## Days 11–30: fix activation and learn manually

Run a single cross-functional activation sprint against the top two causes:

- Add an integration-readiness check before a pilot is signed: supported-system verification, security/procurement checklist, required credentials, named owners, and target dates.
- Replace the ambiguous setup flow with a visible checklist, plain-language instructions, saved progress, owner/contact information, and a clear next step. Show the user whether the system is working, waiting on the customer, or blocked internally.
- Create a standard implementation runbook with kickoff, data validation, first live schedule, and handoff milestones. Hold twice-weekly office hours and trigger personal outreach from inactivity alerts.
- Pair an implementation owner with an engineer for each live integration. Time-box workarounds, record every manual intervention, and turn repeated interventions into product or documentation changes.
- At first value, have the manager complete a real scheduling task during the session. Agree on the recurring weekly workflow, success measure, and next check-in before ending onboarding.

For acquisition, keep it deliberately small. Ask the two retained groups for up to five warm peer introductions, explaining that the product is accepting a limited design-partner cohort. The CEO should also contact roughly 20–30 named ICP accounts and seek learning calls, not generic demos. Use an integration-readiness workshop as the conversion event. Accept no more than four to six new pilots during the period, paced to implementation capacity.

For the reseller, negotiate a written, non-exclusive proposal for a maximum five-to-ten-account readiness cohort. It must specify lead and data ownership, qualification rules, who performs implementation and support, pricing and revenue share, payment timing, brand claims, service expectations, termination rights, and freedom to decline poor-fit accounts. Do not promise a public launch date while these terms are open.

## Days 31–45: run the narrow cohort

Onboard qualified direct and reseller-sourced accounts in small weekly batches. Because procurement can take six to eight weeks, start procurement immediately, but do not call an account acquired until there is a signed commitment and do not start the activation clock until required approvals and credentials are available.

Review the funnel twice each week. For every loss, identify the stage, elapsed customer-wait time versus company-work time, and reason. Compare the new runbook with the historical six accounts using raw counts, time to milestone, support hours, and completion. With this sample size, sequential account reviews are more useful than an underpowered A/B test.

Begin a retention cadence as soon as a site activates:

- day 1: confirm the first real schedule and resolve data-quality issues;
- day 3 and day 7: check whether the next recurring task occurred without staff intervention;
- weekly through week 8: review usage and blockers with the office champion;
- after two inactive days relative to the site's normal scheduling cadence: trigger an owner alert and recovery outreach;
- at weeks 2 and 4: collect a short value check from both the office manager and executive sponsor.

Product changes in this phase should focus on the repeated reasons a real schedule is not produced or repeated. Praise for support is encouraging, but the objective is to preserve that experience while reducing founder and specialist hours per successful site.

## Days 46–60: decide what can scale

At day 60, hold a cohort review and choose one of three actions:

1. **Continue the readiness sprint** if activation improved but retention evidence is still immature. Keep acquisition capped at implementation capacity.
2. **Run a limited reseller launch** only if the activation thresholds are met in more than one batch, retained use among the existing/recovered customers is improving, implementation work is documented and supportable, reseller unit economics are positive at realistic support costs, and the written commercial terms are complete.
3. **Stop the reseller motion** if its leads are poor-fit, it demands a broad campaign before proof, or the economics require volume that the team cannot successfully activate.

Do not hire three salespeople within this window. If at least three new ICP accounts reach first value through the documented process, founder time per account is falling, and there is enough qualified pipeline to keep a seller productive, the next hire can be one sales/solutions generalist. Otherwise, direct the available hiring budget toward the confirmed integration or implementation bottleneck. Add more sellers only after at least one later cohort has reached week-eight retention and the acquisition payback works with full onboarding and reseller costs included.

## Ownership and operating rhythm

| Outcome | Accountable owner | Responsibilities |
|---|---|---|
| Entire funnel and day-60 decision | CEO | Sets the cap on pilots, arbitrates resources, owns the weekly funnel review, and prevents channel commitments from outrunning evidence. |
| Qualified acquisition | CEO/founder | Owns ICP outreach, referral asks, qualification, and honest pipeline stages; does not optimize demo count as the goal. |
| Reseller experiment | CEO | Negotiates the staged agreement and economics and enforces the launch gates. |
| Setup and activation | Product lead | Owns setup-start, integration-completion, and first-value metrics and maintains the experiment backlog. |
| Integration delivery | Senior engineer or designated integration lead | Owns compatibility checks, defect resolution, integration elapsed time, and the technical runbook. |
| Customer implementation and retention | One designated customer-success lead | Owns kickoff, customer action plans, weekly usage, inactivity recovery, and sponsor feedback. |
| Instrumentation and reporting | One engineer or operations owner | Owns event definitions, data quality, cohort dashboard, reason codes, and raw-count reporting. |

One person must be accountable for each outcome even if several teammates contribute. Run a 20-minute funnel review twice weekly for blockers and a 45-minute Friday cohort review for learning and resource decisions. Review acquisition, activation, retention, implementation hours, and economics together; otherwise each function can appear successful while the customer journey still fails.

The central 60-day test is whether a well-qualified dental group can move from approvals and credentials to repeated real scheduling with a predictable process and declining manual effort. Until that is true, the 400-group offer is distribution potential, not validated growth.
