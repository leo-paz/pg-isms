# 60-day plan

Do not commit to the trade-show launch or hire three salespeople yet. The current funnel is not distribution-constrained: 70% start setup, only 35% complete integration, and 22% remain active in week eight. Put differently, half of setup starters fail at integration, and only 31% of starters become week-eight users. Sending hundreds of reseller leads into that motion would amplify implementation load and customer disappointment.

Keep the reseller interested, but replace next month's broad launch with a conditional, nonexclusive design-partner tranche of five well-qualified groups. Settle pricing, margins, lead ownership, implementation responsibilities, support expectations, and exit rights before any campaign commitment.

## Day-60 outcomes

Use office-manager cohorts, not aggregate logins, and report results by practice-management system and group:

- Raise setup starts from 70% to at least 85%.
- Raise completed integrations from 35% to at least 60% of invited managers.
- Get median time from receipt of complete customer credentials/data to validated integration below 10 business days, and median kickoff-to-first-schedule below 14 days.
- Raise week-four scheduling to at least 45% of invitees. For cohorts old enough to measure, raise week-eight scheduling from 22% to at least 35%.
- Retain and expand the two healthy groups; rescue at least two of the other four only where the product now fits, rather than masking churn with exceptional support.
- Sign three to four ICP-matched pilots and get at least two live. A six-to-eight-week procurement cycle makes signed and launched groups separate targets.

Define "retained" as completing a meaningful scheduling action every week for four consecutive weeks, with agreed minimum scheduling volume. Track invitations, setup start, credentials requested and received, integration started, integration validated, first schedule, third schedule, and weekly meaningful use. Every loss must have a reason code and owner.

## Days 1-10: diagnose and contain

The CEO and product lead should interview the champion, office managers, IT/integration owner, and executive sponsor at all six groups. Reconstruct each onboarding timeline and classify every delay as product defect, unsupported PMS or workflow, unclear instructions, missing customer data/credentials, procurement, or weak champion. Compare the two retained groups with the four weak ones to form a narrow initial ICP around PMS compatibility, operating model, pain intensity, data readiness, and presence of a site-level champion.

Pause new broad acquisition. Continue founder-led outreach only to 10-12 groups matching that provisional ICP, using referrals from the two successful customers. Ask the reseller for five ICP-matched introductions, not access to the whole list, with no public launch commitment.

Instrument the funnel and publish a cohort dashboard. Establish one implementation checklist with prerequisites, named customer and startup owners, target dates, and an escalation path. Assign one startup implementation owner to each pilot and schedule kickoff, integration review, and first-live-session dates at kickoff.

## Days 11-30: repair activation and establish the habit

Run two weekly product cycles against the observed activation failures. Prioritize the few changes that remove the most integration abandonment: an upfront compatibility check, a customer-facing progress checklist, clear credential/data instructions, resumable setup, actionable error messages, and internal integration diagnostics. Standardize what can be standardized for the one or two PMS platforms represented in the best-fit cohorts; explicitly defer incompatible platforms instead of selling around them.

Re-onboard the four struggling groups with the new process. Offer a reset only if they still have the problem, a champion, compatible systems, and capacity to complete prerequisites. Record whether concierge help resolves confusion or merely compensates for missing product capability.

Activation is not complete at "integration succeeded." During the first live session, the manager should build or publish a real schedule. Then guide the customer to a third scheduling action within seven days. Give the champion a simple adoption report showing invited, integrated, first-value, and weekly-active managers by site.

For retention, hold brief week-one and week-two reviews centered on whether the product is replacing an existing scheduling step. Add proactive alerts for stalled integrations, no first schedule within 48 hours of integration, and seven days without scheduling. Product and onboarding owners conduct same-week recovery calls and tag the root cause.

By day 30, choose the ICP and supported onboarding path using cohort evidence. Standardize a paid pilot offer with a clear start date, success criteria, implementation boundary, duration, conversion price, and customer obligations. Stop accepting pilots outside that path unless explicitly treated as product discovery.

## Days 31-60: repeat on a small cohort

Launch three to five new sites or groups through the standardized motion, mixing direct referrals with at most five reseller introductions. Stagger starts so implementation capacity is not exceeded. The CEO should still lead qualification and commercial calls, but the implementation owner should run onboarding from the documented playbook; this tests whether success is transferable beyond the founder.

At weeks two, four, and eight, review cohorts rather than anecdotes. Look at conversion and time between every funnel step, scheduling frequency and volume, support hours per launch, expansion to additional sites, and referral intent. Interview both active and inactive managers. Treat praise for support as evidence of relationship quality, not proof that the product is self-serve or scalable.

Convert or expand healthy pilots when their agreed usage outcome is met. For weak pilots, set a dated recovery plan or close them cleanly. Use the successful groups' referrals and a short case study as the primary acquisition asset; optimize the demo only after its claims and workflow match the proven activation path.

Negotiate the reseller agreement in parallel. It should remain nonexclusive and contingent on readiness, include a small initial lead cap, define ICP qualification and handoff, preserve customer and usage-data access, allocate implementation and support work, prevent a forced launch date, and make economics viable after onboarding and support cost. No campaign begins until these terms and the readiness gates below are met.

## Ownership and cadence

| Owner | Accountable for | Weekly evidence |
|---|---|---|
| CEO | ICP decisions, founder-led acquisition, pricing, reseller negotiation, and commercial close | Qualified pipeline by ICP, pilot stage, loss reasons, proposed unit economics |
| Product lead | End-to-end activation and retention outcomes | Cohort funnel, time-to-value, weekly scheduling, interview findings, prioritized friction |
| Engineering/integration lead | Integration reliability, observability, and supported-PMS scope | Failure rates and causes, aging integrations, time to validation, fixes shipped |
| Implementation/customer owner | Kickoff through third schedule, customer prerequisites, rescue, and expansions | Per-account launch plan, blockers, support hours, health and expansion status |
| Data owner (part-time assignment) | Metric definitions and dashboard integrity | Instrumentation gaps, cohort report, source-of-truth audit |

Run a 20-minute funnel/blocker review twice a week and a 60-minute cohort and product-priority review weekly. Maintain one accountable owner per account and one per funnel metric; engineering can contribute fixes, but product owns the conversion outcome.

## Day-45 and day-60 gates

At day 45, continue the reseller beta only if the new process is improving integration completion and time-to-value without unsustainable support hours. Otherwise, pause introductions and keep fixing activation.

At day 60, authorize a larger reseller campaign and the first sales hire only if two consecutive cohorts achieve at least 60% integration completion, at least 35% week-eight scheduling where measurable (with week-four leading indicators for newer cohorts), kickoff-to-first-schedule under 14 days, and acceptable support cost; pricing and reseller economics must also support the desired margin and payback. Hire sales one at a time against demonstrated pipeline capacity. If capacity is the bottleneck before those gates are met, add temporary implementation or integration capacity, not three salespeople.
