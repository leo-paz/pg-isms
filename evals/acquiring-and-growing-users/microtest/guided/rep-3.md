# 60-day plan

## Decision now

Do not commit to the trade-show launch, hire three salespeople, or optimize demos. The paid pilots are enough to work post-validation, but not enough to scale: founder-led acquisition is the only proven source, only two of six groups retain, and the first apparent break is activation rather than lead volume. If the three percentages share the invited-manager denominator, only 50% of starters integrate (35/70), while about 63% of integrated managers make it to week eight (22/35). That makes setup and integration the first repair target. Confirm the denominators before relying on that inference.

Offer the reseller a bounded, nonexclusive evaluation instead: five named introductions, no public launch, no list blast, no minimum or exclusivity, and no booth commitment. A broad campaign can follow only after the activation and retention gates below are met and the economics are settled. If the reseller will only participate with next month's launch, decline while preserving the relationship.

The 60-day objective is to make one narrow dental-group loop work without hidden founder rescue:

`qualified group -> implementation-ready site -> healthy integration + first live schedule -> meaningful weekly scheduling in weeks 7 and 8 -> peer introduction`

Because procurement and integration already take six to eight weeks, leads recruited now cannot produce honest week-eight retention evidence by day 60. The day-60 decision must therefore use the original groups for mature retention evidence, the repair and new cohorts for leading activation evidence, and leave broad channel expansion gated until the new cohorts age.

## Growth record and target cohort

Facts: six multi-site groups paid; all came through founder outreach and onboarding; two retain and refer; four cite integration delay and confusing setup. Retention, support effort, scheduling volume, price, gross margin, reseller terms, and runway are not yet sufficiently specified. Verify runway by day 3; if time-to-zero is binding, the survival plan takes precedence over hiring or channel experiments.

Define the next cohort from the differences between the two retained groups and the four weak groups, not from the label "dental" alone. By day 7, compare PMS vendor/version, number and type of sites, scheduling workflow, urgency, executive champion, office-manager owner, API/credential access, procurement/security status, baseline scheduling frequency, and support hours.

Include a group only when it has:

- the same urgent, repeated scheduling job seen in the retained groups;
- a named buyer champion, office-manager owner, and technical/integration contact;
- a supported PMS and a dated path to credentials, security review, and procurement;
- agreement to provide product telemetry and structured feedback under appropriate permissions; and
- one initial site that can go live before any multi-site rollout.

Exclude unsupported PMS configurations, groups seeking a different primary workflow, and groups unable to name an implementation owner or obtain integration access. Treat the retained-group pattern as a hypothesis until more than two groups reproduce it.

Instrument one cohort ledger with absolute numerators and denominators, not percentages alone. For every group, site, office manager, and acquisition source, record: encounter; qualified meeting; paid-pilot decision; procurement start and completion; invitation; setup start; prerequisites available; healthy integration; first real schedule completed; meaningful scheduling in weeks 2, 4, 7, and 8; referral requested, introduced, and qualified; expansion; support hours; founder hours; implementation cost; and scheduling/sync errors. Preserve original cohort dates when a group is rescued rather than resetting its retention clock.

"Activation" means a healthy, verified PMS sync plus the first real scheduling workflow completed successfully—not a signup, setup-page completion, demo, or test event. "Retained" means the manager completes the group's predeclared recurring scheduling job in both weeks 7 and 8, not merely logs in. Report all-invited conversion as the primary view and integration-ready conversion as a diagnostic view so readiness filtering cannot make the funnel look artificially better.

## Calendar

### Days 0-7: measure, segment, and cap acquisition

- Freeze broad outbound, the sales hires, demo optimization, and any public reseller commitment. Keep existing customer obligations and bounded research running.
- Name one existing team member as implementation lead and move founder onboarding to that person with the founder observing only. Start time-tracking every manual intervention; praise for support is useful, but could conceal a non-scalable service burden.
- Audit all six groups and interview the buyer, technical contact, and a sample of office managers. Product/learning owns why setup is confusing; growth owns where the transition breaks. Code each failure as procurement, credentials, unsupported configuration, sync reliability, unclear value, unclear task, training, workflow switching, or support dependency.
- Reconstruct the 70/35/22 funnel using common denominators and group/site cohorts. Also inspect whether the two retained groups cluster by source, PMS, champion strength, or workflow.
- Have finance/ops calculate the maximum acquisition, onboarding, and ongoing support cost allowed by the current pilot price and target payback. That becomes the support-capacity gate; do not invent attractive channel economics after seeing results.
- Create a security and operations checklist covering least-privilege access, authorization, audit logs, rollback, failed/duplicate scheduling, and the handling of any patient-related data. Verify current privacy, healthcare, marketing, and reseller requirements before changing distribution. Do not use customer names, testimonials, or logos without permission.

### Days 8-21: repair activation manually

- Run a recovery sprint for each of the four weak groups. Give each a named implementation owner, a dated dependency list, a live integration session, and a next-day status update. Separate time waiting on the customer, PMS vendor, procurement, and the startup so "integration time" is actionable.
- Preflight group-level integration and security prerequisites before inviting more office managers. Roll out one site at a time, then invite managers into a checklist that shows owner, status, expected time, and a direct escalation route.
- Product/shipping removes the top observed setup ambiguities; engineering owns sync reliability, diagnostics, idempotency, and rollback. Growth specifies the cohort outcome but does not prescribe a large product build. Prefer the smallest change that removes the measured blocker.
- Observe the two retained groups doing the recurring job. Record what value they obtain, what support makes it possible, and whether they can succeed with the implementation lead instead of the founder. Ask each for up to two named peer introductions only after confirming current successful use; count an introduction as acquisition evidence only if the peer meets the inclusion rule.
- Publish a weekly cohort review showing counts, time in each transition, support cost, error rate, and reasons for loss. Customer praise and reseller reach belong in notes, not the activation numerator.

### Days 22-42: test a repeatable, narrow loop

- Admit at most two new groups, initially at one site each, from retained-customer referrals or tightly matched founder outreach. The cap protects the repair work and reveals whether a non-founder can run onboarding. No group enters merely to fill the cohort.
- The implementation lead runs the documented preflight, kickoff, integration, first-live-schedule session, and handoff. The founder may join discovery but may not rescue setup silently; every intervention is logged.
- CEO/growth may contact no more than ten retained-group lookalikes and track source-specific progression to a qualified opportunity. Compare referrals and founder outbound on qualified rate and time, not raw replies or demos.
- In parallel, negotiate the reseller test. Before accepting even five introductions, settle who owns the lead and customer relationship, approved claims, data sharing and consent, price, commission, payment timing, event and support costs, refunds, attribution, implementation responsibility, service levels, brand use, exclusivity, termination, and reporting. Use the same qualification and pilot terms as the direct cohort unless the business-model owner approves a predeclared comparison.
- If acceptable, let the reseller make five named introductions only. Do not announce a launch. Procurement progression is a leading indicator; it is not activation or proof of the reseller channel.

### Days 43-60: evaluate and branch

- Continue weekly mature-cohort measurement for the six original groups. Report recovered use separately from uninterrupted week-eight retention.
- Review the two new groups on setup and activation milestones, acknowledging that their week-eight result is not yet observable. Schedule their definitive retention review for the actual week-eight date rather than forcing a day-60 answer.
- Compare direct, referral, and reseller sources on qualified opportunities, time to procurement, fully loaded acquisition cost, activation, support hours, and disqualification reasons. Do not combine sources into one flattering average.
- At day 60, choose one of the precommitted branches below. Preserve the reseller relationship and the option to hire later, but do not exercise either because of calendar pressure.

## Gates and branches

Use absolute counts with these percentages, and keep site/group cohorts visible:

1. **Repair gate:** every delayed group has a coded root cause and owner by day 14. By day 30, at least 70% of setup starters with prerequisites available must reach healthy integration and a first live schedule within 14 days; at least 50% of all invited managers must activate within 30 days. There must be no unresolved critical sync or scheduling-accuracy incident.
2. **Retention gate:** at least four distinct groups must show the predeclared meaningful scheduling behavior in both weeks 7 and 8, with at least 60% of their activated office managers retained. Onboarding must be runnable by the implementation lead with no founder-critical step, and two consecutive weeks of support cost must fit the unit-economic envelope set on day 7.
3. **Acquisition gate:** a source must produce qualified groups matching the inclusion rule, not just meetings. Keep a source only if its fully loaded cost and procurement progression fit the predeclared payback envelope and its downstream activation is no worse than the direct cohort.
4. **Reseller gate:** terms are nonexclusive and reversible; price, commission, event cost, support load, lead ownership, and customer ownership produce acceptable economics; at least three of five named introductions qualify; and the repair and retention gates are already met. Reach to 400 groups is not itself evidence.

If the repair gate fails, stop adding pilots and reseller leads, return the observed contradictions to product learning, and focus engineering and implementation on the dominant blocker. If repair passes but mature retention or support economics do not, keep onboarding manual and capped at two groups; do not launch or hire sellers. If mature retention passes but reseller economics or source quality fail, expand only the proven referral/founder loop. Only if all gates pass should the company authorize the next ten reseller introductions. Even then, hire at most one seller after showing that seller capacity—not integration, onboarding, or procurement—is the binding constraint. If implementation capacity is the evidenced bottleneck and runway permits, consider one implementation/integration hire instead of three sales hires.

## Ownership

| Outcome or decision | Accountable owner | Supporting owner / handoff |
|---|---|---|
| Acquisition cap, cohort ledger, referral/outbound/reseller source test | CEO acting as growth lead | Ops/data maintains denominator and cost integrity |
| Segment hypothesis and reasons for success/failure | Product/learning lead | Findings hand to growth for inclusion rules |
| Procurement readiness and concierge activation | Implementation/customer-success lead | Product owns setup artifact changes; engineering owns integration machinery |
| Meaningful retention, support burden, referral request | Customer-success lead | Growth receives qualified introductions and expansion evidence |
| Price, packaging, reseller economics, sales model | CEO plus finance/ops as business-model owner | Growth executes only the bounded channel test |
| Privacy, security, scheduling accuracy, rollback | Named security/engineering owner | Can halt rollout regardless of aggregate growth |
| Day-30 and day-60 gate decision | CEO, with the above owners signing their metrics | Next owner is product/engineering for repair, growth for bounded expansion, or survival owner if runway binds |

This plan preserves the attractive reseller option while refusing to pour 400 prospects and three new sellers into a loop that currently retains only two groups and depends on founder-led service.
