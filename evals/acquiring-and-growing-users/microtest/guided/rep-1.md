# 60-day decision: repair the loop before scaling acquisition

Do not commit to the trade-show launch, hire three salespeople, or optimize demos now. Keep the reseller relationship alive, but reduce it to a bounded introduction test. Founder outreach has already produced six paying pilots; the first clearly broken transition is setup/integration, and only 22% of invited office managers are still scheduling in week eight. Adding leads would feed a leaky, support-dependent loop. The six-to-eight-week procurement and integration cycle also means a campaign launched next month cannot produce trustworthy retention evidence inside this 60-day window.

## Growth record and beachhead

**Known facts:** six multi-site dental groups pay for pilots; all came through founder-led outreach and onboarding; 70% of invitees start setup, 35% complete integration, and 22% schedule in week eight; two retained groups refer peers and praise support; four report integration delays and confusing setup. The reseller claims access to 400 groups, but price, commission, support obligations, lead quality, and exclusivity are unsettled.

**Verify by day 7:** the numerator, denominator, and cohort date behind each percentage; whether metrics are per office manager, site, or group; the practice-management systems, site counts, workflows, sponsors, procurement steps, and support hours for all six groups; whether retained use was independent or sustained by founder intervention; pilot price and subsidy; and whether runway is binding. If runway is binding, the CEO should run a survival plan before this growth plan.

For 60 days, include only groups resembling the two retained customers: the same supported practice-management system and scheduling workflow, an executive sponsor, a named office-manager champion, a technical/procurement owner, and willingness to start with a small number of sites. Exclude unsupported systems, absent workflow access, or groups unable to name an integration owner. This is a beachhead, not a claim that all 400 groups are suitable. The next adjacency is groups with the same system and workflow but a modestly different site count, only after this cohort works.

Use one explicit funnel for every source:

`named account -> qualified discovery -> paid pilot agreed -> kickoff -> setup started -> access ready -> integration live -> activated -> retained -> referred or expanded`

A site is **activated** only when its live practice-management connection is validated and its office manager completes two real, end-to-end scheduling workflows on separate days within seven days, the second without startup staff operating the product. Integration completion alone is not activation. A site is **week-eight retained** only if it performs a meaningful scheduling workflow in both weeks seven and eight without startup staff doing the work. Report site and group outcomes separately, retain every eligible invited site in the denominator, and label reactivated old pilots separately from new-cohort retention.

## Days 0-7: establish truth and stop the leak

- The CEO pauses the trade-show commitment and all three sales hires. No broad campaign is authorized.
- The implementation lead reconstructs all six account timelines from first contact through week eight, including source, nonresponse, procurement, security review, credential wait, setup step, integration error, staff touches, support hours, and reason for stopping. Observe office managers repeating setup rather than relying on praise or retrospective summaries.
- The data owner publishes a cohort sheet with exact numerators and denominators by group, site, practice-management system, and source. Track signed-pilot-to-live time separately from access-ready-to-live time so procurement delay is not hidden inside or excluded from integration performance.
- The team offers the four stalled groups a clearly labeled recovery sprint and protects the two retained groups' service. Recovery can reveal failure modes, but it must not be counted as new retention.
- Product and engineering select the two most common, consequential setup/integration blockers. Shipping owns the interface or workflow changes; engineering owns connector reliability and instrumentation; growth owns the target cohort outcome.

## Days 8-21: run concierge activation without founder rescue

Assign one implementation captain per group and use a standard kickoff: sponsor and champion confirmed, security/procurement path mapped, system version checked, credentials requested, test data validated, go-live date booked, and fallback/rollback documented. Run setup live with the first office manager, record every hesitation, and answer blockers within one business day. Turn repeated questions into a checklist or product fix, not permanent white-glove labor.

Use two of the best-fit referred or directly sourced groups as cohort A, with no more than three pilot sites per group. The founder may sell and attend kickoff but may not configure integrations or schedule on the customer's behalf. Measure staff time by site and intervention type. Do not change price, incentives, or service level between sources without labeling the confounder.

## Days 22-40: test acquisition narrowly

Open cohort B, another two best-fit groups and no more than six sites, only if cohort A's setup starts are on track and no unresolved security, privacy, or integration-severity issue exists. Split the four new groups across referral/founder-direct and reseller introductions where feasible; do not seek statistical certainty from this sample. Its purpose is to find gross channel contradictions and reproduce activation.

Offer the reseller a written 60-day test: up to 10 named introductions that meet the inclusion rule, no trade-show spend, exclusivity, volume promise, or public launch claim. Record introduction-to-meeting, qualification, pilot acceptance, source-specific support, and any requested discount. The 400-account claim is exposure, not acquisition evidence.

In parallel, the business-model owner—not the growth experiment—settles the candidate pilot price, reseller commission, implementation/support allocation, discount authority, ownership of the customer relationship and data, termination terms, and gross-margin/payback guardrail. The reseller cannot advance to campaign status until those economics are acceptable and its customers produce activation and matured retention.

## Days 41-60: remove rescue and make the gated decision

The implementation lead hands cohort B to the documented process while the founder exits onboarding. The customer-success owner checks for independent scheduling weekly and asks for referrals only after observed retained use and with permission. Continue measuring the original pilots and cohort A as they mature. Cohort B will not have honest week-eight evidence by day 60, so report its setup, integration, activation, and early-use indicators as leading evidence—not as retention.

Review the funnel twice weekly operationally and make formal decisions on days 21, 40, and 60. Use these precommitted gates:

| Outcome | Day-60 gate | Decision and next owner |
|---|---|---|
| Setup | At least 80% of eligible invitees start within five business days | If missed, pause new invitations; product/implementation repairs comprehension and kickoff. |
| Integration and activation | At least 60% of all eligible cohort-A sites activate within eight weeks, and at least 70% of access-ready sites activate within 10 business days | If access-ready conversion misses, engineering/product repairs the integration. If only total cycle time misses, CEO/implementation repairs procurement and access coordination. |
| Early independent use | At least 60% of activated sites complete a meaningful workflow in three of their first four post-activation weeks | If activation passes but use misses, stop acquisition and return the workflow contradiction to customer learning and product. |
| Mature retention | At least 45% of all eligible sites in any matured cohort meet the strict week-eight definition, versus the current reported 22% | Do not substitute cohort-B leading indicators. If no cohort has matured cleanly, keep the decision open until its predeclared week-eight date. |
| Supportability | Median implementation/support time is at most six staff-hours per activated site; founder delivery time is zero after kickoff; no more than 20% of activated sites need weekly rescue after week two | If exceeded, keep the process manual and repair it; do not hire sellers to outrun the burden. |
| Reseller lead quality | Of 10 named introductions, at least five qualify and at least two accept the same pilot terms without a special subsidy | If missed, end the reseller test. If met, preserve it as a test only until its activation and week-eight gates mature. |
| Safety and truth | No unresolved severe security/privacy incident; required permissions and current healthcare-data obligations verified; no unsupported claims in outreach | Any severe issue pauses exposure; the security/privacy owner decides remediation and restart conditions. |

Track confidence limits informally by publishing raw counts alongside percentages; with cohorts this small, a single site can swing the rate materially. Also report source, system, site size, price/subsidy, support hours, and founder touches so a favorable result cannot be mistaken for independent channel momentum.

At day 60, **expand one cohort at a time** only if setup, activation, early-use, supportability, and safety gates pass and at least one clean matured cohort passes week-eight retention. Then the CEO may authorize a small reseller pilot and define a later sales-hiring test; three hires are still premature. If the product loop passes but reseller qualification fails, keep founder/referral acquisition and drop the reseller path. If activation passes but retention fails, stop acquisition and repair the workflow. If the loop passes but price or reseller economics fail, the business-model owner renegotiates or rejects the channel. This preserves the reseller option without allowing a calendar-driven launch to override customer behavior.
