# Decision

Do not commit to the broad reseller launch, hire three salespeople, or optimize demos in the next 60 days. The first broken transition is activation: setup starts at 70%, but only 35% complete the integration, and week-eight use falls to 22%. More leads would enter a loop that currently loses most users, while a reseller cohort recruited next month could not reach a trustworthy week-eight outcome inside the period because procurement and integration already take six to eight weeks.

Counteroffer the reseller a bounded design-partner test: at most 10 well-qualified introductions, no exclusivity, minimum volume, launch claim, or non-cancellable campaign spend. The founders should continue selling and onboarding manually while the team repairs and measures the loop. Preserve the 400-group option; do not exercise it until activation, retention, supportability, and channel economics pass explicit gates.

# Growth record

## Known facts and facts to verify

- Six multi-site dental groups pay for pilots, all acquired through founder-led outreach and onboarding. Two remain in use and refer peers; four report integration delays and confusing setup.
- The reported funnel is 70% setup start, 35% integration completion, and 22% scheduling in week eight. On day 1, verify whether all three percentages share the same invited-office-manager denominator, the exact numerators, which sites and practice-management systems are represented, and whether week-eight use means any use or repeated production scheduling.
- This is integration-heavy, regulated workflow software. Deployment requires workflow access, procurement, security/privacy review, integration support, and evidence of real scheduling. Praise, introductions, demos, and signed-but-undeployed pilots are not activation.
- Assumption to test: the two retained groups share a narrow, repeatable profile—practice-management system/version, implementation authority, urgency, workflow, or internal champion—that differs from the other four.
- Runway is not provided. The CEO should verify it immediately; if time-to-zero is binding, a survival plan and switch date take precedence over this growth plan.

## Beachhead and funnel

For this period, include only multi-site dental groups that match the retained groups' workflow and practice-management system, have an executive sponsor and a named office-manager champion, can provide technical access, and agree to a dated implementation plan. Exclude unsupported systems, groups without access authority, and prospects seeking extensive customization. This deliberately narrow rule produces density and comparable cohorts.

Track each group and site through: source -> qualified discovery -> technical/procurement prerequisites complete -> office manager invited -> setup started -> integration completed -> first real schedule created -> repeated production scheduling -> week-eight retention -> unsolicited referral or paid expansion. Define activation as completing the integration and successfully creating a real schedule in the live workflow, not completing setup screens. Define retention before the next cohort starts—for example, production scheduling in at least three of weeks 5-8—with both site-level and group-level results.

For every transition, record numerator and denominator by acquisition source, system/version, group, site, and cohort; elapsed procurement time; elapsed integration time after prerequisites arrive; implementation and founder-support hours; concessions or subsidies; failures and reasons; and any privacy, permission, security, or user-impact incident. Do not blend reseller and founder-led cohorts. With small samples, publish exact counts as well as percentages and treat thresholds as operating gates, not proof of statistical certainty.

# 60-day plan

## Days 1-7: instrument and diagnose

- The CEO/growth owner freezes the three sales hires and broad campaign commitment. Convert the reseller proposal into the capped test above and set a day-30 go/no-go on its commercial terms.
- A growth/ops analyst reconstructs the six-pilot funnel from event data and customer records. Establish unambiguous event definitions, cohort dates, denominators, and a weekly dashboard.
- The product lead and implementation lead review all six deployments, observing the setup and integration workflow with each office manager. Separate waiting on procurement, credentials, vendor/API work, product defects, confusing instructions, and customer abandonment. Interview the two retained groups for the repeated workflow and referral trigger, without treating praise as evidence by itself.
- Select one supported system/version as the initial beachhead. Write a prerequisite checklist, dated implementation plan, owner map, privacy/security checklist, rollback path, and escalation SLA. Obtain appropriate authorization before accessing production or patient-related data.

## Days 8-21: repair the earliest break manually

- The implementation lead runs concierge onboarding for the four stalled groups where need still exists and for no more than three new founder-sourced groups that meet the inclusion rule. Each receives a technical preflight, one named owner on each side, a live setup session, a shared blocker log, and a scheduled activation session.
- Growth specifies the outcomes; product/engineering owns setup and integration changes. Fix the few blockers accounting for most failures, add plain-language status and next-action guidance, and instrument retries and failure states. Do not build broad automation yet.
- Measure prerequisite-to-integration time separately from the procurement clock. Track first live schedule, weekly production use, support hours, and unresolved blockers. Continue direct founder conversations so no apparent improvement is created by hidden rescue.
- Day-14 and day-21 reviews choose among: keep the concierge process if it is producing independent use; repair integration if completion remains the first break; return workflow contradictions to customer-learning work if integrated sites still do not schedule; or pause new starts if security, privacy, support, or user harm is not controlled.

## Days 22-35: repeat the repaired motion and test the channel narrowly

- Start a second comparable founder-led cohort only if the first repaired cohort meets the activation gate below. Use the same inclusion rule and playbook so the result is repeatable.
- Permit the reseller to introduce at most 10 groups. The reseller may recruit for technical qualification or implementation slots; it may not announce general availability or promise launch timing. Measure qualified introductions, response, technical fit, procurement progress, and eventual activation separately. A trade-show appearance, if unavoidable and cheaply reversible, is a research/recruitment test with no volume claim—not a launch.
- The CEO/finance owner, not the growth experiment, settles payer, price, reseller margin, lead ownership, implementation and support responsibility, data use, exclusivity, cancellation, and unit economics. Estimate fully loaded acquisition and onboarding cost and contribution after reseller fees. No reseller scale decision occurs with these fields unresolved.

## Days 36-60: validate repeatability and make the branch decision

- Continue weekly cohort reviews and follow the repaired sites through repeated use. Ask for referrals only after a group reaches retained use; record whether the referral is unsolicited, qualified, and eventually activated.
- Standardize only the manual steps that worked: qualification script, preflight, onboarding checklist, escalation path, and handoff. Measure whether a non-founder implementation owner can produce the outcome within the support budget.
- At day 60, compare founder-led and reseller-sourced cohorts only at equivalent maturity. A newly procured reseller lead will usually have leading evidence, not week-eight retention; do not infer retention from its demo, contract, setup start, or praise.

# Precommitted gates and branches

Use exact counts, and require the following in two consecutive comparable cohorts before scaling acquisition:

- at least 80% of invited office managers start setup;
- at least 70% complete integration once prerequisites and access are available;
- at least 80% of integrated sites create a real schedule within seven days;
- at least 50% of the original invited cohort meets the predefined week-eight retained-use event;
- the motion works with no routine founder intervention and no more than an agreed implementation/support budget per activated site; and
- no unresolved security/privacy incidents or misleading channel claims, with viable contribution economics after reseller fees and support cost.

If setup starts but integration remains below 70%, cap acquisition and keep product/engineering on the integration constraint. If integration passes but real scheduling or retention fails, pause channel work and return the workflow and value contradiction to product/customer learning. If outcomes pass but only through heavy founder support, keep the motion manual and simplify it; do not hire sales yet. If the full loop passes, expand the reseller test to 25 tightly qualified groups and consider hiring one salesperson against the proven playbook before adding more. Stop the reseller path if its leads are poorly qualified, economics remain unresolved, it demands exclusivity or launch claims, or its cohort underperforms founder-led acquisition at equivalent maturity.

# Ownership

- **CEO/growth DRI:** segment rule, acquisition sources, bounded reseller test, cohort review, and branch decisions.
- **Implementation/customer-success DRI:** technical preflight, customer owner map, manual onboarding, blocker SLA, support-hour accounting, and non-founder repeatability.
- **Product/engineering DRI:** setup and integration artifacts, instrumentation, reliability, and rollback; growth owns the cohort outcomes, not the machinery.
- **Growth ops/data DRI:** event definitions, denominators, cohort dashboard, data quality, and founder-versus-reseller comparison.
- **CEO/finance/business-model DRI:** pricing, packaging, payer, reseller economics, and contractual boundaries.
- **Security/privacy DRI:** permissions, data handling, current regulatory requirements, incident review, and deployment approval.

The day-60 output is therefore not “400 leads reached.” It is a defensible decision about whether one narrow dental-group segment can be acquired, activated, retained, supported, and referred through a loop that works without hidden founder rescue—and a preserved reseller option if it can.
