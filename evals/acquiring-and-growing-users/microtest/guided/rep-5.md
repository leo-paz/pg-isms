# Recommendation

Do not accept the reseller proposal as offered, hire three salespeople, or optimize demos in the next 60 days. The first broken transition is after setup begins: only 35% of invited managers complete the integration, and only 22% are still scheduling in week eight. More leads would push customers into that failure and make founder support look like product retention.

Use 60 days to repair and measure one narrow enterprise loop. Keep a reseller option alive only as a small, nonexclusive, evidence-gated test. Because procurement and integration take six to eight weeks, no newly acquired cohort can produce trustworthy week-eight retention within this plan; a day-60 expansion decision must therefore remain provisional until a post-change cohort matures.

## Growth record and definitions

Known facts are six paying pilots acquired and onboarded by the founder; 70% setup start, 35% integration completion, 22% week-eight use; two groups retained and referred peers; four cite integration delay and setup confusion. It is not yet known whether those percentages use the same eligible-site denominator, whether group and site retention differ, which practice-management systems are involved, how much founder rescue each site received, or whether the two retained groups share a segment or workflow. Reconstruct exact counts before interpreting small-sample percentages.

The initial beachhead is the narrowest pattern shared by the two retained groups: supported practice-management system, similar scheduling workflow and site complexity, an executive sponsor, a named office-manager champion, and a dated procurement/integration owner. Until the comparison is complete, exclude groups needing a new bespoke connector, lacking workflow access, or unable to name an accountable implementation owner. That keeps the intervention reversible and dense enough to learn.

Track every site and group through:

`source -> qualified encounter -> procurement/security ready -> setup start -> verified integration -> first real schedule -> repeated weekly scheduling -> week-eight retention -> referral or site expansion`

Define activation as a verified practice-management integration plus one real scheduling workflow completed by the intended office manager without the founder operating the product. Define retention as that site completing its core scheduling workflow in each of weeks six through eight, not merely logging in. Also record elapsed time, startup-caused versus customer-caused delay, support hours, scheduling errors, double bookings, manual rework, and expansion to additional sites.

For every rate, report numerator and denominator by invitation week, source, practice-management system, group, and site; show exclusions and founder interventions. The 70/35/22 baseline is retained only as a provisional comparison until those counts are reconciled.

## Days 0-10: instrument, compare, and cap

- Freeze broad campaigns and sales hiring. The CEO remains the direct acquisition owner, with at most four new qualified groups admitted during the 60 days and no more than two beginning integration at once.
- Reconstruct the six pilot funnels site by site. Compare the two retained groups with the four stalled groups on system, site count, workflow, sponsor, procurement duration, support hours, integration defects, and actual scheduling frequency. A learning owner interviews the executive sponsor and office managers in each group and returns contradictions to the product team.
- Instrument timestamped funnel events and support effort. Publish a weekly cohort sheet with exact counts, median and range of cycle time, causes of delay, and user-impact incidents.
- Give each of the four stalled groups a recovery plan: named customer and startup owners, missing prerequisites, next milestone, date, and an explicit go/no-go. Do not count a customer as activated because support completed setup on its behalf.
- Write a security, data-access, and rollback checklist before touching live workflows. Use explicit permission and minimum necessary data, keep human confirmation for consequential schedule changes, and validate current privacy, marketing, and buyer requirements rather than assuming them.

## Days 11-30: repair activation manually

Run one activation sprint on existing pilots. A product lead observes managers completing setup; an integration engineer owns connector reliability and blocker turnaround; a customer-success owner runs a single checklist and office-hours session. Concierge help is allowed, but its minutes and actions are logged so hidden founder labor cannot masquerade as product performance.

The smallest interventions are a prerequisite check before invitation, a visible integration-status checklist with owner and expected date, clearer setup language, actionable error recovery, and a verified test transaction before live scheduling. Engineering owns durable connector and instrumentation changes; product owns setup artifacts; growth owns the cohort outcome.

In parallel, the founder asks the two retained groups for warm introductions to peers matching the provisional beachhead. Hold six to eight discovery/qualification calls, but admit at most four groups to paid, capacity-dated pilots. No discounts, testimonials, or referral claims should imply proven general availability.

The reseller receives a counterproposal by day 15: at most ten named introductions, nonexclusive, no minimum campaign spend, no launch claim, no lead lock-in, and no trade-show commitment. By day 21, the CEO plus the business-model owner must settle payer, pilot price, reseller share, support burden, ownership of the customer relationship, data permissions, and a maximum affordable acquisition cost. Growth can evaluate source quality; it cannot paper over unsettled partner economics. If the reseller will only provide access in exchange for a broad launch, decline and preserve the relationship for a later gate.

## Days 31-45: run bounded cohorts

Onboard up to four new groups in two waves, prioritized as: retained-customer referrals first, then a reseller micro-cohort only if its terms passed the day-21 gate. Use the same qualification, price, activation definition, and support accounting for both sources. Do not change the demo and onboarding simultaneously; the experiment is setup/integration repair, while acquisition source is a cohort label.

Review twice weekly while integrations are live. Escalate a blocker after two business days, identify whether the startup or customer owns it, and revise the customer-facing milestone. Protect retained users from being turned into unpaid support or references, and stop onboarding if scheduling errors, data-access concerns, or support load threaten existing service.

## Days 46-60: retention work and decision gate

For activated sites, run a weekly workflow review based on observed schedules, not satisfaction alone. Remove repeated manual work, document why a site reverts to its old process, and ask for a referral or second-site rollout only after repeated use with acceptable service quality. Attempt to recover the four stalled groups, but close rather than indefinitely subsidize pilots that miss an agreed prerequisite or no longer have a sponsor.

Precommit these day-60 branches, using exact counts and confidence intervals because the sample is small:

- **Repair is working:** at least 80% of eligible invited managers start within five business days; at least 60% complete verified integration by the mutually agreed milestone; at least 50% of all eligible invited sites reach first real scheduling within seven days of integration; at least 70% of activated sites repeat weekly through weeks three and four; and at least two of the four stalled groups recover an activated site with four consecutive weeks of use. Support should fall below two hours per site per week after week two, with no material increase in scheduling errors or manual rework.
- **Keep manual and continue measuring:** activation clears those gates, but the post-change cohort has not matured to week eight. Maintain the cohort cap, continue founder-led selling, and wait for mature retention. Leading indicators authorize continued learning, not a sales hiring plan.
- **Repair again or pause acquisition:** integration completion remains below 60%, startup-caused delays remain common, fewer than 70% of activated sites repeat through weeks three and four, support stays above the threshold, or user-impact incidents rise. Stop new starts, return the behavioral evidence to learning/product, and fix the next broken transition.
- **Expand later:** only after two consecutive post-change cohorts clear activation and support gates and at least one reaches week-eight retention of 50% or better without hidden founder rescue. Then reopen the reseller channel in another bounded cohort. Hire the first salesperson only when founder-led qualified demand exceeds capacity and the salesperson would reproduce a measured loop; three hires require proven throughput and economics, not access to 400 names.
- **Stop the reseller path:** fewer than six of ten introductions match the inclusion rule, fewer than three reach a qualified procurement call, no paid pilot commitment appears, unit economics fail the day-21 ceiling, the reseller demands exclusivity or misleading launch claims, or referred customers activate materially worse than direct referrals.

## Ownership and cadence

The CEO is accountable for the growth record, direct qualification, cohort cap, and day-60 decision. A product lead owns setup observation and activation criteria. An engineering/integration lead owns connector reliability, cycle time, and instrumentation. A customer-success lead owns the manual onboarding checklist, support accounting, retention reviews, and referral timing. A learning owner investigates why behavior differs between retained and stalled groups. The CEO and business-model owner jointly own price and reseller economics; they cannot delegate those decisions to growth or sales.

Meet for 30 minutes each week around one cohort table. End each review with a decision for every cohort and channel: keep manual, repair, expand, pause, or stop; record the owner and next date. The preserved alternative is the reseller relationship and future trade-show launch, exercisable only after the activation, mature retention, service-quality, and economics gates are met.
