# 60-day plan

## Decision now

Do not commit to the trade-show launch, reseller campaign, or three sales hires. The current constraint is not demo volume: it is the transition from setup to a working integration and then repeated scheduling. Sending hundreds of groups into that funnel would amplify the failure, consume scarce implementation capacity, and make the reseller channel look better or worse for reasons unrelated to demand.

Counteroffer the reseller a 60-day, non-exclusive option: up to six named introductions in the target segment, no public launch commitment, no minimum spend, and no broad lead transfer until price, margin, data-use terms, implementation responsibility, and support obligations are settled. Preserve the trade-show campaign as the next step if the activation and retention gates below are met. Keep founder-led selling and onboarding during the test; do not hire salespeople in this window.

## Growth record and cohort definition

**Facts:** six dental groups pay for pilots; all came through founder outreach and onboarding. Of invited office managers, 70% start setup, 35% complete the practice-management integration, and 22% are still scheduling in week eight. Two retained groups refer peers and praise support; four report integration delays and confusing setup. The reseller claims access to 400 groups, commercial terms are open, and procurement plus integration takes six to eight weeks.

**Assumptions to verify in week one:** the three percentages use the same eligible cohorts and sufficiently mature denominators; the two retained groups account for the retained use; their product configuration, practice-management system, urgency, sponsor, and implementation access differ in useful ways from the other four; and runway is not the overriding constraint. If the denominators are common, the current conditional rates are 50% from setup start to integration and about 63% from integration to week-eight use. Report raw counts as well as percentages.

The initial segment is groups resembling the two retained customers: a named operating sponsor, a site-level office-manager champion, an urgent repeated scheduling workflow, a supported practice-management system, and administrator/security access available at kickoff. Exclude groups without a procurement owner, integration access, or compatible system; log them separately rather than treating them as lost demand.

Use one cohort ledger by group, site, manager, source, practice-management system, and invite date. The funnel is:

`qualified group -> technical/procurement ready -> kickoff -> manager invited -> setup started -> integration validated -> first real scheduling workflow completed -> active scheduling in week 4 -> active scheduling in week 8 -> referral/expansion`

“Integrated” means a verified production data exchange, not credentials entered. “Activated” means an integrated office manager completes a real scheduling workflow without a founder doing it for them. “Retained” means the manager completes the core scheduling workflow in the relevant week, not merely logs in. Track elapsed time at each transition, support hours, founder interventions, errors, acquisition cost, reseller fees, and any subsidy. Compare group- and site-level results so a large group cannot mask failed sites.

## Days 1-7: instrument, diagnose, and protect

- Reconstruct all six pilots from invite through week eight, including exact numerators and denominators, integration type, delay reason, support contacts, and whether use depended on founder rescue. Interview active managers and non-completers separately. Treat “confusing setup” as a hypothesis until session observation and event data identify the exact stopping points.
- Put the two retained groups on a weekly success check: workflow volume, exceptions, unresolved integration issues, and the next site that could adopt. Accept referrals, but make the referral transparent and do not offer rewards that could be mistaken for independent enthusiasm.
- Assign one implementation owner and publish a single readiness checklist covering procurement, system compatibility, administrator permissions, security review, data access, site champion, and target workflow. Verify the applicable patient-data, consent, security, and marketing requirements before any reseller data transfer or production access.
- Freeze broad campaigns and sales recruiting. Establish the commercial floor for a reseller agreement: payer, pilot price, discount, reseller share, support allocation, contract owner, and positive contribution after realistic onboarding/support cost. This is a business-model decision, not evidence that the channel works.

## Days 8-21: repair the activation path manually

- Run observed setup sessions with the four stalled groups, one or two at a time. Offer a scheduled technical preflight, a named implementation contact, a short setup checklist, visible status and next action, and a clear escalation path. Record every manual intervention.
- Prioritize the smallest changes that remove the observed blockers: permission prechecks, compatibility validation, actionable integration errors, saved progress, and unambiguous ownership of the next step. The product owner ships setup changes; the engineering owner fixes integration reliability and observability.
- Re-onboard only technically ready stalled sites. Use the same playbook for each so results are comparable. Do not call a concierge-completed configuration self-serve activation.
- Ask the reseller for the six named introductions under the option. The founder conducts qualification calls using the readiness checklist; the demo is secondary to confirming urgency, sponsor, procurement path, system compatibility, and implementation access.

## Days 22-45: run bounded cohorts

Operate two small cohorts: repaired existing sites and no more than six qualified referral/reseller prospects. For new prospects, record source-to-qualified and qualified-to-paid-pilot movement, but do not claim the reseller is proven from introductions, meetings, or verbal interest. Procurement latency means many reseller prospects will not reach production inside this plan.

For every ready site, hold a kickoff with the sponsor, manager, implementation owner, and a dated first-workflow goal. Provide high-touch onboarding, then deliberately remove founder involvement after the first successful workflow. Run weekly office hours and review product usage plus support tickets. At weeks two and four, ask whether the workflow is happening, what work moved outside the product, and why.

Do not A/B test tiny samples. Use sequential cohorts with one documented playbook version and a change log. The purpose is to show that the same narrow segment can activate repeatedly with bounded support, not to manufacture statistical confidence from six groups.

## Days 46-60: review against precommitted gates

Use the following gates on eligible, mature users and show counts beside rates:

- **Repair/activation gate:** at least 70% of invited managers start setup, at least 60% complete a validated integration, and at least 50% complete a real scheduling workflow within 14 days of technical readiness. Median readiness-to-integration time must be 10 business days or less, with no unresolved critical security or data-integrity issue.
- **Retention gate:** at least 70% of activated managers use the core workflow in week four and at least 60% in week eight, across at least two groups, without hidden founder operation. Only cohorts old enough for a given interval enter its denominator.
- **Repeatability/support gate:** two consecutive cohorts follow the same playbook, and onboarding/support time remains below a capacity ceiling set from the nine-person team before the test. Report exceptions rather than quietly exceeding the ceiling.
- **Channel/economics gate:** reseller-sourced prospects must meet the same qualification and activation criteria as founder/referral prospects. A signed agreement must remain above the predeclared contribution-margin floor after reseller share, campaign cost, implementation, and ongoing support. A list of 400, meetings, or trade-show leads is not a pass.

At day 60, week-eight retention will be available only for users activated near the start and for the original pilots. Later reseller cohorts should be labeled immature. Do not scale on their leading indicators; schedule their week-eight review now.

Decision branches:

- **Expand cautiously** only if activation, mature retention, support, safety, and economics all pass. Then authorize a limited reseller cohort and one sales hire only after a salesperson can follow the documented playbook without founder rescue. The other two hires and the trade-show campaign remain gated on a second mature cohort.
- **Keep manual and repair** if integrated users retain and refer but setup time or support load misses the gate. Continue concierge onboarding for the narrow segment while product and engineering remove the measured bottleneck.
- **Pause the reseller** if its prospects are poorly qualified, terms fall below the economic floor, data/access obligations are unsafe or unclear, or faster acquisition lowers activation or retention.
- **Return to customer learning** if reliable integration does not lead to repeated scheduling, or if managers reveal that the workflow lacks urgency. That is a contradiction about need, not a demo problem.
- **Run survival planning first** if the week-one runway check shows a binding time-to-zero constraint.

## Ownership

The CEO is the single accountable growth owner and owns the day-60 branch decision. The founder/CEO continues qualification and bounded outreach. A product lead owns the activation definition and setup-flow changes; an engineering lead owns integration reliability, security, and instrumentation; a customer-success/operations lead owns readiness, onboarding, retention reviews, and the cohort ledger. A finance/business-model owner approves price, packaging, reseller economics, and the support-cost ceiling. The reseller supplies introductions only until the channel passes its gates. Each weekly review ends with one named owner, due date, and next action for every blocked site.
