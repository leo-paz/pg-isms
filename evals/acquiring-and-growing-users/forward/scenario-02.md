# Raw evaluation output

Case ID: scenario-02
Reviewer ID: growth-forward-001

This marketplace has crossed the initial evidence gate in a narrow beachhead: urgent Houston repair-shop demand produced payment, fulfillment, and some repeat buying. Growth therefore owns concentrated seeding and liquidity. The binding constraint is not national awareness; it is reliable supply density beyond two pump families. Waitlist signups elsewhere are exposure, not city-level liquidity.

For the next 60 days, keep the work in Houston and preserve the current two-family loop as a control.

**Days 1–10: establish the cohort record.** Define a qualified request, useful offer, successful match, fulfillment, repeat purchase, and repeat supplier participation. For every request record pump family, urgency, source, time to first useful offer, match time, fulfillment, service failures, gross economics, subsidy, and founder support. Reconstruct the 60-order baseline where possible and separate the 18-shop cohort from later cohorts. Verify whether the nine repeat shops repeated within a comparable interval and whether all 12 suppliers remained willing and reliable.

**Days 11–30: repair supply breadth manually.** Choose one or two adjacent pump families with observed, unfilled qualified requests. Recruit and onboard relevant Houston suppliers directly, verify inventory or sourcing ability, and manually route requests. Do not buy more demand than the team can fulfill. If a subsidy is necessary, make it bounded, disclosed, attached to a specific liquidity obstacle, and measured per fulfilled order; do not use it to manufacture apparent independent momentum.

**Days 31–45: test the adjacent loop.** Admit a capped cohort of repair shops with those pump needs. Measure qualified-request-to-useful-offer rate, match time, fulfillment, repeat behavior on both sides, cancellations, quality, support load, and contribution after subsidies. Compare it with the mature two-family cohort and inspect whether founder rescue is hiding failures.

**Days 46–60: decide.** Before the test, set thresholds relative to the verified control: expand an adjacent family only if offer coverage, fulfillment, match time, service quality, and repeat behavior are within the accepted control range, economics meet the company-owned limit, and the loop works without hidden founder intervention. Keep it manual if genuine matches occur but the sample or repeat interval is still insufficient. Repair or pause a family if missing supply, quality, subsidy dependence, or support load breaches its limit. Preserve national expansion as an alternative, but switch to it only after multiple adjacent families show durable Houston liquidity and one tightly bounded new-city microcluster can be seeded with committed demand and supply—not merely a waitlist.
