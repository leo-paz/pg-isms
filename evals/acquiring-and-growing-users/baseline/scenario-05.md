# Raw evaluation output

Case ID: scenario-05

Reviewer ID: growth-baseline-001

The primary workflow is marketplace trust, safety, and fairness, with product, operations, and legal or compliance support as appropriate. Growth should not own the response, because the immediate issue is that the marketplace's incentives and protections are producing customer harm. Strong liquidity and repeat use remove any justification for treating more acquisition as the bottleneck.

Immediately pause new paid ranking boosts and the proposed referral campaign. Stop serving or selling boosts in any form that can improve the organic position of providers without regard to price accuracy and service quality. Preserve ranking, quote, complaint, review, messaging, job, refund, demographic-proxy, and boost-purchase data so the team can reconstruct what happened. Tell frontline support how to escalate bait-and-switch, retaliation, and discrimination reports, and give affected homeowners a rapid route to remediation. Protect reviewers from direct provider contact where retaliation is plausible; serious or repeated allegations may justify temporarily limiting a provider while a fair investigation proceeds.

In the first phase, determine scope and mechanisms. Audit whether boosted providers have higher discrepancies between quoted and final price, complaint or refund rates, cancellations, or review-retaliation reports. Review the ranking objective and every feature through which payment affects visibility. Examine quote response time and outcome by relevant homeowner groups while controlling, as far as the data permits, for location, job type, urgency, price band, language, and provider availability. Combine quantitative analysis with complaint review and interviews; an aggregate average can conceal severe segment-level harm. Avoid inferring sensitive traits casually, restrict access to protected data, and involve qualified counsel or specialists where discrimination obligations are implicated.

Then change the system that creates the behavior. Ranking should reward reliable service outcomes such as quote accuracy, responsiveness, completion, complaint resolution, and verified customer quality, with explicit penalties for bait-and-switch and retaliation. Paid placement, if retained at all, should be clearly labeled advertising, separated from organic quality ranking, and subject to minimum eligibility standards. Establish price-disclosure rules, evidence requirements, escalating enforcement, provider notice, and an appeal path. Give homeowners safer review controls, monitor suspicious post-review messages and job cancellations, and prohibit retaliation in provider terms. Add language-access and response-time checks so interventions do not improve the average while leaving immigrant homeowners worse off.

Validate the changes in a bounded rollout. Track quote-to-final price variance, complaint substantiation, retaliation incidents, response-time gaps across groups, cancellations, refunds, successful job completion, homeowner repeat, and provider appeals. Monitor marketplace liquidity as a constraint, but do not trade away safety or equal treatment merely to preserve completed-job counts. Publish internal thresholds for reopening monetization and require an owner outside growth to approve the decision.

Only after harms fall, group disparities are understood and materially addressed, enforcement works, and quality metrics remain stable should growth resume. Start with a small, monitored referral test based on successfully completed jobs. Any paid placement product should return only with clear labeling, quality eligibility, ongoing audits, and automatic suspension when providers breach standards. The sequence is protect users, investigate, repair incentives and controls, verify the repair, and only then reacquire growth risk.
