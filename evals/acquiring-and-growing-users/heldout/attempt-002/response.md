# Held-out response
Case ID: walking-pod-consumer-channel-scale
Generator ID: growth-heldout-generator-002

To: CEO and board
Subject: PaceCircle 90-day operating plan

## Decision now

Do not buy the $250,000 creator bundle. Do not accept or announce the broker proposal in its current form. Do not replace community kickoffs wholesale, and do not hire four growth marketers yet.

For the next 90 days, keep community acquisition at or below its current run rate as a controlled learning and service channel. That authorizes no more than approximately $12,000 of event cash spend—the prior six-month spend prorated to 90 days—and no general scale-up. Founder time must be recorded separately; the prior run rate would consume another 260 founder hours in 90 days, which is the constraint the self-serve test must remove.

The reason is visible after payment, not at the top of the funnel. Community events converted 900 paid members into 720 first-week activated members and 510 week-8 users: 80.0% paid-to-activation and 56.7% paid-to-week-8. Their cash cost was $26.67 per paid member and $47.06 per week-8 user, before founder labor. Creators looked cheaper at payment—$16.67 per paid member—but converted only 30.0% of paid members to first-week activation and 2.7% to week-8 use, for $625 per week-8 user. The creator funnel also first weakens at trial-to-paid (15.0%, versus 75.0% for events) and then collapses further after payment. Buying more reach or adding marketers would multiply an unproven, low-quality loop.

These are source-level aggregates from different periods, not a randomized comparison. Revenue, gross margin, subscription duration, founder loaded cost, refund rates, and a common referral window are also missing. Finance must not present either paid-member CAC as payback economics until those gaps are filled.

## The board-grade cohort

By day 14, launch one instrumented community-sourced cohort early enough to complete week 8 before the board meeting. Within that cohort, randomize eligible entrants at the pod or event level to:

- the current founder-hosted kickoff and rematching process; or
- self-serve pod formation with the same product promise, price, eligibility rules, and walk reminders.

Use a concurrent manual control rather than relying only on the six-month aggregate. Freeze assignment, metric definitions, exclusions, and analysis before launch. A founder may intervene in the self-serve arm only under a predeclared safety or service-recovery rule. Record the intervention and count the pod as rescued; otherwise self-serve success could be manufactured by invisible manual work.

The primary outcome is the percentage of paid entrants who are still logging at least one walk in week 8. The main early indicator is the existing activation event: joining a complete four-person pod and logging three walks in the first seven days. Report the full source-to-outcome funnel for each arm: event attendee, trial, paid, complete pod, first-week activation, week-8 use, and paid referral. Also report:

- time to a complete pod, unfilled-pod rate, rematches, quiet pods, and pod completion and repeat behavior;
- founder and support minutes per pod, per activated member, and per week-8 user;
- the percentage of pods receiving any manual rescue and results both including and excluding rescued pods;
- cancellations, refunds, complaints, unwanted-contact or safety reports, and interaction-quality failures; and
- cash cost per paid, activated, and week-8 member, plus fully loaded cost after Finance supplies an agreed founder-hour rate.

Show numerators, denominators, confidence intervals, missing data, and results by acquisition source and assignment. Do not pool late cohorts that cannot mature to week 8. Retain consent and minimum-necessary data, and do not turn private pod activity into marketing claims.

Before launch, precommit to this replacement rule: self-serve is eligible for staged rollout only if its paid-to-activation and paid-to-week-8 rates are no more than 10 percentage points below the concurrent manual arm, the confidence interval excludes a loss larger than that margin, founder hours fall by at least 75%, no more than 10% of pods need founder rescue, and refunds, safety, and interaction-quality outcomes do not worsen materially. The 10-point non-inferiority margin and 75% labor target are management choices; the CEO must sign them before seeing results. If the cohort is too small to rule out the margin, the result is inconclusive and the manual process stays in place.

## Work during weeks 1–8

The Growth lead owns the cohort record, randomization, funnel instrumentation, cost ledger, weekly data-quality review, and frozen board analysis. Product and Engineering own the smallest reversible self-serve implementation and rescue logging. Community Operations runs the control exactly as documented; founder kickoff and rematch time is work to measure, not free capacity.

In parallel, User Research should examine the creator failure without another large media buy. Recruit separate samples of creator-sourced members who paid but never activated, activated but left before week 8, and remained through week 8. Test whether the ad promise, intended walking behavior, pod expectations, onboarding, match time, or interaction quality differed from the community cohort. Log nonresponse and source; do not treat views, clicks, signups, or favorable comments as evidence that the channel works. At most, use a small, pre-budgeted message or onboarding diagnostic that cannot be mistaken for a scale test. No creator channel receives expansion money until a later source-specific cohort demonstrates acceptable activation and week-8 retention.

Finance supplies gross margin, realized subscription revenue and duration by source, refunds, founder loaded cost, and CAC/payback sensitivity. It should keep cash acquisition cost and founder labor visible as separate lines as well as combined. This determines whether even the strong community behavior is economically scalable.

## Broker channel

The current offer is not a partnership win. It is nonbinding, free, exclusive, pricing is unresolved, and neither employees nor buyers have been studied. Seventy-five employer logos or eligible seats would be exposure, not employee activation or a purchase commitment.

The CEO should counter with a nonexclusive, unannounced discovery period and an option for a bounded pilot with only a few named employers. User Research owns interviews with employees from the intended segment, including people who decline or stop walking, and with benefits buyers and implementation owners. The Business Model owner tests payer, price, packaging, broker economics, support burden, procurement, security, and the exact paid-conversion decision. Legal/Privacy reviews employee consent, employer visibility into activity, data use, publicity, termination, and exclusivity.

Do not start the pilot until the team has a specific employee inclusion rule and repeated behavior to address, and several named employers commit actual deployment access: an internal owner, a communication slot, a launch date, enough opted-in employees to form complete pods, and a dated decision on paid conversion at a stated price or price range. A pilot term must define opt-in, complete-pod formation, first-week activation, week-8 use, support cost, buyer conversion, data access, and stop conditions. If a short learning pilot is free, it remains nonexclusive and time-boxed; free participation cannot count as paid demand. Announce only a signed, deployable relationship, with the counterparty's approval and claims limited to facts.

If those gates are passed early enough for a cohort to mature, the board may inspect its week-8 results separately. Otherwise it should see discovery and commitment evidence only, and no one should label the channel validated.

## Precommitted board decisions

At week 10:

- If self-serve meets every replacement gate, Product stages rollout to later community cohorts, Growth expands only within the same proven segment and venue type, and Operations audits the first two rollout cohorts for hidden rescue. A nearby segment or venue is tested before creators are scaled.
- If early activation holds but week-8 use, quality, or safety fails, cap acquisition, keep the manual process, and have Product and the relevant trust owner repair pod formation or interaction quality before another growth test.
- If outcomes are acceptable only with substantial rescue, retain a documented hybrid service and price its labor. Engineering may automate the specific repeated rematch task; Growth may not call the loop self-serve.
- If the interval is inconclusive, keep the manual control and run additional cohorts. Uncertainty is not a rollout decision.
- If creator research identifies a correctable promise or onboarding mismatch, Growth may propose one capped creator cohort after the product change, with the same activation, week-8, quality, and fully loaded economics gates. Otherwise paid creator acquisition stays paused.
- If the broker discovery and commitment gates fail, decline the proposal and preserve the option to revisit without exclusivity. If they pass, the CEO and Business Model owner may sign the bounded nonexclusive pilot; Growth measures the employee funnel, while paid conversion and channel economics remain explicit commercial gates.

No growth hires are approved at the board merely because traffic, trials, or employer reach increased. If the self-serve community loop passes and one repeatable acquisition bottleneck is clear, hire one owner for that bottleneck and set a cohort outcome for the role before adding another. If retention or pod operations remain binding, assign Product, Engineering, or Operations capacity instead of adding marketers.

Days 71–90 are for executing the branch the evidence supports: staged community rollout, product repair and another controlled cohort, or a bounded employer pilot. The preserved alternatives are the manual community loop, a later corrected creator retest, and a nonexclusive broker pilot. Each becomes active only when its named switch condition is met.
