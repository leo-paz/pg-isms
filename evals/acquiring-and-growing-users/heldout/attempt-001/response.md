# Decision memo

**To:** Founders and board  
**Subject:** Decline the national inventory commitment; prove a regional, installer-independent loop

## Decision

Do not accept the 80-branch deal on its current terms, do not commit $4.5 million to inventory next week, and do not promise 30-day national delivery. Renegotiate it into a milestone-based option or walk away.

The Northern California results are strong enough to continue post-validation growth work in that narrow segment: 10 stores paid for 14 units, nine stores used them through four months of peak-demand days, eight of those nine still use them, and four of the 10 stores ordered a second unit. They are not evidence that the company can supply and support a national channel. The first broken transition is not awareness or signup. It is **qualified site survey → independently commissioned, reliable unit**: founders still attend every survey and resolve commissioning problems. The loop does not yet work without hidden founder rescue.

The proposed commitment is especially poor because it combines a hard-to-reverse inventory bet with two unresolved hardware risks: a new compressor supplier in the next batch and no reliable cold-season field evidence for five months. A distributor's willingness to stock branches is channel interest, not proof of activation, retention, reliable fulfillment, or economics.

Before any inventory purchase, the CFO should also model default-dead status and time-to-zero under a slow-sell, high-warranty case. If that is binding, runway and a dated survival switch decision take precedence over normal growth optimization.

## What is known, and what is not

**Facts:** The paid cohort is 10 independent Northern California grocers and 14 units. Nine stores have demonstrated use over the observed four-month warm-weather interval; eight of those nine remain active, and four of 10 stores have expanded. All installations came through two local energy contractors. Every survey and commissioning still uses founder labor. The next batch changes a critical component, and relevant cold-season evidence cannot arrive sooner than five months.

**Unknowns to verify before the next review:** the number of stores encountered, qualified, surveyed, and rejected by each contractor; why the tenth store did not operate and why one of nine stopped; commissioning time, founder hours, support incidents, failures, warranty exposure, delivery lead time, contractor compensation, gross contribution after freight and support, subsidies or incentives, measured customer energy and reliability outcomes, supplier lot quality, certification implications, distributor returns/cancellation terms, and cash exposure. We should not infer conversion rates without the missing denominators or treat the warm-weather cohort as cold-season evidence.

The current beachhead should remain independent grocers in Northern California with the same load pattern, site requirements, climate exposure, and contractor coverage as the observed cohort. Exclude national chains, unqualified sites, materially colder regions, and installations requiring custom founder engineering. Those are later adjacency tests, not members of the proven segment.

## Replacement growth decision

Run a bounded regional contractor-enablement and hardware-reliability cohort through the cold-season evidence date.

1. Ask the distributor for a conditional agreement that preserves the relationship but creates no $4.5 million obligation and no 30-day promise. Inventory should be released only in small tranches after agreed operating gates and paid, qualified demand. Partner economics, returns, and inventory risk belong in a separate finance/business-model decision. If the distributor requires the present commitment, decline it.

2. Limit the next cohort to at most six new paid sites that satisfy the beachhead rule, with three routed to each existing contractor where feasible. Cap the physical batch at an amount the company can replace and service without threatening survival. This is an evidence cohort, not a volume target.

3. Give each contractor one observed training installation using a written site-qualification checklist, installation specification, commissioning test, troubleshooting tree, and escalation rule. Do not count training as proof. After training, require each contractor to complete two consecutive paid installations without founder attendance or founder execution of the survey or commissioning. Founders may receive a documented escalation, but any intervention that changes the outcome counts as rescue and fails the independence gate.

4. Instrument every redesigned unit for commissioning, peak-event operation, faults, downtime, support, and customer outcomes, with customer consent and minimal necessary data collection. Track compressor supplier and lot, contractor, site/load, weather, incentives, and any product or installation change as confounders. Bench and accelerated tests may reduce risk, but they cannot substitute for the promised cold-season field interval.

5. Review the cohort weekly for operations, after every installation for independence, at 30 and 90 days for use, and at the first reliable cold-season checkpoint in five months for the channel decision. Do not run a nationwide preorder campaign in the meantime. If a small regional preorder is later used to schedule qualified demand, it must be refundable, disclose the unproven delivery window and hardware revision, and never be reported as retained use.

The measured path should be contractor source → store encounter → qualified request → survey → installable site → paid order → delivery → commissioning without founder rescue → operation on relevant peak days → 30/90-day and four-month retained use → second unit or attributable referral. The existing baselines are nine observed operating stores out of 10 paid stores, eight retained users out of the nine observed operators, and four expanding stores out of 10 paid stores. Report store and unit denominators separately.

The board's weekly dashboard should show every numerator and denominator by contractor and cohort: qualification and survey conversion, paid orders, time from order to commissioning, first-pass commissioning, founder interventions and minutes per site, support hours, fault/downtime and warranty events, peak-day use, retained use, second-unit orders and referrals, delivery performance, and contribution after hardware, contractor, freight, subsidy, warranty, and support. Signups alone are not a decision metric.

## Precommitted branches

**Expand regionally, not nationally, only if all gates pass:** both contractors complete their two consecutive scored installations without founder rescue; commissioned units meet the predeclared acceptance test and truthful delivery window; the redesigned batch has no serious safety or reliability event and completes the relevant cold-season field interval; retained use is not materially worse than the current 8-of-9 operator baseline; and unit contribution remains nonnegative after all channel and support costs under bounds set before the cohort. Customer energy/reliability outcomes must also support the sales claims being made. If those conditions hold, test one adjacent regional contractor or a few distributor branches with another capped tranche before reconsidering national rollout.

**Keep manual and bounded** if customers retain and expand but contractor independence is still improving without safety, cash, or truth risk. Continue only the evidence cohort; document founder work and convert it into training, product, or tooling rather than hiding it in channel economics.

**Repair** if any scored installation needs founder rescue, misses commissioning acceptance, or produces a delivery/support burden outside the predeclared bound. Stop adding sites, identify whether the cause is qualification, contractor training, product design, supplier quality, or support tooling, and rerun the scored sequence after the fix. Product owns the hardware correction; engineering owns commissioning machinery; growth owns the channel cohort.

**Pause immediately** for a serious safety or reliability event, compressor-lot drift, a delivery promise the company cannot substantiate, unconsented monitoring, or a survival-case breach. Do not acquire around a product or trust failure.

**Stop this channel path** if the distributor will not accept milestone terms, if either contractor still cannot commission independently after two documented process/product repair cycles, or if fully loaded contribution remains negative without a predeclared and affordable learning subsidy. Return unexpected non-use or weak customer outcomes to customer learning rather than explaining them away as an acquisition problem.

This preserves the national distributor as an option, with a clear switch condition: reopen a larger branch commitment only after installer independence, redesigned-hardware cold-season reliability, retained customer value, supportability, and fully loaded economics are demonstrated in the bounded regional loop.
