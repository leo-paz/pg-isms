# 30-day release plan

The 30-day goal is not “launch the new interface to 600 clerks.” It is to prove that the web notification flow gets urgent cycle-count exceptions handled faster in the paid-pilot segment without breaking the installed queue workflow. Ship the smallest complete path: surface an urgent exception, let the right clerk open and resolve it, record acknowledgement and resolution, and fall back to the current email process. Defer the six-week rules-engine rewrite and any broad campaign.

## Release record and gates

- **State and evidence:** This is a user-visible first release embedded in a redesign of an established queue. The need is supported by six warehouses using manual SMS for two monthly cycles; willingness to pay is supported by three paid pilots. The web implementation itself is not yet validated.
- **Target job:** A clerk or supervisor notices, acknowledges, and resolves an urgent cycle-count exception during the working shift. The initial cohort is the three paid-pilot warehouses, including experienced keyboard-heavy clerks on representative shifts.
- **Scope:** Web notification, acknowledgement, deep link into the existing queue, resolution status, delivery/audit telemetry, feature flag, and a one-step return to email-only operation. Preserve the existing queue's shortcuts, focus order, interruption recovery, and card information hierarchy. If the new card layout cannot meet those requirements by day 8, keep the old cards and add only an urgent marker or notification panel.
- **Deferred:** Rules-engine rewrite, non-urgent notification types, new queue taxonomy, mandatory migration, all-warehouse launch, and broad acquisition campaign.
- **Risk posture:** Alert mistakes are described as reversible, but authorization, privacy, data integrity, and sending an alert to the wrong warehouse are release gates, not acceptable experiments. Existing email stays active during the pilot, and the web path is feature-flagged by warehouse and user.
- **Evidence window:** One complete monthly cycle after the pilot cohort is exposed. If the calendar does not contain a complete cycle by day 30, day 30 is a continuation decision, not an expansion decision.

The following numerical gates are assumptions to confirm with baseline data and the three pilot customers by day 3:

- **Pass:** At least a 20-percentage-point improvement in urgent exceptions acknowledged within 15 minutes, at least a 30% reduction in median acknowledgement time, and no degradation greater than 5% in ordinary queue task time. At least 95% of urgent alerts reach an eligible user, 99% of recorded acknowledgements attach to the correct exception, and experienced-clerk shortcut completion is at least 99% in tested core paths.
- **Change and repeat:** The alert outcome improves but misses either outcome threshold, or ordinary queue time degrades by 5–10%, with no safety or integrity trigger. Make the smallest discriminating change—such as alert placement, focus behavior, or training—and repeat with the same bounded cohort.
- **Pause or rollback:** Any cross-warehouse exposure, unauthorized access, lost or corrupted queue action, or wrong-exception acknowledgement; two urgent exceptions missed because of the new flow; ordinary queue time degradation above 10% over two representative shifts; a critical shortcut failure without a practical workaround; or web delivery below 95% for four hours. Disable the web flag, continue email, preserve logs, and notify pilot leads. The product lead owns rollback; engineering owns diagnosis; customer success owns customer communication.

## Calendar

**Days 1–3 — lock the record and baseline.** The product lead is the release DRI and records cohort membership, exact inclusion/exclusion rules, comparison, thresholds, support rota, rollback procedure, and day-14/day-30 reviews. Analytics captures the prior two cycles' acknowledgement and resolution times, missed exceptions, exception volume, ordinary queue task time, shortcut use, shift, warehouse, and support contacts. Compare the web cohort with its own email/SMS baseline and, where timing allows, a staggered not-yet-enabled pilot warehouse. Account for exception mix, staffing, shift, training, and simultaneous process changes rather than attributing every difference to the interface.

Engineering threat-models warehouse/user scoping, tests the alert-to-exception mapping, and verifies feature-flag isolation and audit logs. Customer success names one supervisor and a backup at each paid-pilot warehouse and schedules the cycle window. Growth may prepare targeted pilot onboarding and interview materials because payment has validated commitment, but it does not start a broad campaign.

**Days 4–8 — harden the smallest path.** Engineering finishes only the notification-to-resolution path, telemetry, monitoring, warehouse/user flags, and email fallback. It does not begin the rules-engine rewrite. Product and design test the flow with 8–12 representative clerks, weighted toward experienced shortcut users and covering multiple shifts. Tests include keyboard-only completion, focus restoration after interruption, old/new card comprehension, duplicate alerts, stale alerts, and returning to the queue. Fix core-path failures. Keep the old layout if compatibility is not demonstrated.

**Days 9–10 — readiness rehearsal.** Run production-like replay or shadow delivery using real-shaped, access-controlled data without replacing email. Confirm that notification recipients, deep links, and acknowledgement records agree with the source exception. Customer success rehearses the support and rollback script with each pilot supervisor. Product signs the release checklist only if security, authorization, privacy, integrity, core path, monitoring, support, and fallback gates pass.

**Days 11–14 — bounded release.** Enable one paid-pilot warehouse for one shift, then two shifts, with email still active. Start with named clerks who have been briefed on the pilot and fallback. Product and engineering watch delivery, acknowledgement mapping, queue latency, shortcut failures, abandonment, and support requests in real time. If the pause rules remain clear for 48 hours, enable the other two paid-pilot warehouses in stages. The CEO's two-week milestone is this bounded, monitored release—not a mandatory release to all 600 clerks.

**Days 15–23 — observe real work and make at most small discriminating changes.** Customer success collects structured support issues and observes both experienced and less-experienced clerks completing actual work. Analytics reports outcomes by warehouse, shift, experience, and exception severity; it separates duplicate email/web exposure and training effects. Product reviews daily operational health but does not move the precommitted outcome gates. Changes during the evidence window are limited to fixes or clearly logged small variants; a material workflow change restarts the evidence window.

Growth owns activation within the paid pilots and may recruit only the next matching, bounded cohort. It can draft the broad campaign, but it cannot publish claims or scale demand based on manual-SMS results as if the released web workflow were proven.

**Day 24 — evidence checkpoint.** Product, engineering, customer success, analytics, growth, and the three pilot supervisors review the record. A stop trigger causes immediate rollback. Weak-but-safe evidence produces one small change and another bounded cycle. Strong early evidence permits planning the next cohort, but not an all-600 mandate before the full monthly evidence window.

**Days 25–30 — complete the cycle and decide.** Keep exposure stable through the trustworthy observation window. On day 30, the product lead makes and records one branch:

1. **Pass:** Keep the three pilots live, expand next to a small cohort of experienced and new clerks under the same fallback, and hand growth ownership of targeted activation/retention for that proven segment. Engineering may then propose rules-engine work against observed bottlenecks rather than as a launch prerequisite.
2. **Change:** Preserve the pilot and email fallback, make the smallest evidence-led workflow or migration change, and schedule another cycle. Product retains exposure ownership.
3. **Pause/rollback:** Return affected users to email, retain logs and customer support, and give engineering the named reliability or compatibility problem. Product does not silently redefine the need.
4. **Incomplete window:** Continue the same cohort until one full monthly cycle completes; do not treat calendar day 30 or launch completion as evidence.

The CEO owns the business appetite for investment, but the product lead owns the release record and user exposure. Engineering owns implementation quality, architecture, and rollback mechanics; customer success owns pilot support and migration communication; analytics owns measurement integrity; growth owns activation and later expansion only after the released workflow passes its gate. No owner may convert the day-14 milestone into a mandatory all-user launch without explicitly accepting a new release record and its unvalidated workflow and migration risk.
