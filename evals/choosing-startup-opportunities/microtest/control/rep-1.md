# Two-week opportunity decision plan

Choose Option C as the working hypothesis and do not spend the $30,000 on ads. C has founder insight, repeated observed pain, direct access to users, and four credible pilot buyers. A is imitation without problem evidence. B combines weak access with a safety-critical domain, so it should be parked unless the team gains clinical expertise, hospital access, and a viable safety and regulatory path. Ad clicks would mostly test messaging and traffic acquisition, not whether any of these problems is urgent or whether the team can solve it.

In days 1–2, agree on a scorecard: problem frequency and cost, user access, willingness to pay, founder advantage, time to a trustworthy pilot, and downside risk. Record the current evidence for all three options and explicitly time-box C rather than treating it as irrevocably chosen.

In days 3–7, interview the eleven controllers while observing several live reconciliation sessions. Map the workflow, exceptions, systems involved, hours lost, error costs, approval constraints, and current workarounds. Ask the four prospective buyers for a concrete paid-pilot commitment: named sponsor, price, data-access requirements, success metric, and start date. Build only a lightweight concierge or clickable prototype around the most common painful step.

In days 8–12, run the prototype on historical or tightly controlled customer data with a human checking every result. Measure time saved, exception coverage, accuracy, setup effort, and whether the sponsor still agrees to pay. Keep the pilot narrow enough that mistakes cannot silently alter financial records.

On days 13–14, hold a decision review. Proceed with C if at least two customers commit to a paid pilot, the team can access the needed data, and the test shows meaningful time savings without unacceptable error or integration risk. Otherwise revise the target workflow once or reopen the opportunity search; do not compensate for weak evidence with ad spend.

The ordered handoff is: opportunity selection → focused customer discovery and workflow validation → pilot/MVP scoping and safety controls → product execution. A returns to problem discovery only if real users surface the pain independently; B first requires a clinical-access and safety/regulatory feasibility handoff before any product work.
