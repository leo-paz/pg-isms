For the next two weeks:

1. On day 1, agree on decision criteria: frequency and cost of the problem, urgency, buyer authority, data and workflow access, willingness to pay, founder advantage, implementation difficulty, and downside risk.
2. During days 1–5, interview all eleven controllers about their latest reconciliation cycle. Observe at least five doing the work, map inputs and exceptions, quantify hours, errors, delays, and current workarounds, and identify who owns budget. Avoid pitching until the workflow is understood.
3. During days 4–9, run a manual concierge test on historical or non-sensitive sample data for one narrow reconciliation task. Measure time saved, accuracy, exception coverage, and how much human review remains. Do not automate broadly yet.
4. During days 7–11, ask the four willing controllers for a paid pilot with a defined price, data-access terms, success metric, and start date. Treat compliments and unsigned interest as weak evidence.
5. During days 10–12, perform a lightweight risk review: data security, integration constraints, error recovery, and whether the workflow can be served without creating unacceptable financial or operational risk.
6. On days 13–14, decide. Continue with C only if the problem is consistently painful, at least two buyers authorize a paid pilot, the team can access representative data, and the concierge test shows meaningful improvement with manageable risk. Otherwise, record what failed and resume opportunity search instead of defaulting to A or B.
