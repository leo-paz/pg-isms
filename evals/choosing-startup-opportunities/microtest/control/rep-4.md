Choose Option C as the working hypothesis. It has founder knowledge, an observed recurring problem, reachable users, and early willingness to pay. Park A unless real users independently surface the problem. Reject B for now: the team lacks access to the workflow, and the harm from mistakes makes it a poor first experiment. Do not spend the $30,000 on ads; clicks would mostly measure creative and targeting, not whether the team can solve a durable problem.

Two-week plan:

1. **Days 1–2 — define the decision.** Set written criteria: repeated pain, reachable buyer and user, access to representative data, a narrow solvable workflow, measurable value, and credible willingness to pay. Interview the eleven controllers and confirm frequency, time spent, error cost, current workaround, decision-maker, and procurement constraints. Ask the four interested controllers what “pay for a pilot” specifically means: amount, signer, timing, and required approvals.
2. **Days 3–5 — observe the work.** Shadow at least five reconciliations and collect sanitized examples. Map the common steps, exceptions, systems involved, and security or integration blockers. Choose one narrow reconciliation task that can be tested safely with a human reviewing every output.
3. **Days 6–10 — run a concierge pilot.** Build the smallest manual or lightweight prototype needed to complete that task for two to four controllers. Measure completion time, accuracy, exception rate, time saved, repeat usage, and whether the customer would trust it in the weekly workflow. Avoid automating irreversible actions.
4. **Days 11–12 — test the commercial commitment.** Present the measured result and ask for a paid follow-on pilot, deposit, or signed pilot agreement. Speak with five controllers outside the founders’ former company to check that the problem is not company-specific.
5. **Days 13–14 — decide.** Commit to C only if the pain recurs, the team can access the workflow and data, the narrow test creates measurable value, and multiple buyers make concrete paid commitments. Otherwise narrow the problem or stop; do not use ad clicks to manufacture a positive signal.

Ordered handoff: once C clears the opportunity decision, hand it first to customer-centered product discovery and prototype iteration, then to founder-led sales after users repeatedly obtain value. If it fails the decision criteria, return to opportunity search with the interview evidence rather than handing off an unvalidated idea.
