# Raw guided output

Decision date: 2026-07-28.

Choose Option C as the direction to deepen, but make the company decision conditional on a two-week validation gate. It starts with unusually strong evidence: two founders know the workflow, eleven named controllers still perform it weekly, and four have offered a discriminating commitment—payment. Do not spend the $30,000 on broad ads. Clicks would test message response, not whether controllers have an urgent reconciliation problem or will adopt a solution.

## Candidate dispositions

- **C — deepen.** Initial wedge: reconcile one common invoice type against one common system for logistics controllers, with exceptions reviewed by a human. Falsifier: the apparent pain is infrequent or cheap, access to representative data is unavailable, or fewer than three of the four prospects sign and pay for a narrowly defined pilot after seeing its limits.
- **B — stop.** Conference hearsay, no hospital access, and severe harm make live-output experimentation inappropriate. Falsifier for the stop: the team obtains a clinical partner, a qualified safety owner, retrospective de-identified cases, and approval for a monitored, non-care-affecting evaluation. Until then, produce no patient-facing output.
- **A — discard.** Funding news supplies neither a named user nor an observed problem. Reconsider only if direct observation uncovers a recurring meal-planning workaround, measurable cost, and users willing to make a predeclared commitment; ad clicks alone do not qualify.

## Two-week plan for C

**Days 1–2: freeze the record and test.** Name the first segment and exact invoice/system pair. For all eleven controllers, record frequency, time spent, error/rework cost, current workaround, data-access constraints, and adoption effort. Write the hypothesis before gathering more evidence: “Logistics controllers who reconcile this invoice/system pair weekly will let us handle a real batch and pay for a human-reviewed pilot because the current workflow costs at least three staff-hours per week.” Keep expansion into other invoice types or industries as a risk, not part of the first test.

**Days 3–6: observe the workflow.** Shadow at least six of the eleven controllers through a real reconciliation cycle. Collect dated measurements rather than opinions: steps, minutes, exception types, error consequences, handoffs, and security or integration blockers. Ask the four prospective payers for representative data and a signed pilot agreement with actual payment or a refundable deposit.

**Days 7–11: run a concierge pilot.** Manually or with a deliberately crude internal tool, process one bounded batch for each participating controller. Require human confirmation before any downstream action. Measure completion time, correction rate, exceptions, controller intervention, and whether the result replaces part of the workaround. Do not automate beyond what is needed to learn.

**Days 12–14: decide against predeclared gates.** Deepen C only if at least three prospects pay, at least four representative workflows were observed, the pilot cuts measured handling time by at least 50% without worse errors, and one narrow data path is feasible. Stop or revise the segment/wedge if payment or adoption fails; stop if safety, access, or integration makes the wedge unusable. Review expansion only after this gate.

Use at most a small, named pilot budget for secure data handling and the concierge test. Preserve the remainder of the $30,000 until need and adoption—not clicks—are demonstrated.

Hand primary ownership now to `learning-from-users` with the written hypothesis above, the eleven-controller sample, the observation and paid-pilot thresholds, and the 2026-07-28 review date.
