# Two-week opportunity decision plan

## Initial dispositions

- **Option C — deepen.** The founders have direct workflow knowledge, eleven named users still perform the job weekly, and four have offered to pay for a pilot. The initial wedge is invoice reconciliation for logistics controllers. Record the current workaround, time and error cost, adoption burden, buyer, and the dated enabling change behind any “why now” claim; none of those should be invented from the current evidence. A plausible expansion path can be tested later across adjacent accounts-payable exception workflows.
- **Option A — discard.** Funding news and ad clicks are neither an observed problem nor demand evidence. Reconsider only if the team later finds a named user with a costly recurring workaround that the team can access and understands.
- **Option B — stop.** It is a second-hand conference idea with no hospital access and high downside. Do not expose patients or make diagnostic decisions. Reopen only if the team first gains a clinical partner and workflow access, then can define a reversible, monitored, non-patient-harming test and a concrete need.

Do not spend the $30,000 on ads. Clicks would compare messages and targeting, not establish that any problem is urgent or that the team can deliver safely. At most, a tightly bounded campaign could later recruit a predeclared research sample under the learning plan.

## Days 1–14: test Option C

**Days 1–2 — freeze the learning plan.** Hand primary ownership to `learning-from-users` with this hypothesis: “Logistics controllers who reconcile invoices weekly have a sufficiently costly workflow that they will commit money, workflow access, and a named operator to a narrow reconciliation pilot.” The sample is the eleven identified controllers, including the four who expressed willingness to pay. Before contact, define success as at least four signed paid pilots, each granting access to a redacted real workflow and naming a weekly operator. Define failure as fewer than four such commitments or evidence that adoption, data access, or exception risk overwhelms the benefit. Set the review for day 14.

**Days 3–5 — observe the work.** Shadow or reconstruct the actual weekly workflow with the sample. Capture volume, time, error and exception cost, present tools, who feels the pain, who pays, required integrations, security constraints, and what a crude version must do to be useful. Ask for the commitment defined above; do not count praise, survey interest, or another promise to pay as a pass.

**Days 6–11 — run the smallest enterprise workflow test.** For committed participants, deliver a manual or lightweight reconciliation service on redacted real invoices rather than building a broad product. Establish each participant’s baseline, then measure time saved, exceptions caught, false matches, operator effort, and whether the controller actually uses the result in the weekly workflow. Keep humans responsible for exceptions and make the pilot easy to reverse.

**Days 12–13 — test the wedge and its limits.** Collect payment, observed use, renewal intent backed by a concrete next commitment, adoption friction, and reasons for refusal. Check whether the same evidence supports only logistics invoice matching or an adjacent exception-management path. Record unresolved timing and expansion risks separately from proof of the initial need.

**Day 14 — decide.** Deepen only if the predeclared paid-access threshold is met and real workflow use produces meaningful benefit without unacceptable adoption burden. Otherwise diagnose whether the failed assumption was problem intensity, segment, access, wedge, timing, or delivery. Preserve the workflows and constraints learned; narrow or test an evidence-supported adjacent problem instead of jumping to A or B.

## Ordered handoff

The immediate handoff is to `learning-from-users`, with the hypothesis, eleven-controller sample, four-paid-pilot threshold, workflow measures, and day-14 review above. If that gate passes, hand the validated wedge to `shipping-and-iterating-products` for the smallest repeatable release, then to `designing-business-models` for buyer, pricing, and sales architecture. `Acquiring-and-growing-users` becomes primary only after the segment demonstrates real use and payment; until then, acquisition activity may only recruit the defined learning sample.
