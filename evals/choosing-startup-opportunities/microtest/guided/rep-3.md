## Decision now

Do not spend the $30,000 on ads. Clicks would measure message response and perhaps help recruit a research sample; they would not distinguish curiosity from an urgent problem, successful adoption, or willingness to pay. The team already has much cheaper and more discriminating access.

Use these dated dispositions on July 14, 2026:

- **A — discard.** It has no named user, observed problem, workaround, cost, founder advantage, or evidenced “why now”; funding news is not demand evidence. Reopen only if firsthand observation reveals a specific user repeatedly struggling with a costly meal-planning job and willing to test a crude solution.
- **B — stop.** It is conference hearsay, the team lacks hospital access, and an error could harm a patient. Reopen only with a clinical owner, access to an observed workflow, and a safe retrospective or non-diagnostic test in which outputs cannot affect care. A production prediction trial is not an acceptable first test.
- **C — deepen.** It begins with founder experience, eleven reachable users doing a weekly workaround, and four stated pilot buyers. The provisional wedge is the narrowest recurring invoice-reconciliation workflow shared by those controllers. Its falsifier is that direct observation shows low cost or infrequency, materially different workflows, inadequate data access, or fewer than three firms making the predefined paid-pilot commitment.

## Two-week plan: July 14–28

**Days 1–2: freeze the learning contract.** Give `learning-from-users` this hypothesis: “Controllers at logistics companies repeatedly spend material time resolving the same class of invoice-reconciliation exceptions, and a narrow assisted workflow can reduce that burden enough that at least three firms will pay for a monitored pilot.” Sample all eleven controllers, including the four apparent buyers, across as many companies and workflow variants as access permits. Predefine the evidence threshold: observe at least six real reconciliation sessions; document frequency, time, error/rework cost, current tools, adoption constraints, and data/security approvals; find one repeated exception class across at least three firms; and obtain at least three signed pilot commitments with a real price or deposit. Review on July 28.

**Days 3–6: observe before proposing.** Shadow the actual weekly work and collect de-identified examples where permitted. Separate the problem, segment, access, timing, and delivery assumptions. Record the smallest useful wedge, the adoption effort, disqualifiers, and dated evidence for any claimed enabling change. Do not pitch a broad automation platform or count compliments as evidence.

**Day 7: gate the build.** If the repeated workflow and access thresholds pass, choose one exception class and hand a precise workflow to `shipping-and-iterating-products`. If they fail, stop the build and use the preserved observations to test one adjacent reconciliation problem; do not jump to A or B merely to keep moving.

**Days 8–12: run a concierge pilot, not a blueprint.** With explicit human review and rollback, process a small batch for the committed firms and measure baseline versus pilot cycle time, corrections, completion, and operator effort. Keep the product narrow. Treat possible expansion into adjacent exception resolution or close workflows as a hypothesis, not part of this gate.

**Days 13–14: make the decision.** Mark C `deepen` only if the observation, shared-workflow, access, and paid-commitment thresholds pass and the pilot shows a concrete benefit. Otherwise mark it `stop` with the failed assumption and retained learning. Record remaining timing, integration, data-access, and expansion risks plus the next falsifier.

## Ordered handoff

1. **Now: `learning-from-users`** owns the explicit hypothesis, sample, evidence threshold, interpretation, and July 28 review.
2. **After the observation gate: `shipping-and-iterating-products`** owns the smallest monitored concierge pilot.
3. **Only after real use and payment: `acquiring-and-growing-users`** owns a narrow beachhead and repeatable acquisition; ads may then test a channel, not choose the company.
4. **After credible need is established: `designing-business-models`** owns durable pricing, sales, and channel architecture.
