Reviewer ID: choosing-baseline-001

Do not rank the five labels yet. They are solution categories borrowed from funding news, not evidence of a worthwhile problem. The job for the next two weeks is to find a specific group of people with a recurring, costly problem that the founder can understand unusually well and reach repeatedly.

In days 1–2, list three reachable communities where she has firsthand access or domain knowledge, recruit five people from each, and define observation questions around recent behavior rather than reactions to pitches. In days 3–7, conduct fifteen interviews and, where possible, watch people complete the troublesome workflow. Record frequency, current workaround, time or money lost, who owns the problem, and evidence that they have already tried to solve it. Do not mention the five products until the end, and treat enthusiasm as weaker evidence than behavior.

In days 8–10, cluster the observations into at most three problem statements and recruit three additional people per cluster to test whether the pattern recurs outside the first contacts. In days 11–13, run a small commitment test for the strongest problem: ask for a paid pilot, a scheduled implementation session, data access, or another costly next step. On day 14, produce an opportunity brief comparing the clusters on pain, frequency, reachable users, founder advantage, and observed commitment. Choose a direction only if one problem has repeated behavioral evidence and a credible next learning step; otherwise run another search cycle rather than selecting from the fashionable menu.
