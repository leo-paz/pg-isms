# Raw evaluation output

Case ID: selected-direction-interviews

Reviewer ID: choosing-forward-001

The primary route is `learning-from-users`. The team has already selected a problem and segment using observed behavior plus paid commitments; opportunity choice should not keep reopening the decision while the exact workflow is unresolved.

Use a behavior-first interview and observation guide:

1. “Show me the last late payment you reconciled from first notice to closed books.” Capture artifacts, systems, handoffs, roles, elapsed time, re-entry, and approval points.
2. Ask where information originates, how it is matched, what exceptions occur, and how errors are discovered and repaired. Request redacted examples rather than opinions.
3. Quantify frequency, labor, delays, write-offs, compliance exposure, and downstream work. Identify who feels the pain, who controls data, who approves a new tool, and who pays.
4. Reconstruct the current workaround and why it persists. Test the smallest useful wedge: which step could be replaced first, what output must be trusted, and what migration or integration effort would block adoption?
5. Close with an actual next commitment—workflow access, a redacted dataset, a prototype session, or the already discussed paid pilot—not a hypothetical purchase question.

Sample 12-15 independent property-management firms across small, mid-sized, and larger portfolios; different accounting systems; owner-operated and finance-team workflows; and both the three design partners and at least six non-committed firms. Include two firms that recently rejected or abandoned a reconciliation tool. Within several firms, observe both the operator and the approver so the team does not mistake user pain for buying authority.

Before interviews, write the hypothesis, sample, interpretation rules, and review date. Opportunity choice resumes only if the study falsifies a direction-level assumption—for example, the painful workflow is not recurrent outside the three friendly firms, no viable initial segment shares a common wedge, required data access makes adoption infeasible, or committed partners withdraw when asked to run real work. Variation in steps or feature requests is normal product-learning input; it is not by itself a reason to restart idea selection.
