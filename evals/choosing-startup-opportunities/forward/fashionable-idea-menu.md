# Raw evaluation output

Case ID: fashionable-idea-menu

Reviewer ID: choosing-forward-001

Do not rank the five labels yet. They are borrowed solution categories, not evidenced opportunities. The job this week is to build a small set of problem-and-user records grounded in the founder's own experience and access, then choose which hypothesis deserves user learning.

Two-week search plan:

- Days 1-2: inventory the founder's domains, people she can reach, workflows she has used, and work she genuinely wants to understand for years. List specific moments when a named user struggled, improvised, paid, waited, or abandoned a task. Treat the five fashionable ideas only as possible enabling technologies, never as evidence of demand.
- Days 3-5: shadow or reconstruct recent work with 10-12 reachable people in the two strongest domains. Ask them to show the last occurrence, current workaround, time or money lost, who owns the pain, and what makes switching difficult. Log observations without pitching a product.
- Days 6-7: form at most three candidate records. Each must name the user, observed problem, workaround and cost, founder fit, adoption effort, smallest useful wedge, plausible outward path, dated why-now evidence, disqualifiers, and one cheap discriminating test. Discard candidates supported only by trends, clicks, or hypothetical enthusiasm.
- Days 8-11: run each test under `learning-from-users`: for example, attempt a sale, deliver the result manually for one demanding user, or ask a user to commit workflow access, time, data, or money. Define the sample and pass threshold before starting. Prefer evidence that users will overcome the real adoption cost.
- Days 12-13: compare actual intensity and founder access. Mark every candidate `deepen`, `stop`, or `discard`, with a falsifier. A narrow group urgently accepting a crude result beats broad polite interest; ambitious upside counts only if the wedge has a believable expansion route.
- Day 14: choose one problem and wedge, not one fashionable category. Record the decisive evidence, timing and expansion risks, and hand `learning-from-users` a hypothesis, sample, evidence threshold, and dated review.

If no candidate clears its precommitted test, the correct decision is another bounded search cycle in the strongest domain—not selecting the least unsupported item from the original menu.
