# Raw evaluation output

Case ID: ambition-versus-urgency

Reviewer ID: choosing-forward-001

Choose B: the inventory workflow for machine shops. Fourteen shops exhibit the same concrete, error-prone workaround; six will overcome adoption effort to pilot an ugly version next month; and the founders have relevant experience plus access to more of the segment. That combination is stronger opportunity evidence than a theoretical market-size claim with no prototype use.

Frame the initial wedge narrowly: replace one costly inventory-spreadsheet reconciliation or error-prevention step for the reachable shop profile. Before expanding the vision, document the workflow, frequency and cost of errors, buyer and operator, integration burden, and why a crude tool is preferable to the current workaround. Use the six pilots under `learning-from-users` to define a sample and a precommitted threshold based on real workflow completion, repeated use, and a discriminating commercial commitment. Record a plausible expansion path into adjacent inventory or production workflows, but do not count that path as validated demand.

The choice is wrong if the apparent urgency fails under real adoption conditions. For example, after seeing the required integration and running live work, the pilots do not use the wedge repeatedly, will not entrust it with the needed data, or will not make the agreed payment or equivalent hard commitment—and the broader reachable sample shows the same failure. That would falsify the segment/wedge or adoption assumptions even though the spreadsheet problem exists.

It would also become wrong relative to A if the wellness direction later produced stronger type-appropriate evidence: a specific reachable user group repeatedly using the prototype, overcoming its actual adoption burden, and making a hard commitment, with founder fit and a credible wedge. Until then, B's narrow urgency is not a retreat from ambition; it is an evidenced starting point whose expansion risk is explicit.
