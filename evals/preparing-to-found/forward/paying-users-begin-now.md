Do not spend six months preparing in the abstract. You have a tested working relationship, urgent users, payment intent, and a product that can be tested now. Choose the **begin** branch: run a bounded operating trial before making every company commitment irreversible.

Your binding unknown is whether the urgency becomes repeatable paid use—not whether you can look like founders. Over the next 30 days:

1. Choose one of the three companies as the first design partner and identify the specific workflow, buyer, success measure, price, and start date.
2. Ask for a paid pilot or signed purchase commitment. Deliver the smallest reliable version, onboard the user yourselves, and observe usage and support burden directly.
3. Split explicit responsibilities for product, customer work, and operations. Record major decisions and note where your four-year relationship has not yet been tested—for example, money, unequal time, or disagreement under customer pressure.
4. Before the trial becomes a company, agree on commitment level, roles, ownership expectations, personal runway, and what happens if one person does not leave their job. Use `forming-founder-partnerships` for the detailed terms.

Set a day-30 gate: continue if at least one customer has paid or made an equivalent firm commitment, the tool solves the promised problem, both of you still want the operating work, and a credible path exists to serve the next two customers. Change scope if users care but delivery is too broad; stop or return to preparation if urgency disappears or the partnership cannot support the commitment. The next owned action is to schedule a paid-pilot scoping call with the most urgent company within 48 hours.
