Decide by mechanism, not by New York’s reputation. The actors are both founders, and the decision is whether customer and investor access in New York exceeds the full family, immigration, cost, and operating-distraction burden relative to deliberate access from Toronto.

First name your industry and the access you actually need during the next 12 months: particular customers, specialized hires, investors, regulators, suppliers, or peers. Separate those mechanisms. Your committed domain investor in Toronto weakens a generic “capital requires New York” claim; it does not answer whether target customers or specialized talent are unusually concentrated there.

Create a two-column evidence record:

- **New York advantage:** named target customers reachable there, meetings or sales cycles that fail remotely, required talent unavailable in Toronto, investors uniquely suited to the next round, and the expected value and timing of each advantage.
- **Full move cost:** immigration feasibility and timing, housing and compensation changes, effects on the founder with young children, loss of Toronto relationships, and the customer time consumed by moving.

Then run a reversible six-week access test while staying in Toronto. One founder can spend two concentrated weeks in New York, with pre-booked customer meetings and investor conversations, while the other protects product and family continuity. Also test substitutes: monthly customer trips, a local salesperson or adviser, a coworking base, and deliberate investor travel. Track qualified customer conversations, sales-cycle movement, recruiting leads, investor follow-ups, cost, and product disruption.

Set the rule before seeing results: move only if the test produces a material, repeatable customer or talent advantage that cannot be captured through the substitutes, the immigration path is viable, and both founders judge the family and operating cost acceptable. Otherwise stay in Toronto and fund the best access substitute. Review at the end of six weeks and again before the next financing or hiring phase, because funding geography, remote norms, and industry clusters change. The next action is for one founder to draft the named-access hypothesis and book the New York test by Friday.
