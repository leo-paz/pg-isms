Treat the next year as a sequence of readiness tests, not as a year of performing “startup.” Your decision is whether founding is the right next step after graduation; you do not need to commit to a company now.

Start with an honest inventory: things you have built and owned end-to-end, time spent with real users, domains you understand, people with whom you have done difficult work, financial runway, and any health, family, immigration, or career constraints. The binding unknown is probably not your age or credentials. It is whether you like the work of finding a real problem, shipping under ambiguity, supporting users, and persisting with a collaborator.

Use a prepare branch with three owned-work cycles:

1. In the next two weeks, choose a domain you can access and interview 10–15 people doing real work in it. Look for a repeated costly problem; do not begin with a pitch.
2. Over the following 8–10 weeks, build the smallest useful solution, put it in users’ hands, and personally support it. Set an evidence target such as five weekly active users, three repeat users, or one user willing to pay.
3. Repeat with the same domain if evidence compounds; otherwise change the problem, not merely the branding. Complete at least one demanding cycle with a possible collaborator so you learn how you divide work and handle setbacks.

Spend the remaining months deepening whichever signal is strongest: work inside the domain, continue serving users, or join a small operating startup if firsthand company experience is the highest-value gap. Avoid pitch competitions, speculative fundraising, indiscriminate networking, and startup credentials that do not create ownership, users, or difficult execution.

Set dated gates now: review after each 10-week cycle and make the graduation decision three months before graduation. Continue toward founding only if you have evidence of sustained motivation, a problem or urgent users worth serving, a collaborator tested through real work, and workable personal runway. Otherwise join a strong startup or pursue another path while preserving the option to found later. Your next owned action is to book the first five domain interviews by next Friday.
