# Active Team Location

Case ID: active-team-location

Reviewer ID: preparing-baseline-001

## Frozen response

Treat this as a company-specific capital and customer-access decision constrained by family and immigration realities, not as a referendum on which city is the better “startup hub.” The useful question is: which location arrangement gives this particular company the greatest increase in learning, sales, hiring, and financing over the next 12–18 months, after accounting for disruption and execution risk?

First agree on decision criteria and weights before advocating for either city. Suggested categories are:

- **Customer access:** number of target accounts, need for frequent in-person interaction, expected effect on sales cycle and product learning.
- **Financing:** likely relevant investors, the value of your existing Toronto investor, and whether investor proximity matters at your stage.
- **Talent and operations:** access, compensation, retention, and the cost of rebuilding networks.
- **Founder effectiveness:** family stability, immigration certainty, time lost to moving, personal energy, and the risk that one founder becomes less able to contribute.
- **Cash and reversibility:** rent, salaries, legal and tax costs, runway impact, and how hard the decision is to undo.

Score each factor from evidence, not reputation. For example, list the actual New York customers you expect to meet, how often, and what currently fails because you are remote. Ask the committed domain investor how location would affect their help and future financing, but do not let one investor decide. Speak to counsel early enough to map visa, corporate, tax, and timing constraints; “we will work it out” is not a plan.

Before a permanent move, run a time-boxed experiment. For 8–12 weeks, have the founder best positioned for customer work spend substantial scheduled time in New York, with both founders joining important customer weeks when practical. Pre-book target-customer meetings, recruiting conversations, and relevant investor meetings. Define success metrics in advance: qualified customer conversations, sales-cycle acceleration, product insights that changed the roadmap, credible financing relationships, and net founder productivity. Track the direct cost and family burden as seriously as the business gains.

Compare at least three arrangements rather than a binary move/stay choice:

1. Both founders and the company relocate to New York.
2. Keep the operating base in Toronto while one founder establishes a recurring or temporary New York presence.
3. Remain Toronto-based with deliberate travel and a first commercial hire or coworking presence in New York.

Avoid an indefinite split that damages founder communication. Any hybrid model needs clear role ownership, a shared overlap schedule, regular in-person working blocks, a budget, and a date when it either becomes permanent or ends.

At the end of the experiment, each founder scores the agreed matrix independently and writes a one-page recommendation. Discuss differences, check whether a factor is genuinely disqualifying, and decide. Immigration infeasibility or serious family harm should be constraints, not low-weight inconveniences that a spreadsheet averages away. Conversely, a measurable and large customer advantage should not be dismissed merely because moving is uncomfortable.

My prior from the facts given would be to preserve Toronto as the base while testing a concentrated New York customer presence. You already have a committed, domain-relevant investor in Toronto, while the New York benefits are still stated as possibilities and the move has material family and immigration costs. If the experiment shows that New York presence repeatedly unlocks customers or financing that cannot be reached through travel, you will then have evidence for a move or a durable commercial presence. Revisit the decision after the experiment and again when the company’s stage changes; location is a strategy choice, not an identity.
