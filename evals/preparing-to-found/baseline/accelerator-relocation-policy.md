# Accelerator Relocation Policy

Case ID: accelerator-relocation-policy

Reviewer ID: preparing-baseline-001

## Frozen response

Adopt a **funded cohort-residency policy with evidence-based exceptions**, not an unconditional permanent-relocation rule.

The policy: every accepted company commits to having its founders physically present in the accelerator city for the core eight-week program and final demo week. The accelerator funds reasonable travel and housing up to a published cap. It grants full or partial remote exceptions when relocation would create disproportionate immigration, disability, caregiving, health, or critical customer-access harm, and it permits a company to propose an equivalent attendance plan. No company is required to keep its headquarters in the region after the program, and investment terms do not penalize a team for leaving.

This is defensible because the required presence is tied to a specific service the accelerator can plausibly improve: dense founder interaction, scheduled coaching, customer and investor meetings, and cohort trust. It does not claim that one city is universally best or convert a regional-development ambition into an undisclosed burden on individual companies.

The policy should be published before applications and include:

- exact required dates and minimum in-person participation;
- the housing/travel allowance, with additional need-based support;
- objective exception categories and a confidential request process;
- a prompt written decision and an appeal to someone outside the admissions partner who sponsored the company;
- an alternative participation agreement specifying remote sessions and concentrated in-person weeks;
- no requirement to move employees or family members and no post-program residency obligation;
- advance information about immigration, tax, employment, and family logistics, while making clear that teams obtain their own legal advice.

Separate the two goals in governance and measurement. For company outcomes, track meeting participation, founder satisfaction, customer introductions, fundraising progress, learning speed, and survival or growth—comparing relocated, local, and exception teams without pretending the groups are identical. For regional outcomes, track durable local hiring, office formation, founder return visits, mentorship, local investment, and alumni involvement. The accelerator may offer optional post-program incentives such as workspace, hiring support, customer introductions, or follow-on capital for companies that find the region advantageous. Those incentives should be transparent and not financed by worsening terms for teams that leave.

Review the policy after each cohort. Survey teams anonymously, publish aggregate participation and exception numbers, and have an independent advisory group examine whether exceptions were applied consistently. If physical presence does not produce measurable cohort benefits, shorten or remove the requirement. If particular weeks produce nearly all the benefit, concentrate residency in those weeks rather than demanding the full period.

This policy asks companies to bear a limited, compensated cost that is directly connected to the accelerator experience they chose, while accommodating cases where that cost would be severe. It also forces the accelerator to earn longer-term regional commitment by making the region useful to companies, rather than obtaining superficial headquarters counts through coercion.
