Do not impose a blanket nine-month relocation requirement. By tomorrow, the accelerator director should recommend a conditional, opt-in local-access policy: fund relocation or structured local residencies only when a team can name a specific mechanism the region materially improves—such as lab infrastructure, regulators, customers, specialized talent, investors, or suppliers—and when that advantage exceeds the team’s full cost.

For each company, record its industry, the needed mechanism, evidence the city improves it, family and immigration constraints, existing customers or capital elsewhere, viable substitutes, and a dated review point. A biotech team might justify a longer local stay because facilities and suppliers are location-bound; a remote SaaS team might meet the same objective through customer visits, hiring, or a shorter residency. Parents and visa holders should not be penalized for constraints unrelated to company quality.

To preserve the funders’ local-impact objective, replace the mandate with measurable commitments such as local hiring, supplier spend, customer pilots, mentor hours, or time spent using regional infrastructure. Offer relocation grants, immigration support, childcare support, and a reversible four-to-six-week residency before any longer move.

Decision rule: approve a nine-month relocation only when the team-specific access benefit is evidenced, cannot be obtained through a cheaper substitute, and still outweighs personal and operating burdens. Otherwise use the least disruptive local-impact commitment.
