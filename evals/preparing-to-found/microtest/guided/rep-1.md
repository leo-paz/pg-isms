# Guided microtest — repetition 1

Recommend rejecting the blanket nine-month relocation mandate and replacing it with a **team-by-team, mechanism-based local-access compact**.

The accelerator’s objective is regional impact; forced presence is only one possible mechanism and imposes unequal costs. Tomorrow, commit to this policy: each accepted company documents its industry, the specific local advantage it needs—such as labs, customers, talent, investors, regulators, suppliers, or peers—and the evidence that being here improves access. The accelerator then offers a subsidized, reversible local trial matched to that mechanism, with a default duration of six to eight weeks and a longer stay only when the measured benefit exceeds the team’s full cost.

For example, a biotech team may justify sustained presence for lab or regulatory access, while a remote SaaS team may meet the same regional goal through local hiring, customer pilots, or recurring cohort weeks. Family, immigration, cost, existing customers, and disruption must be recorded for every team; visa holders receive immigration review before any location commitment. Each plan gets an owner, a measurable local-impact target, and a dated continue/change/stop review.

This is defensible to funders because it ties public support to observable local outcomes without pretending that identical physical presence creates identical value. The accelerator should publish cohort-level results—local jobs, pilots, supplier spend, investment, or partnerships—and revisit the policy after the first review cycle. The next owned action is for the accelerator director to deliver a one-page compact and team assessment template to the city tomorrow, with the first policy review scheduled eight weeks after the cohort begins.
