Recommend rejecting the blanket nine-month relocation mandate and replacing it with a **team-specific local-access compact**: every company must produce measurable local impact, but relocation is required only when a documented local advantage exceeds the team's full costs and cannot be obtained another way.

By tomorrow, give funders a one-page policy with this decision gate for each team:

1. Record the team's industry and the local mechanism it needs—such as a biotech lab, regulated partners, customers, specialized hires, investors, or peers—and the evidence that being here materially improves access.
2. Record the burdens, including family and caregiving needs, immigration restrictions, cost, existing customers or capital elsewhere, and operating disruption. Visa holders should not be required to move without team-specific immigration advice.
3. Test substitutes first: a four-to-six-week residency, scheduled customer or investor visits, local hiring, lab access agreements, or a locally owned pilot.
4. Require relocation only if that reversible test shows a material advantage that substitutes cannot reproduce. Otherwise let the team remain where it operates best while committing to a local milestone.

Make the compact enforceable through outcomes: each team chooses a local-impact milestone—local customers served, hires made, research or supplier partnerships, capital deployed, or founder programming delivered—with an owner, evidence target, and review date. Review the cohort after the initial access test and continue, change, or stop each location arrangement based on observed results.

This is defensible because it preserves the city's legitimate impact goal while avoiding a mandate whose costs and benefits differ sharply between a lab-dependent biotech team, a remote SaaS company, a parent, and a visa holder.
