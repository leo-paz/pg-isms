Recommend rejecting the blanket nine-month relocation mandate. By tomorrow, the accelerator director should propose a conditional local-impact policy: require every company to make a measurable contribution to the region, but require relocation only when a team-specific access advantage justifies its full cost.

For each team, record its industry; the local mechanism it actually needs (such as a biotech facility, customers, specialized hires, investors, suppliers, or regulators); evidence that being present improves access; and the burdens imposed by family care, immigration status, existing facilities or customers, cost, and disruption. Then choose the least costly reversible test—for example, a short lab residency for a biotech team, scheduled customer and investor visits for a remote SaaS team, or a local pilot delivered without moving. Move only if that test shows an important advantage that cannot be obtained deliberately from elsewhere.

Give the city a scorecard based on outcomes rather than residence: local pilots completed, local vendors and hires used, regional customers served, investor or mentor engagements, and founder programming contributed. Each team's agreement should name its experiment, owner, evidence threshold, allowed substitutes, constraints, and a review date before any longer commitment. This proves local impact while avoiding a policy that arbitrarily penalizes parents, visa holders, or companies whose work depends on facilities elsewhere.
