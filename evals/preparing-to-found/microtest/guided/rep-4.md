# Guided Microtest Response

- Variant: guided
- Rep: 4
- Agent task: `/root/prep_micro_guided_4c`

Recommendation: reject the blanket nine-month relocation rule. By tomorrow, have the accelerator director propose a **team-specific local-impact compact**: the program creates access to the city, then each company chooses the lowest-cost way to use it.

For every team, record its industry, the mechanism the city is supposed to improve (such as lab infrastructure, customers, specialized talent, investors, regulators, or suppliers), evidence that local presence improves that mechanism, and the full burdens created by moving—especially caregiving, immigration, cost, existing customers, and operational distraction. Then offer a reversible local-access test, such as a four-to-six-week residency, scheduled customer and investor meetings, temporary lab access, or periodic in-person weeks. Require relocation only if that test shows that sustained presence produces an important advantage that cannot be obtained through a substitute.

Make local impact measurable without equating it with residence: track local pilots, procurement, hires, partnerships, mentor hours, and follow-on activity. Biotech teams may benefit from sustained access to facilities; remote SaaS teams may create equal or greater local value through customers and hiring without moving families; visa constraints must be checked before any commitment.

Set a dated gate after the access test: move, continue a hybrid arrangement, or stay based on observed benefit versus total cost. This gives funders a credible impact mechanism while avoiding a mandate whose burdens differ radically by team.
