# Survival decision — July 20, 2026

## Decision

Do not make the four September hires. Freeze all new hiring and nonessential commitments today. Begin fundraising now, but manage the company as though the seed extension will not close. Preserve the core product team, keep a narrow product roadmap tied to retention and expansion, and do not double sales commissions across the board.

The operating objective is to reach December 31 with at least $500,000 cash and a credible path to lower burn or new financing. Effective August 1, cap monthly cash outflow at $240,000, $70,000 below the current level. At unchanged receipts of $165,000, that produces a $75,000 monthly net burn. If management cannot identify $70,000 of recurring savings from contractors, discretionary programs, recruiting, travel, software, founder compensation, and low-priority roles by August 5, it must propose the smallest role reduction needed to reach the cap, with changes effective no later than September 1.

## Why this is the survival choice

The company has approximately 6.6 months of runway at its current $145,000 burn, before allowing for timing differences or deterioration. Using a simple daily allocation for the remainder of July, cash would be about $909,000 on July 31. With no change, it would fall to roughly $764,000 after August, $619,000 after September, $474,000 after October, $329,000 after November, and $184,000 on December 31.

The approved hires raise burn to $227,000 beginning September 1. On the same assumptions, cash would be about $537,000 after September, $310,000 after October, $83,000 after November, and would run out around mid-December. That plan makes an unsigned November financing responsible for payroll. “Probably” is not a financing instrument, and even a willing investor can be delayed by diligence, documents, holidays, or market conditions.

The operating signals do not justify that risk. MRR grew only $8,000 over four months, about 6.8% total, while monthly logo churn is 3.2%. Gross margin is healthy, but the company is not yet showing the retention or growth required to hire ahead of evidence. The $165,000 in collected receipts is useful, but it should not be treated as durable recurring revenue without reconciling it to $126,000 MRR.

Cutting all product work would also be a mistake. With elevated churn and two imminent renewals, product capacity is part of revenue defense. Doubling commissions indiscriminately would add variable cost before the company has shown that sales capacity, rather than product fit or retention, is the constraint. Any temporary incentive should pay only on collected expansion cash with acceptable terms, not on bookings alone.

## Dated operating plan

**July 20–24:** Inform the board and team that the September hiring plan is suspended. Pause offers, recruiters, and new vendor commitments. Start a 13-week cash forecast updated every Friday, showing cash receipts, payroll, taxes, payables, and downside cases. Reconcile the difference between MRR and collections by customer and payment type. Assign one executive to collections and one to expense reduction.

Open the financing process immediately. Ask the likely extension investors for a written indication, amount, conditions, and timetable, while also contacting alternative insiders and a small set of external investors. Target at least $1.25 million of new cash; at a $75,000 burn this provides substantial execution time and transaction buffer. No hiring or spending decision may count financing until funds have cleared.

**By August 5:** Deliver the $240,000 outflow plan and identify each recurring saving, owner, effective date, and contractual cost. Protect customer support, renewal-critical engineering, security and reliability work, and the small set of product improvements directly implicated in churn or expansion. Stop speculative features, broad brand programs, and work without a named customer or measurable near-term outcome.

**By August 15:** Treat the two renewals, representing $19,000 MRR, as the first decision gate. The CEO should personally sponsor them, document objections, and offer concessions only when the cash and retention economics are explicit. If both renew at economically acceptable terms, keep the $240,000 cap. If either is lost or materially downsized, do not replace the lost receipts with hopeful pipeline: lower the September outflow cap dollar-for-dollar for the expected monthly cash loss and prepare an additional $25,000 monthly contingency reduction.

**By August 31:** Run the founder-led expansion test across all 20 reachable accounts. Record conversations, qualified needs, proposals, signed expansions, incremental MRR, expected collection dates, and product gaps. Success means at least $10,000 of signed incremental MRR across several accounts, with first cash expected by October and no major custom-development obligation. If successful, continue the motion with founders and existing staff and consider a narrowly targeted commission on collected expansion revenue. If unsuccessful, do not add sales expense; use the evidence to refine positioning, retention work, and the financing narrative.

**September 1:** Do not onboard the four planned hires. Confirm that actual August outflow and the September run rate meet the cap. If they do not, execute the preplanned contingency reductions immediately. Maintain a minimum internal cash floor of $400,000: once the forecast shows cash crossing it within eight weeks, only payroll, taxes, essential infrastructure, customer support, and directly collectible revenue work may be committed.

**October 1:** Require either a signed financing term sheet with a credible closing path or operate on the assumption that no extension will arrive in 2026. If there is no term sheet, reduce the November outflow target to $220,000, prioritizing reversible cuts first but making role reductions if necessary. Do not wait until November to discover that November financing slipped.

**October 31–December 31:** Require financing to be funded by October 31 before reversing any restriction. Even after funding, defer the four-hire package until retention and growth gates improve: monthly logo churn below 2% for two consecutive months and net new MRR of at least $10,000 per month for two consecutive months. Review cash and survival actions at every board meeting. By December 15, approve the first-half 2027 plan based only on cash on hand and contracted receipts.

At a $75,000 burn from August through December, the company should end December near $534,000, before renewal or timing variance. That is not comfort; it is enough room to make deliberate choices. The board should approve this revised plan now and record the hiring authorization as suspended, not merely delayed on the CEO’s expectation.
