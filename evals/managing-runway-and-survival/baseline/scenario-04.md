# Decision: run a Series A process, not a survival process

As of July 20, 2026, the primary workflow should be a planned Series A fundraising process designed to capitalize an already working enterprise channel. The cash position does not presently make this a survival problem.

At the current cash figures, net burn is about $120,000 per month ($440,000 of cash expense less $320,000 of collections). A static calculation therefore gives roughly 26.7 months of runway: $3.2 million divided by $120,000. That is conservative in one sense because collections have risen every month, but it is not a forecast. The difference between $2.4 million ARR, equivalent to $200,000 of monthly recurring revenue, and $320,000 of monthly collections also needs reconciliation; annual prepayments, implementation revenue, or collection timing may make the recent cash figure unusually favorable. Even so, the absence of a hiring step, covenants, overdue liabilities, or major concentration removes the common triggers for an emergency runway intervention. One percent monthly revenue churn and nine months of approximately $170,000 in monthly net new ARR further indicate positive operating momentum.

## Runway work that should come first

Spend no more than one week producing a board-ready, 24-month cash model before launching outreach. This is diligence and round-sizing work, not a reason to delay the raise or impose broad cuts. The model should have three cases:

- A base case that converts the existing ARR, churn, gross margin, billing terms, and collection lag into cash by month.
- An acceleration case showing the exact hiring and program spend funded by the Series A, with realistic ramp time and sales productivity.
- A downside case with net new ARR cut by half, collections delayed, and planned growth spending gated by measurable triggers.

The model should reconcile ARR to invoices and collections, separate recurring from nonrecurring cash, include taxes and working-capital timing, and show runway both before and after the proposed growth plan. Establish two operating guardrails: review the plan if forward runway falls below 18 months, and take corrective action before it falls below 12 months. No layoffs or emergency expense freeze are justified by the facts given. Continue normal cost discipline and do not begin the full acceleration hiring plan until the financing closes.

## Round size and dilution

Target an $8 million to $10 million primary round, with a willingness to close nearer $8 million if it fully funds the plan. Treat $12 million as a hard ceiling unless the additional capital has a specific, staged use and does not materially worsen terms. The correct final number is the amount that funds roughly 24 months of the post-close operating plan, reaches a credible next-financing milestone, and leaves at least six months of execution buffer. Likely milestones should include materially higher ARR, demonstrated enterprise sales efficiency across more than the founding team, stable churn and gross margin, and a repeatable implementation and customer-success motion.

Aim for 15% to 20% ownership sold in the financing, inclusive of any option-pool increase negotiated as part of the round. For reference, $8 million at 18% implies about a $44.4 million post-money valuation; $10 million at 20% implies a $50 million post-money valuation. These are negotiation anchors, not entitlements. Do not enlarge the round merely to justify a higher headline valuation, and do not accept more than roughly 25% effective dilution unless the investor and terms are unusually valuable. Model founder and employee ownership on a fully diluted, post-pool basis before agreeing to a term sheet.

## Investor and governance choice

Choose the lead partner, not just the firm or the highest valuation. Rank candidates by direct enterprise go-to-market experience, quality of references from companies that struggled as well as succeeded, speed and candor in decision-making, ability and reserves to support a later round, relevant recruiting reach, fund age, and absence of competitive conflicts. A slightly lower price from a partner who can help build the channel and behaves well under pressure can be superior to a nominally richer offer. Speak privately with at least three founders per serious lead, including one whose company missed plan.

Use a three-seat board initially: one founder/CEO seat, one lead-investor seat, and one genuinely independent seat mutually approved by common and preferred holders. If the founders require a second common seat, defer the independent rather than creating a five-person board prematurely, but preserve a clear method and deadline for adding that independent. Limit observers, especially where competitive information is sensitive.

Economic and control terms should be conventional: 1x non-participating liquidation preference, broad-based weighted-average anti-dilution, standard pro rata and information rights, and no full-ratchet protection, participating preference, redemption right, or cumulative dividend. Protective provisions should cover true investor-class matters such as issuing senior securities or selling the company, not ordinary budgets, hiring, pricing, or routine operating decisions. Review option-pool sizing, founder vesting changes, drag-along provisions, and any pay-to-play language as carefully as valuation.

## Process and timing

Use July 20 through early August to finish the model, cap-table scenarios, data room, metrics definitions, cohort and ARR bridge, enterprise pipeline evidence, customer references, deck, and investor list. Begin warm introductions and selected early meetings in August, then run the main lead meetings in a tightly grouped process from late August through September. Summer schedules make parallel outreach important. Set explicit dates for partner meetings and term-sheet decisions, seek two credible lead paths, and avoid serial conversations that let momentum dissipate.

Plan for eight to twelve weeks from formal launch to signed financing, with another several weeks for documentation and closing. That points to a term sheet in September or October and a close in October or November, well before runway creates negotiating pressure. Reforecast monthly throughout the process. If the raise slips, the company still has time to pause discretionary acceleration and rerun the process; it should not manufacture urgency now. The financing story is straightforward: a capital-efficient company with substantial existing runway is raising from strength to scale a channel supported by nine months of evidence.
