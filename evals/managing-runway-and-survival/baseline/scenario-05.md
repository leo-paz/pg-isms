# Survival plan: July 20–September 30, 2026

The company is in a cash emergency now, not when the bank balance reaches zero. The $1.5 million pipeline is worth zero in the survival forecast until contracts are signed and cash timing is credible. Effective July 20, the CEO should stop all hiring and the rebrand, freeze nonessential purchases and travel, and require board approval for any new commitment. This is not a blanket halt to operations: dispatch, customer support, collections, payroll administration, tax compliance, insurance, and essential hosting remain protected.

## What the numbers say

The controller should publish two daily forecasts: a bank-date base case containing only cash already collected or contractually due from solvent customers, and a downside case in which both invoices are late. Over the next 30 days, known outflows are at least $640,000: $290,000 of payroll, taxes, insurance, and hosting, $115,000 of other commitments, and $235,000 of recurring cash expense. Expected ordinary collected gross profit adds $78,000. If the full $180,000 of accepted invoices arrives on time, the indicative August 20 balance before savings is $238,000 ($620,000 + $78,000 + $180,000 - $640,000). If neither invoice arrives, it is only $58,000. Exact daily timing may produce a lower interim balance.

After that, the stated recurring economics imply at least $157,000 monthly burn if $235,000 is the complete recurring expense figure, and potentially $447,000 if that figure excludes the $290,000 payroll/tax/insurance/hosting bundle. The controller must resolve that definition on July 20. Management must use the higher number until reconciled. Even the favorable August collection case does not provide a safe path through September under the higher interpretation.

## Actions in the first 72 hours

By end of day July 20, establish a cash-control group consisting of the CEO, controller, and operations lead. The controller owns a daily bank reconciliation, a 13-week receipts-and-disbursements forecast by exact date, and a register of every commitment, cancellation right, deposit, and late-payment consequence. No expenditure enters the forecast merely because it is budgeted, and no pipeline enters as cash.

The operations lead should identify the minimum people, carriers, systems, insurance, and vendor payments required to keep dispatch safe and avoid stranding loads. Create a written continuity playbook with on-call ownership, customer communications, backup access, and an orderly handoff procedure. Do not make indiscriminate cuts that disable dispatch.

By July 21, assign a senior owner to each accepted invoice. Obtain written confirmation of the amount, approval status, remittance date, and payment method; ask whether either customer can pay early or make a partial payment. Resolve documentation or portal issues immediately. Financing against receivables may be considered only after comparing fees, recourse, liens, and customer effects.

Also by July 21, give the named qualified advisers the daily forecast and commitment register. Employment counsel should guide any hiring rescissions, furloughs, layoffs, notice, wage, and benefit obligations. Tax, insurance, contract, and insolvency advisers should determine which payments can be delayed, renegotiated, or must be protected. Management should not improvise creditor preferences or continue taking obligations the company may not be able to perform.

By July 22, present the board with a survival budget. Renegotiate or cancel the $115,000 of commitments where lawful, seek landlord and vendor extensions, reduce software and professional services, and prepare a headcount reduction that preserves the dispatch core. Set two controls: a minimum unrestricted cash floor of $200,000 and a target of no more than $100,000 monthly net burn by August 31. These are triggers, not assurances; the board may require a higher floor after reviewing load-related liabilities.

In parallel, start a time-boxed financing and strategic process. Existing investors, credible new investors, lenders, and potential acquirers receive a consistent data room and weekly deadline. A pipeline customer helps only if it signs a commercially sound contract and pays a nonrefundable deposit or accelerated first invoice; verbal confidence does not defer cuts.

## Dated decision branches

**July 24:** If the downside forecast breaches zero or the $200,000 floor before August 20, immediately implement the first advised cost reduction and secure legally documented deferrals. If the floor still cannot be maintained, begin an advised controlled wind-down plan while dispatch remains intact. Do not wait for an invoice due date to prepare it.

**July 31:** Require written payment confirmation for the invoice due August 5 and quantified financing indications. If neither exists, implement the deeper headcount/vendor plan on the adviser-approved timetable. If new financing is only discussed, treat it as unavailable.

**August 5:** Credit only the actual amount received from the invoice due that day; the prompt does not specify how the $180,000 is split, so the controller must track each amount separately. If payment is missing, escalate with the customer that day, revise the forecast to zero receipt until a new written date is verified, halt every non-continuity payment allowed to be halted, and activate the next restructuring step.

**August 12:** If no binding financing term sheet, signed customer cash commitment, or sufficient collected receivable exists to keep the 13-week forecast above the floor, the board chooses between a financed restructuring and controlled sale/wind-down. “Wait another week” is not a third option.

**August 18:** Apply the same cash-received rule to the second invoice. If both invoices have paid and financing is binding, continue only under the survival budget; do not restart hiring or the rebrand. If either remains unpaid, move to the board’s preselected downside path with adviser supervision.

**August 31:** Continue independently only if unrestricted cash stays above the approved floor through September 30, monthly net burn is at or below $100,000, dispatch continuity is staffed, and committed financing or collected customer cash supports the next 13 weeks. Otherwise, execute the sale, restructuring, or wind-down route already prepared, including prompt, coordinated communications to employees, customers, carriers, and vendors.

**September 15:** Reforecast through December 31 using collected cash and binding obligations. Any financing must be closed, not merely under diligence. Any pipeline contract must have a reliable payment schedule and acceptable service burden. A renewed floor breach triggers the advised downside plan immediately.

**September 30:** The board records one of two outcomes: a funded, lower-burn company with protected dispatch and at least 13 weeks of credible liquidity, or an orderly transaction/wind-down already underway. Hiring and brand work can be reconsidered only after that liquidity test is met; survival cash is not a growth budget.
