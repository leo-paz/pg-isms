# Decision for July 20–October 17, 2026

Approve the sales lead’s staged plan: raise outbound spending from $18,000 to $36,000 per month immediately for a 90-day trial, and authorize one salesperson only if the first expanded cohort meets a pre-agreed economic gate. Do not approve unrestricted growth, but do not keep the current freeze either.

The company is not in a runway crisis. It generates about $105,000 of cash each month before any change ($310,000 collected revenue less $205,000 total cash expense) and has $2.4 million in cash with no debt. The current cash balance alone covers about 11.7 months of current expenses even if revenue suddenly went to zero; under the actual stable business, cash is increasing. Doubling channel spend adds only $18,000 per month, reducing the monthly cash surplus to approximately $87,000 before the salesperson. The maximum incremental channel exposure over 90 days is $54,000, or 2.25% of cash. That is a bounded, affordable risk.

The evidence also supports testing scale. Each recent $18,000 cohort produced $72,000 of new annualized gross profit. That equals $6,000 of monthly gross profit and implies roughly three months of payback, consistent with the reported sub-four-month cash payback. It has repeated across three cohorts without harming support performance. Revenue churn is low at 1.1% monthly, gross margin is high at 86%, and ten months of stable or improving results reduce the chance that the company is scaling into a deteriorating base.

Frugality should mean refusing waste, not refusing a repeatable investment with fast payback. Holding spending at an arbitrary number despite strong unit economics preserves cash but sacrifices more expected value than it protects. The remaining uncertainty is whether the channel retains its efficiency at twice the volume. A staged test is the right way to answer that question.

## First 30 days: test the marginal dollar

From July 20 through August 19, spend $36,000 on outbound. Keep the existing $18,000 program intact and separately tag the additional $18,000 so the team can distinguish marginal performance from the performance it would have received anyway. The sales lead should document prospects, opportunities, signed contracts, expected collection dates, discounts, and channel costs in one cohort report. Finance should verify the revenue and gross-profit calculations; support should report response-time performance weekly.

Before spending begins, define “reproduces the payback” precisely. The full $36,000 cohort should produce at least $144,000 of new annualized gross profit, or the separately tracked incremental $18,000 should produce at least $72,000. In either formulation, expected cash payback must remain below four months. Count only signed business with a credible collection schedule, subtract discounts, refunds, direct delivery costs, and expected early churn, and use actual cash receipts as soon as they occur. Pipeline alone does not pass the gate. Because four months cannot elapse during the first month, the hiring gate may use a conservative forecast based on signed contracts and collections already received, but finance must sign off on it.

Also require three operating guardrails: gross margin on the new cohort should remain near the company’s 86% level, support must stay within its existing response target, and the expansion must not cause a material rise in churn or overdue receivables. No single noisy week should stop the trial, but a persistent support breach, evidence of low-quality customers, or a payback forecast above four months should halt the extra $18,000.

Begin recruiting during the first month so the company does not lose another month if the gate passes, but make any offer contingent on the August 19 review. Set the role’s compensation and quota so its fully loaded cost is recoverable from conservative gross profit within twelve months. Do not count uncommitted pipeline toward that calculation.

## Days 31–90: scale only on evidence

If the first-month gate passes, continue the $36,000 monthly channel budget through October 17 and hire one salesperson. Give that person a defined segment, ramp plan, and quota rather than allowing the hire to blur measurement of the channel test. Track the August and September cohorts separately. Review weekly leading indicators, but make budget decisions at the end of each cohort to avoid reacting to normal sales-cycle volatility.

If the first-month gate fails, immediately return channel spending to $18,000 and do not hire. Diagnose whether the marginal spend reached a saturated audience, reduced lead quality, lengthened collection time, or overloaded the team. The original channel remains justified by its three successful cohorts unless its own economics have deteriorated. A failed doubling test is not a reason to shut down a proven base program.

If the gate passes but support performance later misses target for two consecutive weeks, pause the marginal spend until capacity is restored. If either later cohort forecasts payback above four months or produces less than $72,000 of annualized gross profit per $18,000 spent, stop further expansion and revert to the proven level. Conversely, do not increase beyond $36,000 during this 90-day period even if the first results are excellent; two additional cohorts will provide useful evidence about saturation and execution before another step-up.

## October 17 decision

At the end of 90 days, compare all expanded cohorts on spend, signed and collected revenue, annualized gross profit, forecast and realized payback, churn, receivables, sales-cycle length, and support response. Make $36,000 the ongoing base only if at least two expanded cohorts meet the sub-four-month payback gate and the service and retention guardrails remain intact. Retain the salesperson if the channel has enough qualified volume and the person’s ramp evidence supports the twelve-month fully loaded payback standard; evaluate the role as a durable capacity investment, not as a disposable 60-day experiment.

This plan puts at most $54,000 of additional channel cash at risk while the company continues generating cash. It converts the real question—whether the channel scales—into observable evidence, preserves a clear stop mechanism, and removes the founder’s unsupported $18,000 ceiling without abandoning financial discipline.
