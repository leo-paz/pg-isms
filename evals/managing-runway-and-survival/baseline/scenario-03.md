# Survival and milestone plan through January 31, 2027

Management should reject all three proposed actions: do not declare the company default alive, do not order tooling now, and do not hire the five people. At the current $330,000 monthly burn, $1.8 million provides only about 5.5 months before allowing for the study or tooling. The company is pre-revenue, the two-month spending history is too short to establish a durable trend, the pilot letters produce no cash, and an unsigned Series A is not a financing source. The company should be managed as default dead until recurring or contractually committed cash receipts exceed a deliberately reduced spending plan.

## Cash policy

Immediately divide the cash conceptually into three pools: operating cash through January, a study reserve, and a minimum liquidity floor. Use the high study estimate, $950,000, for solvency decisions until fixed-price bids or executed contracts establish a lower number. Set a January 31 minimum unrestricted cash floor of $300,000. Those two protections leave only $550,000 for all operating spending before January 31, which demonstrates that the existing organization is unaffordable.

The target plan should be:

- July 20 through August 31: no more than $200,000, including transition costs and unavoidable commitments.
- September through December: no more than $75,000 per month.
- January overhead, excluding study payments: no more than $50,000.

That uses $550,000 and preserves $1.25 million: the $950,000 study reserve plus the $300,000 floor. These are cash caps, not accounting targets. A 13-week cash forecast should be updated every Friday with actual bank balance, committed payments, payment dates, and variance to cap. Nothing should be counted as cash until it is in the bank, and nothing should be excluded merely because its invoice has not arrived.

Reaching these caps will require an immediate restructuring. Freeze hiring, backfills, bonuses, travel, discretionary software, and work not essential to the January readiness path. Renegotiate or pause facilities, consultants, and lab work; reduce staffing to the smallest team that can preserve the core asset, records, vendor relationships, fundraising process, and study readiness. Management should prepare the reduction plan within five business days and include all separation, wind-down, and contract costs in the August cap. If transition costs make the cap impossible, reduce the later monthly caps by the same amount rather than consuming the study reserve or cash floor.

## Milestones and gates

By August 7, approve a named owner for cash, a payment-approval matrix, the 13-week forecast, a complete obligations register, and the reduced organization. Any new commitment above $10,000, or extending beyond January, should require CEO and board-level cash review.

By August 31, obtain written vendor estimates that break the pivotal study into setup, enrollment or execution, analysis, and closeout payments; identify cancellation exposure and the maximum cash due by month. Also obtain an independent written schedule of the nonclinical, operational, quality, and documentation work management believes is necessary before study start, using the company's existing qualified advisers. The business plan should use the latest date and highest credible cost, while avoiding management claims about clinical or regulatory outcomes.

By September 30, convert the three pilot letters into evidence with economic value: proposed pilot scope, budget, procurement owner, approval path, expected decision date, and whether a refundable deposit or paid preparatory work is possible. The operating forecast may include none of this money until an agreement is executed and cash is received. At the same time, build a financing data room and run multiple financing and strategic-partner conversations; the survival plan must still work with zero proceeds.

By October 31, require three items for a January study plan: a defensible study budget and cash schedule, confirmed operational readiness against the company's professional-adviser plan, and vendor capacity held without material nonrefundable exposure. If projected January 31 cash after the full contracted study commitment would fall below $300,000, the study is not yet funded.

On November 30, make the tooling decision. Do not place the $420,000 order merely because December is the last delay date. Place it only if cash already received—not an expected round or unsigned term sheet—covers the tooling, the high-case study commitment, all operating costs through January, and the $300,000 floor. The order must also be on the critical path to a board-approved milestone. Because cancellation forfeits $252,000, lack of either condition means continue negotiating a staged order, smaller deposit, transferable slot, or cancellation protection, and otherwise decline the commitment.

By December 15, hold the study-start gate. Begin January contracting only if the scope, total price, payment calendar, readiness evidence, and funding are documented and the downside forecast retains the cash floor. If the estimated cost remains a $650,000–$950,000 range, reserve $950,000. A term sheet may justify continued preparation but not irreversible spending. If the gate fails, preserve the vendor relationship and reset the start date; starting an underfunded pivotal program would create a larger survival risk than a delay.

## January outcomes

There are only two acceptable January 31 states. In the funded state, the company has started or is ready to start the study, has reserved its full contracted cost, retains at least $300,000 unrestricted cash, and has not added permanent headcount ahead of workload. In the preservation state, it has deferred the study and tooling, held monthly overhead at or below $50,000, and retained enough cash to pursue a funded restart.

Report weekly cash and monthly runway under three cases: no financing and $950,000 study cost; no financing and $650,000 cost; and financing actually received. The board should revisit hiring only after cash receipt and a workload-based staffing plan. Survival is the governing milestone: no forecasted financing, nonbinding hospital interest, or two-month trend can substitute for banked cash and reversible commitments.
