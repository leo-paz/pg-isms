## Bounded test portfolio

### 1. Voluntary supplier reuse and cross-buyer pull

- Hypothesis and action: outside-participant work has value beyond buyer compulsion. Maya tags every eligible packet and records reminder source, prior packet, buyer, fresh release, completion, and chase touches.
- Operational person: Maya.
- Baseline: 17 of 21 requested packets completed, 11 reused for a second request from the same buyer, five suppliers returned without a reminder, and none completed for a second buyer.
- Bounded exposure: only the current three buyers, eight suppliers, two audit firms, and 21 scheduled packets. A second-buyer observation counts only when a real buyer need exists and the supplier makes a fresh recipient-specific release; Latchline must not manufacture demand or transfer prior answers.
- Metric: supplier second-packet completion without a buyer or Latchline reminder; authentic second-buyer completion; packet completion; and buyer chase touches per eligible packet versus that buyer's pre-pilot sample.
- Confounders: buyer insistence, questionnaire similarity, packet complexity, supplier familiarity, and simultaneous procurement-policy changes.
- Date: instrument July 20; exposure through September 5; read September 18.
- Disconfirming signal: repeats occur only after buyer insistence, no outside participant returns voluntarily, or a fresh second-buyer request is declined.
- Preserved option: the buyer-owned core and tenant-specific exports remain usable if all outside interfaces are removed.
- Pass/change/stop: proposed bounded-pass threshold is at least three suppliers completing a second packet without a reminder and at least two buyers showing fewer chase touches than their own baseline; proposed expansion signal is at least two suppliers each completing for a second buyer under fresh releases. Change to an integrated-core hypothesis if the bounded pass occurs only under buyer compulsion. Stop the platform bet if there is no voluntary repeat and no other recurring complement value, or if a hard limit is breached. Rationale: repeated behavior is more discriminating than stated satisfaction in this small cohort. Error tradeoff: the thresholds risk a false negative from low opportunity, while looser thresholds would mistake compelled workflow for a participant loop. Revision rule: Maya may correct an eligibility or instrumentation definition by August 7, before examining outcome totals; after that, thresholds do not move and low opportunity is reported rather than backfilled.
