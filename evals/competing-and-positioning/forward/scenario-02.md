## Record

Product row: capital-light software plus enterprise/integration and security-sensitive constraints. Messaging and prototypes are reversible within days; permanent free pricing, navigation convergence, and unsafe permission-control changes are harder to reverse. Enterprise commitment evidence may take longer than the 30-day window.

Verified facts:

- The startup has 14 people and 36 paying mid-market customers.
- Users choose it because one operator can resolve cloud permission failures in minutes without waiting for a central security team.
- Practitioner recommendations generate 73% of new deals.
- The incumbent bundles a free, visually similar dashboard and permission-fix button.
- Its workflow still requires central approval and a two-day deployment.

Bounded inferences:

- Practitioner autonomy and elapsed time to resolution—not the button—are the likely causal differentiators.
- The incumbent has chosen `bundle`; its ability and incentive to reproduce operator-level autonomy safely are unknown.
- The referral share suggests a practitioner-led distribution advantage, but its repeatability and economics are unknown.

Unknowns include renewal behavior, competitive losses, customer overlap with the suite, implementation and security-review burden, willingness to keep paying, incumbent development velocity, and whether approval requirements are architectural, policy-driven, or temporary.

Alternative map:

- Manual/status quo: wait for central approval or escalation. It preserves organizational control but delays resolution.
- Direct integrated alternative: the suite’s free dashboard. It lowers visible price and resembles the interface, but retains central approval and two-day deployment.
- Startup: paid, operator-led resolution in minutes, with 36 paying customers and practitioner-driven distribution.
- Same-resource alternative: allocate budget and operational attention to the existing suite or internal security process.
- No platform-native, independent-complement, or ecosystem alternative is established by the facts; retain these as unknowns rather than inventing them.

Advantage chain:

`operator-level authority and workflow -> one-person remediation capability -> resolution in minutes without central waiting -> paid use and practitioner recommendations -> copy barrier unknown -> continuing workflow-learning mechanism unknown`

Advantage maturity: `observed-advantage`. User-visible effect and commitment exist, but no disproportionate duplication barrier or continuing-refresh loop has been demonstrated.

Diagnostic selections:

- Incumbent: `oblique-entry`. Compete on autonomous resolution and deployment latency, not its suite navigation or bundle price.
- Incumbent response: `bundle` observed; end-to-end copying remains unknown.
- Transition evidence: omitted because no technology transition, changed cost curve, or builder migration is established.
- Platform exposure and platform-dependency action: omitted because no external platform dependency is stated.

## Thirty-day execution

### Days 1–3: Preserve the advantage

- Freeze the zero-price, navigation-copy, and parity announcement.
- Use one provisional sentence in private sales and research contexts: “One operator resolves cloud permission failures in minutes, without waiting for central approval.”
- Instrument, where already safely possible, time from failure detection to resolution, number of people involved, approval steps, and deployment time.
- Do not alter production permission controls. All prototypes remain read-only, human-reviewed, monitored, and removable.

### Test 1: End-to-end substitution

Hypothesis: the suite’s similar button does not substitute for the startup’s complete resolution workflow.

- Operational person: designated customer-research lead.
- Baseline: startup resolution in minutes by one operator; suite requires central approval and two-day deployment. Account-level distributions are unknown.
- Bounded exposure: invite all 36 customers; conduct at most 12 practitioner sessions and six paired security-approver sessions. Observe a recent incident record or safe sandbox workflow; make no production permission changes.
- Metric: elapsed detection-to-safe-resolution time, people and approvals required, deployment delay, and whether each alternative completes the same job.
- Confounders: incident severity, pre-existing suite deployment, customer policy, respondent selection, and sandbox-versus-production differences.
- Readout date: 2026-07-24.
- Disconfirming signal: in at least half of eight or more valid paired observations, the suite reaches the same safe outcome with no additional approval or meaningful deployment delay.
- Preserved option: retain current product, pricing, controls, and navigation.
- Pass condition: at least eight valid paired observations, with six or more showing the startup completes the job without the suite’s approval or deployment delay.
- Change condition: the distinction exists only for a narrower incident or customer type; narrow the target segment and claim accordingly.
- Stop condition: a safety limit is breached, or the suite demonstrates end-to-end substitution in at least half of valid paired observations.
- Threshold status: proposed. Six of eight requires a visible majority while tolerating workflow variation. It risks false confidence from a small, favorable sample versus delaying action in a fast response. Revise after the readout using observed variance and segment differences; never generalize beyond the sampled workflows.

### Test 2: Paid commitment against free

Hypothesis: customers will continue making paid commitments because autonomy and resolution time outweigh the free dashboard.

- Operational person: designated sales lead.
- Baseline: 36 customers already pay; renewal, expansion, and competitive-loss rates are unknown.
- Bounded exposure: every otherwise eligible renewal, expansion, or live competitive deployment decision through 2026-08-08, capped at ten. Quote the current paid offer; do not introduce permanent free pricing.
- Metric: signed paid renewal, expansion, or deployment commitment, stated decision reason, and selection of the suite instead.
- Confounders: contract timing, procurement freezes, discounts, relationship strength, suite ownership, and incidents occurring during the test.
- Readout date: 2026-08-08.
- Disconfirming signal: among at least four eligible decisions, two or more choose the suite specifically because free pricing outweighs the approval and deployment differences.
- Preserved option: current paid pricing; later reversible packaging or proof-of-value offers.
- Pass condition: among at least four eligible decisions, three or more make a paid commitment and explicitly identify operator autonomy or resolution time as a decision reason.
- Change condition: customers value the outcome but a specific packaging, procurement, or assurance obstacle prevents commitment.
- Stop condition: no eligible customer makes a paid commitment and the suite achieves committed substitution in at least two decisions.
- Threshold status: proposed. Three of four is intended to distinguish willingness to pay from compliments while limiting exposure. It risks overreacting to a few atypical deals versus waiting through enterprise sales latency. If fewer than four decisions occur, keep the current paid posture, make no market-wide claim, and extend the test through the next four eligible decisions.

### Test 3: Practitioner-forward positioning

Hypothesis: practitioners recommend an autonomy-and-outcome promise more often than generic dashboard language.

- Operational person: designated growth lead.
- Baseline: practitioner recommendations produce 73% of new deals; message-level referral conversion is unknown.
- Bounded exposure: up to 12 consenting practitioner customers, split evenly between two truthful descriptions: operator-led resolution in minutes versus generic permission-dashboard/fix-workflow language. No public parity claim.
- Metric: qualified introductions made within seven days, message comprehension, and the reason the practitioner was willing to forward it.
- Confounders: advocate enthusiasm, account size, existing referral relationships, unequal networks, and small samples.
- Readout date: 2026-07-31.
- Disconfirming signal: generic dashboard language produces as many or more qualified introductions.
- Preserved option: existing referral language and channels.
- Pass condition: at least three total qualified introductions, with the autonomy message producing at least twice as many as the generic message.
- Change condition: practitioners understand the distinction but need different wording, evidence, or a narrower incident example before referring.
- Stop condition: the autonomy message is misunderstood as bypassing required safety controls or produces fewer introductions.
- Threshold status: proposed. A twofold difference is large enough to act on despite the small sample, but risks false positives from network differences and false negatives from low referral timing. Repeat with the groups reversed or revise the message after the first readout before scaling.

### Test 4: Make the outcome provable

Hypothesis: an audit-ready, read-only resolution receipt would make the autonomy advantage credible to central security approvers without recreating central approval.

- Operational person: designated product lead.
- Baseline: no fact establishes that customers lack such proof or that it blocks decisions.
- Bounded exposure: a removable prototype shown to at most eight practitioner/approver pairs; no production permissions, workflow, or navigation changes.
- Metric: request to use the artifact in a real security review, the specific objection addressed, and approver confirmation that it improves assurance without adding approval latency.
- Confounders: prototype novelty, politeness, account-specific controls, and hypothetical responses.
- Readout date: 2026-08-05.
- Disconfirming signal: approvers require central pre-approval regardless of the artifact, or practitioners report that it slows incident resolution.
- Preserved option: discard the prototype with the core workflow and customer relationship unchanged.
- Pass condition: at least three of eight pairs request the artifact for a real review and the approver identifies a specific objection it resolves.
- Change condition: one or two pairs request it; narrow the artifact to their evidenced requirement and retest.
- Stop condition: no real-use request, any unsafe permission exposure, or any added mandatory approval step.
- Threshold status: proposed. Three real-use requests exceed polite interest while containing engineering cost. It may miss a valuable minority requirement or overfit a few accounts. Revise only from named review objections and actual use, not survey enthusiasm.
