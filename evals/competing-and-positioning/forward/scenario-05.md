Decision as of July 14, 2026: the open model is a substitute for the extraction component, a potential complement to the startup’s workflow, and only a bounded transition threat to the end-to-end product. Do not pivot immediately into model training. Integrate it reversibly and test whether substitution reaches the workflow, renewal, and switching layers.

Current branches:

- Rival attention: `use-competitor-evidence`, limited to evidence affecting quality, economics, deployability, customer switching, or dependency risk.
- Advantage maturity: `observed-advantage`. Workflow-driven renewals are observed; disproportionate copying difficulty and continuing refresh are unproved.
- Transition evidence: `bounded-option`. The extraction cost curve changed, but no customer migration, end-to-end substitution, or sustained application movement is established.
- Rival-response action: `adapt`.
- Platform exposure: `bounded-platform-pilot`.
- Platform-dependency action: `redesign`, using a replaceable extraction-provider interface and retained fallback.
- Technology-transition action: `integrate`.
- Incumbent family omitted: no incumbent response is identified in the facts.
- Platform creation omitted: no developer ecosystem or complement platform is proposed.

The record:

- Verified: $4 million ARR; procurement customers renew because of captured playbooks, exception routing, and audit trails; extraction is one workflow step; the open model matches the extraction benchmark at one-tenth inference cost; it lacks workflow, integrations, and compliance history.
- Bounded inference: customers may face switching costs in playbook migration, integrations, approval configuration, security review, and audit continuity.
- Unknown: performance on the startup’s production distribution, total serving cost, license and data terms, security acceptability, replaceability, customer willingness to switch, integrated-suite competition, and whether model training has paid demand.
- Product row: enterprise/integration software with procurement, security, interoperability, implementation, trust, and renewal latency. Shadow evaluation is reversible within weeks; a model-training pivot is slower and materially less reversible.

Advantage chain:

`captured playbooks + routing + audit trail → customer-specific approval orchestration → governed, traceable contract decisions → observed renewals → copy barrier unknown → continuing integration/playbook refresh plausible but unproved`

Material alternatives are the current workflow, manual email/document/spreadsheet coordination, an internal build around the open model, model-only extraction, integrated procurement or contract suites, and independent extraction providers. Their adoption, distribution, switching economics, and development velocity remain unknown and must be tested rather than inferred from the benchmark.

Eight-week plan

1. Customer substitution test — July 14–27

Operational person: proposed VP Customer Success. Baseline: the current renewal cohort’s workflow usage, contracted ARR, renewal status, and recorded reasons for renewal. Bounded exposure: every account renewing within 120 days plus a proposed five-account stratified active-customer sample; no production change. Metric: signed renewal or migration action, written security/workflow requirements, and whether a model-only alternative can satisfy each required playbook, routing, integration, and audit step. Confounders: relationship quality, budget timing, discounts, services, and general procurement delays. Disconfirming signal: a customer signs a model-only migration or non-renewal that is contingent only on extraction capability and price. Preserved option: retain the current product, extractor, contracts, and customer data path.

Proposed threshold: classify product-level substitution if at least 25% of the predeclared renewal-cohort ARR signs such a migration or non-renewal. Rationale: ARR-weighting prevents several small interviews from overriding economically significant commitments. Error tradeoff: the threshold may detect transition late, but avoids pivoting on stated preference or one atypical account. Revision rule: change it only before evidence collection or after a subsequent complete renewal cohort, with the reason recorded. Pass: no committed model-only substitution and required workflow steps remain. Change: some committed switching but below threshold, triggering repositioning and segment analysis. Stop the integration-led thesis: threshold is met.

2. Production-distribution quality and economics test — July 21–August 10

Operational person: proposed ML lead. Baseline: the current extractor’s predeclared production-derived evaluation metric, latency, extraction-serving cost, and human correction rate. Bounded exposure: permitted or de-identified historical documents in shadow mode; outputs cannot affect contracts or users. Metric: paired quality, critical-error count, latency, full serving cost, and required human correction—not the supplier’s benchmark alone. Confounders: document mix, caching, hardware assumptions, prompt tuning, batch size, and excluded monitoring or review costs. Disconfirming signal: benchmark parity fails on production data or savings disappear after infrastructure and review. Preserved option: current extractor remains deployable.

Proposed threshold: the lower confidence bound is no more than one percentage point below the current extractor on the predeclared metric, there are no additional critical extraction failures, and extraction-serving cost falls at least 50% without higher human correction. Rationale: this discounts the advertised 90% inference saving for integration overhead while protecting workflow reliability. Error tradeoff: it may reject a useful cheaper model whose small quality loss is harmless. Revision rule: revise only from measured downstream business impact in the bounded workflow pilot, never merely to make the model pass. Pass: proceed to pilot. Change: tune or restrict the model to low-risk clauses. Stop: critical errors or failure of both quality and cost predicates.

3. Compliance, portability, and exit test — July 21–August 10

Operational person: proposed security/legal lead. Baseline: current contractual, privacy, security, deletion, audit, and incident-response obligations. Bounded exposure: non-production deployment with no unapproved customer data. Metric: written license and data rights, customer-data training policy, deployment control, logging, deletion, vulnerability response, version pinning, exportability, and demonstrated rollback to the current extractor. Confounders: assuming self-hosting resolves license or operational risk, undocumented model changes, and temporary evaluation terms. Disconfirming signal: incompatible data/IP terms, an unmet mandatory customer control, unpinned behavior, or failed rollback. Preserved option: no customer data migration and continued use of the current extractor.

Proposed threshold: every current mandatory control must be satisfied or explicitly waived by its accountable owner, and rollback must be demonstrated within one business day without workflow or audit-history loss. Rationale: a cheaper component cannot weaken contractual trust. Error tradeoff: this may reject an economically attractive model before its compliance tooling matures. Revision rule: controls change only through the normal security/legal process before reassessment. Pass: authorize a bounded pilot. Change: keep shadow-only while named gaps are remediated. Stop: incompatible rights, customer-data exposure, or rollback failure.

4. End-to-end workflow and commercial test — August 11–31

Operational person: proposed Head of Product. Baseline: matched current-workflow cycle time, routing correctness, audit completeness, extraction correction rate, and renewal ARR performance. Bounded exposure: proposed three-to-five consenting accounts, parallel or staged processing, mandatory human review, no autonomous approval, and immediate rollback. Metric: end-to-end time to approved decision, routing and audit failures, user corrections, support load, signed renewals, discount demands, and signed model-only switching commitments. Confounders: contract complexity, reviewer learning, implementation support, price changes, and account health. Disconfirming signal: cheaper extraction worsens the governed outcome, or customers commit to leaving despite retained workflow performance. Preserved option: revert each account to the current extractor without changing its playbook, integrations, or audit history.

Proposed threshold: zero additional critical routing or audit failures, median end-to-end cycle time no more than 5% worse, extraction correction no more than one percentage point worse, and renewal ARR performance no more than five percentage points below the predeclared matched baseline. Rationale: integration must preserve the outcome customers renew for, not merely extraction accuracy. Error tradeoff: a small pilot can miss rare failures or overreact to account mix. Revision rule: rerun on the next predeclared cohort if confidence is insufficient; do not broaden exposure meanwhile. Pass: integrate behind the replaceable interface. Change: restrict deployment by clause, customer, or risk tier. Stop: any critical trust failure or the commercial substitution threshold from Test 1.

Decision gate — September 8, 2026

- `integrate`: Select if quality/economics, compliance/rollback, and workflow tests pass while product-level substitution remains below its proposed threshold. Operational person: Head of Product. `competing-and-positioning` records the integrate decision. Then `engineering-for-leverage` productionizes the replaceable adapter; `shipping-and-iterating-products` gates rollout; `designing-business-models` decides whether savings fund margin, packaging, or price tests; `learning-from-users` monitors subsequent renewals.

- `reposition`: Select if customers continue committing to the governed workflow but extraction commoditization measurably changes price expectations or evaluation criteria. Proposed threshold: at least 25% of sampled renewal ARR requests extraction unbundling or a lower extraction-linked price, with no signed model-only migration. Operational person: VP Product Marketing. `competing-and-positioning` records the reposition decision around governed approvals rather than extraction. Then `learning-from-users` validates the promise; `designing-business-models` tests packaging; `acquiring-and-growing-users` tests it in live opportunities; `communicating-clearly` publishes only the validated position.

- `rebuild`: Select if customer commitments validate the workflow but the existing architecture prevents portability or causes the pilot to fail its cycle-time or reliability threshold, and at least two customers sign commitments conditional on the rebuilt architecture. This cutoff is proposed to avoid rebuilding from technical preference alone; it risks waiting too long, and is revised only after another commitment cohort. Operational person: CTO. `competing-and-positioning` records the rebuild decision. Then `engineering-for-leverage` designs the modular architecture; `shipping-and-iterating-products` stages a reversible migration; `learning-from-users` reconfirms requirements; `designing-business-models` verifies return on the rebuild.

- `partner`: Select if a provider passes every compliance and portability predicate, offers verifiable service levels the startup cannot meet during the period, and has lower twelve-month total cost than the internal path while preserving fallback. The total-cost comparison is proposed because inference price alone is incomplete; it may undervalue strategic control and is revised when actual support or migration costs arrive. Operational person: COO. `competing-and-positioning` records the partner decision. Then `designing-business-models` validates total economics; `engineering-for-leverage` preserves provider portability; `shipping-and-iterating-products` gates release; `learning-from-users` confirms customer acceptance.

- `pivot`: Select only if end-to-end substitution is committed and model training independently earns committed demand. Proposed threshold: at least 50% of predeclared at-risk renewal ARR signs model-only migration or non-renewal, and paid training commitments cover the first twelve months of incremental training-build cost, with data rights and capability validated. Rationale: a pivot should require collapse of the present thesis and financing-quality evidence for the new one. Error tradeoff: the high bar may delay entry, but prevents abandoning $4 million ARR for the layer already showing commoditization. Revision rule: reconsider only after a complete renewal cohort or signed training contracts. Operational person: CEO. `competing-and-positioning` records the pivot decision. Then `learning-from-users` validates the new segment; `designing-business-models` verifies training economics; `building-and-evolving-organizations` identifies capability gaps; `raising-and-governing-capital` takes the evidence-backed change to the board; `shipping-and-iterating-products` protects existing customers during any transition.

The board should authorize the reversible integration program and the September 8 evidence gate, not an immediate training pivot. The benchmark weakens ownership of extraction technology; it does not yet invalidate the workflow business customers are demonstrably renewing.
