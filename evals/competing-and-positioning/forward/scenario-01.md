Case ID: scenario-01
Reviewer ID: positioning-forward-012

State/ownership: learning-first
Primary owner: learning-from-users

Verified evidence: The startup is six weeks old and has a polished demo, but no observed user workflows, interviews with people who attempted the problem, usage, payment, or other commitment evidence. Three funded rivals advertise similar natural-language formula features. Funding and advertised features do not establish user value or justify parity work. Stop broad rival surveillance and feature matching.

One hypothesis: Finance or operations analysts who repeatedly translate requests into spreadsheet formulas experience enough debugging and rework pain to adopt and commit to a tool that completes that job on their real workbooks.

Retained alternatives/dependencies: Retain the three advertised rivals as unverified direct alternatives. During research, identify the actual manual/status-quo path and any other tools users employ; do not assume them. Determine switching constraints from real workflows. The investor’s matrix request is a communication dependency, not product evidence: provide known advertised claims with unknowns clearly marked, plus this validation plan—not a parity roadmap.

Dated gate: July 28, 2026. Recruit eight proposed qualified participants who attempted this job within the previous 30 days. Observe their existing workflow before exposing the demo, then test the same narrow job on their own workbook. Advance to positioning only if at least three independently complete the job and at least two either pay/sign a dated live-data pilot or return for a second real-workbook use. These thresholds are proposed to reduce false positives from enthusiasm and isolated anecdotes; they risk missing a real need because of the small sample or commitment latency. After the readout, revise them only from observed recruitment or decision-cycle evidence—not rival activity. If the gate fails, change or stop the hypothesis and run another learning cycle.

Handoffs:

1. `learning-from-users`: Define the participant screen and commitment standard by July 15; conduct and interpret workflow observations and prototype sessions; distinguish repeated patterns from compliments.
2. `acquiring-and-growing-users`: Recruit the eight qualified participants from the hypothesized segment, prioritizing people with a recent attempted workflow rather than generic spreadsheet users.
3. `shipping-and-iterating-products`: Expose only the reversible demo needed to test the observed job; instrument completion, errors, fallback, repeat use, and commitment. Do not build rival-parity features during the gate.
4. `competing-and-positioning`: Return only after the gate passes. Compare the alternatives users actually reveal by outcome, switching constraints, tradeoffs, distribution, and control; use rival evidence only when it changes the user thesis, alternative map, capability gap, or response.
