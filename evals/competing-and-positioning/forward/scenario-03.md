As of: Day 0; no calendar date supplied.

Handoffs:

1. `learning-from-users` measures direct-user commitment and native-scheduler substitution.
2. `acquiring-and-growing-users` caps marketplace advertising and runs independently measured channel tests.
3. `engineering-for-leverage` designs replacements for each restricted API and the migration architecture.

5. `designing-business-models` recalculates marketplace economics after the revenue share.

Decision: select `diversify` for the next 90 days. Do not spend the full acquisition budget on marketplace ads. Cap marketplace advertising at a proposed 50% of acquisition spend; use the remainder for independently measured direct and alternate-channel acquisition. The cap preserves the strong current channel while avoiding a deeper irreversible dependency before direct-user, portability, API, substitution, and exit evidence exists. The error tradeoff is slower near-term growth versus avoiding amplified exposure to a platform controlling 81% of new-team acquisition, six APIs, policy, and a native substitute. Reconsider the cap only at the Day-90 gate.

Record:

- Verified facts: the app is profitable; 81% of new teams arrive through the marketplace; it uses six platform APIs; a 20% revenue share is now required; two APIs will be restricted in 90 days; the platform has released a basic native scheduler; marketplace users retain better than direct-web users; marketplace-ad conversion remains excellent.
- Bounded inference: the platform controls distribution, technical access, tolls, and a bundled substitute, so dependence is strategically material.
- Unknowns: post-share channel economics; exact restriction scope; replacement feasibility; user and data portability; migration cost; direct-account commitment; why marketplace retention is better; native-scheduler adoption and end-to-end substitution; whether users would follow the app elsewhere.
- Product row: capital-light software with enterprise/integration dependencies.
- Constraint and latency: two API restrictions bind at Day 90; retention and migration evidence may take most of the window.
- Reversibility: advertising allocation and bounded product tests are reversible; further platform-specific acquisition and API coupling increase switching cost.

Alternative map:

| Alternative | Outcome and switching position |
|---|---|
| Current platform-integrated app | Strong distribution and observed retention, but subject to revenue share, API control, and platform policy. |
| Platform-native scheduler | Basic substitute with privileged distribution and control; usage, quality, velocity, and willingness to switch are unknown. |
| Direct-web version | Existing independent path with worse observed retention; direct commitment, acquisition economics, and switching friction remain unmeasured. |
| Other independent schedulers | Material direct alternatives, but their adoption, differentiation, lead, and switching costs are not supplied. |
| Manual/status-quo scheduling | Likely predecessor category, but its workflows and persistence must be established rather than assumed. |
| Same-resource alternative | Marketplace ads compete with direct and alternate-channel experiments for the acquisition budget. |

Advantage chain: marketplace discovery and six-API integration → embedded access and workflow capability → likely lower friction → better marketplace retention observed → causal commitment effect, copy barrier, and continuing refresh unknown. Advantage maturity: `unsupported`; the retention difference is real, but its mechanism and defensibility are not established.

Diagnostic selections:

- Rival attention: `use-competitor-evidence`, limited to native-scheduler substitution, distribution, control, and workflow outcomes.
- Incumbent: `fortified-avoid-head-on`; observed response classes are `bundle` through the native scheduler and `block`/toll through API restrictions and revenue share.
- Transition evidence: `bounded-option`; policy and product changes are observed, but user movement is unknown.
- Platform exposure: `bounded-platform-pilot`.
- Rival-response action: `adapt`.
- Platform-dependency action: `diversify`.
- Technology-transition action: `reposition`, provisionally toward only those cross-platform or advanced outcomes that the substitution test validates.
- Platform creation is inapplicable: the company is deciding dependence on an external platform, not creating its own ecosystem.

Tests are independently adjudicated even where cohorts overlap:

1. **Policy test.** Operational person: platform-partnership lead. Baseline: announced 20% share and two restrictions in 90 days. Exposure: no new irreversible platform commitments while official terms, affected functions, account scope, dates, and export rights are verified. Metric: authoritative confirmation of every controlling term. Confounders: plan, region, grandfathering, or partner-tier exceptions. Readout: Day 14. Disconfirmation: any material difference from the announcement. Preserved option: capped marketplace acquisition. Proposed condition: pass if every controlling term is verified; change if any item remains ambiguous; stop new marketplace ads if restrictions arrive earlier or remove a critical function. Rationale: policy ambiguity can invalidate all downstream tests. Error tradeoff: temporary caution versus acting on incomplete terms. Revision rule: rerun immediately after any policy amendment.

2. **Post-share economics test.** Operational person: finance lead. Baseline: the app is profitable, but channel-level economics after the share are unknown. Exposure: marketplace spend remains within the 50% cap. Metric: contribution margin and CAC payback per retained active marketplace team after the 20% share. Confounders: cohort mix, annual billing, attribution, and seasonality. Readout: Day 30 and Day 90. Disconfirmation: negative contribution margin or payback beyond the company’s existing approved ceiling; if none exists, use a proposed 12-month ceiling. Preserved option: alternate-channel spend. Proposed pass/change/stop: pass if margin is positive and payback is within the ceiling; change allocation if only one holds; stop marketplace ads if contribution margin is negative. Rationale: conversion alone does not establish economic value. Error tradeoff: a 12-month ceiling may reject slower but valuable cohorts. Revision rule: replace it with a board-approved cohort ceiling when available.

3. **Direct-user commitment test.** Operational person: user-research lead. Baseline: no measurement of whether marketplace users would follow outside the platform. Exposure: offer a policy-compliant direct account to 10% of eligible new marketplace teams or 30 teams, whichever is greater, capped at 100. Metric: verified team-admin activation plus one completed scheduling workflow outside platform-only identity. Confounders: incentives, duplicate accounts, administrator permissions, and onboarding differences. Readout: Day 45. Disconfirmation: interest without activation or workflow completion. Preserved option: existing marketplace experience remains unchanged. Proposed pass/change/stop: pass at ≥30% completion; change the proposition at 15–29%; stop treating direct-following as established below 15%. Rationale: completed use is more discriminating than stated intent. Error tradeoff: the cutoff may understate users who need longer migration support. Revision rule: revise only from observed completion latency, not survey enthusiasm.

4. **Portability test.** Operational person: product-operations lead. Baseline: data portability is unknown. Exposure: 20 consenting teams export and independently verify their users, schedules, settings, and required history. Metric: percentage of required objects exported accurately and usable without platform-only access. Confounders: unusual integrations, permissions, and legacy records. Readout: Day 45. Disconfirmation: missing critical objects, unverifiable exports, or platform-only identifiers. Preserved option: source data remains intact. Proposed pass/change/stop: pass at ≥95% required-object fidelity with zero critical data loss; change below 95% if repairable; stop migration claims after any critical unrecoverable loss. Rationale: migration requires near-complete fidelity. Error tradeoff: stringent fidelity may delay exit readiness. Revision rule: tighten, never loosen, after discovering additional required objects.

5. **Migration rehearsal.** Operational person: release lead. Baseline: no rehearsal has been reported. Exposure: migrate 20 consenting teams to the direct path with rollback available. Metric: completed core workflow, support incidents, rollback success, and continued direct access. Confounders: team complexity, training, and API outages. Readout: Day 60. Disconfirmation: critical workflow failure, unrecoverable data, or inability to roll back. Preserved option: return each team to its prior configuration. Proposed pass/change/stop: pass if ≥90% complete the workflow, rollback succeeds for every attempted rollback, and there are zero critical incidents; change for noncritical failures; stop after any unrecoverable or critical incident. Rationale: exit credibility requires executed migration, not documentation. Error tradeoff: a small cohort may miss rare failures. Revision rule: expand exposure only after a clean readout.

6. **Alternate-channel test.** Operational person: growth lead. Baseline: direct-web retention is worse; comparative retained-team CAC is unknown. Exposure: the non-marketplace 50% of acquisition spend is split into separately attributed direct and one additional channel. Metric: CAC per team still active at 30 days, activation, and contribution margin. Confounders: creative, audience mix, attribution, and cohort seasonality. Readout: Day 75 with final update Day 90. Disconfirmation: signups without retained activation. Preserved option: retain the marketplace allocation. Proposed pass/change/stop: pass a channel at retained-team CAC ≤1.25× marketplace CAC after revenue share; change at 1.26–2.0×; stop that channel above 2.0×. Rationale: a modest premium is justified for customer ownership and diversification. Error tradeoff: short-window retention may favor fast-activating cohorts. Revision rule: replace the 30-day proxy when longer observed retention matures.

7. **Restricted API-A replacement.** Operational person: engineering lead. Baseline: current dependence exists; replacement capability is unknown. Exposure: reversible replacement for 10% of consenting eligible teams or 30 teams, whichever is greater. Metric: core-task completion, error rate, latency, and critical incidents versus the current API. Confounders: team complexity and unrelated outages. Readout: Day 60. Disconfirmation: missing core workflow or any critical incident. Preserved option: instant rollback to the current API while available. Proposed pass/change/stop: pass at ≥95% task completion, no critical incidents, and ≤10% latency degradation; change for repairable misses; stop that replacement after a critical incident. Rationale: restriction risk demands near-current reliability. Error tradeoff: the threshold may reject a viable but slower fallback. Revision rule: revise only if users demonstrate that latency does not affect commitment.

8. **Restricted API-B replacement.** Operational person: engineering lead. Baseline, exposure, metric, confounders, readout date, disconfirmation, preserved rollback, and proposed ≥95% completion/zero-critical-incident/≤10%-degradation thresholds are adjudicated separately from API-A. Rationale: success on one API cannot mask failure on the other. Error tradeoff and revision rule: identical to API-A, but revised only from API-B evidence.

9. **Native substitution test.** Operational person: user-research lead. Baseline: the native scheduler exists, but adoption and substitution are unknown. Exposure: at least 30 target teams complete the same real scheduling workflow with the app and the native scheduler, with order randomized. Metric: completed outcome, time, errors, subsequent choice, and 30-day continued use. Confounders: familiarity, setup assistance, workflow complexity, and platform promotion. Readout: Day 75. Disconfirmation: preference without completed use or a segment adequately served by the native option. Preserved option: continue serving differentiated segments and workflows. Proposed pass/change/stop: preserve the current positioning if ≥60% choose and continue using the app for a named superior outcome; reposition at 40–59%; stop targeting the basic-scheduling segment below 40%. Rationale: a clear behavioral majority is needed before claiming differentiation. Error tradeoff: the threshold may obscure smaller valuable segments. Revision rule: segment the result only using predeclared workflow differences.

Day-90 platform-dependency branches, in precedence order:

- **`stop`:** Select if marketplace contribution margin is negative and no viable migration exists, or API loss causes an unrecoverable critical workflow/data failure. Operational person: GM. `competing-and-positioning` records the stop decision. Ordered handoffs: `acquiring-and-growing-users` halts marketplace ads; `shipping-and-iterating-products` removes affected exposure; `engineering-for-leverage` retires unsafe dependencies; `learning-from-users` verifies remaining users’ needs. Proposed threshold favors avoiding critical loss; revise only after the failed gate is demonstrably repaired.

- **`migrate`:** Select if marketplace economics or native substitution fails its gate while direct commitment, portability, and migration rehearsal all pass. Operational person: GM. `competing-and-positioning` records migration. Ordered handoffs: `learning-from-users` confirms the affected segment; `engineering-for-leverage` executes the migration architecture; `shipping-and-iterating-products` stages rollout and rollback; `acquiring-and-growing-users` moves spend to direct channels; `designing-business-models` validates independent economics. The proposed all-three exit-readiness requirement risks delaying migration but avoids stranding users; revise only from executed migration evidence.

- **`redesign`:** Select if the platform remains economically valuable but either restricted API cannot meet its gate and a core workflow can be preserved by removing that dependency. Operational person: engineering lead. `competing-and-positioning` records redesign. Ordered handoffs: `engineering-for-leverage` specifies the decoupled architecture; `shipping-and-iterating-products` runs a reversible release; `learning-from-users` verifies workflow outcomes; `acquiring-and-growing-users` keeps acquisition capped during rollout. The proposed zero-critical-incident requirement favors reliability over speed; revise only after clean production evidence.

- **`double-down`:** Select only if all policy, economics, direct-user, portability, migration, both API, alternate-channel, and native-substitution gates pass, and marketplace retained-team CAC after the revenue share is proposed to be at least 20% better than the best alternate channel. Operational person: growth lead. `competing-and-positioning` records the decision. Ordered handoffs: `designing-business-models` confirms economics; `engineering-for-leverage` preserves portability and API fallbacks; `shipping-and-iterating-products` gates expanded exposure; `acquiring-and-growing-users` may scale marketplace spend; `learning-from-users` monitors commitment. The 20% cushion compensates for concentration risk; it may sacrifice near-term volume. Revise only after at least one longer-term retention cohort matures.

- **`diversify`:** This is the selected action now and remains the Day-90 result when the platform is still viable but the complete double-down predicate is not met and no higher-precedence branch applies. Operational person: GM. `competing-and-positioning` records continued diversification. Ordered handoffs: `acquiring-and-growing-users` maintains multiple measured channels; `engineering-for-leverage` reduces API coupling; `shipping-and-iterating-products` preserves direct and rollback paths; `learning-from-users` continues direct-commitment measurement; `designing-business-models` updates channel economics. The proposed residual rule favors preserving options under uncertainty; revise when a later cohort decisively satisfies another branch’s complete predicate.
