Position the company as the auditable, multi-cloud incident-resolution control plane—not an AI-summary product or an “open platform.” Its defensible value is joining changes across clouds, enforcing rollback playbooks, and preserving the audit trail. Treat summaries as a replaceable workflow component; do not copy the provider’s native interface.

For the next 90 days:

- Protect the core workflow from the provider’s historical-event restriction by prioritizing customer-owned event capture, retention, and identity portability.
- Keep selling through the marketplace because it drives 64% of new accounts and better retention, but do not commit the full acquisition budget before testing post-fee unit economics. Run controlled marketplace-versus-direct acquisition tests and test whether customers will renew or buy outside it.
- Negotiate with the provider while preparing direct billing and alternative identity paths. Reprice marketplace plans or reduce incentives if the 22% share makes acquisition uneconomic.
- Do not launch a free SDK or declare an open platform based on two consultancy exporters with no demonstrated purchasing impact. Instead, document and stabilize a narrow exporter API for those consultancies and several customer design partners, then measure adoption, influenced pipeline, and support cost.
- Evaluate the open model inside complete incident-resolution workflows—accuracy, latency, security, and rollback outcomes—not just summary benchmarks. Keep the model layer interchangeable.

At day 90, expand the SDK only if integrations measurably influence purchases or retention; increase marketplace spending only if contribution margins remain attractive after the fee change. The strategic goal is to retain marketplace distribution while removing marketplace and API dependency from the product’s core value.
