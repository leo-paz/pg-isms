## Decision

For the next 90 days, position the company as the **cross-cloud incident-resolution control plane**: it joins changes across clouds, enforces rollback playbooks, and preserves an auditable resolution record. Treat AI summaries as a replaceable workflow component, not the category or headline.

Do not declare an open platform, publish an unrestricted free SDK, copy the provider’s summary interface, or commit the full acquisition budget to marketplace advertising. Those moves would imitate the incumbent where it has distribution advantage while deepening dependence immediately before worse terms and restricted data access.

Maintain the marketplace as a bounded channel while pursuing `diversify`: establish direct buyer relationships, test an alternate acquisition path, redesign historical-event ingestion for portability, and rehearse migration. Use the open model only behind a model-neutral summary layer after end-to-end resolution testing.

## Evidence record

Verified facts:

- 52 paying enterprise customers and renewal tied to cross-cloud timelines, enforced rollback playbooks, and auditable records establish committed core-product value.
- Sixty-four percent of new accounts originate through one provider’s marketplace.
- Marketplace cohorts retain better than direct cohorts.
- The provider controls identity, billing, and event APIs; in 120 days its share rises from 8% to 22% and historical-event access will be restricted.
- The provider has a free native summary; an open model matches the startup’s summary benchmark.
- Two consultancies independently built exporters against the private API.
- No customer is known to have purchased because of an exporter.

Bounded inferences:

- The defensible position is resolution governance across clouds, not summary generation.
- The fee change raises the platform toll by 14 percentage points; applied to the current 64% acquisition mix, that represents an 8.96-point increase in blended new-account revenue share if mix and pricing remain unchanged. Profit impact is unknown.
- Exporters show builder initiative but not a builder-user-demand loop.
- Higher marketplace retention may reflect customer selection, procurement convenience, implementation support, or account maturity rather than marketplace causality.

Unknowns:

- Whether marketplace buyers will transact or renew directly.
- Replacement feasibility for restricted historical events.
- Direct-channel CAC, procurement latency, retention, and implementation burden.
- Whether either AI alternative substitutes for end-to-end incident resolution.
- Exporter recurrence, user value, support cost, governance needs, and attributable demand.
- Current contract details, portability rights, restriction scope, and migration costs; these must be revalidated.

## Alternative map

- Manual/status quo: engineers reconstruct incidents across cloud consoles and documents. It avoids migration but loses joined history, enforced playbooks, and unified auditability.
- Provider-native summary: fixes summary creation, has installed distribution and bundling power, but is not shown to provide cross-cloud resolution governance. Expected response: `bundle`.
- Open model: changes summary-component cost and replaceability; benchmark parity does not prove workflow substitution.
- Direct workflow competitors or internal tools: potential substitutes, but no adoption, switching, or capability evidence is supplied.
- Integrated-core exporters: potentially useful customer-controlled portability features; demand is unverified.
- Independent consultancy exporters: demonstrate capable builders, but no observed recurring user value or attributable acquisition.
- Other product work: reliability, API replacement, portability, and direct-channel development compete with SDK work for the same small team.

## 90-day test portfolio

### 1. Direct-buyer and portability commitment

- Operational person: proposed Head of Customer Success.
- Baseline: 64% of new accounts come through the marketplace; buyer willingness to leave it is unknown.
- Exposure: invite 12 marketplace customers approaching a commercial or security decision to evaluate direct contracting, direct identity, and export/migration documentation; no forced migration.
- Metric: number completing procurement/security review and signing a direct renewal, amendment, or dated migration commitment.
- Confounders: renewal timing, discounts, account size, marketplace credits, and procurement policy.
- Date: readout by October 12, 2026.
- Disconfirmation: no customer accepts a discriminating direct commitment, or direct operation loses required identity, billing, support, or audit capability.
- Preserved option: continue marketplace transactions during testing.
- Proposed pass/change/stop: pass at 3 signed direct commitments; change at 1–2 by identifying the blocking dependency; stop forced migration at 0. The cutoff is proposed because three demonstrates repeat behavior without risking the installed base; false positives could overstate portability, while false negatives could preserve dependence. Revise only after observing eligible-account volume and procurement-cycle length.

### 2. Alternate-channel acquisition

- Operational person: proposed Growth lead.
- Baseline: direct cohorts retain worse, acquisition economics are not supplied, and full-budget marketplace advertising is untested.
- Exposure: cap marketplace advertising at a proposed 10% of the 90-day acquisition budget and run an equal-budget direct/account-based cohort aimed at the same segment.
- Metric: qualified enterprise opportunities reaching security/procurement, acquisition cost per such opportunity, and signed annual contract value; retention is recorded but cannot mature within 90 days.
- Confounders: attribution, marketplace credits, account size, sales-assist intensity, and cohort maturity.
- Date: interim August 28; decision October 12, 2026.
- Disconfirmation: direct acquisition produces no procurement-stage opportunity while marketplace acquisition does.
- Preserved option: retain organic marketplace listing and remaining budget.
- Proposed pass/change/stop: diversify spend if direct produces at least 3 procurement-stage opportunities without greater sales effort per opportunity than marketplace; change message or segment at 1–2; stop scaling direct paid acquisition at 0. The cutoff is proposed to detect repeatability with limited capital; it risks rejecting a slow enterprise channel or accepting a small-sample anomaly. Revise after measuring actual sales-cycle and eligible-account volume.

### 3. Restricted historical-event replacement

- Operational person: proposed Engineering lead.
- Baseline: the product depends on provider event APIs, and future historical-access loss is announced; replacement completeness is unknown.
- Exposure: implement one reversible ingestion/archive path for three consenting design partners, without removing the production API path.
- Metric: proportion of timeline events reconstructed, audit-record completeness, latency, operating cost, and successful failover during incident replay.
- Confounders: incident type, event volume, retention period, customer permissions, and undocumented API behavior.
- Date: technical rehearsal by September 15; customer-verified readout October 1, 2026.
- Disconfirmation: required events cannot lawfully or reliably be captured, or the replacement degrades rollback/audit integrity.
- Preserved option: existing API use until restrictions take effect.
- Pass/change/stop: pass only when all three customers verify that replayed timelines preserve the events required for their rollback and audit procedures; redesign when gaps have identified replacements; begin migration away from the affected workflow when a required event class has no credible replacement. No invented numeric completeness threshold substitutes for customer procedure verification.

### 4. End-to-end AI substitution

- Operational person: proposed Product lead.
- Baseline: the open model matches a summary benchmark; neither it nor the native summary is proven to resolve incidents end to end.
- Exposure: blinded replay of 10 previously resolved, customer-approved incidents through the current model, open model, and native summary where access permits; no autonomous production action.
- Metric: correct change linkage, correct playbook selection, unsafe omission rate, time to verified rollback decision, and audit-record completeness.
- Confounders: incident difficulty, model prompting, unavailable provider context, reviewer familiarity, and retrospective hindsight.
- Date: September 20, 2026.
- Disconfirmation: benchmark parity fails to preserve rollback choice, resolution time, or audit completeness.
- Preserved option: keep current summary implementation and model abstraction.
- Proposed pass/change/stop: integrate the open model only if it is non-inferior on every safety-critical rollback and audit judgment across the 10 replays and improves cost or latency; change prompts/tooling for noncritical gaps; stop substitution on any unsafe rollback recommendation. This strict proposed cutoff favors avoiding high-cost false acceptance; it may reject a useful model after one ambiguous case. Revise only after independent incident-review adjudication.

### 5. Voluntary builder loop and executed rollback

- Operational person: proposed Partnerships/Product lead.
- Baseline: two consultancies built exporters; repeat use, customer value, demand attribution, support burden, and governance are unknown.
- Exposure: give only those two builders a revocable, rate-limited sandbox and documented exporter subset; no public SDK or marketplace.
- Metric: repeat builds, recurring customer use, named workflow value, support hours, security incidents, and signed customer demand explicitly contingent on the complement.
- Confounders: consultancy services revenue, founder attention, free support, pre-existing customer demand, and novelty.
- Date: remove access and execute rollback by September 22; final readout October 12, 2026.
- Disconfirmation: no recurring customer use, no attributable commitment, excessive support, security breach, or rollback harms core use/direct relationships.
- Preserved option: core product and private internal interfaces remain independent of the trial.
- Proposed pass/change/stop: expansion evidence requires both builders to deliver a complement used in at least two separate customer workflows, plus at least one signed customer commitment attributable to a complement; bounded exposure applies if recurring value exists but demand/governance/economics remain open; otherwise stop. These proposed cutoffs distinguish a loop from bespoke consulting, but may miss a narrow high-value complement; revise after recording eligible customers and support load.
- Mandatory rollback evidence: actually revoke trial credentials, remove trial documentation/endpoints, run core incident workflows, and confirm directly with participating customers that core operation and the commercial relationship remain unharmed. A rollback plan alone does not qualify.
