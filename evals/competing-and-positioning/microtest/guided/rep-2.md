## Decision

Do not declare an open platform, copy the native summary interface, or commit the acquisition budget to marketplace advertising.

For the next 90 days:

- Position the company as the auditable, cross-cloud incident-resolution and rollback-control system—not as an AI-summary product.
- Preserve the marketplace as a bounded acquisition channel while immediately diversifying acquisition and establishing direct buyer relationships.
- Redesign the provider-dependent event, identity, and billing boundaries; rehearse export and migration before access restrictions take effect.
- Run a revocable, private builder-loop pilot using the two consultancies. Do not publish a free SDK until recurring customer value and attributable demand are observed.
- Evaluate the open model only as a replaceable summary component inside the workflow. Do not claim substitution from its benchmark result.
- Allocate only a capped marketplace test budget before the fee change; compare contribution economics and retention against direct acquisition before spending further.

The 90-day promise should be: **“Resolve incidents across clouds with enforced rollback playbooks and an audit-ready timeline, regardless of which model or cloud produced the summary.”**

## Evidence record

Verified from supplied materials:

- Customers renew because of cross-cloud timelines, enforced rollback playbooks, and auditability.
- AI summaries are one workflow step.
- The provider supplies 64% of new accounts and controls critical identity, billing, and event APIs.
- Its revenue share is scheduled to rise from 8% to 22% in 120 days; historical-event access will be restricted.
- It launched a free native AI incident summary.
- An open model matches the startup’s summary benchmark but lacks end-to-end incident-resolution testing.
- Two consultancies independently built exporters against the private API.
- No known purchase is attributable to an exporter.
- Marketplace cohorts retain better, but buyer willingness to follow the startup elsewhere is untested.

Bounded inferences:

- The native summary threatens a feature, not yet the integrated incident-resolution job.
- Marketplace retention may reflect account quality, procurement convenience, or cohort mix rather than channel causality.
- The exporters show builder capability and initiative, but not a builder-user demand loop.
- Restricted historical access threatens the completeness—and therefore the audit value—of the core product.
- The higher revenue share can weaken unit economics, but current margins, CAC, payback, and contract terms are unknown.

Unknowns requiring validation:

- Exact effective dates, grandfathering, data-export rights, API limits, and identity/billing migration terms.
- Whether customers will contract directly or through another marketplace.
- Direct-channel CAC and retention after controlling for customer characteristics.
- Whether an open model preserves resolution quality, safety, latency, and auditability.
- Whether exporters create recurring user value or attributable demand.
- Security, support, governance, and economic costs of exposing an SDK.

## Alternative map

- Manual/status quo: engineers correlate cloud consoles, tickets, chat, and change logs; switching is easy technically but costly during incidents.
- Direct competitor: another cross-cloud incident-management product; capability and adoption are unknown.
- Platform-native alternative: the provider’s free summary, with superior distribution and integration but no supplied evidence of cross-cloud rollback enforcement or equivalent audit records.
- Same-resource alternative: spending engineering and acquisition capacity on the existing integrated workflow and direct distribution.
- Integrated core: retain summaries, timelines, playbooks, and audit records as one controlled product.
- Independent complement: consultancy-built exporters; actual customer pull remains unknown.
- Open model: a possible summary supplier, not yet an end-to-end product substitute.

Incumbent-response hypothesis: `bundle` is observed for summaries; `block` is a credible risk through restricted event access. Its distribution and control are strong, while its ability and incentive to reproduce the cross-cloud controlled workflow remain unknown.

## Ninety-day test portfolio

### 1. Direct-buyer portability

- Operational person: Head of Customer Success.
- Hypothesis/action: marketplace customers will preserve or renew their relationship under a direct contract or alternate marketplace.
- Baseline: willingness is untested; marketplace cohorts retain better.
- Exposure: approach 12 marketplace customers spanning renewal timing and account size; do not alter active contracts.
- Metric: signed direct/alternate-channel renewal, procurement authorization, or written commitment naming required migration conditions.
- Proposed threshold: at least 4 of 12 provide one of those commitments by September 12. Rationale: enough discriminating evidence to justify a migration path without risking the whole base. Error tradeoff: may undercount customers with slow procurement. Revision rule: segment results by procurement constraints and extend only qualified cases.
- Confounders: renewal timing, discounts, account size, procurement policy, and customer health.
- Disconfirmation: zero commitments or consistent refusal to transact outside the provider.
- Preserved option: continue marketplace renewals while improving portability.
- Pass/change/stop: pass at ≥4 commitments; change the offer or segment at 1–3; stop assuming portable demand at 0.
- Decision owner/action: `competing-and-positioning` records `diversify`, `migrate`, or continued bounded use.
- Handoffs: `learning-from-users` interprets commitment evidence; `acquiring-and-growing-users` recruits accounts; `designing-business-models` tests contract economics; `shipping-and-iterating-products` exposes only necessary migration tooling.

### 2. Alternate-channel acquisition

- Operational person: Head of Growth.
- Hypothesis/action: direct or alternate-channel acquisition can produce qualified enterprise opportunities without the provider marketplace.
- Baseline: 64% of new accounts come from that marketplace; comparable direct-channel economics are unknown.
- Exposure: cap each of direct outbound and one alternate channel at 10% of the normal 90-day acquisition budget; cap incremental marketplace ads at 10%.
- Metric: procurement-qualified opportunities and expected contribution margin after channel fees, reported separately by channel.
- Proposed threshold: a non-provider channel produces at least 3 procurement-qualified opportunities with expected contribution margin no worse than 20% below the existing marketplace cohort by October 1. Rationale: identify a credible diversification path without demanding mature retention in 90 days. Error tradeoff: pipeline quality may not predict renewal. Revision rule: replace expected margin with realized economics as deals mature.
- Confounders: cohort size, sales assistance, promotions, segment mix, and marketplace procurement convenience.
- Disconfirmation: no qualified opportunity outside the provider or materially worse economics across both alternatives.
- Preserved option: retain the marketplace for accounts that require it.
- Pass/change/stop: pass and expand the better alternate channel; change targeting if qualification occurs without viable economics; stop that channel if neither occurs.
- Decision owner/action: `competing-and-positioning` records the channel implication.
- Handoffs: `acquiring-and-growing-users` runs acquisition; `designing-business-models` computes economics; `learning-from-users` checks buyer motivation.

### 3. Restricted-API replacement and migration rehearsal

- Operational person: Engineering lead.
- Hypothesis/action: the company can preserve audit-complete timelines despite restricted provider history.
- Baseline: the product relies on provider event APIs; no replacement or rehearsal is supplied.
- Exposure: one internal environment and two consenting customers; read-only shadow capture with no production enforcement changes.
- Metric: percentage of required audit events captured, ordering accuracy, retrieval latency, and successful replay after provider history becomes unavailable.
- Proposed threshold: 100% of events classified as audit-required, at least 99% of all expected test events, and successful replay for both customers by September 15. Rationale: audit claims cannot tolerate missing required evidence. Error tradeoff: a small pilot may miss rare event classes. Revision rule: add every newly discovered required class and rerun before expansion.
- Confounders: event volume, cloud configuration, clock skew, undocumented API behavior, and customer-specific permissions.
- Disconfirmation: any required class cannot be captured lawfully or replayed reliably.
- Preserved option: narrow supported workflows or migrate affected ingestion before restrictions.
- Pass/change/stop: pass to a gated rollout; change architecture if completeness fails but lawful alternatives exist; stop the affected audit claim if no reliable route exists.
- Decision owner/action: `competing-and-positioning` records whether the promise remains supportable.
- Handoffs: `engineering-for-leverage` builds the replaceable ingestion boundary; `shipping-and-iterating-products` gates rollout and rollback; `learning-from-users` identifies customer-required audit events.

### 4. End-to-end model substitution

- Operational person: Incident-product lead.
- Hypothesis/action: the open model can replace or supplement the current summary component without degrading incident resolution.
- Baseline: benchmark parity exists; end-to-end resolution performance is untested.
- Exposure: replay 20 de-identified historical incidents, followed by shadow use in up to 5 consenting customer teams; no autonomous rollback action.
- Metric: materially incorrect assertions, omitted decision-critical facts, time to accepted resolution plan, operator correction rate, and audit-trace completeness.
- Proposed threshold: zero decision-critical false assertions, no worse than a 10% increase in median time to an accepted plan, and complete provenance for every tested incident by September 22. Rationale: model savings or independence cannot trade away controlled resolution. Error tradeoff: small samples may miss rare failures. Revision rule: expand testing by incident class before production use.
- Confounders: incident complexity, operator familiarity, prompt differences, and replay hindsight.
- Disconfirmation: any untraceable decision-critical falsehood or repeated workflow degradation.
- Preserved option: retain the current model or use the open model only for low-risk drafting.
- Pass/change/stop: pass to a reversible production pilot; change scope if quality is uneven; stop substitution on critical error.
- Decision owner/action: `competing-and-positioning` records `integrate` or continued observation.
- Handoffs: `learning-from-users` defines accepted workflow outcomes; `shipping-and-iterating-products` controls exposure; `engineering-for-leverage` implements a replaceable model interface.

### 5. Voluntary builder-user loop and executed rollback

- Operational person: Partnerships lead.
- Hypothesis/action: bounded exporter interfaces produce recurring customer value and demand.
- Baseline: two consultancies built exporters; no purchase is known to be exporter-driven.
- Exposure: invite those two consultancies and at most two additional qualified builders into a private, revocable interface; maximum five consenting customer deployments; no public SDK promise.
- Metric: repeat builder releases, repeated customer use across two incident cycles, user-confirmed workflow value, complement-attributable qualified demand, support hours, and security incidents.
- Proposed threshold: by September 30, at least 2 builders each maintain a working complement, at least 3 customers use one in two separate incident cycles, and at least 1 qualified buyer explicitly conditions evaluation or purchase on a complement. Rationale: distinguishes openness from an ecosystem loop. Error tradeoff: the threshold may miss narrow but valuable complements. Revision rule: preserve a bounded interface if recurring value exists but demand attribution remains unresolved.
- Exposure limit: zero cross-tenant data exposure, zero unauthorized write access, and no more than 40 cumulative support hours.
- Confounders: consultancy incentives, free engineering help, existing customer relationships, novelty, and founder-led sales.
- Disconfirmation: no repeated customer use, no repeat builder maintenance, or a breached exposure/support limit.
- Preserved option: keep exporter capability integrated or retire the interface without affecting core timelines, playbooks, contracts, or direct customer access.
- Rollback: on August 31, remove pilot credentials for 72 hours and verify that all five core deployments, direct support paths, timelines, rollback enforcement, and customer access continue without the complements; collect explicit confirmation from each affected customer.
- Pass/change/stop: recurring value with unresolved pull selects `expose-bounded-interfaces`; every expansion predicate selects `expand-ecosystem`; absent loop/value or any breached limit selects `stop-platform-bet`; validated core-owned need plus absent complement value selects `keep-integrated`.
- Decision owner/action: `competing-and-positioning` records the applicable platform-creation outcome.
- Handoffs: `shipping-and-iterating-products` gates exposure and performs rollback; `engineering-for-leverage` bounds the interface; `learning-from-users` adjudicates customer value and attributable demand.

## Precommitted platform-creation outcomes

### `keep-integrated`

- Observable condition: the test validates that the need belongs in the core product, while independent complements demonstrate no recurring value.
- Proposed decision threshold/date: the core need is confirmed through repeated use by at least 3 exposed customers, but no complement is used across two incident cycles by September 30.
- Operational person: Incident-product lead.
- Positioning decision owner/action: `competing-and-positioning` records `keep-integrated`.
- Handoffs: `shipping-and-iterating-products` removes and rolls back trial exposure; `engineering-for-leverage` retires interface work; `learning-from-users` reconfirms the affected need and segment before integrated shipping.
- Rollback proof: core timelines, enforcement, contracts, support, and direct customer access remain functional during the executed 72-hour removal.

### `expose-bounded-interfaces`

- Observable condition: complements create recurring value within the safe surface, but attributable demand, repeatability, governance, support, or economics remains unproved.
- Proposed decision threshold/date: at least 2 customers use a complement across two incident cycles with no breached limit, while one or more expansion predicates remain absent on September 30.
- Operational person: Partnerships lead.
- Positioning decision owner/action: `competing-and-positioning` records the bounded decision and unresolved predicates.
- Handoffs: `shipping-and-iterating-products` retains only revocable exposure; `engineering-for-leverage` hardens only the bounded interface; `learning-from-users` names and closes every remaining evidence gap.
- Rollback proof: the August 31 removal confirms that core operation and direct customer relationships do not depend on complements.

### `expand-ecosystem`

- Observable condition: repeat capable builders, recurring user value, complement-attributable demand, and credible governance and economics are all demonstrated.
- Proposed decision threshold/date: at least 2 repeat builders, 3 customers with recurring use, 1 complement-attributable qualified buyer, no breached limit, documented governance controls, and support/economic viability by September 30.
- Operational person: Partnerships lead.
- Positioning decision owner/action: `competing-and-positioning` records `expand-ecosystem`.
- Handoffs: `learning-from-users` names and closes residual gaps; `governing-platforms-and-communities` defines access, review, abuse, incident, and appeal rules; `designing-business-models` tests developer/store pricing, support cost, and complement economics; `engineering-for-leverage` hardens approved interfaces; `shipping-and-iterating-products` gates release; only then `acquiring-and-growing-users` owns builder adoption and user-side liquidity.
- Rollback proof: the executed removal verifies that neither the core product nor direct customer relationships require the ecosystem.

### `stop-platform-bet`

- Observable condition: the builder-user loop or complement value is absent, or any exposure, support, or harm limit is breached.
- Proposed decision threshold/date: zero repeated customer use, zero repeat builder maintenance, cross-tenant exposure, unauthorized writes, or more than 40 support hours by September 30.
- Operational person: Partnerships lead.
- Positioning decision owner/action: `competing-and-positioning` records `stop-platform-bet`.
- Handoffs: `shipping-and-iterating-products` removes and rolls back exposure; `engineering-for-leverage` records and retires the failed interface; `learning-from-users` validates any remaining integrated-product need before shipping.
- Rollback proof: removal must leave core timelines, rollback enforcement, audit records, contracts, support access, and direct customer relationships unharmed.

These numerical cutoffs are proposed operating gates, not established market truths. They prioritize avoiding irreversible platform and dependency mistakes; all should be revised after the recorded readouts based on segment mix, procurement latency, and observed failure modes.
