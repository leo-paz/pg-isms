Decision: position the company as the cloud-neutral, auditable incident-resolution workflow—not an AI-summary product and not yet an open platform. For the next 90 days, preserve marketplace access only as a bounded channel, diversify acquisition, redesign restricted-event dependencies, and run a revocable SDK builder-loop test. Do not copy the provider’s summary interface, publish an unrestricted free SDK, or commit the full acquisition budget to marketplace ads.

### Decision record

- As of: 2026-07-14
- Stage: 19-person enterprise startup; 52 paying customers.
- Product row: enterprise/integration, with marketplace dependency.
- User/job/outcome: enterprise incident teams need to reconstruct changes across three clouds, execute governed rollback playbooks, and retain an auditable incident record.
- Constraint: in 120 days, marketplace share rises from 8% to 22%, historical-event access is restricted, and the provider controls identity, billing, APIs, and 64% of new-account distribution.
- Evidence latency: acquisition signals can appear within 30–60 days; enterprise conversion, deployment, and renewal evidence may exceed 90 days.
- Reversibility: ad allocation and bounded interfaces are reversible; unrestricted SDK commitments, deeper API dependence, and loss of historical data are harder to reverse.

Verified facts:

- Customers pay and renew for the joined timeline, enforced rollback workflow, and audit record.
- AI summaries are only one workflow step.
- The provider has distribution and control advantages and has launched a free native summary.
- Marketplace cohorts retain better, but acquisition source may be confounded with customer fit.
- Two consultancies independently built exporters using the private API.
- No purchase is known to be attributable to an exporter.
- The open model matches the summary benchmark but lacks end-to-end incident-resolution evidence.

Bounded inferences:

- Summary generation is becoming a commodity component.
- The provider can bundle summary functionality but has not demonstrated substitution for the cross-cloud governed workflow.
- Concentrated distribution and critical APIs create bargaining and continuity risk.
- The exporter activity is a builder signal, not evidence of an ecosystem or moat.

Unknowns:

- Whether marketplace customers will buy or renew through a direct channel.
- The economics of marketplace acquisition after the fee increase.
- Which historical-event use cases fail under the restriction.
- Whether the open model preserves end-to-end resolution quality.
- Whether complements create recurring customer value or attributable demand.
- Portability of identity, billing, data, and deployed integrations.

### Position and alternatives

Promise: “Resolve incidents across clouds with a governed timeline, executable rollback playbooks, and an audit-ready record.” AI summaries should be described as replaceable assistance inside that system.

Alternative map:

- Manual/status quo: cloud consoles, chat, tickets, and hand-built incident records; fragmented but already approved.
- Direct rivals: other observability or incident-management products; capabilities and switching evidence are unknown.
- Platform-native: the provider’s free summary; strongly distributed but narrower than the verified cross-cloud workflow.
- Same-resource alternative: customers’ internal engineering work and incumbent monitoring spend.
- Integrated core: bounded export and automation capabilities maintained as part of the product.
- Independent complements: consultancy-built exporters; actual user value and demand attribution remain unknown.
- New model: a potential summary component, not yet an end-to-end substitute.

Advantage chain:

`cross-cloud event joining + encoded rollback controls + immutable incident record`
→ `integrated incident-resolution capability`
→ `faster, governed resolution with auditability`
→ `observed payment and renewal`
→ copy barrier remains unproved
→ refresh through broader integrations, workflow evidence, and reliable restricted-data substitutes.

Advantage maturity: observed-advantage, not defensible-system.

### Ninety-day portfolio

No test authorizes an unrestricted SDK or deeper irreversible marketplace dependence.

**1. Direct-buyer portability test**

- Operational person: Head of Sales.
- Baseline: 64% of new accounts arrive through the marketplace; no tested evidence that buyers follow outside it.
- Exposure: offer a direct contracting, billing, and renewal path to a bounded set of upcoming qualified opportunities and renewals while leaving marketplace purchase available.
- Metric: signed direct contract or written procurement approval permitting direct purchase; separately record conversion time and stated blockers.
- Confounders: account size, discounts, sales ownership, procurement calendar, and marketplace credits.
- Date: readout by day 60.
- Disconfirmation: qualified buyers consistently require marketplace procurement or refuse direct contracting.
- Preserved option: retain marketplace listings and fulfillment.
- Pass: at least two qualified enterprises make a discriminating direct commitment.
- Change: expressed willingness exists but procurement cannot finish by day 60; retain a limited direct-path test and revise timing from observed procurement steps.
- Stop: no direct commitment and repeated evidence that marketplace procurement is mandatory.
- Threshold: proposed; two commitments reduce single-account noise while remaining feasible for 52-customer scale. Error tradeoff: a false positive understates channel dependence; a false negative delays diversification. Revision rule: revise only from documented procurement-cycle evidence, not lead volume.

**2. Alternate-channel acquisition test**

- Operational person: Growth lead.
- Baseline: marketplace cohorts retain better than direct cohorts; acquisition mix and fit are not controlled.
- Exposure: reserve—not exhaust—the 90-day acquisition budget across matched marketplace and direct/partner campaigns; cap marketplace spending at a reversible tranche.
- Metric: qualified enterprise opportunities reaching the same predeclared sales stage per dollar, plus buyer-fit attributes; do not use raw leads.
- Confounders: marketplace credits, account size, campaign maturity, brand search, sales follow-up, and cohort age.
- Date: first readout day 45; final readout day 75.
- Disconfirmation: direct/partner channels produce no comparable qualified opportunities while marketplace economics remain viable after the 22% share.
- Preserved option: scale the better channel after readout.
- Pass: a non-marketplace channel produces at least two matched qualified opportunities without a worse projected contribution margin.
- Change: opportunity quality is comparable but economics remain uncertain; extend only the best alternate channel.
- Stop: no qualified alternate-channel opportunities by day 75.
- Threshold: proposed; the small count is directional, not proof of repeatable growth. Error tradeoff: diversification can lower short-term volume; overcommitting to marketplace can lock in structurally worse economics. Revision rule: update using observed stage progression and contribution economics.

**3. Marketplace economics and policy test**

- Operational person: Finance lead.
- Baseline: revenue share will rise from 8% to 22% in 120 days; current account-level contribution economics are not supplied.
- Exposure: no new long-lived marketplace-only commitments; model existing and prospective cohorts under the new share.
- Metric: account-level contribution after revenue share, servicing, cloud costs, incentives, and expected retention.
- Confounders: marketplace credits, discounts, expansion, support burden, and cohort mix.
- Date: day 30.
- Disconfirmation: the higher fee remains justified by demonstrably superior incremental retention and acquisition economics.
- Preserved option: continue profitable marketplace acquisition within a cap.
- Pass: marketplace remains positive and superior on matched incremental contribution; retain a bounded allocation.
- Change: positive but inferior economics; use marketplace selectively while diversifying.
- Stop: negative expected contribution or economics dependent on unverified retention assumptions.
- Threshold: proposed: require positive contribution and superiority to matched alternatives before increasing spend. Error tradeoff: conservatism may sacrifice near-term growth; optimism may buy unprofitable lock-in. Revision rule: replace assumptions with realized cohort costs and stage conversion.

**4. Restricted historical-event replacement**

- Operational person: Engineering lead.
- Baseline: the workflow depends on provider event APIs; historical access will be restricted.
- Exposure: inventory affected workflows, prototype customer-owned ingestion/storage or alternate sources for a bounded set of representative incidents, and rehearse migration.
- Metric: percentage of required timeline events reconstructed, rollback-policy checks preserved, audit completeness, latency, and migration effort.
- Confounders: incident type, retention window, permissions, event-volume differences, and prototype-only access.
- Date: inventory day 15; prototype day 45; migration rehearsal day 60.
- Disconfirmation: critical audit or rollback evidence cannot be reproduced lawfully and reliably.
- Preserved option: maintain current API integration until the policy deadline.
- Pass: representative incidents preserve required timeline, rollback, and audit outcomes using a customer-portable source.
- Change: only named event classes fail; redesign those workflows and disclose limitations.
- Stop: the substitute breaks required audit integrity or creates unacceptable operational risk.
- Threshold: proposed: no missing event required for a rollback decision or audit assertion. Error tradeoff: strictness increases engineering cost; looseness risks the core promise. Revision rule: only customer-approved workflow requirements may redefine what is required.

**5. End-to-end summary substitution test**

- Operational person: Product lead.
- Baseline: the open model matches a summary benchmark but has no end-to-end incident-resolution evidence.
- Exposure: shadow-run it on a bounded, permissioned set of historical incidents; no autonomous actions or customer-visible replacement.
- Metric: investigator correction rate, missed material events, unsupported claims, time to approved rollback decision, and audit-record consistency versus the current component.
- Confounders: incident complexity, evaluator familiarity, prompt differences, and incomplete historical data.
- Date: day 45.
- Disconfirmation: benchmark parity fails to preserve resolution or audit outcomes.
- Preserved option: retain the current summary component and model abstraction.
- Pass: non-inferior end-to-end outcomes with no new material audit or rollback errors.
- Change: useful only for limited incident classes; integrate behind routing and monitoring.
- Stop: any material unsupported claim affects a rollback decision or audit record.
- Threshold: proposed: zero material decision/audit errors and no worse median correction burden. Error tradeoff: this may reject a cheaper useful model; a looser rule risks trust and workflow integrity. Revision rule: revise only after classifying errors with incident owners.

**6. Voluntary builder-loop and rollback test**

- Operational person: Product lead.
- Baseline: two consultancies built exporters; recurring value and attributable demand are absent.
- Exposure: invite those builders and at most three additional capable builders to a revocable, scoped SDK covering export/read interfaces only. No public launch, marketplace, write access, or promise of permanence.
- Metric: repeat builder use, successful supported deployments, recurring customer use, customer outcome, attributable purchase/expansion intent, support load, and security incidents.
- Confounders: consultancy services revenue, bespoke customer requests, free support, founder attention, and pre-existing purchase intent.
- Date: rollback rehearsal before first external exposure; rollback verification by day 30; builder-loop readout day 75.
- Disconfirmation: builders do not return, customers do not use complements repeatedly, no demand is attributable, or exposure/support limits are breached.
- Preserved option: keep exporters as customer-specific integrations or retire the interface.
- Rollback proof: remove access for a consenting test tenant before readout and verify both that the core incident workflow still works and that the direct customer relationship, support path, data access, and contract remain intact.
- Pass: repeat capable builders produce recurring safe customer value; this permits only `expose-bounded-interfaces` unless all expansion predicates also pass.
- Change: recurring value exists but demand, governance, support, or economics remain unknown; retain only bounded interfaces.
- Stop: no completed builder-user loop, no recurring value, failed rollback, or any exposure/support/harm breach.
- Threshold: proposed: at least two independent capable builders return after first deployment, and at least two customers repeatedly use complements; ecosystem expansion additionally requires attributable demand and credible governance/economics. Error tradeoff: strictness may miss an early ecosystem; permissiveness could create costly obligations from consultancy projects. Revision rule: revise only from observed independent deployments and customer behavior.

### Platform-creation outcome commitments

**keep-integrated**

- Condition/date: by day 75, a validated export/integration need belongs in the core product, but independent complements show no value.
- Operational person: Product lead.

- Proposed threshold: customers demonstrate the need through repeated core-product use or committed deployment, while complement use remains absent. Revise only from observed customer behavior.

**expose-bounded-interfaces**

- Condition/date: by day 75, complements create recurring customer value within the safe surface, but demand pull, repeatability, governance, support, or economics remains unproved.
- Operational person: Product lead.
- Positioning owner/action: `competing-and-positioning` records `expose-bounded-interfaces`.
- Handoffs: `shipping-and-iterating-products` retains only revocable exposure → `engineering-for-leverage` hardens only the bounded interface → `learning-from-users` names and closes every unresolved evidence gap.
- Core/direct relationship proof: the rollback rehearsal verifies continued core operation and direct ownership of support, contracts, and customer access.
- Proposed threshold: the builder-loop pass condition is met without any breached limit. Revise only from repeat usage and measured support/security evidence.

**expand-ecosystem**

- Condition/date: by day 75, all are observed: repeat capable builders, recurring user value, complement-attributable demand, and a credible governance and economic path.
- Operational person: Product lead.
- Positioning owner/action: `competing-and-positioning` records `expand-ecosystem`.
- Handoffs: `learning-from-users` closes residual evidence gaps → `governing-platforms-and-communities` defines access, review, abuse, incident, and appeal rules → `designing-business-models` tests pricing, support cost, and complement economics → `engineering-for-leverage` hardens approved interfaces → `shipping-and-iterating-products` gates release → `acquiring-and-growing-users` owns builder adoption and user-side liquidity.
- Core/direct relationship proof: executed removal shows the startup can withdraw the SDK without breaking the core product or losing the direct customer relationship.
- Proposed threshold: every listed predicate must pass; no proxy such as SDK downloads qualifies. Revise only with attributable customer commitments and verified economics.

**stop-platform-bet**

- Operational person: Product lead.
- Positioning owner/action: `competing-and-positioning` records `stop-platform-bet`.
- Handoffs: `shipping-and-iterating-products` removes exposure → `engineering-for-leverage` records and retires the failed interface → `learning-from-users` validates any remaining integrated-product need before shipping.
- Core/direct relationship proof: rollback verification must show continued core workflow operation and intact direct contracts, support, and customer access.
- Proposed threshold: any breached limit overrides all value signals; otherwise absent recurring complement value selects stop. Revise only after a new bounded test with newly observed evidence.
