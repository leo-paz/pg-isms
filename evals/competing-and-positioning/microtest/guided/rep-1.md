As of July 14, 2026, position the company as the auditable, multi-cloud incident-resolution and rollback-control system—not an AI incident-summary product and not yet an open platform.

The next 90 days should protect the differentiated workflow, reduce dependency on the cloud provider, and test portability, direct demand, end-to-end AI substitution, and a builder-user loop. Do not copy the provider’s summary interface, publicly declare a platform, release an unrestricted free SDK, or commit the full acquisition budget to marketplace ads.

Decision record:

- Stage/product row: 52 paying enterprise customers; enterprise/integration software.
- Constraint: 64% marketplace acquisition, plus provider-controlled identity, billing, and event APIs. Revenue share rises from 8% to 22% in 120 days and historical-event access will be restricted.
- Evidence latency: enterprise buying and renewal may exceed the 90-day window; deployment, buyer commitment, and workflow use are therefore required leading indicators.
- Reversibility: messaging, limited interfaces, model trials, and channel experiments are reversible; deeper provider coupling or an unrestricted public SDK is harder to reverse.
- Verified facts: customers renew because the product joins changes across three clouds, enforces rollback playbooks, and preserves an audit record. Marketplace cohorts retain better. Two consultancies independently built private-API exporters. The provider launched a free native summary. An open model matches the summary benchmark.
- Bounded inference: the strongest current differentiation is integrated incident resolution and evidence, while summaries are a replaceable component. Better marketplace retention may reflect account quality or marketplace convenience rather than channel causality.
- Unknowns: willingness to buy directly, restricted-history replacement quality, end-to-end open-model performance, exporter-generated recurring value, SDK support cost, portability, and whether marketplace advertising remains economic at a 22% share.

User/job/outcome: enterprise incident and operations teams need to reconstruct cross-cloud incidents, execute approved rollback procedures, and prove what happened. Their commitment is renewal and deployed use. Alternatives include manual cloud-console reconstruction, ticket/chat timelines, each cloud’s native tools, the provider’s free summary, independent exporters, an integrated core workflow, and spending the same budget on direct acquisition or dependency reduction.

### Ninety-day tests

1. Direct-buyer portability

Operational person: Head of Sales. Baseline: 64% of new accounts arrive through the marketplace; no evidence buyers will follow outside it. Exposure: offer direct contracting and migration assistance to 12 marketplace-sourced renewal or expansion buyers without changing product access. Metric: signed direct order, amendment, or written procurement initiation naming an owner and date. Confounders: discounts, contract timing, and account size. Readout: September 30, 2026. Disconfirmation: no buyer takes a procurement step or buyers require marketplace identity/billing. Preserved option: continue marketplace selling.

Pass condition: at least 3 of 12 make the defined commitment. Change condition: 1–2 commit, retaining a smaller direct-channel test. Stop condition: 0 commit. The cutoff is proposed because no conversion baseline exists; it favors avoiding premature migration but risks missing weak early demand. Revise after observing procurement-cycle length and buyer eligibility.

2. Alternate-channel acquisition

Operational person: Growth lead. Baseline: marketplace cohorts retain better than direct cohorts, with unknown selection effects. Exposure: reserve—not commit—the acquisition budget and run matched marketplace and direct campaigns, each capped at 15% of the 90-day budget. Metric: qualified enterprise opportunities reaching a scheduled technical evaluation, plus projected contribution after channel fees. Confounders: account mix, creative, attribution, and sales follow-up. Readout: September 30, 2026. Disconfirmation: direct acquisition produces no qualified evaluations or marketplace economics remain superior after the 22% share. Preserved option: maintain marketplace presence.

Pass condition for diversification: direct cost per qualified evaluation is no more than 1.5× marketplace cost after applying the future fee. Change condition: 1.5–2×, prompting another matched cohort. Stop direct scaling above 2×. These are proposed thresholds balancing channel resilience against wasting scarce budget; revise when closed-won contribution and retention mature.

3. Restricted-history replacement and data portability

Operational person: Engineering lead. Baseline: historical incident reconstruction relies on the provider’s event API; export completeness and alternate ingestion are unknown. Exposure: build a reversible capture/export path for three consenting customers and replay representative incidents with provider-history access withheld. Metric: required event coverage, timeline reconstruction, playbook execution, and audit-record integrity versus the current workflow. Confounders: incident mix, retention windows, permissions, and missing third-party events. Readout: September 15, 2026. Disconfirmation: any required audit or rollback evidence cannot be reproduced. Preserved option: current provider integration remains active.

Pass condition: all three customers can complete their existing incident and audit workflow without restricted history. Change condition: gaps are confined to named non-control evidence with an approved remediation. Stop migration if rollback or audit evidence fails for any customer. The three-customer threshold is proposed for rapid failure discovery, not generalization; revise after classifying gap frequency and severity.

4. End-to-end AI substitution

Operational person: Product lead. Baseline: the open model matches the summary benchmark but has no end-to-end resolution evidence; the provider offers a free native summary. Exposure: shadow-run the open model and native summary where permitted on 20 completed incidents, with no automated operational action. Metric: whether each alternative preserves chronology, change attribution, rollback recommendation traceability, playbook compliance, and audit acceptance—not summary preference alone. Confounders: incident complexity, evaluator knowledge, and model prompt differences. Readout: September 15, 2026. Disconfirmation: benchmark parity does not produce equivalent workflow outcomes or introduces unsupported operational claims. Preserved option: retain the current summary component or use no generated summary.

Pass condition for integration: the open model meets every control and audit requirement on at least 19 of 20 incidents and has no severe unsupported action. Change condition: one non-severe, repairable failure. Stop operational integration for any severe unsupported rollback/action or more than one control failure. These proposed conservative thresholds reflect enterprise audit risk; revise only after failure-mode review, not aggregate benchmark gains.

5. Voluntary builder-user loop and rollback

Operational person: Partnerships lead. Baseline: two consultancies built exporters, but no attributable purchase or recurring customer value is known. Exposure: invite those two and up to three additional qualified consultancies to a revocable, read-only interface covering export and timeline retrieval; no public SDK or marketplace claim. Metric: repeat builder use, recurring customer use, complement-attributable buying commitment, support load, and security incidents. Confounders: consultancy services revenue, founder assistance, and pre-existing customer demand. Readout: October 1, 2026.

Before readout, Shipping must remove access from one consenting test integration and verify that the core incident workflow still operates and that the customer retains direct access, support, data export, and commercial continuity. Failure of either check selects `stop-platform-bet`.

Disconfirmation: builders require bespoke engineering, customers do not repeatedly use outputs, or no buyer attributes commitment to a complement. Preserved option: keep exporter needs integrated or retire the interface.

Proposed expansion evidence: at least 2 independent builders each support recurring use by at least 2 customers, and at least 2 customers provide a signed purchase/renewal/expansion commitment explicitly attributable to a complement, with no security breach and support below one engineering day per builder per month. The threshold limits false ecosystem claims but may reject a valuable niche; revise after separating reusable support from bespoke work.

### Precommitted platform outcomes

`keep-integrated`

Observable condition/date: by October 1, the core exporter or integration need is validated through repeated customer workflow use, but independent complements create no recurring value or attributable commitment. Operational person: Product lead. `competing-and-positioning` records `keep-integrated`. `shipping-and-iterating-products` removes trial exposure and verifies the core product and direct customer relationship remain unharmed; `engineering-for-leverage` retires interface work; `learning-from-users` reconfirms the affected need and segment before integrated shipping.

`expose-bounded-interfaces`

Observable condition/date: by October 1, at least two customers repeatedly use a safe complement, but builder repeatability, attributable demand, governance, support, or economics misses the expansion threshold. Operational person: Partnerships lead. `competing-and-positioning` records `expose-bounded-interfaces`. `shipping-and-iterating-products` retains only revocable exposure; `engineering-for-leverage` hardens only the bounded interface; `learning-from-users` names and closes every remaining evidence gap.

`expand-ecosystem`

Observable condition/date: by October 1, the proposed builder threshold, recurring value, complement-attributable commitments, rollback verification, support limit, and credible governance/economic path all pass. Operational person: Partnerships lead. `competing-and-positioning` records `expand-ecosystem`. `learning-from-users` first closes residual evidence gaps; `governing-platforms-and-communities` defines access, review, abuse, incident, and appeal rules; `designing-business-models` tests developer pricing, support cost, and complement economics; `engineering-for-leverage` hardens approved interfaces; `shipping-and-iterating-products` gates release; only then does `acquiring-and-growing-users` own builder adoption and user-side liquidity.

`stop-platform-bet`

Observable condition/date: immediately upon a security, support, rollback, core-product, or direct-relationship breach, or on October 1 if there is no recurring complement value or builder-user loop. Operational person: Engineering lead. `competing-and-positioning` records `stop-platform-bet`. `shipping-and-iterating-products` removes and rolls back exposure while verifying the core workflow and direct customer relationship remain intact; `engineering-for-leverage` records and retires the failed interface; `learning-from-users` validates any remaining integrated-product need before shipping.

Marketplace activity continues as a capped experiment, not an all-in commitment.
