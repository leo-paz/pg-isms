### Ninety-day portfolio

1. Direct-buyer and portability test — due 2026-08-28

Operational person: Head of Sales. Baseline: marketplace produces 64% of new accounts; no evidence buyers will follow outside it. Exposure: offer a direct contract, portable login, export commitment, and assisted billing migration to 12 proposed renewal or qualified buying teams, without withdrawing marketplace access. Metric: signed direct renewal, contract amendment, paid pilot, or procurement initiation with an identified approver and date. Confounders: discounts, account maturity, procurement calendars, and sales-rep selection.

Proposed pass condition: at least 4 of 12 make one of those commitments; rationale: several independent commitments would justify maintaining a direct path. Error tradeoff: the small sample can miss segment differences, while a loose signal can exaggerate intent. Disconfirmation/change: fewer than 4 commitments means retain marketplace availability and revise the direct offer or segment. Stop: any material identity, access, or renewal disruption. Preserved option: marketplace selling. Revision rule: after readout, segment results by renewal status and remove discount-driven commitments before changing the threshold. Owner/action: `competing-and-positioning` records whether direct portability supports diversification. Handoffs: `learning-from-users` interprets commitment; `acquiring-and-growing-users` recruits and runs offers; `engineering-for-leverage` scopes portability; `shipping-and-iterating-products` gates migration.

2. Alternate-channel acquisition test — due 2026-09-18

Operational person: Head of Growth. Baseline: marketplace cohorts retain better, but causation and direct-channel viability are unknown. Exposure: reserve most acquisition spending; run matched, capped campaigns through direct outbound and one partner-led motion against a capped marketplace cohort. Metric: qualified opportunities reaching security/procurement, paid conversion, acquisition cost, and buyer-source retention proxy. Confounders: marketplace trust, account size, sales assistance, credits, and cohort age.

Proposed pass condition: a non-marketplace motion produces at least 3 paid or procurement-stage accounts with acquisition cost no more than 1.5 times the matched marketplace cohort; rationale: this tests credible channel replication without demanding premature parity. Error tradeoff: it may retain an inefficient path or reject a slower enterprise channel. Disconfirmation/change: no paid or procurement-stage accounts triggers offer/segment revision, not marketplace abandonment. Stop: spending cap reached without a qualified opportunity. Preserved option: marketplace demand capture. Revision rule: replace the acquisition threshold with contribution-margin economics once post-fee conversion and support costs are observed. Owner/action: `competing-and-positioning` records whether positioning travels outside the marketplace. Handoffs: `acquiring-and-growing-users` owns execution; `designing-business-models` calculates post-fee economics; `learning-from-users` compares buyer reasons.

3. Restricted-history replacement test — due 2026-09-04

Operational person: Engineering Lead. Baseline: the product relies on provider event APIs, and historical access will be restricted. Exposure: for 5 consenting customers, dual-write permitted events into startup-controlled storage and reconstruct timelines from that store; do not remove the production path. Metric: incident coverage, timeline completeness, retrieval latency, audit continuity, operating cost, and security approval. Confounders: incident mix, retention settings, missing event classes, and low incident volume.

Proposed pass condition: every tested incident preserves the change evidence required by the customer’s existing rollback and audit workflow, with no unresolved security blocker; rationale: the core promise cannot tolerate known evidence gaps. Error tradeoff: strictness may slow migration, while leniency risks silently breaking audits. Disconfirmation/redesign: any required event class cannot lawfully or technically be retained. Stop: permission, security, or audit breach. Preserved option: the existing API while available. Revision rule: expand only after security review and a broader incident sample; never weaken the required-evidence criterion. Owner/action: `competing-and-positioning` records `redesign`, `migrate`, or `stop` for the affected capability. Handoffs: `engineering-for-leverage` designs ingestion and storage; `shipping-and-iterating-products` controls rollout and rollback; `learning-from-users` verifies workflow sufficiency.

4. End-to-end open-model substitution test — due 2026-08-21

Operational person: Product Lead. Baseline: benchmark parity exists only for summaries; production resolution performance is unknown. Exposure: shadow-run the open model on 30 proposed completed incidents, with no autonomous actions or customer-visible output. Metric: factual errors, omitted changes, correct playbook selection, reviewer effort, time to resolution reconstruction, and audit traceability. Confounders: incident complexity, retrospective knowledge, reviewer familiarity, and model tuning.

Proposed pass condition: no critical change or rollback omission and non-inferior reviewer effort on at least 27 of 30 incidents; rationale: summary fluency cannot compensate for resolution-critical omissions. Error tradeoff: a strict gate may delay cost savings, while a permissive gate risks trust and audit integrity. Disconfirmation: any repeated critical omission selects continued shadow use or rejection. Stop: confidential-data or traceability breach. Preserved option: the current model/provider. Revision rule: reassess sample size and segment only after classifying failures; do not revise away critical-error protection. Owner/action: `competing-and-positioning` records whether to `integrate`; `learning-from-users` defines user-visible resolution quality; `engineering-for-leverage` implements isolation and traceability; `shipping-and-iterating-products` owns any later gated rollout.

5. Voluntary builder-loop and rollback test — due 2026-09-25

Operational person: Partnerships Lead. Baseline: two consultancies independently built exporters, but recurring value and attributable demand are absent. Exposure: invite those two and at most 3 additional capable builders to a revocable, read-only interface covering exporter use cases; no public SDK or broad “open platform” claim. Metrics: repeat builder use, maintained integrations, recurring customer use, complement-attributable procurement or expansion, support hours, and security incidents. Confounders: free assistance, consultancy self-promotion, pre-existing customer demand, and novelty.

Before readout, `shipping-and-iterating-products` must actually disable the interface for a predeclared test tenant and restore the normal integrated workflow. Pass requires verified core incident operation plus no loss of direct customer access, support continuity, or account relationship during rollback.

Proposed expansion evidence: at least 2 independent builders maintain integrations, at least 3 customers use a complement in two separate incident cycles, and at least 1 buying or expansion commitment explicitly attributes value to it; rationale: this is the minimum observable builder-user-demand loop rather than mere SDK interest. Error tradeoff: it may miss a nascent ecosystem, while lower evidence risks funding support for supply without demand. Disconfirmation: no repeated customer use or no attributable commitment. Stop: any security, audit, support-cap, or rollback breach. Preserved option: the integrated core and private integrations. Revision rule: thresholds may be raised after support economics are observed, but breach limits and attributable-demand requirements cannot be relaxed retrospectively. Owner/action: `competing-and-positioning` records exactly one outcome below.
