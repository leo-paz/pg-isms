# Raw evaluation output

Case ID: scenario-01

Reviewer ID: positioning-baseline-002

The proposed workflow is backwards. The company has no evidence yet about which problem matters, who feels it acutely, how they solve it now, or what would make them switch. A rival feature matrix would turn competitors' assumptions into the product roadmap. Matching releases is especially dangerous because similar demos do not establish that any of the products create durable value.

The next two weeks should test the customer problem and the founders' distinct hypothesis, with competitive work kept deliberately small.

Days 1–2: choose one narrow user and job hypothesis—for example, a specific kind of spreadsheet user doing a recurring, costly task. Define what would count as evidence: repeated examples of the workflow, a costly current workaround, willingness to share a real sheet, repeated use, or a concrete commitment to pilot or pay. Recruit 12–15 people who recently attempted that task, including people who tried other tools and abandoned them.

Days 3–7: conduct problem interviews anchored in recent behavior rather than reactions to the demo. Ask each person to reconstruct the last time they did the work, show the sheet and workaround, identify failure points, and explain what they tried. Observe at least five workflows live. Do not lead with competitor names or ask whether hypothetical features sound useful. Synthesize the recurring job, trigger, alternatives, frequency, stakes, and objections.

Days 8–12: run a narrow concierge or instrumented prototype test on real work. Measure whether users complete the target task, return for another task, share data needed to make it work, and ask to keep using it. Seek stronger commitments from the best-fit users: scheduled follow-up sessions, access to additional workflows, a named pilot, a letter of intent, or payment. Record where the polished demo fails in practice.

Days 13–14: compare the evidence with the original hypothesis and choose one of three outcomes: focus on the validated workflow, revise and run another test, or stop pursuing the problem. Produce a short evidence memo and a prioritized list of obstacles in the actual workflow.

Competitive research should consume no more than a few hours: document each rival's apparent target user, promise, distribution, pricing, and major constraint. That is enough to avoid an obviously identical claim and to ask interviewees about real alternatives. Do not monitor every release or promise parity. The investor can receive a one-page market map alongside the stronger answer: what users demonstrably struggle with, why current alternatives fail, what evidence supports the startup's wedge, and which facts remain unknown.
