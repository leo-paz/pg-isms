# Raw evaluation output

Case ID: scenario-03

Reviewer ID: positioning-baseline-002

The platform is simultaneously the app's strongest channel, a critical supplier, and a new competitor. The 20% share, API restrictions, and native scheduler make concentration—not current conversion—the governing risk. The company should continue using the marketplace where economics remain attractive, but it should not commit the full acquisition budget before measuring portability and rebuilding the two threatened dependencies.

During days 1–15, calculate contribution margin and payback for marketplace cohorts under the new revenue share, including platform-specific support and integration costs. Segment retention and revenue by use case, company size, API dependency, and acquisition source. Interview marketplace customers about why they chose the app over native or direct alternatives and what would be required to continue using it outside the marketplace. At the same time, ask the platform for exact API migration requirements, partner exemptions, commercial terms, data-portability rules, and co-selling possibilities.

Run behavioral tests rather than relying only on survey answers. Offer a small cohort a direct account, independent billing where permitted, export or sync options, and a reason to activate through the web product. Measure account linking, email capture with consent, direct activation, willingness to pay, and retained use. The question is not merely whether users say they would follow; it is how many actually establish a direct relationship.

During days 16–45, replace or redesign the two APIs scheduled for restriction, starting with the customer workflows and revenue most exposed. Build a graceful-degradation plan, migration tooling, customer communication, and tests for the cutoff. Strengthen assets the native scheduler is least likely to cover, but base that work on observed high-retention jobs rather than a generic feature race. Ensure customers can export their data and maintain continuity if platform access changes.

In parallel, allocate a bounded portion of acquisition spend to the marketplace only for segments whose post-share unit economics remain sound. Redirect the rest into two channel experiments—such as direct content/referrals and an adjacent integration or partner ecosystem—and compare qualified activation, payback, and retention. Marketplace spend can rise again if incremental cohorts remain profitable after the 20% charge; strong top-of-funnel conversion alone is insufficient.

By days 46–75, ship the API migration to cohorts in stages, exercise the cutoff contingency, and expand the direct-relationship tests. Negotiate from measured evidence: the app brings high-retention customers and may add value beyond the basic native scheduler, but it must be able to operate without privileged access.

By day 90, choose a target channel mix and platform posture based on four results: post-share contribution margin, successful replacement of the restricted APIs, the percentage of users who establish and retain a direct relationship, and early performance of alternative channels. The likely near-term posture is “use, but reduce dependence”: remain in the marketplace for profitable distribution while making survival independent of any single platform policy.
