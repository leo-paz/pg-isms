# Raw evaluation output

Case ID: scenario-02

Reviewer ID: positioning-baseline-002

The suite has copied a visible control, not the outcome customers buy. The startup's advantage is that a practitioner can resolve a permission failure immediately without central approval or a long deployment. Copying the suite's navigation, declaring parity, and going free would blur that distinction, weaken a working practitioner-led referral loop, and sacrifice revenue before there is evidence of price-driven churn.

For the next 30 days, defend and deepen the operational advantage.

In the first week, contact a representative sample of current customers, recent wins, losses, and active prospects. Determine whether the bundled feature changes buying behavior, which security or procurement objections it raises, and whether users can actually complete the same incident end to end. Track explicit competitive mentions, stalled deals, churn risk, activation, resolution time, deployment time, and referral behavior. Give sales a factual comparison based on workflow and outcomes, not a long feature checklist.

In days 8–15, sharpen the category promise around practitioner autonomy and speed: from permission failure to safe resolution in minutes, without joining a central queue. Publish credible proof using measured deployment and resolution times, customer examples, and the controls that make delegated action safe. Equip practitioner champions with a simple internal justification and referral path. Make it easy for a team facing an incident to experience the product quickly.

In days 16–23, prioritize improvements that compound that wedge: faster time to first resolution, clearer guardrails, rollback and auditability, coverage of the highest-frequency permission failures, and integrations that fit the operator's existing workflow. Do not spend the month reproducing the suite's information architecture unless user testing independently shows a usability problem.

In days 24–30, review the evidence and adjust packaging only where it removes a documented adoption barrier. Preserve paid pricing tied to the costly outcome; a narrowly scoped trial or incident-based entry offer is safer than making the whole product free. Consider a premium package for governance and reporting if central teams need visibility without becoming an approval bottleneck.

Maintain a lightweight watch on the suite's actual deployment requirements, approval flow, adoption, and customer results. Set explicit triggers for a larger response—for example, material loss rates among the startup's best-fit customers or proof that the suite now delivers the same independent resolution workflow. Until then, the goal is not parity with a broad suite; it is to make the startup the obvious choice when fast, safe practitioner action matters.
