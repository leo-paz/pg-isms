# Six-week learning plan

Assumed calendar: July 15-August 25, 2026. Current decision: do not build the larger dashboard and do not release the $60,000 growth budget. The thirty requests are evidence of interest, but the four observed sessions contradict the proposed diagnosis. None of the companies has yet produced deployment, repeated use, a signed commitment, or payment evidence. Growth may recruit the learning sample; it does not yet own channel optimization.

## Learning record

- **Product, stage, segment, sample:** Enterprise recruiting SaaS, pre-deployment and pre-revenue. Focus on recruiting teams actively importing candidates and subject to manager approval for retries. Recruit 8 companies: the 2 offering sandbox data plus 6 others with a failed import in the last 30 days. Require participation from both the recruiter who imports and the manager who controls retries. This incident-based, actor-paired sample avoids selecting only enthusiastic dashboard requesters or unusually accessible companies.
- **Primary claim:** When a candidate import fails, requiring a manager to approve every retry creates enough delay and coordination cost that recruiters abandon the workflow and the product; a fast, bounded recovery path will cause successful imports and repeated recruiter use.
- **Causal rivals:** The real blocker could be analytics visibility, unclear notifications, poor source-file quality, lack of recruiter authority, training, integration defects, or a broader deployment/procurement delay. The dashboard request may describe a desired output without explaining abandonment.
- **Existing evidence and contradiction:** Thirty HR directors requested a larger analytics dashboard. In four screen shares, recruiters could already see analytics, yet abandoned after import failure and repeated manager approval. Two companies will share sandbox data. No company has deployed or paid.
- **Disconfirming evidence:** The claim is weakened if recruiters still do not retry or return after recovery is fast; if clean files fail at the same rate; if manager approval is not the time-consuming step; if successful import users then cannot complete their job because of analytics; or if repeated sandbox use does not lead to an authorized deployment step or commitment.
- **Recruitment and consent:** The growth lead recruits against the inclusion rule and may not pitch the dashboard or ads. The founder/learning owner conducts recent-incident interviews and interprets results. Obtain written sandbox consent, data scope, retention/deletion dates, recording consent, and named access. Prefer synthetic or de-identified candidate data; exclude production PII, companies without a recent incident, respondents who cannot expose the workflow, and dashboard-request-only calls with no import behavior to examine.
- **Risks and limits:** Never bypass an actual authorization control. A manager can pre-authorize a bounded sandbox retry session. Cap concierge work at four hours per company per week, log every manual intervention, and stop any path that exposes candidate data beyond consent. Manual repair is a probe, not an unrecorded permanent service.

## Weekly plan

1. **July 15-21 — Instrument the question.** The founder/learning owner logs the four known sessions as observations, not conclusions; recruits the 8-company sample; interviews each recruiter-manager pair about the most recent import failure, sequence, workaround, elapsed time, consequence, and approval step; and defines event fields for attempt, failure cause, notification, approval request, approval time, retry, successful import, next session, and deployment action. Record dashboard needs only after reconstructing the incident.
2. **July 22-28 — Establish the baseline.** Observe at least 10 recent or live import incidents across the sample where safe. For the two consenting sandboxes, reproduce representative failures with de-identified data and measure time, steps, approvals, repair work, and abandonment. Classify failures by file quality, product defect, notification, authority, and process delay. Review on July 28; stop the proposed recovery test if approval delay is absent in both sandboxes.
3. **July 29-August 4 — Run a bounded comparison.** In each sandbox, compare the existing retry flow with a concierge recovery session: diagnose the file, make any manual repair visible, and use one manager-pre-authorized retry window rather than silently bypassing approval. Hold analytics constant. Measure successful completion, time-to-recovery, recruiter effort, manager interruptions, and unprompted next action. Product engineering only supplies reversible instrumentation or a throwaway sandbox aid; it does not begin a dashboard build.
4. **August 5-11 — Test repetition.** Ask the same recruiters to perform new, independent imports under the recovery path without founder prompting. Look for at least three successful import sessions per company across the test and capture whether recruiters return, reuse the workaround, invite a colleague, or request an authorized deployment step. Review on August 11; if only assisted sessions succeed, diagnose the manual dependency rather than counting them as adoption.
5. **August 12-18 — Test enterprise commitment.** Remove concierge help where safe and observe another import cycle. Ask the economic buyer and deployment owner for a concrete next step: a dated security/integration review, a signed time-bounded pilot, or a paid/deployment commitment. Ask users with successful imports to complete the analytics job with the current dashboard; this directly tests the analytics rival. Do not treat a meeting, praise, signup, or subsidized sandbox activity as demand validation.
6. **August 19-25 — Decide and hand off.** Freeze the evidence on August 24 and hold the decision review on August 25. Report company-level behavior, failure causes, repetitions, manual interventions, harms, confounders, and commitments. Apply the thresholds below without averaging away a failed company or upgrading weak evidence.

## Decision thresholds and stop rules

- **Pass for a shipping handoff:** Both sandbox companies complete at least three successful, independent import sessions over at least 14 days; each recruiter returns for a later import without founder prompting; median recovery time falls by at least 50% against that company's baseline; the manager-interruption count falls; and at least one company starts a dated deployment/security process or signs a time-bounded pilot commitment. Product/shipping then owns the smallest reversible import-recovery and approval-flow change. This is permission to build and iterate, not permission to scale acquisition.
- **Pass for a growth handoff:** At least 3 companies in the narrow segment deploy or sign a paid, dated commitment and show repeated import use. Only then does growth own channel experiments, starting with a small staged test rather than automatically spending $60,000.
- **Continue learning, once:** If behavior improves in only one sandbox or the cause is mixed, the founder/learning owner may run one predefined two-week extension on the unresolved rival with a newly recruited qualifying company. No open-ended concierge work.
- **Fail:** If neither sandbox company repeats use after fast recovery, the approval bottleneck is absent, or successful imports reveal another dominant blocker, stop the recovery build. Preserve the evidence and return the problem/segment decision to opportunity choice. If observed successful-import workflows show analytics is the blocker, write a new falsifiable analytics learning record; do not convert the original requests directly into a dashboard specification.

## Ownership handoffs

| Result | Next owner | Handoff contents |
|---|---|---|
| Import/approval claim remains uncertain | Founder/learning owner | Company-level evidence, rival still unresolved, next bounded test, threshold, and stop date |
| Shipping threshold passes | Product/shipping owner | Narrow segment, observed workflow, minimum recovery/approval change, event instrumentation, safety constraints, and acceptance measures |
| Growth threshold passes | Growth lead | Validated segment, exact behavior and commitment evidence, acquisition hypothesis, staged budget cap, and stop criteria |
| Claim fails or points to a different problem/segment | Opportunity-choice owner | Preserved observations, disconfirmed claim, costs and risks, and the unresolved alternative; no silent pivot |
| Any settled decision needs circulation | Communications owner | Proportional claim, evidence strength, decision, owner, review date, and explicit unknowns |

The decision record owner is the founder/learning lead through August 25. Every handoff is accepted by a named owner in the record; until its threshold is met, ownership does not drift to product or growth.
