# Six-week learning plan

Decision now: do not build the larger dashboard and do not approve the $60,000 acquisition program. Thirty requests show interest, not a diagnosis or demand. The four observed sessions point to a more immediate import-and-approval bottleneck, while two sandbox-data offers are useful workflow-access evidence but neither deployment nor payment. The founder owns the learning question through the August 25, 2026 decision review; the growth lead may recruit the sample, but may not optimize channels yet.

## Learning record

- **Product, stage, and segment:** Enterprise recruiting SaaS, pre-deployment and pre-revenue. The working segment is recruiting teams that regularly bulk-import candidates and require manager-controlled retries. The sample will contain 4–6 companies, at least 8 recruiters and 3 approving managers, including the two sandbox volunteers but not relying on them alone. Include teams with a candidate import in the past 30 days, a known retry policy, and permission to use scrubbed or synthetic sandbox data. Deliberately sample both recent import successes and failures and more than one file/source format. Exclude HR directors who cannot expose the operator workflow, production-only data, active hiring decisions, and participants recruited only because they requested the dashboard.
- **Claim:** When a recruiter’s candidate import fails, mandatory manager approval before each retry creates enough delay and lost control that the recruiter abandons the product before reaching analytics. Removing that delay in a bounded sandbox flow should increase completed imports and downstream analytics use.
- **Causal rivals:** The real cause could instead be unclear error visibility, bad source-data quality, insufficient retry authority, missed approval notifications, the approval process’s elapsed time, or an analytics view that is genuinely inadequate after a successful import. The claim is disconfirmed if prompt retries do not improve completed imports or return use, or if recruiters complete imports but still cannot do the analytics job without a larger dashboard.
- **Evidence already observed:** Thirty HR directors asked for a larger dashboard. In four screen shares, recruiters could already see analytics but abandoned after imports failed and every retry needed manager approval. Two companies offered sandbox data. No company has deployed, repeated production use, signed a commitment, or paid. The request and behavior conflict, so neither the feature request nor the causal diagnosis is validated.
- **Recruitment, consent, and risk:** The growth lead recruits against the inclusion rule from the 30 requesters and from non-requester accounts in the same segment, with the founder approving the final sample. A data/security owner obtains written sandbox consent, scope, retention, and deletion terms. Use synthetic or de-identified candidate records, least-privilege access, optional recording, participant withdrawal, and deletion by September 1, 2026. No test result affects a real candidate. Log privacy incidents and stop immediately on unexpected personal data exposure.

## Weekly cadence

1. **July 15–21 — reconstruct behavior.** The founder interviews 8 recruiters and 3 managers about the most recent import: exact sequence, error, workaround, approval path, elapsed time, consequence, and who would approve or pay. A product analyst instruments sandbox events for import start, failure class, approval request, approval, retry, success, analytics action, abandonment, and seven-day return. Review July 21; stop recruiting any company that cannot meet consent or data rules.
2. **July 22–28 — observe the current flow.** In at least three companies, observe representative scrubbed imports under the existing approval process. Record failure-to-retry time, completion, intervention, downstream analytics task completion, and return behavior. Do not pitch the dashboard. Review July 28 to select the common failure classes and verify that the test can be run safely.
3. **July 29–August 4 — run a reversible comparison.** Within each participating company, compare the current flow with a bounded concierge rescue: a manager pre-authorizes retries for the session, support identifies the failure class, and the recruiter can immediately retry in the sandbox while every action remains logged. Keep error messages and analytics unchanged so the comparison isolates retry delay/authority as far as practical. Review August 4 for confounders such as file quality, support effort, novelty, and manager presence.
4. **August 5–11 — repeat without hand-holding.** Give the same recruiters another representative import and downstream analytics task. Remove live support while retaining the bounded pre-authorization. Observe whether they can recover, complete the import, use analytics, and voluntarily return within seven days. Cap concierge work and record minutes per import so custom service does not silently become the product. Review August 11.
5. **August 12–18 — test commitment and the rival.** Ask qualifying companies for a dated, narrowly scoped pilot commitment that requires their own recruiter and manager time and defines the import workflow; do not count verbal enthusiasm. For recruiters who successfully imported but still failed the analytics task, observe the exact missing decision or action before showing any dashboard concept. Review August 18; a dashboard build is considered only if this post-import behavioral rival meets its own threshold below.
6. **August 19–25 — interpret and hand off.** The founder and analyst lock the event log, separate observations from inference, document exceptions, privacy or workflow harms, support cost, and company-level results, then make the August 25 decision against the thresholds. No aggregate may hide that all positive behavior came from one company.

## Thresholds and stop rules

The import/approval claim passes only if the sample includes at least 3 companies, 8 recruiters, and 16 observed import attempts; at least 70% of failed imports in the bounded immediate-retry condition reach successful import and a defined analytics action, with at least a 30-percentage-point improvement over the existing flow; median failure-to-success time falls by at least 50%; at least 5 of the 8 recruiters independently repeat the workflow or return to the downstream job within seven days; and at least 2 companies accept a dated pilot that commits real operator and manager time. Report company-level outcomes as well as totals.

The dashboard rival passes only if, after successful imports, at least 5 recruiters across 3 companies fail the same important analytics job because the current view lacks the same information, and at least 2 companies include that job in a dated pilot commitment. Statements that a larger dashboard would be nice do not count.

Stop a session on personal-data exposure, an effect on a real hiring decision, or an unbounded support requirement. Stop the test early if fewer than 3 eligible companies consent by July 28, if the instrumentation cannot distinguish import failure from approval delay, or if the rescue cannot be isolated safely; record that as insufficient evidence, not failure or validation. Do not spend the $60,000 during these six weeks.
