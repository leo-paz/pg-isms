# Raw forward output

Case ID: compliments-without-commitment

Reviewer ID: learning-forward-002

## Learning Record / decision artifact

**Product, stage, and sample.** This is an unvalidated B2B automated-close product. The initial segment is finance teams whose close still requires manual reconciliation and cross-system data collection. Recruit eight companies that completed a close in the last 30 days, take seven or more business days to close, and have both a controller who performs the work and a budget owner. The twenty prior finance-leader interviews are discovery input, not eighteen validations: none produced workflow access, data, a controller introduction, a pilot, or a payment discussion.

**Claim and rivals.** The claim is: controllers in this segment repeatedly lose consequential time to work the product can automate, and a company with that problem will let the team inspect the real workflow and data, deploy a bounded test, and make a paid commitment. Rivals are social-desirability praise, the finance leader not being the operator, security or integration friction, insufficient urgency, an adequate existing workaround, or a visibility/approval problem rather than an automation problem. Disconfirming evidence includes refusal to involve the controller, refusal to expose even safely redacted artifacts, no recent costly workaround, no deployment owner, or no willingness to discuss price after value is demonstrated.

**Recruitment and safeguards.** The founder owns recruitment and interpretation. Include only the qualified companies above; exclude friends, investors, people without a recent close, and companies recruited merely because they are easy to access. Obtain permission before observing work, minimize and redact financial data, define retention and deletion, and do not move production data until security review permits it.

**Test.** On assumed dates July 15–31, 2026, observe the controller reconstruct the most recent close and collect the sequence, artifacts, workaround, delay, consequence, approver, and current spend without pitching. For qualified companies that expose the same costly step, offer a scoped concierge or sandbox test using redacted data, with a named deployment owner, success metric, security path, decision date, and price. On August 3–September 15, run no more than three such tests through two close cycles where timing allows; log setup work, repeated use, exceptions, manual custom work, and whether the customer pays or signs a commitment contingent only on the predefined test result.

**Thresholds and review dates.** At the July 31 access review, continue only if at least five of eight companies provide controller-level workflow observation and at least three expose usable data or artifacts for a bounded test. At the September 15 decision review, the demand threshold is at least three deployed tests and at least two customers that both use the workflow in two close cycles and pay or sign a dated paid commitment. A letter, undeployed pilot, compliment, or meeting does not count. Stop this cycle if fewer than three companies grant workflow/data access, if the same job does not recur, if required custom work would consume the survival runway, or if a material security risk cannot be bounded.

**Decision.** Freeze a table for all eight companies containing the observed event, workflow evidence, costly behavior, data access, operator and buyer, deployment state, repeated use, price response, confounders, harms, and result; attach the signed or paid evidence and record a pass/fail against the thresholds. If workflow evidence is strong but the product is not yet deployable, hand the falsifiable specification to shipping while learning retains interpretation. Only the repeated-use-plus-payment pass hands this segment to growth. A partial result starts one explicitly revised learning cycle; failure returns the preserved evidence to opportunity choice rather than relabeling praise as validation or pivoting silently.
