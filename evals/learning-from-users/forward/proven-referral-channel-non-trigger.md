## Ownership decision

The primary route is **growth execution** for the proven restaurant segment and accountant-referral channel. Six months of repeat use, 82% retention, and paying accounts are behavioral evidence, so growth can improve referral throughput and activation and run bounded expansion among restaurants that satisfy the same segment definition. It should preserve cohort retention, payment, and source quality as guardrails rather than optimizing signups alone.

Expansion that is genuinely a different segment is first an opportunity-choice question; selecting that adjacent segment does not itself validate it. Ownership returns to user learning when a material need, workflow, or demand assumption becomes uncertain—for example, referred lookalike restaurants sign up but do not complete the workflow, repeat use or payment drops below a predefined cohort guardrail, operators exhibit a different job or approval process, or accountants no longer produce qualified customers. Learning then defines the sample and threshold and diagnoses the break; growth may recruit that sample but should pause channel optimization for the uncertain cohort. Normal variation in an otherwise validated funnel is not by itself a trigger to send the established segment back to discovery.
