## Long-latency Learning Record

**Product, stage, and sample.** This is a high-harm clinical diagnostic with a nine-month outcome latency. Learning owns distinct performance, workflow, safety, and procurement claims. The sample comprises prespecified retrospective specimens from two hospitals, representative of the intended-use population and important subgroups, plus the operators and cases in one supervised laboratory shadow workflow. Access is useful only if labels, inclusion criteria, and intended use are appropriate; convenience samples cannot establish performance.

**Claims and rivals.** The performance claim is that the locked diagnostic meets a clinically accepted sensitivity and false-positive threshold on held-out samples. The workflow claim is that trained lab staff can run and interpret it within the intended turnaround without unsafe workarounds. The safety claim is that invalid results, subgroup failures, and discordant cases are detected and handled. The procurement claim is that a hospital will execute a dated conditional paid pilot when those gates, security, integration, and support terms are met. Rivals include spectrum or label bias, site effects, leakage from tuning, operator assistance that will not exist in practice, downstream confirmatory testing, integration delay, and committee interest without authority or budget.

**Protocol and safeguards.** Before touching outcomes, the clinical lead, an independent statistician, hospital experts, and the procurement committee ratify the intended use, reference standard, subgroup analysis, sample-size calculation, missing-data rules, and acceptance gates. For this record, the explicit assumption to ratify is sensitivity of at least 95% with a lower 95% confidence bound of at least 90%, and a false-positive rate no greater than 5% with an upper 95% confidence bound no greater than 7.5%. Use de-identified data under approved consent/IRB and data-use terms, lock the model before held-out testing, keep hospital results separate as well as pooled, and exclude samples used for tuning from the final estimate.

**Milestone 1 — retrospective performance.** By the assumed protocol-lock date August 14, 2026, preregister the analysis. Each hospital evaluates its held-out set blindly; an independent reviewer audits labels, exclusions, subgroup results, confidence intervals, calibration, invalids, and discordant cases. Review on November 13, 2026. Failure at either site's prespecified safety/performance gate stops clinical advancement and returns the cause to scientific learning; passing permits workflow testing but does not validate outcomes or demand.

**Milestone 2 — supervised shadow workflow.** From November 16, 2026 through January 29, 2027, run the locked system beside standard care. It must not direct patient decisions. Record specimen failures, operator interventions, turnaround, interpretation disagreements, escalation, integration burden, near misses, and performance drift. The assumed workflow gate is at least 95% protocol completion, at least 90% of valid results within the agreed turnaround, no serious safety event, and no unmitigated systematic subgroup or operator failure. Expert and safety review occurs February 5, 2027, with an immediate stop and rollback for a serious incident or threshold breach.

**Milestone 3 — procurement and prospective evidence.** Ask the committee now for a signed conditional pilot defining the numerical gates, deployment owner, budget/payment, integration, monitoring, rollback, and decision date. A signature is procurement evidence, not clinical validation; an unsigned expression of interest does not count. If milestones 1 and 2 and the signed terms pass, shipping may prepare the bounded monitored deployment. Start the assumed prospective pilot February 15, 2027 and freeze the outcome analysis no earlier than November 15, 2027, nine months after the last included decision point as the protocol specifies. Standard care remains available, adverse events and discordances are reviewed independently, and the system rolls back on the prespecified safety triggers.

**Decision artifact and ownership.** Maintain one versioned evidence table linking each claim to its cohort, protocol version, threshold, result, confidence interval, confounders, harms, and signed procurement evidence. At each dated review, record pass, continue, or stop without combining unlike evidence. A retrospective pass advances only performance; a shadow pass advances only workflow and safety; a conditional contract advances only procurement. Only the monitored prospective outcome gate, safe repeated deployment, and paid commitment together support a validated handoff to growth. Failure returns the relevant scientific question to learning or, if the intended use is untenable, sends the preserved evidence to opportunity choice. No two-week interview sprint can substitute for any of these gates.
