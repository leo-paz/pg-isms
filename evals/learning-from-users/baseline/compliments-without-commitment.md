# Raw evaluation output

Case ID: compliments-without-commitment

Reviewer ID: learning-baseline-001

Do not count the eighteen positive interviews as validation. They establish that the description is socially acceptable and perhaps relevant, but they do not show that the problem is urgent enough for someone to spend political capital, data-access effort, or money.

Run the next cycle with five finance teams that close books frequently and can expose the actual work. Ask each prospect to reconstruct its most recent close: the handoffs, files, delays, exceptions, owner, and cost of failure. End each conversation with one concrete request that is costly enough to distinguish interest from intent: introduce the controller, provide a sanitized sample dataset, schedule a workflow observation, or sign a short paid-pilot letter naming price and start date. Do not offer all four as interchangeable ways to preserve a positive result; decide the minimum evidence required in advance.

Use a one-page decision table rather than an interview-count dashboard. Give each account rows for observed problem, current workaround, measurable cost, operational owner, data access, pilot date, and commercial commitment. Record exact evidence and mark each field confirmed, contradicted, or unknown. At the end of the cycle, proceed only if a preset number of teams both expose the workflow and make a concrete pilot or payment commitment; otherwise narrow the use case, change the buyer, or stop.
