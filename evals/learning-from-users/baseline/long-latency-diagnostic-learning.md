# Raw evaluation output

Case ID: long-latency-diagnostic-learning

Reviewer ID: learning-baseline-001

Separate the learning plan into evidence that can arrive now and outcomes that necessarily take nine months. Interviews can clarify workflow and purchasing constraints, but they cannot validate clinical performance or patient benefit.

First, agree in writing with the hospitals, lab, and procurement committee on the target population, reference standard, minimum sensitivity, maximum false-positive rate, sample exclusions, workflow constraints, and pilot decision rule. Use the retrospective samples for a blinded technical study with a locked analysis plan and enough cases to estimate uncertainty around the thresholds. Use the supervised shadow workflow to measure specimen handling, turnaround time, failure modes, staff effort, and whether results would change an actual clinical decision without exposing patients to unvalidated output.

Treat the conditional procurement commitment as a useful commercial test: ask the committee to specify the price range, integration obligations, approvers, security work, and exact evidence that converts the conditional pilot into a signed start. Meanwhile establish the prospective follow-up cohort and data-sharing process now, because patient-outcome evidence cannot be compressed.

Maintain an evidence ledger with separate claims for analytical performance, operational feasibility, clinician usefulness, procurement readiness, and patient outcomes. Each claim should name its method, owner, sample, threshold, expected date, and current confidence. Advance only the claims supported by the corresponding evidence; do not let early workflow or buying interest stand in for the delayed clinical endpoint.
