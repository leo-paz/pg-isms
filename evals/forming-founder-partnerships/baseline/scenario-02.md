# Raw evaluation output

Case ID: scenario-02

Reviewer ID: founder-partnership-baseline-002

Lina should not unilaterally reclaim Mateo's ownership. Mateo disclosed the caregiving constraint and consulting day in advance, his actual work matches that disclosure, and there is no evidence of concealment or retaliation. The workload imbalance is real, but it is a commitment and operating-model problem, not proof that Mateo forfeited past contributions. Their existing documents remain controlling until properly reviewed and amended.

During the first week, they should meet with a neutral facilitator or experienced founder adviser and make the disagreement explicit. Lina should describe the concrete sales, support, and decision burden she can no longer carry; Mateo should state the hours and responsibilities he can reliably sustain while caring for his father. They should also have startup counsel review the 2022 founder, vesting, and IP documents before either person threatens an ownership transfer or departure.

For the remainder of the 30 days, they should run a written operating reset with observable commitments. It should name the owner and deadline for each customer, sales, support, and product responsibility; reflect Mateo's actual 16-hour availability and disclosed consulting day; give Lina authority in the areas she must operate; and provide a short deadlock path so the two stalled product decisions are decided rather than revisited indefinitely. A weekly review should compare promises with completed work. If the imbalance affects current compensation, they can make a prospective compensation adjustment appropriate to the company's cash position without pretending that compensation and already-earned ownership are the same thing.

They should test the enterprise proposal rather than making Mateo's full-time return depend on two encouraging calls. Over the month, Lina and Mateo should agree on a small evidence plan, such as a defined set of buyer interviews, a paid pilot or other credible purchase commitment, implementation requirements, and the revenue or pipeline threshold that would justify the direction. They should decide in advance what evidence would cause them to proceed, stop, or run a longer test. Mateo must likewise say whether meeting that threshold would actually bring him back full time, on what date, and what caregiving contingency could change that promise.

At day 30, they should decide among explicit arrangements. They can continue with unequal time commitments but redesigned roles, authority, compensation, and realistic expectations; agree on a dated path for Mateo to resume fuller participation; or negotiate a separation under the existing documents. Any change to ownership or vesting should be prospective, documented by counsel, and consider both past contribution and future commitment. The partnership should continue only if each can accept the same operating arrangement, the decision mechanism works, and the month demonstrates dependable execution. If Lina requires a full-time cofounder now and Mateo cannot honestly be one, a respectful separation is better than preserving the title while resentment accumulates.
