# Raw evaluation output

Case ID: scenario-01

Reviewer ID: founder-partnership-baseline-002

Mara should not announce Ishan as a cofounder this week or lock in permanent ownership merely because their résumés and intentions look good. The unresolved question is not whether either person is accomplished; it is whether they can make and ship something together under pressure. Four conversations do not answer that.

The employment agreements are the immediate gating issue. Before either person creates code, designs, customer materials, or other potentially assignable work, each should have independent startup counsel review the invention-assignment and related employment provisions. Counsel should identify what they may safely do before August 1, what notices or waivers are needed, and how to keep the design partner's information and the new company's work separate from either employer. They should not use employer time, equipment, accounts, code, or confidential information.

They should then agree in writing to a time-boxed working trial rather than a final founder marriage. The trial agreement should cover confidentiality, ownership of trial work, the design partner relationship, expenses, authority, and what happens to the work if they separate. It should not promise permanent founder equity. Before August 1, they can use whatever counsel says is safe to turn their stated commitments into specifics: personal cash runway, the meaning of eighteen months full time, salary expectations after the eight-month period, outside activities, roles, who decides what, and which events would make either person stop.

From August 1 through the August 21 prototype deadline, they should run a real operating sprint. Mara and Ishan should jointly scope a prototype that can be delivered honestly in three weeks, assign clear owners, put intermediate customer reviews and a rollback plan on the calendar, and work through at least one consequential disagreement instead of smoothing it over. Mara should watch for reliability, speed, candor about bad news, receptiveness to challenge, treatment of the customer, and whether responsibility is genuinely shared. Ishan should be evaluating her on the same facts.

They should schedule a formal go/no-go discussion immediately after the customer review. If the work confirms mutual trust and compatible standards, they can then negotiate ownership based on expected long-term contributions and sign current founder, vesting, IP-assignment, governance, and departure documents with counsel. Equity should vest over time rather than becoming fully earned at formation. If the sprint exposes a serious mismatch, they should end the trial cleanly under its written terms. Ishan's insistence on an announcement and settled ownership before any shared work is itself something Mara should discuss directly; if he will not accept a fair trial and legal cleanup, she should not form the partnership.
