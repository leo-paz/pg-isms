# Raw evaluation output

Case ID: scenario-04

Reviewer ID: founder-partnership-baseline-002

The company should evaluate Rowan as an executive hire, not as a potential founder. Priya and Sam's founder relationship is settled, and Rowan is not asking for founder status, control, or founder ownership. The relevant decision is whether he can lead the current 48-person engineering organization and build the systems, managers, and hiring capacity needed for the next stage.

Before the final interviews, Priya, Sam, and Mei should agree on a scorecard tied to the actual job. It should include delivery and reliability, organizational design, management of managers, hiring quality and pace, retention and performance management, cross-functional leadership, technical judgment, and the ability to scale from 48 engineers toward 100 without assuming that headcount growth is itself success. They should define the outcomes expected at 90 days, one year, and eighteen months and weight the criteria before hearing Rowan's final answers.

The work sample should use a realistic company problem. For example, Rowan could receive the current team structure, delivery constraints, and hiring plan, then present how he would diagnose the organization, sequence a reorganization if needed, develop leaders, and make tradeoffs when hiring falls behind. Interviewers should ask independent, behavior-based questions and record evidence before debriefing together. They should test his claim that he can scale past 100 by asking for specific examples of systems he personally built, what broke, what he delegated, and how he measured results.

The two missed hiring targets are not automatically disqualifying, but the references should be extended to understand the target quality, the causes of the misses, when Rowan surfaced the risk, what he changed, and whether he took responsibility. The successful reorganization should receive the same scrutiny: results, unwanted attrition, delivery impact, and Rowan's precise role. Additional references from a former manager, peer, and direct report would help distinguish a compelling story from repeatable performance.

By July 31, the hiring group should compare the collected evidence with the predetermined scorecard and make a clear hire or pass decision. If Rowan meets the bar, a VP title and market executive option grant are normal employment terms to benchmark against comparable Series C roles, with standard vesting and board approval. The offer should include the agreed success outcomes and an onboarding review cadence. If the evidence supports leading 48 engineers but not the required scaling job, the company should not award the role on confidence alone; it can discuss a narrower position only if that is a real organizational need and Rowan wants it.
