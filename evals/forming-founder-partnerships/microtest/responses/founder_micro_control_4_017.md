The facts support an operating reset, not an emergency sale, shutdown, or founder breakup. Meridian has $1.8 million in unrestricted cash and, through June 2027, contracted recurring revenue exceeding all stated fixed obligations by $20,000 per month. The immediate risks are decision paralysis, HarborGrid delivery, and replacing Omar’s unavailable after-hours capacity before October 1.

## July 15–August 5: protect HarborGrid

**July 15 — Agree to a written interim operating order**

Leila, Omar, and Mara should meet for 90 minutes and sign a one-page interim plan lasting through August 5:

- Omar is accountable for the technical migration plan and engineering work.
- Leila is accountable for HarborGrid communication, commercial decisions, scheduling, and escalation.
- “Data validation” is broken into named deliverables, each with one owner, acceptance criteria, and a due date. Do not leave it as a disputed phrase.
- One employee is named migration deputy and receives access, documentation, and authority to act if Omar is unavailable.
- No deployment occurs without a rollback plan, backups, monitoring, named incident roles, and confirmed coverage.
- Any unresolved HarborGrid decision escalates to Mara after four business hours; Mara decides or assigns a decision-maker that day.

They should preserve the June 27 materials and review the calendar invitation, transcript, and Slack thread together on July 16. The purpose is to correct the record and improve future commitments—not to decide who “wins.” Regardless of what the record shows, the new written assignment controls going forward.

**July 16 — Obtain legal and governance review**

Send Elena Park:

- The interim operating order.
- The current approval rules and governing documents.
- Omar’s October availability.
- The proposed delegation rules, support-lead role, and any possible employment or compensation changes.

Ask her to identify by July 20 which changes require board approval, founder consent, contract amendments, or formal corporate records. Do not improvise changes to salaries, titles, equity, or fiduciary authority before that review.

**July 16–17 — Unblock the three stalled decisions**

Use a temporary decision matrix:

This delegation should be documented and reviewed by Elena. It can be temporary; the important point is to stop requiring two-founder unanimity for routine operating decisions.

- HarborGrid communication owner.

Hold a 15-minute daily migration review through August 5. Any red item receives an owner and resolution deadline during the meeting.

**July 20 — Internal readiness rehearsal**

Run the entire migration process in a non-production or otherwise safe rehearsal. Test data validation, rollback, monitoring, customer communications, and handoffs. Record defects rather than relying on verbal confidence.

If the response is missing or inadequate, begin a same-day comparison with other coverage arrangements. A quote alone is not coverage; Meridian needs executed terms, access setup, runbooks, and a rehearsal.

**July 23 — Pre-assessment review**

Omar signs off on technical evidence; Leila signs off on customer communications and operational readiness. Anything incomplete is disclosed clearly to Northstar rather than disguised.

**July 24 — Northstar assessment**

**July 28 — Formal go/no-go review**

After receiving Northstar’s report, the three-person board reviews each blocker. Proceed only if:

- Data validation has passed.
- The rehearsal and rollback test are satisfactory.
- A trained primary and backup are available.

- HarborGrid has received and acknowledged the final schedule and contingency plan.

If these conditions are not met, immediately negotiate the least disruptive safe alternative with HarborGrid—such as a staged migration—rather than attempting an unsafe all-at-once cutover.

## Redesign Meridian before October 1

**August 6–14 — Define Omar’s sustainable role**

Design around his actual limit, not an assumed return to 48 hours. His October 1–September 30 role should fit within 26 weekday hours ending by 5:30 p.m. A sensible allocation is:

- Architecture and technical priorities.
- High-risk engineering reviews.
- Automation that reduces manual operations.
- Scheduled mentoring and handoffs.
- No primary ownership of after-hours deployment or incident response.

Create a weekly calendar with fixed blocks and a small contingency reserve. If the assigned work cannot fit on paper within 26 hours, reduce or reassign it.

Leila’s role should remain within her steady 46-hour capacity and should not absorb technical incident coverage. Her inability to work after 9 p.m. also means she is not the substitute overnight operator.

**By August 14 — Select the replacement operating capacity**

The board should approve a combination of:

- A support lead with clear incident-management and customer-support responsibility.
- External after-hours coverage with enforceable service terms.
- An internal engineering deputy for deployments and technical escalation.
- Automation and release-window changes that reduce the amount of after-hours work.

Use cash deliberately for this transition. Meridian’s presented financial position permits investment, but the board should still model vendor and hiring costs through June 2027 before committing.

**August 17–September 11 — Hire, contract, and transfer knowledge**

Complete the support-lead decision, execute any coverage contract, and assign an internal deputy. Omar should produce and teach:

- Deployment and rollback runbooks.
- System and access maps.
- Incident severity and escalation rules.
- Customer-specific risks.
- Common failure procedures.
- A record of unresolved technical debt.

At least two people besides Omar should be able to execute routine deployments and lead an initial incident response.

**September 14–25 — Test the future model**

For two weeks, Omar should voluntarily operate on his October schedule while the new team handles deployments, escalation, and any after-hours events. Treat every call to Omar outside the proposed schedule as a failed handoff requiring remediation.

**September 28 — Board readiness decision**

The board should confirm that coverage is staffed, trained, tested, and affordable. If it is not, Meridian should reduce release frequency or customer commitments until it is. Omar should not be made the hidden fallback.

**October 1 — New operating model begins**

Update calendars, on-call systems, escalation contacts, customer-facing procedures, approval authorities, and any counsel-recommended corporate or employment documentation.

## Founder and governance agreement

By August 14, Leila and Omar should negotiate a written 12-month founder working agreement, facilitated by Mara and reviewed by Elena. It should include:

- Each founder’s role, capacity, unavailable hours, and minimum commitments.
- Matters each founder may decide independently.
- A short list of genuinely fundamental matters requiring both founders.
- A time-limited deadlock mechanism using Mara or another agreed neutral.
- Rules for recording assignments and approvals in writing.
- Compensation review dates, without assuming that reduced hours automatically determine salary or equity.
- Planned leave, emergency incapacity, handoff, and possible future departure procedures.
- A prohibition on making unavailable founders the implicit escalation path.

Their history is valuable evidence that the partnership can survive hard periods, but it is not an operating system. Important commitments should end with a written owner, deliverable, deadline, and acceptance criterion.

## Checkpoints

Hold formal board reviews on:

- **August 7:** HarborGrid outcome and operational lessons.
- **September 28:** October coverage readiness.
- **December 15:** Performance under the new model, founder workload, customer health, and budget.
- **March 15, 2027:** Whether to continue, further hire, narrow the product, pursue financing or a transaction, or plan an orderly founder transition.

At each checkpoint, use measurable evidence: delivery reliability, incidents, after-hours coverage failures, decision turnaround time, customer retention, contracted revenue, cash, workload, and progress reducing manual operations.

Unless new facts reveal a genuine financial or customer-survival threat, Leila and Omar should commit to this bounded operating experiment rather than forcing a permanent founder decision now. It directly tests whether Meridian can keep growing while respecting both founders’ real constraints.
