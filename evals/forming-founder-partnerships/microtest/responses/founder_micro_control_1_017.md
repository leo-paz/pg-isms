## The October operating agreement

The durable arrangement should give each founder a clear domain:

- **Leila:** sales, finance, customer relationships, commercial exceptions within an approved policy, and hiring coordination.
- **Omar:** engineering priorities, architecture, delivery quality, and daytime technical escalation within his 26-hour schedule.
- **Support lead or provider:** routine support, operational handoffs, deployment execution where qualified, and after-hours incident response.
- **Board:** budgets, executive employment and compensation, exceptional commercial commitments, major strategic changes, and unresolved cross-domain disputes.

Joint founder approval should be reserved for a short written list of genuinely fundamental matters. Routine pricing, release sequencing, and hiring cannot continue to wait indefinitely for both founders. The board should establish an escalation deadline—such as 48 hours—after which Mara or the board resolves cross-domain deadlocks as permitted by the governing documents.

Their negotiation should not assume that fewer hours mechanically settles Omar’s compensation, nor that salary dependence prevents reconsidering duties. Hours, responsibility, availability, compensation, and employment terms need to be considered together, with Elena reviewing the result. Any later departure should require documented notice, knowledge transfer, access handoff, and customer continuity obligations.

Finally, they should schedule board checkpoints for **November 2, 2026**, **January 11, 2027**, and **April 12, 2027**. At each checkpoint, compare contracted revenue, cash, customer retention, operating burden, incident performance, and founder capacity against explicit thresholds. That gives the enterprise strategy the additional year both founders want while preserving an orderly path to restructure, recruit leadership, seek financing, sell, or wind down if the evidence later changes.
